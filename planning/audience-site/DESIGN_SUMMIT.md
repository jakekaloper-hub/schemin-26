## Summit charter and business mechanics
Business objective: make the existing Schemin universe understandable, immersive and accessible to a viewing audience while preserving one world, one canon and controlled publication.

Audience: returning league members, interested newcomers and readers exploring the Commonwealth. These are working audience hypotheses, not completed research.

Chair: Closer perspective. Product accountability: GM; audience positioning: Agent. Architecture: Architect; delivery: Setup Man and Groundskeeper. Provenance: Librarian; publication evidence: Scout. Independent acceptance procedure: Umpire. Creative work: Beat Writer plus temporary project-local visual design capability. These are proposed responsibilities within existing authority, not new permanent Directors.

Each simulated day uses America/New_York time:
09:00–09:30: evidence and previous action readback.
09:30–11:00: proposal and adversarial examination.
11:15–12:00: department working session.
13:00–14:30: revised proposal and scenario walkthrough.
14:45–15:30: decision meeting.
15:30–16:00: action register, dissent and next-day handoff.

Sessions are decision-focused. A failed motion must be revised, rejected with reasons or carried forward with an owner. A director may not invent evidence to close an action. Planning decisions below are recommendations within the simulation, not actual votes or release receipts.

## Day 1 — What business are we building?
Morning motion: “Build a destination for every Schemin file.”

Agent: “An audience does not want our file system. It wants a reason to enter, a place to begin and something worth returning for.”
GM: “League members need the newest memo immediately. Newcomers need the world explained. A single dramatic doorway may serve neither.”
Beat Writer: “If the first screen is scores and standings, we have reduced the Commonwealth to a decorative league dashboard.”
Architect: “And if the first screen requires a cinematic sequence, the regular reader pays a tax every visit.”

Afternoon revision:
Agent: “One homepage, two clear intentions: follow the league or explore the Commonwealth. The latest eligible memo remains immediately reachable.”
Umpire: “Do not label anything latest until publication resolution proves it.”
Closer: “Accepted as a proposed product principle. The homepage must explain Schemin, expose current reading and invite exploration without requiring an introduction to football.”

Decision D01: one public destination serving multiple intentions, with no mandatory intro sequence.
Dissent retained: editorial order remains a design hypothesis; visitor observation must test it.
Action A01 — GM/Agent: specify three visitor journeys by Day 2. Deliverable: journey steps, destinations and failure conditions.

## Day 2 — What content can we honestly offer?
Librarian: “The site must not treat public repository presence as publication approval.”
GM: “If inventory work takes weeks, the audience sees nothing.”
Scout: “Then inventory the demonstration sources first. Do not audit every historical file before proving one journey.”
Beat Writer: “We can introduce twelve characters without claiming twelve finished novels.”

Architect: “Separate identity records, publication status and presentation. The publishing selection may reference approved records; it cannot approve them.”
Umpire: “An eligible chapter must be identified precisely. ‘Some chapter exists’ is insufficient.”
Closer: “Narrow the initial inventory to the proof, then extend it for the first edition.”

Decision D02: first inventory one connected journey plus a Twelve overview, latest memo candidate and available novel entry point. Unknown eligibility remains unresolved.
Decision D03: retain original canonical identity when teams change names; avoid equal-depth biography fabrication.

Action A02 — Librarian with Scout challenge: source table containing canonical ID, path, version/hash where available, eligibility basis, dependencies and missing assets. Due before implementation content selection.
Carryover: no claim that the newest uploaded memo is the newest released memo.

## Day 3 — What does immersion actually mean?
Visual design perspective: “A strong full-bleed image, deliberate type, controlled movement and atmospheric transitions.”
Setup Man: “That can also describe a slow page with unreadable controls.”
GM: “A grid of clean cards can be fast and still feel generic.”
Beat Writer: “The environment should connect the pages. A territory should feel related to its character and the scene the reader reaches.”

First motion: animated entrance and expansive map as the defining experience.
Umpire challenge: “What happens on a phone, keyboard navigation or reduced-motion settings?”
Visual revision: “Atmosphere must survive without animation. Artwork, composition and typography carry identity first. Motion helps orientation.”

Decision D04: visual benchmark consists of three connected screens—entrance, location/character view and reader—shown at desktop and mobile widths. No autoplay sound or mandatory loading spectacle.
Rejected alternative: buying a generic fantasy template and assuming its aesthetic constitutes Schemin identity.
Action A03 — Visual design under creative authority: one art-direction board and concrete screen specifications using eligible existing artwork. Compare actual crops, type sizes and navigation, not adjectives.
Carryover: aesthetic quality requires founder review of a working preview.

## Day 4 — How much world must the first proof contain?
GM: “A one-character proof risks making the universe feel small.”
Architect: “Twelve bespoke experiences before validation risks twelve unfinished experiences.”
Agent: “Communicate the ensemble broadly; demonstrate depth narrowly.”
Beat Writer: “Choose a journey with a meaningful relationship or consequence, not the easiest available records.”

Librarian: “The Atlas already distinguishes physical zones, owner domains, travel and state. A redesign cannot quietly replace that model.”
Visual design: “Can we redraw a more beautiful map?”
Scout: “Not by inventing terrain and presenting it as established fact.”

Decision D05: show the Twelve where approved material permits; fully develop one representative connected journey for the proof. Missing content is not filled with invented canon.
Decision D06: reuse canonical geography and identities. Inspect existing atlas presentation before deciding what to retain. A schematic view must not imply precise distances.
Action A04 — Architect and world-domain reviewer: trace one location to character and eligible publication using actual records; identify missing relationship links.
Dissent retained: visual map quality may require substantial presentation work even when data reuse is successful.

## Day 5 — Reader, memo and historical record
Beat Writer: “The novel deserves sustained reading space. The memo deserves quick browsing and issue access.”
GM: “Can both use one layout?”
Beat Writer: “A shared design system, yes. Identical layout, no.”
Architect: “The reader prototype must be inspected. A historical test count does not establish current reload behavior or content integration.”

Agent: “Should we market selectable POV reading at launch?”
Umpire: “Only for accepted content and functioning paths. A selector cannot create missing manuscript.”
Closer: “Our promise must fit available reading.”

Decision D07: distinct memo and novel reading layouts share navigation and visual identity. PDF access may be retained alongside accessible HTML when an eligible adaptation exists.
Decision D08: no rewriting approved manuscripts to fit the interface. Character POV paths are conditional on actual accepted scene manifests and working reader behavior.
Action A05 — Architect/Beat Writer: reader integration checklist covering direct links, chapter navigation, text fidelity, reload and progress behavior; memo access checklist including issue identity.
Carryover: historical archives can expand progressively; unfinished seasons must not appear complete.

## Day 6 — Architecture and public viewing
Architect: “The website is a consumer of canonical content, not a second operating system.”
GM: “What is the minimum technology choice?”
Setup Man: “We cannot responsibly choose before inspecting the existing application and deployment constraints. A stack name today does not resolve compatibility.”
Warden: “View-only means visitors cannot alter canonical records. It does not prevent copying public text or downloading published files.”

Initial motion: accounts and cloud-synced progress.
Bookkeeper: “That adds services and maintenance before proving demand.”
GM concession: “Use device-local progress initially if implemented and tested; make its limits clear.”

Decision D09: no-login public viewing; no audience editing endpoints. No credentials in browser bundles. Accounts and cross-device sync are deferred.
Decision D10: content selection references upstream authority. No new parallel geography, character canon or manuscript authority.
Action A06 — Architect/Setup Man: inspect build structure, reader, atlas, deployment needs and publication integration; compare the smallest compatible options.
Unresolved: framework and hosting; must be selected from evidence rather than familiarity.

## Day 7 — Economics, maintainability and publication workflow
Bookkeeper: “Installed plugins and trial credits are not a sustainable budget.”
Agent: “A no-spend constraint cannot become an excuse for a bland experience.”
Groundskeeper: “Most of the value here is presentation and integration. Spend is not our first dependency.”

GM: “Who updates the site every Wednesday?”
Librarian: “Approved publication selection should drive the update. Staff should not manually recreate every memo page.”
Umpire: “Automation still needs to reject drafts and broken references.”
Setup Man: “And each published version needs a known rollback target.”

Decision D11: zero incremental spend. No new subscription, metered service or domain purchase is assumed.
Decision D12: build an explicit, repeatable publication/update process with validation and rollback. A published website must not silently expose all repository folders.
Action A07 — Groundskeeper/Bookkeeper: hosting/access/cost verification and update ownership.
Action A08 — Librarian/Setup Man: publication workflow sketch and failure/recovery procedure.
Dissent retained: a desired custom domain may need later owner authorization and verified availability; it is not on the proof’s critical path.

## Day 8 — Delivery program and capacity
Closer: “The summit needs a delivery brief. We are not approving the whole universe at once.”
GM: “Do not turn the small proof into permission to permanently underdeliver.”
Architect: “Then distinguish proof scope from first-edition scope and expansion scope.”

Proposed proof:
1. Distinctive homepage with direct access to eligible memo content.
2. Atlas exploration of one established location.
3. Connected character view, with ensemble context.
4. Real approved reading content.
5. Reliable return navigation on desktop and mobile.

Proposed first edition after proof acceptance: homepage, Twelve overview, eligible memo archive, audience atlas and approved novel reader.
Deferred: accounts, comments, monetization, live AI guide, multiplayer features and automatic live league updates.

Groundskeeper: “Can we promise a completion date?”
Setup Man: “Not before integration findings reveal dependencies and available capacity.”
Closer: “Commit to ordered deliverables and exit conditions now; estimate dates after investigation.”

Decision D13: staged delivery with dependency gates; no invented deadline or staffed capacity.
Action A09 — Closer: integrated brief and work packages.
Action A10 — Architect: estimate after source inspection, separating reusable components, repairs and new presentation work.
Carryover: further implementation remains a distinct phase from the requested summit.

## Day 9 — Adversarial review and reopening decisions
Umpire: “The first version of our proof could pass its links and still fail Jake’s experience.”
GM: “Agreed. We need functional and experiential acceptance.”
Scout: “And every public claim must survive the source check.”
Beat Writer: “Do not convert the acceptance report into audience-facing clutter.”

Challenge 1: a beautiful homepage leads to an unreadable PDF on a phone.
Revision: demonstrate actual memo reading/access behavior on mobile; do not judge only the homepage.

Challenge 2: an atlas looks precise but only abstract topology exists.
Revision: preserve topology and clearly frame the view; new authoritative artwork requires world review.

Challenge 3: progress is advertised but disappears in a tested reload.
Revision: remove the promise or repair and retest before acceptance.

Challenge 4: “latest” resolves from filename or date rather than release authority.
Revision: use publication resolution; retain explicit week links.

Challenge 5: all Twelve appear, but several cards lead nowhere.
Revision: every displayed control must have an honest destination; introductions may exist without fabricated available chapters.

Decision D14: the acceptance matrix below is mandatory for the proposed proof. Any blocking failure returns to its owner; no aggregate score can hide it.
Action A11 — Independent reviewer: prepare adversarial scenarios and evidence template. Actual acceptance remains pending a build.
Dissent retained: founder visual judgment cannot be replaced with a numerical quality score.

## Day 10 — Reconciliation, resolution and handoff
Closer: “Read back what changed because of challenge.”
Agent: “The promise narrowed from ‘all content’ to a coherent entry into eligible content. The world remains broad; demonstration depth is bounded.”
Architect: “Engineering accepts art direction as essential scope. We cannot substitute a generic content interface.”
GM: “Product accepts inspection and publication gates. We cannot promise nonexistent reading paths.”
Bookkeeper: “Cost discipline stays explicit, but does not prohibit strong design work within existing capabilities.”
Umpire: “I accept the proposed acceptance procedure for planning purposes. I do not certify a website that has not been built.”
Librarian: “The summit is a derived planning record. It supersedes the earlier superficial summit outline, not canonical project controls.”

Final proposed resolution: proceed from the summit to current-state inspection and a concrete website design brief. Evaluate a working connected proof before expanding to the public first edition. Preserve existing systems, approved canon and founder experiential judgment.

The board does not claim ten calendar days elapsed, that named humans attended, that all Directors executed independently, that a website exists, or that release gates passed.

## Work packages and dependency order
WP1 — Current-state inspection. Architect accountable, Librarian supplies authority. Find current atlas, reader, website-related tasks and eligible content. Exit: evidence-based integration findings and precise gaps.
WP2 — Experience design. GM accountable with creative ownership. Produce actual entrance, atlas/location and reader screen specifications; define all three journeys. Depends on WP1 content availability.
WP3 — Publishing and integration design. Architect accountable. Select compatible stack/hosting, define derived publication selection, identity links and update process. Depends on WP1; reconcile with WP2.
WP4 — Connected proof implementation. Architect owns code; visual capability owns presentation. Depends on the applicable design handoff and verified inputs.
WP5 — Independent review and repair. QA distinct from creator. Run matrix; fix failures and retest affected journeys.
WP6 — Founder preview. Present the working proof, evidence and limitations. Aesthetic disagreement returns to focused design revision.
WP7 — First-edition expansion and deployment readiness. Expand eligible archive/ensemble/reader/atlas coverage after proof acceptance. Verify hosting, public update and rollback behavior before a release decision.

No calendar delivery commitment is made before WP1 establishes component readiness and capacity. No implementation or deployment is represented as completed by this plan.

## Decisions and unresolved inputs

Open for investigation: current component paths/behavior, actual eligible publication set, deploy-compatible framework, existing hosting access/limits, asset portability and presentation suitability.
Founder judgment reserved: whether the concrete visual experience meets the intended atmosphere. This is not a request to approve routine reversible technical choices.
