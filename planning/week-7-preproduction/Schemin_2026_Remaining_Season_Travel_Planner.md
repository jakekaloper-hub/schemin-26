## How to use the physical environment column

**Existing feature → proposed seasonal dressing → NEW proposed subsite.** The first phrase comes from the registered parent location; bracketed provisional/interpretive status is retained. The bold dressing and subsite entries are editorial possibilities, not weather forecasts, registered place-names, approved construction or locked scenes. A named subsite can be a small useful patch of existing terrain, not a new building. Both sides of each fixture share the same candidate.

The planning anchor is **not automatically the actual venue or main base**. Try the existing ridge, yard, channel, court or pavilion first; use the proposed subsite only if it improves both characters’ motives. All named subsite offsets, ingress, accessibility and permissions are **UNVERIFIED**. Displayed graph bounds still end at the original registered anchor, not at that unregistered subsite; actual arrival-to-departure travel requires recalculation after a site is accepted. “Stay” likewise does not validate a local transfer. No new graph edge is implied.

Seasonal dressing follows the regional autumn brief, not exact future weather. Week 7: no snow; birds need not have left; warmer wetlands differ from exposed coast and Upper Valleys. Week 8 may support Halloween details selectively. Weeks 9–14 require a fresh seasonal/continuity choice rather than repeating October decoration or assuming snow. El Niño requires separate atmospheric propagation evidence; ordinary wind does not prove its presence.

Registered source states also matter: Jackson’s historical contamination needs cleanup evidence before a pristine reset; LLC’s prior damage needs repair/scar continuity. New sites must pass the existing candidate/venue process; a neutral move still needs a recorded override. None is promoted by this document.
