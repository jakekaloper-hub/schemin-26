## 3. Round one: authors open the field

The room begins with people, not a prop list. The following are simulated editorial contributions, not statements by the named writers.

- **World-depth lens:** What ordinary seasonal work makes this place feel occupied when the principals are absent? Let harvest, repairs, damp clothing or a closing daylight window reveal culture without narrating an encyclopedia.
- **Ensemble lens:** Which rival has an objective that is not simply defeating the other? Make the encounter interrupt that objective rather than manufacture six identical challenges.
- **Setup/payoff lens:** Plant one legible detail that could matter later. Do not promise six Halloween sequels or secretly lock Week 8's plot.
- **Propulsion lens:** Enter late enough that something is already happening. What immediate decision cannot wait until the weather improves?
- **POV/voice lens:** Let the two figures notice different aspects of the same autumn day. One notices a practical inconvenience; another sees a chance to prolong an enjoyable moment. Avoid universal ominous narration.
- **Systems/limits lens:** Let a physical rule stay reliable: wet leaves slip, a concealed view prevents certainty, wind changes an exposed route. No convenient magical exception to rescue a gag.
- **Human-scale action lens:** Find expressive, awkward, generous or stubborn behavior before naming the emotion. Do not make embarrassment the required payoff of every matchup.

### Three competing issue approaches

**A. One Commonwealth harvest celebration.** Strong shared identity and opportunities for crossing lives. Rejected as the current issue spine: it invents an event, pressures travel/geography and risks turning all six fixtures into fairground stations.

**B. Six escalating seasonal scares.** Strong immediate Halloween recognition. Deferred as a Week 8 research direction, not selected now: it peaks too soon and makes every character react to the same machinery.

**C. Six lives interrupted by a changing season.** Selected for exploratory development. Autumn changes what people are doing, but each encounter has a different engine: intimacy, obligation, play, concealment, status or hospitality. Some scenes can be funny and some quiet. A scene may contain no pumpkin at all.

**Dissent retained:** approach C could become six tasteful but inert atmosphere studies. The propulsion counterweight therefore requires a meaningful choice or change of relationship in every developed sequence, not just beautiful leaves.

## 4. Round two: divergent encounter pitches

All incidents below are fictional proposals. No result, prior event, owner psychology or permanent location is asserted as fact.

### TDS × Duckhook: who is allowed to linger?

**Route A — One More Hole.** A private round should have ended before dusk. One figure keeps finding a reason for another shot; the other wants to leave without admitting why. Damp ground and fading light narrow their choices. The scene's turn is not who hits farther, but who realizes that continuing the contest is also continuing the company.

**Route B — The Wrong Invitation.** A small seasonal preparation at the host's grounds is mistaken for an event intended for the visitor. The visitor begins helping; the host must either correct the assumption or let the unexpected collaboration change the afternoon. This needs no elaborate prank or invented festival.

**Room preference:** explore A for its quiet contrast with louder matchups. Keep B as a warmer alternative if the current character packets do not support A's implied intimacy. Golf remains optional; do not invent why Pittsy's closed.

### DK × El Niño: what is a fortress for?

**Route A — The Open Door.** DK is trying to complete an ordinary preparation when weather makes the impressive entrance the least useful part of the stronghold. The choice is whether to preserve ceremonial order or let the practical work move through the space differently. No helpless crowd is invented to make him heroic.

**Route B — The Missing Sound.** Preparation is interrupted not by louder thunder, but by one familiar sound going absent: loose shutters, birds at a known ledge, or workers' tools, only if the location packet supports it. DK misreads the absence, checks the wrong thing and must change his attention. The weather is a material pressure, not an opponent delivering dialogue.

**Room preference:** B for further writing because it offers a genuinely different sensory engine. Do not assume a disappearing sound proves magic or that birds have migrated. If this becomes generic storm foreboding, return to A's tangible task.

### ObiWan × Seven Deadly Chins: can a game remain a game?

**Route A — The Last Warm Patch.** Both seek the same sheltered patch on an otherwise exposed approach. An initially playful negotiation becomes a contest of territory, etiquette and movement before weapons need appear. The season creates the scarce comfort; the characters create the comedy or friction.

**Route B — A Duel With Something Else to Do.** A challenge arrives during a practical seasonal task. Each tries to continue the task while refusing to yield the contest. The physical choreography reveals conflicting priorities; it cannot be solved by a borrowed film quotation.

**Room preference:** A as the cleanest departure from automatic heroic combat. Reserve B if a canon-supported task offers better personal specificity. No new elevated terrace, bridge or dangerous edge is invented for convenience.

### Mud Dogs × Chili: a shared pleasure or another thing to win?

**Route A — Let It Pass.** Their hunt gives them a fleeting view worth enjoying before either can turn it into proof of superiority. One motion could spoil the moment for both. The choice is between preserving the sighting and claiming the encounter. No animal is killed, photographed or awarded in this pitch.

**Route B — The Borrowed Shape.** A seasonal field-edge object resembles a person just long enough to expose how differently the hunters read the landscape. It is not automatically a trap planted by either. One can reveal the mistake or let the other keep looking, creating a choice about trust rather than another clever prank victory.

**Room preference:** A. It restores a moment of genuine enjoyment to the rivalry and avoids presuming the wetland contains a cornfield. B stays conditional on an actual cultivated edge and should be dropped if geography does not support it.

### Red Leopards × HMB: what changes when nobody is watching?

**Route A — Before the First Point.** Preparations for the private game take longer than the game itself initially seems likely to. A small cooperative act makes their competitive behavior more pointed when they finally begin. The belt can sit in frame without becoming the explanation of every gesture.

**Route B — The Rule Nobody Needed.** A harmless condition interrupts the game. They agree on an improvised, clearly fictional accommodation, then discover it exposes a disagreement neither expected to care about. Resolve character positions, not the eventual winner.

**Room preference:** A. It gives the already strong basketball premise a contrasting opening and allows rivalry to be affectionate, tense or both. Keep the established no-audience intimacy. Do not create slippery dangerous play, a cliff stunt, a public court or a real league rule.

### Slob × LLC: who gets to feel at home?

**Route A — Leave That There.** The host is in the middle of deciding what to keep out and what to put away for the season. The visitor treats one apparently trivial object with unexpected care or dismissiveness. Their poker game is then colored by the different value assigned to it. The object must be drawn from existing approved assets or clearly proposed, not a fabricated precious heirloom.

**Route B — Too Early for the Scare.** Preparations for a later Halloween surprise are encountered before they are ready. Instead of a successful fright, the pair must inhabit the awkward moment afterward. One offers to help; accepting the help risks surrendering control of the room. The card game can wait rather than swallowing every interaction.

**Room preference:** B. It permits warmth and awkward humor without the prior dot brief's automatic poker-tell payoff. The reveal must not rely on an unsupported helicopter or a newly invented haunted mansion.

## 5. Round three: all twelve production domains challenge the pitches

These are original simulated domain checks, not opinions attributed to the professionals.

1. **Production design:** Reject a kit of identical pumpkins placed in six different rooms. Every selected prop must have a user, material and task. DK's preparation and Slob's unfinished scare need different object vocabularies.
2. **Environment design:** Check each site against the host location and route. Keep wetland greens, exposed coastal damp and valley color distinct. A field-edge pitch fails if no suitable land exists.
3. **Character VFX:** Weather must not rewrite anatomy. Preserve current canonical references, including TDS's single body with three serpent heads and Wilson's integrated four-legged gorilla-centaur. Do not turn El Niño into an actor in a costume.
4. **Costume:** Seasonal response should be selective: fastening a layer, moving a glove, drying cloth. Do not give everyone an identical cloak or Halloween outfit that hides recognition.
5. **Performance:** Stage what each character wants before adding dialogue. A decision to stay, help, protect a view or surrender a warm spot is more readable than a caption declaring rivalry.
6. **Stunt blocking:** Keep the duel alternative and basketball spatially legible. No edge-of-cliff escalation simply because it looks dramatic; visible positions and safe coherent surfaces come first.
7. **Pose/silhouette:** Test whether an important gesture reads with faces obscured. A hunt's arrested movement and a host's half-offered helping hand need different silhouettes.
8. **Unit stills:** Choose the instant immediately before or after the decisive choice, not automatically the biggest action. Proposed stills must not predetermine a Week 7 victory.
9. **VFX pipeline:** Limit unnecessary simulations. Wind, foliage and wet surfaces should reinforce the intended beat; more moving elements are not automatically more story. Actual generation remains gated.
10. **Color finishing:** Maintain a shared issue feel without one orange grade. The wetland and sheltered interior should remain distinct environments even at thumbnail scale.
11. **Editing:** Do not order six stories by the same arrival/challenge/joke/reveal sequence. Contrast the sustained pause of the hunt with the interrupted preparation at Slob's, and make page turns change understanding.
12. **Sound/motion:** For a still memo, imply sound through physical evidence, not invented audio delivery. DK's absence-of-sound premise must remain understandable in prose and image; for future film, validate a distinct sound plan separately.

**Production counterweight verdict:** The six preferences are development hypotheses, not production approvals. The quiet concepts need evidence of actual dramatic movement. The more playful concepts need original character behavior, not nostalgia references doing the writing.

## 6. Independent critical pass: objections retained

A separate critical assignment proposed three alternative issue engines: **Contested Atlas** (terrain changes what counts as competence), **Six Contracts** (rivals disagree about the terms of acknowledgment), and **Unfinished Testimony** (an ambiguous trace precedes the choices that produced it). These are genuine alternatives to a purely chronological issue, not an endorsement of the preferred frame.

The strongest objection is that choosing “six lives interrupted” from a pitch alone could repeat dot's earlier mistake in a more formal voice. The Author Room therefore downgrades that choice to a **working hypothesis**. Before choosing issue architecture, test one matchup in three short forms: continuous encounter, negotiated acknowledgment, and trace-first reconstruction. Do not make Jake pick from abstractions.

The critical pass also suggested an opponent designing a rival's entrance, and an ordinary interruption making a public persona harder to maintain. Retain both as optional engines, but do not use either six times. A new NPC, passage or social audience remains proposed connective fiction, not evidence that it exists in canon.

One contribution incorrectly said El Niño must have character choices. **Rejected:** the current environmental-only constraint takes precedence. Human principals can make choices in response to the weather; no humanoid performance, speech or unsupported interiority is assigned to El Niño. The error is preserved here to demonstrate that a review is not automatically authority.

### Selected next development unit

After the three-form architecture test above, the simulated Author Room recommends drafting **two contrasting, outcome-neutral prose treatments**: Mud/Chili's *Let It Pass* and Slob/LLC's *Too Early for the Scare*. This choice tests the room's range: wonder and restraint versus awkward hospitality. It is not a claim these are the final matchup plots.

For each, prepare one short continuous scene and two alternate turns. Include both characters' active wants, the scene's change, exact source constraints and one production-board challenge. Then compare them with the original concepts. If they only add seasonal texture without improving the story, reject them and develop the next alternative. No need for Jake to supply the jokes, endings or shot plan.

The remaining four preferences receive brief encounter outlines only until the architecture and contrast tests prove useful. Week 8 keeps the larger seasonal possibilities open; it does not inherit a locked haunted house or corn maze from this session.
