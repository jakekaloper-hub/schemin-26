# Schemin ’26: Autumn Arrives Before Halloween
## Week 7 early-preproduction creative brief

**Status: exploratory recommendations only.** Prepared October 24, 2026 from Jake’s new seasonal direction, current repository evidence and earlier personal story hooks. No story lock, artwork, repository edit, result, route approval or canon promotion occurred. This is editorial synthesis, not an authenticated Director meeting.

## Editorial decision

Make Week 7 feel like a world changing season, not six Halloween attractions. Autumn should affect light, clothing, sound, footing, hospitality and choices. Use pumpkins, harvested materials and early preparations selectively. Reserve the larger corn-maze, haunted-house and full Halloween premises for Week 8, with its fixtures and venues still to be resolved.

Three approaches considered:
1. Decoration on every page: immediately recognizable but visually repetitive and geographically false.
2. A Halloween plot for every matchup: stronger novelty but crowds out Jake’s personal hooks and peaks too early.
3. **Recommended: regional autumn plus two or three plot-relevant seasonal details.** Keep the six stories distinct; make the season matter when it improves the scene.

Jake’s direction supplies a proposed Week 7 weather treatment: no snow, cold gusts in exposed northern country, birds still present, and warmer southern/coastal settings. This is fictional scene direction, not a real-world wildlife forecast or a new permanent climate law. Specific weather still needs the venue’s continuity check.

## Regional autumn palette

- **Northern Spine:** exposed dark ridges, conifers and mixed timber, colder gusts, wool and tightly secured equipment. No new snow or frozen lakes in this Week 7 treatment. Show bird activity only where locally plausible. Do not turn every northern mountain into a deciduous orange forest.
- **Upper Valleys/Foothills:** patchy gold/rust in mixed woodland and orchards, harvest traffic, dry leaves, cool shaded approaches and wind at exposed overlooks. Sheltered interiors remain inviting rather than wintry.
- **Central Basin:** grain carts, markets, stored harvest, fair preparations and straw. Best candidate region for a later corn-maze premise, subject to an actual approved agricultural site and route.
- **Western Tablelands:** tawny grass, dry seed heads, canvas pulling in the wind and cool evenings. Not an eastern maple forest.
- **Eastern Storm Coast:** wet dark foliage with selective seasonal color, copper and slate, salt-worn shutters and abrupt gusts. Warmth is relative; do not assume every coast is balmy.
- **Southern Wetlands:** substantial surviving green, humid air, reeds, cypress-like trees, mud and harvest goods on raised dry ground. Pumpkins can arrive as traded or cultivated goods; they do not imply a vast dry field in the swamp.
- **Inner Sea Littoral:** warmer salt-air terraces and harbor commerce. A pumpkin in a market shipment is more credible than blanket northern foliage.

## Six matchup overlays: preserve the story, change the air

### Three Dreaded Snake vs Dr. Duckhook
**Existing hook:** private waterfall/jungle golf, attire contrast, Pittsy’s closure callback.
**Seasonal layer:** keep the wet landscape mostly green; a harvest hamper or uncarved pumpkins sit on dry terrace storage, not scattered through every fairway. Waterfall mist and damp leaves can affect a tactical shot without becoming winter weather.
**Best use:** one small human detail, such as an opponent refusing the dry towel to avoid appearing uncomfortable. Preserve the golf story. The compact course remains a candidate, not an established 18-hole resort. Pittsy’s closure cause stays unknown.

### D0nkey K0ng vs El Niño
**Existing hook:** an open character situation at the stronghold; no compulsory television reference.
**Seasonal layer:** Upper Valleys harvest preparations, stacked unlit lanterns, shutters and leaves collecting at the entrance.
**Story pressure:** the stronghold is being made welcoming while weather exploits its openings. DK must choose between maintaining a perfect display and changing the room to work in the actual conditions. El Niño remains environmental, not a speaking guest or humanoid opponent. Ordinary autumn wind is not automatically El Niño.

### ObiWan Jacoby vs Seven Deadly Chins
**Existing hook:** duel, verbal challenge and physical choreography.
**Seasonal layer:** selective valley color below, cold gusts at the elevated terrace, dry leaves revealing changes in the wind. No snow; no invented cliff height or new bridge.
**Story pressure:** weather changes the advantage and gives someone a plausible excuse. The characters’ response to that excuse matters more than a pumpkin-shaped prop.

### Mud Dogs vs The Chili Cheesers
**Existing hook:** two hunters, the same fictional fourteen-point buck, territorial rivalry and a possible photograph after the eventual verified result.
**Seasonal layer:** warm wetland understory, reeds, dry browse islands and one harvest/scarecrow detail only if a suitable cultivated edge is established. No instant cornfield in open water.
**Story pressure:** local knowledge versus theatrical misdirection. Keep the buck encounter and the photograph conditional; do not predeclare a successful hunt or winner.

### Red Leopards vs His Majesty’s Blood — GOTW
**Existing hook:** private first-to-eleven half-court contest, belt envy, no spectators.
**Seasonal layer:** wet dark coastal woodland with a few ochre leaves; gusts audible beyond a dry, stable playing surface. One arrival pumpkin or harvest basket is sufficient, and can be omitted entirely.
**Story pressure:** a small change in conditions tests whether the rivalry is about winning or proving superiority. Do not move the court to a dangerous cliff edge. The Red-home GOTW exception and site activation remain unresolved; relocate coherently if the approved venue differs.

### Slob on my Dobb vs The LLC
**Existing hook:** poker in the man-cave, a startling entrance and a disputed-looking hand.
**Seasonal layer:** cool foothill arrival, warm interior light, a partly carved pumpkin or folded scare display waiting near the entrance. No need for a whole haunted mansion.
**Story pressure:** the scare can become a rehearsal for reading an opponent’s tells. LLC’s warmer origin does not make Slob’s home coastal. Helicopter arrival remains an unsupported technology candidate.

## Four strongest candidate beats

### 1. The Third Hunter — Mud Dogs / Chili
At the edge of a proposed dry cultivated patch, one hunter notices the other’s familiar hat among the reeds. It belongs to a scarecrow. Someone has borrowed the silhouette as a decoy; the rival notices birds landing on it before he gives away his own position. Now both know the hunt includes a second contest: who can distinguish a real sign from bait.

**Why it works:** the scarecrow changes information and choices rather than merely decorating the page. The buck’s movement, hunt result and owner of the final photograph stay open. If the agricultural edge cannot be grounded, drop this beat rather than invent a farm.

### 2. The Practice Flinch — Slob / LLC
The entryway’s unfinished Halloween scare is spotted too soon. Slob thinks the attempt was a failure; LLC wonders whether the real purpose was to study his reaction. At the poker table, both begin interpreting the wrong thing: one suppresses every expression, the other deliberately overplays a harmless reaction.

**Why it works:** an amusing seasonal moment becomes psychological tension. Neither is automatically the fool. A later card reveal, not an author-imposed gag, resolves the advantage. Keep the game mechanics valid and the monetary stakes unasserted unless supported.

### 3. No Weather Excuses — Red / HMB
Before play, a gust disturbs the belt’s carefully chosen display. Its owner restores it before anything else. The other player notices. When conditions later change during a disputed point, either can insist on the narrow advantage or offer a replay that makes defeat harder to excuse.

**Why it works:** the atmosphere exposes priorities and sharpens the already personal rivalry. The proposed replay is a character choice, not a new real league rule, and no final score or winner is supplied. Keep the belt safe and the court dry.

### 4. The Unlit Lantern — DK / El Niño
The room is prepared for a harvest gathering that has not begun. One lantern refuses to stay lit near the open threshold; the rest of the room looks perfect. DK can keep correcting the display for appearances or move the table, close part of the hall and accept a less impressive but usable welcome.

**Why it works:** this is a small, recognizable frustration with a choice, not another generic battle against weather. It preserves El Niño’s environmental form and lets the stronghold feel inhabited. No supernatural cause is asserted unless the governing scene establishes it.

**Recommended balance:** explore The Third Hunter and The Practice Flinch as the clearest seasonal story devices. Keep Red/HMB and ObiWan/Chins mostly atmospheric. Use DK’s lantern beat only if it supports the chosen story, and leave TDS/Duckhook primarily lush and golf-driven. Do not force all four beats into the issue.
