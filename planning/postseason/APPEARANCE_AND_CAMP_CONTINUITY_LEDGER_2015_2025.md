## The two different kinds of seasonal legacy

**Contender-camp prestige:** Every playoff entrant earns a temporary room or pavilion inside the secure Contenders' Quarter. Repeat appearances can add recognized historical plaques, familiar groundskeepers and training preferences; they cannot change seeds, favoritism or physical safety. A top seed's bye is an earned first-round scheduling exemption with observation, recovery and opponents to study — *never* a fictional win or secret trial.

**Beach tenure:** Missing the playoffs or being eliminated moves a character socially away from championship eligibility and toward beach lodging. That move is painful even if the beach is attractive. A proven long-term beach attendee could have an annual reserved cabana, a favorite bar, a familiar bartender, a good grill, a well-established temporary pavilion and a running reputation. But no person possesses a permanent beach estate absent verified identity and a separate in-world approval. **Everyone still wants the trophy more than the best beach view.**

### The passage to the beach — physical continuity

1. Nonqualifiers arrive after the regular-season qualification lock, with a full view of the championship upland from the beach.
2. The first-round casualties are escorted from their former contender house along the public result court to a lower coastal path. Friends and taunters are waiting at the beach gate.
3. Semifinal casualties descend in sight of a championship arena that they can no longer enter as finalists. Their withdrawal means only two secure residences remain occupied.
4. The beaten finalist attends the final award before being received among the eleven unsuccessful competitors. For a returning former champion, this journey carries a different weight than an opening-round exit.
5. After the final, both the sea-facing beach and grandstands clear; the entire island closes to public use until next postseason.
