# SUMMIT ONE — Twelve editorial/technical questions

The following is **written simulation**, a deliberately adversarial workshop, not literal verbatim Director messages. Moderator Closer requires a concrete answer and dissent for each angle.

### 01. What is an obstacle course, as opposed to a disposable spectacle?
**Beat Writer:** "A contest needs opposing wills. Every station has to force a choice one character could make differently from another. Otherwise we have expensive scenery, not drama."
**GM:** "The player experiences a known objective, the spectator sees a readable win condition, and the island remembers the cost. Three acts are not necessarily three unrelated sports."
**Analyst:** "Score provenance cannot be reverse-engineered from in-world obstacle timing. Use football fantasy results as source truth, then construct stage choreography that supports rather than predicts the winner."
**Decision:** Define per-challenge objective, two competing strategies, three causally connected physical phases, an official outcome lock, and consequences. **Reject:** a roulette wheel of disconnected trials, fake direct conversion of fantasy scores into meters.

### 02. How far can the challenge menu expand?
**Beat Writer:** "Build an extensive grammar rather than a thousand arbitrary prose blurbs."
**Pitching Coach:** "Use a curated library of challenge components, with reproducible combinations and forbidden pairings."
**Scout:** "Tag every idea as NEW FICTION. A real 2018 score doesn't certify that a 2018 joust occurred."
**Decision:** Twelve macro families, each with ten approved seed ideas (120 curated); compositional expansion across three-stage objectives/terrain/era produces many more possibilities **only after validation**, not claims that thousands of individually authored games exist.

### 03. How violent can the island become?
**Warden:** "Higher stakes, not permanent principal death. Secure audience separation, safe exits, documented controls over projectiles and blunt encounters."
**Commissioner General:** "Survival-game references can inform tension, not copied branding, characters or sets. Avoid staged torture or implied lethal casualties."
**Beat Writer:** "A controlled sword fight or sumo bout can feel brutal and important with injury, shame and exhaustion, while keeping the no-death principal rule."
**Decision:** Contest impacts include fatigue, injury risk, public humiliation, forfeited status and elimination; lethal intent, unexplained magic, spectator harm as amusement are excluded.

### 04. Does permanent round designation kill variety?
**Architect:** "No. Permanent stadium identity differs from a permanent sport. Iron Quarry remains QF-only, but can stage wrestling, strategic siege simulation, climb, or tactical engineering within its rock and safety envelope."
**Groundskeeper:** "Bounded modular apparatus can be assembled and removed by the seasonal workforce; foundation, topography, entries, stands, drainage and service lanes remain fixed."
**Librarian:** "Venue history must identify what was installed and when. A 2025 camera tower cannot be visible in 2015."
**Decision:** Fixed arena and round law; variable bounded challenge rigs with audited historical state. Do not change stone geometry to suit each scene.

### 05. Where do storms and explosions go?
**Setup Man:** "Distinguish controlled in-arena effect from persistent world damage. A staged burst uses designated blast berms; real structural destruction requires service access and repairs."
**Groundskeeper:** "Keep a retired quarry gallery, charred stands, abandoned obstacle carcasses, repaired gates, and old impact scars. Archive every replacement or restoration."
**Umpire:** "No invented historical disaster presented as fact; no physics-exempt terrain reset between matches."
**Decision:** Persist only explicit approved scenic scars. Dormant old grounds and licensed effect zones carry age; Last Field is maintained, never arbitrarily ruined.

### 06. Can low-contact games feel worthy of the semifinals?
**Bookkeeper:** "Card games, chess, poker, resource auctions and coded puzzles are comparatively inexpensive and generate conflict through stakes, wagers and audience cognition."
**Beat Writer:** "Make mental contests physical and stageable: moving giant chess pieces, negotiating over enclosed vault keys, strategy boards whose outcomes require a final arena demonstration."
**Agent:** "A knockout card hand can be gripping when cameras show bluff, hands, reaction and consequences. The audience must understand the stakes before the decisive reveal."
**Decision:** Tactical/tabletop contests may headline, provided each has physical agency, clear neutral rules, three escalating stages and an intelligible finish.

### 07. How do spectators witness three simultaneous QFs?
**Agent:** "Broadcast identity should be as strong as the stadium identity."
**Commissioner:** "A neutral, professional central desk keeps the all-League story legible; avoid giving a favored entrant privileged camera time."
**Setup Man:** "Public screens, camera routes, power backups, safety and transmission need an approved island technology exception. No magical global satellite distribution."
**Decision:** Three venue crews, a central editorial control room, match-specific replays, shared bracket reveal and beach cutaway; no pre-certification winner call.

### 08. How does the bridge affect the event?
**Architect:** "The monumental mainland causeway needs abutments, load limits, tide aperture, raised spans and connection to an actual mainland route. It cannot be an arbitrary line off the Atlas edge."
**Groundskeeper:** "Separate spectator ingress, contender transport, vendors, service, media, and emergency egress. If the bridge closes, the harbor relief must remain viable."
**Scout Master:** "The crossing itself becomes the public procession, but hospitality capacity must exist on the far shore."
**Decision:** A testable 23-node connected planning topology with distinct access modes, not fabricated GIS meters. Island becomes a seasonal destination, not a shortcut connecting existing owner lands.

### 09. Who runs eleven seasons of operations?
**Clubhouse Manager:** "Contest stewards, arena crews, licensed referees, first-aid, groundskeepers, hospitality teams, camera operators, electricians, curators and cleanup staff require continuity. Not eighteenth-century ghosts running a 2025 broadcast booth."
**Bookkeeper:** "Modern stadium upgrades incur capital costs; build them by stages, not all in the first recorded year."
**Commissioner:** "Contestants receive neutral temporary quarters; alumni have familiarity, not an inherited right to points or arena advantages."
**Decision:** Year-scoped staff and infrastructure lifecycle; secure contenders' fortress, bye house, minimal single beach resort. Keep 12 characters as primary POV only.

### 10. How do we encode mythology without contradicting history?
**Librarian:** "Sports facts: 2015–25 real scores and team-slot IDs. Island fictionalization: invented geography, physical forms, sites, witnesses, scars. Never swap labels."
**Scout:** "Early records have team slots but insufficient long-run human owner proof. No false permanent historical owner compounds."
**Umpire:** "The Last Field on the new island conflicts with old Central Basin candidate. Founder has authorized direction, but approved Atlas mutation must explicitly supersede the old candidate."
**Decision:** Immutable source fixture, separate fiction record, season-scoped physical scene and explicit canon-promotion receipt.

### 11. What exactly is the winner's ceremony?
**Commissioner General:** "A protected Michael Farrell remembrance ceremony can be culturally important without invented biographical stories or historic naming dates."
**Beat Writer:** "Walk past the eleven inscriptions, bring both finalists through the fixed gates, certify the outcome, then present Belt and Trophy with solemnity."
**Agent:** "Modern audience coverage should distinguish the title ceremony from the resort comedy."
**Decision:** One final ground across all seasons in proposed fiction; distinct Belt supremacy and memorial Trophy. No unverified memorial chronology.

### 12. What makes this a product, not merely a lore document?
**GM:** "A browsable obstacle archive, real championship bracket, eleven-year evolving venue states, dynamic page contracts, and a source-bound 2026 future interface."
**Architect:** "Use world/atlas authority and current Node production compilers; no second independent map database or new OS."
**Closer:** "One model: fixture truth, venue ecology, obstacle contract, annual wear state, scene/broadcast package, Umpire gate, then derived Atlas/Memo/Novel views. The beach stays a simple visible background."
**Decision:** Implement a queryable creative obstacle registry, compatibility validator, annual material-state ledger, scene-binding compiler and interactive public PREVIEW without prematurely writing active world.

# SUMMIT TWO — Convert the creativity to a working production system

**Closer asks:** "Which ideas can be built now without pretending new Atlas geography is already canon?"

**Architect:** "A venue capability matrix, e.g. vertical movement allowed at Iron Quarry, safe barriers required for blade/horse work, tidal windows necessary at Breakwater. The physical engine must reject incompatible challenges. This is not a photoreal Newtonian solver: it is the grounded ruleset and site-state interface."

**Beat Writer:** "Define a production brief with its three acts plus opponent countermoves, not three interchangeable labels. Carry fatigue, weather, objective and memory across the acts. Make a chess final feel like a competition through physical staging and the consequence of the winner's move."

**Librarian:** "Each historical venue remembers prior allocated fixtures, but **new obstacle selections are NOT established 2015–2025 canon**. Assignments are creative proposals and can be enriched without modifying score authority. Deserted rigs and cracked stone require an approved world-state event, not an image alone."

**Scout:** "Stage envelopes must be compositional, not score simulations. NFL points remain external exact data. Verify source-year settings and avoid translating a 2024 real-world fantasy outcome into a 2015 winner."

**Groundskeeper:** "For impact areas create a \`SCAR\`, \`REPAIRED\`, \`DORMANT\`, \`REFURBISHED\` timeline with closure/safety tags. Last Field remains continuously maintained, though its booths and suites evolve."

**Umpire:** "Ship the candidate registry, conflict tests and preview. Hold active location IDs, modern-mainland broadcast technology and uncorroborated Michael Farrell history. Make the preview say it is fictional."

## Revise the plan — accepted implementation sequence

1. Create a **creative Obstacle Lexicon** with 120 distinct seeds across twelve families, each specifying three-phase pattern, physical equipment, real site constraints, result presentation and permanent/temporary effect.
2. Create a **physical capability and material-state matrix** for nine venue candidates; enforce Founder F04 round-only restrictions.
3. Add an evidence-preserving **fixture × challenge composition** export for all 56 historical contests. Historical challenges remain proposed fiction and do not replace prior illustrative scripts without editorial review.
4. Add a **temporal heritage ledger** for each venue (original surfaces, damage scars, repairs, dormant structures, periodic broadcast upgrades). No unapproved permanent physical event promoted.
5. Add tests for bad combinations, missing physical constraints, incomplete three-stage scenarios, source drift, pre-2026 technology, and Last Field contamination.
6. Add the resulting content to the already-built interactive island exhibit as a navigable challenge library.
7. Run actual Node and browser tests, capture the results and note any outstanding release blockers. Do not declare the whole project published until the actual Atlas/Tech/independent QA gates are evidenced.

**Summit conclusion:** The title remains the motive. Sports, duels, wagers, puzzles, jousts, archery, races and staged military challenges are *ways of testing two opposing powers*; none is the single identity of the island. Permanence comes from the venues and memory, while novelty comes from a validated catalog of meaningful contest forms.
