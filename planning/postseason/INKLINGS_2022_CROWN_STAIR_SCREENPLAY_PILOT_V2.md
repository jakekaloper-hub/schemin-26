# INKLINGS / CHAMPIONSHIP ISLAND
## THE FIRST SEED HAS TO CLIMB — 2022 SEMIFINAL
**HIST-POST-2022-SF-1 · Crown Stair · Red Leopards vs El Niño**

**Production status:** Original editorial screenplay draft V2; fictional arena history, not documentary evidence of real-world athletic competition or any owner’s actual condition.

**Locked ESPN facts:** Red Leopards finished the 2022 regular season **13–1**, earned a first-round bye and lost the semifinal to El Niño **97.96–116.32**. El Niño had qualified at **7–7** and defeated Javonte's InferNO Knee in the prior quarterfinal. El Niño advanced to the championship, which is a separate match at the permanent Last Field.

**Proposed fictional ceremonial draw:** *Clockwork Siege* — a directly performed three-phase engineering confrontation, with equal kits, safe tested mechanisms and a final upper-landing structural demonstration. No professional engineer is allowed to complete a stage for either contender.  
**Historical site consequence:** An **engineered support repair line at the upper scoring landing** becomes a permanent, safely restored trace in the Crown Stair archive.  
**Spatial continuity:** Crown Stair is a semifinal-only arena. The maintained Last Field is visible beyond the ridge but not used for this matchup.

---

### EXT. CROWN STAIR — EARLY EVENING

The two semifinal arenas operate at opposite ends of the island's upper precinct.

From the foot of Crown Stair, spectators see an enormous sequence of terraces climbing toward a high ceremonial court. The final stadium is distant beyond the ridge. Its gates remain closed.

Rows of limestone seats are crowded with people who have crossed the causeway from different parts of the Commonwealth. At the lower steps, a score historian arranges the public programme.

The first page bears the number **13–1** beneath RED LEOPARDS.

The next identifies EL NIÑO, a seven-win regular-season qualifier who has already survived one elimination round.

A woman in the crowd points toward the empty, protected bye residence behind the upper stands.

**SPECTATOR**  
The Red Leopards had the week to watch. Is that not enough?

Beside the main court, the BROADCAST ANALYST hears the question over the crowd's murmur.

**ANALYST**  
A bye gives you rest. It does not give you a place at the Last Field.

**PLAY-BY-PLAY**  
Tonight somebody will earn that place. These two still have to face one another.

The ceremonial selection board opens. A sealed plate is lifted from a slot.

**CLOCKWORK SIEGE.**

The stadium's enormous steps are not themselves the machinery. A temporary engineering court has been installed on stable, load-inspected platforms against the lower upper-landing walls. The original stone is visible around the rig.

An EVENT ENGINEER inspects the kit and stands behind a guarded line. The engineer may verify safety; the engineer cannot build or adjust either contender's mechanism once the competition starts.

Two identical workstations are separated by a barrier. Each contains a hand-cranked lift, a small counterweight, approved links, a set of braces and one brass representation of the season's contested standard.

Both contestants must build and operate the devices with their own hands.

RED LEOPARDS waits at the left bench, still and deliberate.

EL NIÑO studies the upper landing, then the mechanism, then the route from the court toward the empty final stadium.

**RED LEOPARDS**  
Everything here can be measured.

**EL NIÑO**  
So can a record.

The arbiter gives a short whistle.

### PHASE ONE — THE ASCENT

The opening objective is posted above both benches: personally assemble a mechanism that can raise the brass standard to a specified mark without straining its supports.

Red Leopards immediately chooses a rigid frame. Each piece goes into place in a direct line: foundation brace, upright, crossbeam, lifting arm. The assembly has little wasted motion. When Red Leopards turns the crank, the standard climbs.

From the high stands, the mechanism looks confident.

El Niño assembles the same components differently. The main support is set back from the lifting arm; a second joint allows slight controlled movement before the weight reaches its height. It takes more adjustments. El Niño stops twice to examine the angle and turns the crank again.

The standard rises slowly.

**PLAY-BY-PLAY**  
Red Leopards is showing why the regular season was so strong. There is barely a movement wasted.

**ANALYST**  
And El Niño has introduced a joint that can move. We will see whether that is flexibility or a mistake.

The arbiter tests the opening position and records the safe completion of both demonstrations. No fantasy-football point is awarded by the fictional device.

Red Leopards looks to the crowd. El Niño looks to the joint.

### PHASE TWO — THE COUNTERWEIGHT

The middle-stage objective changes: raise the same standard while the platform's counterweight shifts laterally across a protected track.

The move is announced before either contestant touches a tool. Both have the same amount of time to reconfigure the mechanism and the same inspected parts.

Red Leopards reinforces the rigid frame. The decision makes sense: prevent movement by adding strength. The fast construction leaves room to inspect each fastener personally.

El Niño loosens the design at one connection and installs a second permitted brace, allowing the structure to move slightly while staying within the verified load envelope.

A public diagram shows the lateral shift. The audience understands what the test is asking.

Red Leopards turns the crank.

The moving counterweight pushes the frame sideways. The upper beam begins to lean. A mechanical stop engages safely before any part reaches the audience-facing boundary. Red Leopards releases pressure, steps back, and chooses a different attachment point.

El Niño turns the crank on the separate workstation. The flexible joint moves, absorbing the tested lateral force. The standard rises but settles at an uncertain angle.

Neither result is enough to settle the trial.

Red Leopards works with increasing urgency. A fastener scrapes against the support plate, leaving a shallow line at the upper scoring landing's replaceable inlay. An official records it.

The stand above the court erupts when Red Leopards repositions the brace and gets the mechanism moving again.

**ANALYST**  
They are both still here. The difference is how each responded when the apparatus refused the first idea.

**PLAY-BY-PLAY**  
And there is a cost to each correction. Look at the time the Red Leopards has spent rebuilding the angle.

El Niño tries a tighter pull and discovers a weakness in the flexible joint. The crank stops. El Niño removes the load personally, adjusts the approved brace, and resumes.

No helper steps across the line.

The two devices come to rest near the inspection marker.

### PHASE THREE — THE UPPER LANDING

For the final passage the contenders must lift the same protected standard to its highest legal marker, then complete a witnessed, non-destructive inspection of their own mechanism.

A transparent safety screen closes between the testing rigs and the spectators. A catch tray beneath each apparatus holds any loose bolt or brace. No platform edge is left open.

Red Leopards takes the first adjustment. The frame is strong but has little remaining tolerance for lateral movement. Red Leopards makes one final counterbrace, tests the crank and waits for the judge's signal.

El Niño returns to the flexible arrangement. The earlier difficulty remains visible. The mechanism has not become magically stronger; El Niño must use the available parts with enough care to avoid the same fault.

Both begin the final lift under direct supervision.

The upper clockwork turns. El Niño's joint dips and recovers. The brass standard rises.

Red Leopards's lift climbs sharply, then stalls near the high mark. The counterweight presses sideways. Red Leopards stops the mechanism and reaches for the permitted adjustment without leaving the contestant's own work area.

The final attempts continue. The physical action is tense and uncertain; the source result cannot be derived from the movement of these fictional weights.

El Niño completes a final personal adjustment.

Red Leopards answers by tightening the main brace.

The arbiter calls an end to the demonstration. Both mechanisms are secured before anyone approaches the rig.

The camera cuts from the separate workstations to the empty Last Field gates on the distant ridge.

**PLAY-BY-PLAY**  
There is still nothing to award until the Contest record is certified.

### RESULT CERTIFIED

The central result keeper appears at the upper landing with a sealed document.

**RESULT KEEPER**  
Official semifinal record. Red Leopards: **97.96**. El Niño: **116.32**.

A beat.

**RESULT KEEPER (CONT'D)**  
El Niño advances to the championship.

For a moment, the crowd struggles to absorb what has happened.

Red Leopards finished the regular season **13–1**. The bye protected the first round. Neither fact guaranteed a victory here.

El Niño had arrived after a seven-win regular season and an actual quarterfinal success. Now another verified result has opened the way to the final.

The public bracket changes. One of four semifinal standards falls. The Last Field still has two empty gates until the other semifinal result is confirmed.

El Niño turns toward the stadium beyond the ridge.

Red Leopards does not move.

**RED LEOPARDS**  
Thirteen wins.

**EL NIÑO**  
And this was the one we both had to play.

No one has to explain the meaning of the exchange.

### EPILOGUE — THE REPAIRED LINE

After the crowd clears, the chief groundskeeper inspects the apparatus.

The temporary rig has done its work. The upper landing's replaceable inlay carries an engineered support repair line where a bracket scraped the surface. The original historic stone is sound.

A NEW GROUNDSKEEPER picks up a smoothing tool.

**NEW GROUNDSKEEPER**  
Should we remove the mark?

**CHIEF GROUNDSKEEPER**  
Make it safe.

**NEW GROUNDSKEEPER**  
And the mark?

**CHIEF GROUNDSKEEPER**  
That stays in the record. We keep the piece.

The worn plate is safely removed, dated and preserved; a replacement is checked against the surface's original geometry.

Down the island's shoreward path, the defeated Red Leopards approaches the quiet seasonal resort at Losers' Beach. The beach is beautiful. It is not the championship.

At Crown Stair, the lights begin to go dark.

The Last Field remains lit in the distance.

FADE OUT.
