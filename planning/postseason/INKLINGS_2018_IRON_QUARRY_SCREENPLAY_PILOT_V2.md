# INKLINGS / CHAMPIONSHIP ISLAND
## THE TENTH OF A POINT — 2018 QUARTERFINAL
**HIST-POST-2018-QF-1 · Iron Quarry · Kardiac Kids vs Schreck Chappell**

**Production status:** Editorial screenplay revision V1; fictional physical scene, not an attested historical sporting event. No real person's appearance, biography or actual injury is asserted by the historical franchise alias.

**Verified ESPN fixture:** Kardiac Kids 114.37 defeats Schreck Chappell 114.27 in the 2018 quarterfinal. Season uses six qualifiers and two first-round byes. No other result is changed.

**Proposed ceremonial draw:** *Arrow and Sprint* — protected archery stations alternating with separated marked running lanes. Both contenders perform each action personally. The Iron Quarry remains permanently quarterfinal-only.  
**Persistent historical trace:** Original marked multi-sport marker on the **lower quarry shelf** is damaged and replaced after a documented safe inspection; the old plate survives in the island archive.  
**Camera identity:** Quarry's western upper bench, original stone terraces, lower shelf marker and the public result court. The newer Last Field is NOT the location of this scene.

### EXT. THE IRON QUARRY — MORNING

The quarry is a stadium made from a wound in stone.

Terraces rise from the old extraction face. Long pale scars run down the limestone where workmen removed blocks long before this year's tournament. A second set of scars is newer: patched seating, replaced handrails, the square outlines of old competition rigs lifted off the lower shelf.

Spectators fill the banks. Some have come over the island causeway to see the opening of the championship bracket. Others are residents of Losers' Beach already, watching what they will not be allowed to win.

On the east wall, an official marker announces **2018 — QUARTERFINAL**.

An EVENT MARSHAL checks two short archery lanes. High backstops stand between the range and the audience; the sprint lanes remain secured behind locked sliding barriers until the bows have been lowered and the firing line is cleared.

A broadcaster leans over the stone rail, microphone in hand.

**PLAY-BY-PLAY**  
Two contenders. One quarterfinal. The first official result will decide who takes the next road up to Crown Stair.

**ANALYST**  
They drew Arrow and Sprint. It is a combined trial: precision first, speed after the all-clear. Same target course. Same running stations. No one else can take the shot or run the lane for either entrant.

An arbiter shows the selected ceramic draw tablet to both opponents, then turns it toward the camera.

*ARROW AND SPRINT.*

No winner is displayed.

KARDIAC KIDS comes through the lower western gate, carrying the approved bow. SCHRECK CHAPPELL enters opposite, inspecting the quarry's incline and the weather above the western ledge.

The two exchange a look. Their clothes and features remain unassigned pending historical character reference approval; their movements carry the tension.

**SCHRECK CHAPPELL**  
The straight way looks shorter.

**KARDIAC KIDS**  
Until you have to turn.

The arbiter raises a flag.

### PHASE ONE — THE OPENING LINE

The first target stands at the rear of an enclosed shooting gallery. Each entrant has a separate booth and a matched bow. A safety barrier prevents either from running through a firing zone while the other is shooting.

Kardiac Kids sights the low ring. The first arrow lands just inside the scoring circle. Kardiac Kids lowers the bow and waits for the range signal, resisting the urge to sprint early.

Schreck Chappell chooses an outside angle, allowing the arrow to travel in a flatter arc. It strikes near the first mark. Not an obvious victory. Not a defeat.

Only after the marshal sounds a clear horn do the range shutters close and the running gates open.

Now Kardiac Kids and Schreck Chappell sprint personally along mirrored quarry lanes. The quarry floor is dry and graded; a worn portion of the stone has been covered with stable temporary footing.

Kardiac Kids accelerates early, rounds the inner flag too tightly, and plants a foot to avoid stepping beyond the legal line.

Schreck Chappell takes the outer flag, giving up the shortest distance for a cleaner turn. The move brings the two almost level as they arrive at separate receiving marks on the lower shelf.

**PLAY-BY-PLAY**  
Neither has settled it. Kardiac took the inside; Schreck found room to carry speed.

**ANALYST**  
Both choices cost something. The next passage will make them pay for the part they gave away.

The arbiter moves two tokens on an *in-world demonstration board*. The display does not claim to be the ESPN fantasy score.

### PHASE TWO — THE COUNTER

For the second station, the shooting booths shift higher on secured mechanical tracks. A narrow opening between shield panels changes the sightline to the target. The course underneath remains the same quarry; the removable stage rig makes the new obstacle possible.

Both competitors approach the same sequence, one at a time.

Kardiac Kids raises the bow and notices the front panel has made the low trajectory less useful. The bow is lowered. A new stance. Kardiac Kids takes a second sightline and fires through the protective gap. The arrow lands on the marked backing plate.

Schreck Chappell watches the result through a neutral spectator aperture, then enters the separate firing booth. The first instinct is to repeat the flatter trajectory from phase one. Schreck stops, steps back, corrects the angle, and releases.

The second target receives its mark.

A camera catches the tiny hesitation at Schreck's hand after the release. Neither character may delegate this correction to anyone else.

The range is cleared again.

This sprint is uphill, along two parallel stone-faced ramps with non-slip temporary surface and side rails. At the midpoint, a pivot gate requires an actual direction change.

Kardiac Kids waits for Schreck Chappell to commit to the higher pivot, then takes the protected lower route; the ramp leads to the same finishing shelf but demands a tighter final turn.

Schreck Chappell holds the high line and reaches the last flag with enough momentum to take the inside of the last curve.

They arrive almost together. Breathing hard. Uncertain.

A spectator on the upper bench starts to cheer, then sits down when the official does not raise a flag.

**ANALYST**  
That is what this quarry does. It forces you to choose what you can afford to lose.

### PHASE THREE — THE LOWER-SHELF MARKER

The final station is the simplest to describe and the hardest to watch.

A single approved target plate stands at the back of a protective shooting bay. After the final arrow, the contestants must complete their own last run to the lower-shelf marker. The arrow course closes before the sprint corridor opens. The course may look savage from the upper terraces, but its hazards are bounded and inspected.

Kardiac Kids enters the firing booth first. There is no conversation. The arrow strikes the outer protected scoring circle. The bow is placed down.

Schreck Chappell takes the next turn. A small adjustment of the rear foot. One breath. The arrow goes out through the safe range and lands close enough that the spectators cannot tell from their seats what the official will record.

The marshal signals CLEAR.

Both competitors burst into their respective running lanes.

Kardiac Kids drives up the first incline. Schreck Chappell answers on the opposite side. The camera stays wide long enough for the audience to understand that there are two bodies acting, two lanes, and one shared final objective.

At the last pivot Schreck Chappell seems to have gained the cleaner angle.

Kardiac Kids refuses to cut the barrier and instead uses a shorter, controlled step that preserves momentum into the final approach.

Hands reach separate legal faces of the lower-shelf marker.

The worn plate shudders against its removable mount. A corner chips off; a small fragment drops into a sealed capture tray rather than onto the public concourse.

The arbiter calls time. Neither contender celebrates.

An official opens a sealed ledger at the result platform. The broadcaster takes off the headset to listen.

### RESULT CERTIFIED

**ARBITER**  
The official, externally verified quarterfinal record: Kardiac Kids, **114.37**. Schreck Chappell, **114.27**.

A long, almost unwilling pause.

**ARBITER (CONT'D)**  
By one tenth of a point, Kardiac Kids advances.

Only then does the survivor's standard rise.

The real fantasy-football result is the authority. The fictional Arrow and Sprint demonstration has dramatized its closeness; no arrow distance, running time, or fabricated stage result has been converted into the official score.

Schreck Chappell stands by the broken marker a moment longer. Kardiac Kids looks toward the route climbing out of the quarry.

**SCHRECK CHAPPELL**  
One tenth.

**KARDIAC KIDS**  
It was enough.

There is no cruelty in the reply. Nothing could make the margin larger.

### EPILOGUE — THE MARK THAT REMAINS

While spectators begin the long walk to the public bracket court, the chief groundskeeper inspects the lower quarry shelf. The original marker has suffered a chipped corner, but the permanent stone underneath is secure.

A worker starts carrying away the damaged plate.

**GROUNDSKEEPER**  
Keep that one. Date it.

**WORKER**  
For a tenth?

**GROUNDSKEEPER**  
Especially for a tenth.

A replacement multi-sport marker is installed, inspected and safely secured. The damaged original enters the island's physical-history archive, preserving an identifiable trace of this encounter even after the arena is made ready for another season.

Schreck Chappell descends the shoreward path toward the single, quiet resort at Losers' Beach. Kardiac Kids remains eligible for the semifinal at Crown Stair.

At the top of the Iron Quarry, the crowd's arguments are already starting.

FADE OUT.
