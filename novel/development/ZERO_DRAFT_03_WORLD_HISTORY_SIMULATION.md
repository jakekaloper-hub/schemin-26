**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Status:** DISCOVERY / NON-CANONICAL

# COMMON PRESSURE BATTERY
Every candidate receives the same abstract shocks:
P1 dominant figure loses influence;
P2 underestimated figure gains legitimacy;
P3 rivals benefit from cooperation;
P4 secure institution fails;
P5 valuable outsider becomes contested;
P6 powerful actor makes irrational-seeming exchange;
P7 expected successor fails publicly;
P8 irrelevant person becomes indispensable.

# I. INLAND SEA — SIMULATION

## Deep history
**~430 years pre-page-one — The Highwater Compact.** Shore communities standardize harbor marks, flood refuge obligations and rights of rescue. Later states claim they created these customs; evidence suggests fishing and marsh communities did.

**~310 years — The Salt Succession.** A century of falling/rising water shifts the inland shoreline. Competing calendars later compress several crises into one legendary war.

**~220 years — The Drowning Settlement.** Rival powers deliberately inundate disputed lowlands through an enormous hydraulic intervention—or inherit a natural catastrophe and falsely claim control over it. The distinction is unresolved.

**~140 years — The Chart Schism.** Temple astronomers, pilots and land registrars split over whether shoreline determines sovereignty. Two legal traditions persist.

**~70 years — The Last Great Rise.** Water temporarily returns, destroying new settlements and teaching a generation that exposed land is not permanent.

## Thirty years before page one
**-30:** A failed harvest causes emergency grain shipments across political lines. Several future principals' families become economically entangled.
**-27:** A storm destroys the old neutral navigation school. Reconstruction is funded jointly by rivals, creating a generation educated together.
**-24:** Surveyors discover shoreline markers inland from accepted borders. Findings are sealed.
**-21:** A delta fever kills laborers and nobles indiscriminately; marsh healers gain prestige while suffering discrimination afterward.
**-18:** A salvage boom begins as water retreats unusually quickly.
**-15:** A joint expedition disappears. Survivors give incompatible accounts. This becomes the generation's shared failure.
**-12:** A new salt corridor makes an obscure dryland refuge commercially essential.
**-9:** Credit crisis. Merchant financiers save ports they later gain leverage over.
**-6:** A border marriage temporarily settles competing land claims.
**-3:** The sea falls past a level absent from reliable records.
**-1:** A causeway appears pointing toward land that official maps insist never existed.

## Institutions connecting lives
1. **The Sounding Houses** — train navigators, surveyors and weather readers across borders.
2. **Refuge Law** — ancient obligation requiring any harbor to shelter vessels during named storm conditions, even enemies.
3. **The Shore Registry** — records land claims, marriages tied to property, salvage rights and burial sites.
4. **The Grain Convoys** — seasonal multinational logistics system.
5. **The Lantern Courts** — traveling arbitration held at ports rather than capitals.
6. **The Pilots' Wake** — funerary fraternity maintaining memory of those lost on the sea.

## Three zero-source characters
**Mara Venn**, apprentice surveyor, daughter of a failing ferry owner. Her measurements contradict official shoreline history.
**Sister Orel**, sincere keeper of the Pilots' Wake. She believes religious memory matters more than political truth, but discovers her own order edited records.
**Tavin Ro**, aging grain factor who knows rival cities survive only because ordinary workers quietly violate official embargoes.

## Relationship ecology
Sincere friendship: two former Sounding House students now serve opposing polities.
Broken friendship: survivors of the -15 expedition disagree over whether someone was abandoned intentionally.
Family bond: ferry family divided over selling newly exposed ancestral land.
Intimate/political bond: border marriage becomes unstable because resurfaced terrain changes what the dowry legally contains.
Ideological rivalry: shoreline-law jurist vs fixed-boundary jurist.
Economic dependency: headwaters need delta ports; delta needs upland grain/timber.
Concealed debt: a powerful house survived -9 only because an apparent rival secretly guaranteed its loans.
Shared failure: missing joint expedition.
Category change: former teacher/student become competing claimants when the student's survey threatens the teacher's life's work.

## Pressure results
P1: Dominant shoreline custodian loses influence → old claims reopen; allies don't abandon him uniformly because their marriages/property depend on his precedents.
P2: Underestimated marsh figure gains legitimacy → medical/navigation knowledge becomes political authority, angering institutions that used that knowledge without granting status.
P3: Rivals cooperate → grain + port dependency creates private cooperation before public reconciliation.
P4: Registry fails → property, inheritance and marriage law destabilize simultaneously. Ordinary families suffer first.
P5: Outsider contested → a foreign hydrologist/mapmaker can authenticate competing histories; kidnapping is less useful than credibility.
P6: Irrational exchange → creditor relinquishes profitable salvage rights for control of an apparently worthless inland ferry crossing; later geography makes motive legible.
P7: Expected successor fails → navigation examination disaster damages family legitimacy but may reveal institutional corruption.
P8: Irrelevant indispensable → Mara's mundane measurements become essential because every elite map copied the same ancient error.

### Emergent surprise
The simulation generated a story not about the sea's secret, but about **ownership when geography itself invalidates inherited certainty**. Marriage, graves, debt and memory all become land politics. Mara Venn naturally threatens to outrank several source-derived principals in narrative importance.

# II. ROADS OF ASH — SIMULATION

## Deep history
**~510 years — The First Binding.** Communities swear to maintain sections of an already ancient route network in exchange for protected passage.
**~380 years — The Bridge Wars.** States learn that destroying infrastructure hurts victors as badly as enemies. Road neutrality emerges from trauma, not idealism.
**~260 years — The Ash Pilgrimage.** A religious movement declares road maintenance a form of service to the dead and unborn.
**~170 years — The Toll Heresy.** A wealthy corridor attempts hereditary ownership of passage; rebellion ends with ambiguous compromise.
**~95 years — The Silent Mile.** A major road segment collapses into subterranean works, revealing that the road network is older and stranger than official history admits.

## Thirty years before page one
**-30:** Famine in the eastern basin proves three rival states depend on one western bridge.
**-28:** A convoy massacre leads to creation of mixed-jurisdiction escort companies.
**-25:** Children of elites and labor families enter a new Road School together.
**-22:** Major bridge rebuilt using a technique attributed to a famous engineer; anonymous workers actually solve the critical failure.
**-19:** Religious split over whether abandoned roads remain sacred.
**-16:** A mountain pass closes; an unofficial marsh bypass becomes indispensable.
**-14:** A convoy disaster kills members of several prominent families and creates shared guilt.
**-11:** Debt financiers begin buying maintenance obligations from bankrupt towns.
**-8:** Courier reform centralizes information and quietly creates surveillance capacity.
**-5:** A popular labor figure prevents a general road strike from becoming civil violence.
**-2:** Three unrelated structures fail using the same fracture pattern.
**-1:** Someone removes an ancient marker from a route everyone believes permanent.

## Institutions
1. **Road Oath** — cross-border legal status governing travelers and custodians.
2. **Mile Houses** — sanctuary/inn/court/relay stations.
3. **The Measure Guild** — engineers, surveyors, masons and inspectors.
4. **Ash Orders** — religious communities that maintain dangerous stretches.
5. **Convoy Bonds** — financial contracts distributing transport risk.
6. **Courier Houses** — information network officially neutral, practically powerful.

## Three zero-source characters
**Ilyan Kes**, junior bridge inspector whose father died in the -14 convoy disaster; discovers failures share impossible geometry.
**Mother Sen**, elderly Ash Order leader who sincerely believes roads bind moral communities, not merely markets.
**Pera Doss**, Mile House cook/keeper whose informal knowledge of travelers exceeds the courier archive.

## Relationship ecology
Sincere friendship: elite negotiator and labor-born engineer educated together at Road School.
Broken friendship: two convoy survivors disagree over who ordered retreat at -14.
Family: inspector investigating structure his celebrated uncle certified.
Intimate/political: marriage joins toll family to road-neutral religious family.
Ideological: sacred-public-road advocate vs contractual privatizer.
Economic dependency: creditor owns obligations; labor guild controls whether repairs occur.
Concealed debt: frontier leader once received illegal passage that saved his people.
Shared failure: convoy disaster.
Category change: rival corridor commanders become collaborators after discovering simultaneous failures.

## Pressure results
P1 dominant custodian loses influence → enforcement fragments; local Mile Houses begin interpreting oath law themselves.
P2 underestimated labor figure gains legitimacy → practical repair authority becomes political power.
P3 rivals cooperate → shared bridge forces cooperation even while propaganda remains hostile.
P4 secure institution fails → Courier House data integrity collapses; nobody knows whether messages were delayed, altered or forged.
P5 outsider contested → engineer with lost-builder knowledge is courted by states, guilds and religion.
P6 irrational exchange → financier trades profitable toll rights for maintenance liability on a failing route; may understand future rerouting.
P7 successor fails → heir cannot complete Road Oath ritual, exposing gap between hereditary prestige and competence.
P8 irrelevant indispensable → Pera Doss remembers who actually traveled through her Mile House when official courier records prove false.

### Emergent surprise
The world generated a conflict about **trust infrastructure** rather than physical infrastructure. Roads matter because law, credit, religion, messages and memory all travel on them. Pera—the cook/keeper—became unexpectedly powerful without acquiring office or combat ability.

# III. SHATTERED CONCORD 2.0 — SIMULATION

## Revision
Remove generic council-of-houses center. The Concord is redefined as a **distributed web of reciprocal obligations**. No central throne or parliament controls it. Rights exist only because distant communities continuously perform obligations for one another.

A mountain polity receives winter grain because its ancestors maintain a pass.
A river city receives timber because it guarantees neutral courts.
A frontier culture receives legal recognition because it provides refuge.
If obligations cease, rights unravel.

## Deep history
**~470 years — The Years Without Guests.** Fragmented communities refuse refuge to outsiders during cascading famine/war; mass death becomes foundational shame.
**~390 years — The First Reciprocities.** Local promises are linked into larger chains.
**~280 years — The Witness Reform.** Agreements require third-party cultures to remember and certify them, making outsiders essential.
**~190 years — The False Fulfillment.** Powerful states learn to technically satisfy obligations while violating their spirit.
**~110 years — The Unremembered War.** Fighting occurs largely through withdrawal of obligations rather than armies; later histories disagree whether it was a war at all.

## Thirty years before page one
**-30:** Refuge failure kills hundreds despite a technically valid exemption.
**-26:** Young people from several regions are exchanged as Witness apprentices.
**-23:** A famous reciprocity is discovered to depend on a mistranslated phrase.
**-20:** Disease forces enemies to fulfill medical obligations to one another.
**-17:** A respected Witness falsifies certification to prevent famine.
**-14:** The lie is privately discovered by several future principals; exposing it would invalidate years of lawful exchange.
**-10:** A wealthy actor begins buying debts created by failed obligations.
**-7:** One region fulfills an ancient duty nobody expected it still to honor, restoring its reputation.
**-4:** A generation begins asking why descendants owe promises made centuries earlier.
**-2:** Multiple communities legally refuse obligations using the same obscure exception.
**-1:** A Witness archive burns, destroying proof of hundreds of reciprocal rights.

## Institutions
1. **Witness Houses** — outsiders certify agreements they do not benefit from directly.
2. **Guest Right** — enforceable refuge obligations.
3. **Reciprocity Chains** — rights/duties spanning many cultures.
4. **Memory Courts** — oral and written adjudication when records conflict.
5. **Exchange Fosterage** — adolescents live abroad to learn another region's obligations.
6. **Debt Houses** — controversial market in unfulfilled obligations.

## Three zero-source characters
**Enna Tal**, Witness apprentice whose first assignment reveals her teacher's old falsification.
**Corren Vale**, village physician legally required to treat people from the polity blamed for his brother's death.
**Old Rusk**, itinerant reciter who remembers agreements no surviving archive contains but is notorious for embellishment.

## Relationship ecology
Sincere friendship: exchange-fostered youths from culturally hostile regions.
Broken friendship: co-witnesses split over the -17 falsification.
Family: parent/child disagree whether inherited promises are moral obligations.
Intimate/political: cross-regional marriage acts as informal guarantor of a reciprocity chain.
Ideological: obligation-as-sacred vs consent-of-living-generation.
Economic dependency: debt buyer profits from failures while also preventing cascading collapse.
Concealed debt: a celebrated region's status rests on another culture quietly covering its failed obligation.
Shared failure: principals knew of falsification and chose silence.
Category change: former ideological rivals become co-defendants when archive destruction threatens both.

## Pressure results
P1 dominant figure loses influence → certifications once accepted on reputation are challenged.
P2 underestimated figure gains legitimacy → obscure oral historian becomes essential after archive loss.
P3 rivals cooperate → reciprocal chains make cooperation morally ambiguous: genuine reconciliation or self-preservation?
P4 institution fails → archive destruction does not merely lose records; it destabilizes the reality of rights.
P5 outsider contested → neutral Witness becomes valuable because neutrality itself is scarce.
P6 irrational exchange → debt magnate forgives a major obligation to acquire the right to witness another chain.
P7 successor fails → heir refuses inherited duty on ethical grounds, not incompetence.
P8 irrelevant indispensable → Old Rusk's unreliable memory becomes the only surviving evidence of a community's rights.

### Emergent surprise
Revision solved much of the generic-politics problem. The world generated a central philosophical conflict: **Can the living be morally bound by promises made by the dead when society itself depends upon everyone pretending the answer is yes?** Old Rusk becomes simultaneously indispensable and untrustworthy.

# CROSS-WORLD FINDINGS

## The three invented-character test
PASS in all three. More importantly:
- Mara Venn (Inland Sea) can plausibly become a primary POV.
- Pera Doss (Roads) became a powerful information node without formal status.
- Old Rusk (Concord) creates epistemic danger because the world needs testimony it cannot trust.

The league is no longer required to generate every important character.

## Relationship-history test
Inland Sea produces relationships through ecological dependence and changing property.
Roads produces relationships through repeated movement and institutional contact.
Concord 2.0 produces relationships through inherited moral/legal obligations.

## Oracle elasticity
All three absorb the common pressure battery without literal sports translation.
Roads has the widest mechanical elasticity.
Inland Sea creates the strongest effects on private/ordinary life.
Concord 2.0 creates the strongest moral/philosophical consequences.

## Unexpected generation test
**Inland Sea:** generated ownership/memory conflict stronger than its original "drowned mystery."
**Roads:** generated trust/information conflict stronger than its original infrastructure premise.
**Concord 2.0:** transformed itself from generic politics into inherited-obligation civilization.

# COUNCIL DELIBERATION

**Beat Writer:** Inland Sea remains the richest emotional environment. Its best emergent stories concern marriages, graves, professions, property and identity—not ruins.

**Architect:** Roads remains the most causally productive machine. But the trust-infrastructure discovery is more interesting than road engineering itself.

**Librarian:** Concord 2.0 now deserves equal consideration. Witness culture and reciprocity chains naturally produce decades of shared history and provenance-sensitive storytelling.

**Scout:** No simulated relationship above should be represented as a factual analogue of owner relationships. Verified Source Canon remains sparse. Competitive facts—such as the 2025 podium and known trades—may later be entered separately into the Oracle ledger.

**Umpire:** All three now survive. Do not hybridize them yet. The next danger is falling in love with clever systems rather than people.

**Closer:** Zero Draft 03 has achieved its purpose. We now have three worlds that generate consequences independently. The next test should no longer add worldbuilding breadth. It should go narrower and deeper.
