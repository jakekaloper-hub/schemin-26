# G11–G16 — NARRATIVE ARCHITECTURE CLOSURE
**Status:** Bullpen working decision

## G11 POV architecture
Tested: single hero; 12-source ensemble; 5–7 constellation; convergence.
**Decision:** Convergence-Constellation, approximately 5 primary POV functions, final count adjustable before scene outline.
Rules:
- at least one zero-source POV;
- no POV exists to report lore;
- not all source-derived principals need POV;
- POV access expands as separate social/geographic systems collide.

## G12 one-novel macrostructure
**Act I — The World Still Works:** ordinary life demonstrates obligations before crisis; contradictions appear locally.
**Act II — The Same Words Mean Different Things:** disputed geography turns private problems into institutional conflicts; alliances form for mixed motives.
**Act III — The Cost of Being Right:** factions discover legally/morally coherent solutions that are mutually destructive; relationships fracture/recombine.
**Act IV — What the Living Choose to Carry:** resolution comes through choices about which obligations deserve continuation, reinterpretation or release—not discovery of a magic/legal answer.

No sequel dependency.

## G13 inciting incident
Options tested: newly exposed territory; registry contradiction; neutral intermediary death; failed ceremony; outsider arrival.

**Selected working inciting incident:** A routine survey connected to newly exposed land produces measurements that make two historically valid obligations impossible to satisfy simultaneously.

Why: begins mundane; affects ordinary people; cannot be solved by killing antagonist; naturally expands from household/property problem into civilization question; gives Mara relevance without making her chosen.

Umpire: PASS.

## G14 central dramatic question
Can a civilization preserve the protection created by inherited promises without forcing the living to obey a geography—and moral settlement—that no longer exists?

Personal form: when keeping your word harms someone the promise was meant to protect, what does faithfulness require?

## G15 mystery architecture
Mysteries enrich rather than power the plot:
- why historical shoreline records diverge;
- what truly happened in the failed expedition;
- whether the Drowning Settlement was deliberate;
- which institutions knowingly preserved useful falsehoods;
- whether rare metaphysical phenomena are memory, nature, divine action or interpretation.

No Book-I solution required for ancient metaphysics. Present human causality must remain understandable.

## G16 ending classes
Three endings tested without fixing exact character outcomes:
A Restoration — repair old system. Rejected as too conservative/easy.
B Destruction — abolish obligations. Rejected as thematically simplistic.
C Costly Reconstitution — characters preserve the moral purpose of reciprocity while breaking/reinterpreting parts of inherited form; resolution requires real losses and leaves no faction fully vindicated.

**Working ending class: C.**
This defines thematic closure, not who lives/wins or exact final scene.

## Umpire
PASS G11–G16 for planning. Exact POV roster and irreversible deaths remain downstream decisions; do not force them before character/scene architecture earns them.
