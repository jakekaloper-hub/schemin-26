# SCHEMIN ZERO DRAFT — ARCHITECTURE STRESS TEST 02
**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Status:** DISCOVERY / NON-CANONICAL
**Test set:** Shattered Concord, Inland Sea, Roads of Ash, Second Sky
**Gate:** No prose authorized

## Method
Each architecture is tested against the same failure conditions:
1. Can all 12 principal transformations exist without feeling assigned a slot?
2. Can relationships predate and outweigh public conflict?
3. Can original non-league characters matter independently?
4. Does geography cause story rather than decorate it?
5. Are there multiple currencies of power?
6. Can weak/strong positions reverse plausibly?
7. Can Oracle events create pressure without literal translation?
8. Can the world survive multiple books?
9. Does the premise permit intimate/private stories?
10. Does the architecture resist obvious franchise imitation?
11. Can history remain contested?
12. Can the supernatural remain uncertain if desired?

Scoring is diagnostic, not canon selection: 1 weak, 3 workable, 5 exceptional.

# A. SHATTERED CONCORD

## Character-placement test
All 12 transformations fit easily because the confederated structure accommodates diplomats, custodians, frontier cultures, labor leaders, merchants, environmental experts and regional powers.

This is also its danger: almost anything fits. A world that accepts every idea without friction may have no distinctive identity.

## Relationship test
Exceptional. The Concord itself can create relationships that cross borders: fosterage, arbitration, intermarriage, military service, road rights, debt guarantees, religious offices and shared legal education.

Negotiator / Veteran Custodian works particularly well because adaptive bargaining collides with precedent.
Veteran / Popular Champion gains class tension without requiring villainy.
Debt Magnate / Labor Figure becomes structurally necessary rather than cosmetic.

## Oracle torture test
Example abstract pressure: a previously dominant source identity suffers repeated defeats.
The Concord can translate this into eroding legitimacy, failed votes, lost clients, a rival legal interpretation gaining traction, or private doubt—without needing a battlefield defeat.

Example abstract pressure: an underperforming identity suddenly surges.
This could alter marriage negotiations, patronage, creditor confidence, religious interpretation or access to a council seat.

PASS.

## Failure mode
The world risks becoming "political houses around a council"—functional but familiar. The Umpire requires a distinctive institution stranger than a parliament, tournament or throne contest.

## Verdict
**SURVIVES**, but only if its central civic mechanism becomes original enough to justify the architecture.

# B. INLAND SEA

## Character-placement test
Strongest transformation pressure of the four. Characters cannot simply occupy castles; they need ecological and economic reasons to exist.

- Negotiator: upland water-right intermediary / multilingual boundary broker.
- Veteran Custodian: keeper of pre-recession shoreline law and ceremonial claims.
- Frontier Philosopher: horse-country defender of newly exposed salt corridors.
- Popular Champion: dock/labor organizer, ferryman-warrior or floodwall veteran.
- Marsh Culture: delta people whose "marginal" knowledge becomes central as water retreats.
- Information Patient: chart archive, courier intelligence, salvage registry or temple records.
- Blue-Collar Spoiler: shipwright, levee engineer, caravan/portage master.
- Southern Predator: headwater polity controlling forest products and river source access.
- Storm Reader: navigator/climatologist/priest of seasonal patterns.
- Debt Magnate: finances salvage, grain movement and port reconstruction.
- Marsh Navigator: pilot through changing shoals and drowned ruins.
- Dryland Host: salt-road oasis/caravan patron controlling refuge between receding ports.

No character needs to be assigned a kingdom.

## Relationship test
Very strong because water makes enemies economically interdependent.
A headwater ruler can hate a delta polity yet depend on its ports.
A creditor can finance a rival's floodwall because its collapse would destroy his own debt book.
A navigator may possess intimate knowledge of an enemy coastline.
Old shoreline marriages can become legally ambiguous as territory reappears.

## Ordinary-life test
Exceptional. Recession changes wells, fishing, disease, farming, transport, property, burial grounds, ports and family livelihoods. The world has stakes below elites.

## Oracle torture test
Weekly power changes can map into confidence, access, contracts, expeditions, reputation, religious interpretation, discoveries, political coalitions or delayed consequences. Geography need not change weekly.

PASS.

## Deep-time test
"The Peace That Erased Its Makers" and "The Empty Century" work naturally. Receding water can reveal evidence but must not become a convenient exposition machine.

## Failure mode
The Sea itself could become the protagonist. Every mystery could devolve into "what is underwater?" The story could become archaeological puzzle fantasy rather than human drama.

## Umpire condition
No more than one major revelation in a narrative phase should be solved simply because water exposes a ruin. Characters must create consequences; geography only creates conditions.

## Verdict
**SURVIVES STRONGLY.**

# C. ROADS OF ASH

## Character-placement test
Equally strong, but through institutions rather than ecology.

- Negotiator: right-of-passage broker between jurisdictions.
- Veteran Custodian: senior figure in a road oath/order whose authority predates states.
- Frontier Philosopher: mounted protector of long-distance corridors.
- Popular Champion: bridge crews / convoy guards / labor fraternities.
- Marsh Culture: masters of causeways outsiders cannot maintain.
- Information Patient: courier intelligence and route records.
- Blue-Collar Spoiler: engineer/forge/caravan figure becomes indispensable.
- Southern Predator: controls a dangerous but crucial jungle corridor.
- Storm Reader: predicts seasonal route viability.
- Debt Magnate: finances caravans, repairs and toll concessions.
- Marsh Navigator: finds "impossible" bypasses.
- Dryland Host: controls caravan sanctuary and provisioning.

## Relationship test
Best of field. Roads create repeated contact among people who are not political allies. Shared journeys, hostage exchanges, apprenticeships, convoy disasters, toll disputes, rescue debts and old road oaths generate believable prehistory.

## Ordinary-life test
Excellent. A bridge failure raises grain prices hundreds of miles away. A closed pass separates families. A toll war can bankrupt villages without a battle.

## Oracle torture test
Excellent. Almost any power shift can alter route access, credit, confidence, escort contracts, toll policy, information speed or diplomatic reach.

PASS.

## Deep-time test
"Inherited Machine" becomes extremely strong. But the builders must not become a generic hyper-advanced precursor civilization whose lost technology solves the plot.

## Failure mode
Infrastructure can become bloodless. Characters may spend too much narrative time discussing routes, tolls and repairs. The mythic/emotional register needs independent religion, family structures, death customs and sacred geography.

## Umpire condition
Roads cannot merely be ancient Roman roads with fantasy names. Their social and legal consequences must be stranger and deeper than transport infrastructure.

## Verdict
**SURVIVES STRONGLY.**

# D. SECOND SKY

## Character-placement test
Some transformations flourish: Storm Reader, Negotiator, Navigator, Frontier Philosopher. Others require conspicuous invention to justify their role.

This is the first architecture where the council can feel itself "placing the twelve." That is a warning.

## Relationship test
Isolation makes old relationships harder to generate naturally unless there is a strong cross-plateau institution. Descent licenses risk becoming the single explanation for too many connections.

## Ordinary-life test
Strong locally: agriculture, altitude sickness, travel, burial, weather, isolation. But the permanent cloud premise demands many downstream answers and could monopolize exposition.

## Oracle torture test
Moderate. Power changes can influence descent rights and routes, but repeated Oracle pressure risks overusing the same mechanisms.

## Failure mode
Premise-first fiction. Readers may remember "the cloud world" more than the people.

## Verdict
**HOLD / BENCH.** Do not discard its best elements. Atmospheric verticality, forbidden descent and changing weather may be harvested later without making them the entire cosmology.

# Hybridization test

The Architect proposed combining Inland Sea + Roads of Ash: an ancient transport network surrounding and crossing a receding inland sea.

**Umpire objection:** obvious temptation to keep every good idea. Hybridization can produce feature bloat and erase the clarity that made each architecture strong.

**Beat Writer counter:** one or two inherited roads/causeways may deepen Inland Sea without making the Roads architecture co-equal.

**Librarian ruling:** do not hybridize architectures yet. Preserve the possibility as an explicit branch.

# POV stress

## Constellation POV
Works best in Concord and Inland Sea. Allows 5–7 primary viewpoints while the remaining principals retain consequential off-page lives.

Critical rule: **all 12 source-inspired characters do not need POV chapters.**

## Convergence POV
Works best in Inland Sea and Roads. Regional narratives can initially appear unrelated before resource, route, legal and relationship dependencies converge.

## Spine + Satellites
Rejected for now. It creates gravitational pressure to make the Trade-Jedi-derived character "Jake but fantasy," violating the no-guaranteed-protagonist principle.

# Supporting-character intrusion test
A healthy architecture must permit an invented character to become more important than a source-inspired principal.

Test character: **an apprentice surveyor whose mother owns a failing ferry and whose measurements contradict the official boundary record.**

Concord: useful but risks becoming plot courier.
Inland Sea: naturally central to land claims, family survival and historical mystery.
Roads: naturally central to bridge/right-of-way disputes and ancient measurements.
Second Sky: requires more premise-specific scaffolding.

PASS strongest: Inland Sea / Roads.

## Next decisive test
The remaining three must now undergo a **relationship-history simulation**:
- create 20–30 years of pre-novel relational history;
- establish 4–6 institutions that repeatedly bring characters together;
- add original supporting characters;
- test one friendship, one family bond, one economic dependency, one ideological rivalry, one concealed debt, one old shared failure, and one relationship that changes category;
- then inject the same fictionalized Oracle shocks into all three architectures and compare which produces the richest consequences with the least author forcing.

Only after that should the Closer recommend a foundation.
