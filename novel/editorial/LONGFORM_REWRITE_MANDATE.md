# Longform Rewrite Mandate — Prologue through Chapter II
Status: BINDING FOR V2
Leads: Beat Writer + Librarian. Umpire gate.

The first drafts are ZERO DRAFTS. Their factual spine, chronology, canonical objects and useful scene ideas survive; their compressed recap rhythm does not.

## Literary objective
Build an original epic-fantasy novel with deep history, geography, social institutions, competing POV interpretations, patient scene construction, consequential dialogue and ordinary inhabitants. Draw from the broad craft traditions of historical/epic fantasy—deep time and mythic geography on one side, political consequence and human pressure on the other—without imitating any living author's distinctive prose.

## Applies retroactively to all three opening units
The Prologue, Chapter I and Chapter II must feel written by the same novelist in the same world. A deeper Prologue changes how later scenes read; therefore Chapter I and II must be rewritten, not merely appended.

## Mandatory changes
- Replace matchup-by-matchup recap cadence with 4–6 substantial scene movements.
- Let off-page characters enter through rumor, couriers, Book slips, weather, letters, damaged objects and public reaction.
- Give territories ordinary life: workers, clerks, cooks, scouts, stable hands, caddies, attendants, travelers.
- Let institutions have procedures and friction.
- Use numbers sparingly as physical evidence: receipts, stone, ledgers, boards.
- Expand dialogue and subtext.
- Do not explain every symbol.
- Preserve uncertainty. No retroactive prophecy.
- Preserve owner-character canon absolutely.
- Preserve factual chronology absolutely.
- Never fabricate missing Draft Night picks/history.
- Humor emerges from people and world logic, not constant punchlines.
- Winning does not equal virtue; losing does not equal incompetence.
- Every scene must alter knowledge, relationship, territory, reputation, or an object.

## Continuity consequences
Prologue establishes Harrowgate as a working name for the Compact's central hall/record institution, pending final naming approval. If retained, Chapters I–II may refer to its clerks, notices and recordkeeping.
The public preservation of results becomes a social fact: scores can become inscriptions, broadsheets and Book records.
The courier network becomes recurring connective tissue.
The championship receipt and Belt are parallel artifacts: documentary proof versus possession.
Chapter I must feel like the first disruption of a world introduced in the Prologue.
Chapter II must show society reacting to Week I conclusions before Week II overturns many of them.
