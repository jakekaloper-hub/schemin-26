# SCHEMIN INHABITANT ONTOLOGY V1

**Status:** COMMISSIONER-DIRECTED CANON PATCH  
**Date:** 2026-09-29  
**Purpose:** Define what kinds of beings exist in the Schemin universe without forcing visual canon into unsupported species lore.

## Prime rule

**Visual form does not automatically create a species.**

The universe distinguishes:
- members of established peoples/populations;
- singular one-of-a-kind beings;
- natural phenomena;
- inhabitants whose broader species/population status remains intentionally undefined.

Legal/social personhood follows sapience and civic recognition, not human anatomy.

## Population-backed inhabitants

The following are not unique biological anomalies. Their world contains others of their own kind:

### Jake Kaloper / ObiWan Jacoby — Trade Jedi
Human. Humans are a widespread people in the world.

### Wilson Look / D0nkey K0ng — Arsenal Gorilla Warrior
A member of a gorilla-bodied people. Current canon remains **GORILLA**, never centaur.

### Kevin Zeek / Red Leopards — Predator Board
A member of an anthropomorphic leopard/jaguar people. His strength and status are individual; his body form is not unique.

### Jordan Hollingshead / Slob on my Dobb — Frat-Bro Berserker
A member of a broader horned rough-country human-beast people/culture. His exact personality and extreme physique are individual; his broad body-form is not unique.

### Bobby Mitchell / Mud Dogs — Swamp-Born Menace
A member of a sapient wetland beast/pack people. Mud Dogs may hold territory, form communities, remember routes and participate in Compact society through their own customs.

### Ben Whipple / Seven Deadly Chins — People's Champ
Human. He belongs to broader rough-country/laboring human communities. His size, style and reputation are individual.

## Singular beings

These characters are currently **one of a kind**. Do not infer a broader race, village, species, or hidden population from their form.

### Austin Byars / The Belt Keeper
A singular supernatural humanoid champion. His nature may remain partially mysterious. No "Belt Keeper species" exists unless later canon explicitly creates one.

### Zach Wilson / Dr. Duckhook
A singular anthropomorphic white duck-person. Ordinary ducks can exist; no civilization of duck-people is implied.

### David Babb / The LLC — Hostile Takeover
A singular individual in his exact world-role/embodiment. Do not extrapolate a species or population of LLC/corporate beings from his visual identity.

## Cross-division co-resident peoples

### Phillip Pitts / Three Dreaded Snake
Body lock remains one reptilian humanoid body with three serpent heads. He belongs to a broader **reptilian people/culture**. The exact prevalence of his three-headed morphology remains open unless separately established.

### Brandon Pryor / Chili Cheesers — Chili Outlaw
Human. He belongs to broader **human frontier/tableland communities**.

### TDS + Chili co-residence lock
TDS's reptilian population and Chili's human frontier communities inhabit/coexist inside the same wider western-headwater contact zone even though their 2026 League division affiliations differ.

This is a governing proof that:
- League divisions are not racial/species borders;
- League divisions are not absolute sovereign borders;
- inhabitants of different divisions can share towns, roads, markets, watersheds and civil jurisdictions;
- a division overlay may cross or overlap ordinary civil geography.

## El Niño — natural force, not a character body

**El Niño is a moving storm/weather system in the Schemin universe.**

It is not:
- a humanoid elemental;
- a civic person;
- a storm warrior;
- a human character wearing storm effects.

Its "actions" are physical weather consequences:
- wind;
- rain;
- flooding;
- storm surge;
- lightning;
- route closures;
- infrastructure damage;
- crop/river effects;
- altered travel and battle conditions.

El Niño may move across any division because weather ignores League affiliation. Its 2026 Pizza membership is a League classification, not a territorial restriction on the storm.

Narration may personify the storm metaphorically, but artwork and world ontology must not render a literal humanoid El Niño unless Commissioner canon changes.

## Civic law

Sapient nonhuman peoples and singular sapient beings can:
- own or hold land where local law permits;
- contract;
- participate in Compact/Contest institutions;
- maintain households, retainers or communities;
- enter ordinary civic spaces unless anatomy/scale creates a practical architectural constraint.

The world does not react to a leopard-person, gorilla-person or Mud Dog as if fantasy has suddenly appeared; these forms are part of ordinary reality.

Singular beings may attract curiosity because they are unique, not because nonhuman life itself is unknown.

## Architecture consequence

Buildings should be designed for the inhabitants who actually use them:
- population-backed nonhuman communities develop appropriate doors, stairs, furniture, roads, stables, drainage and public space;
- singular beings may require bespoke adaptations;
- Dr. Duckhook's club may be physically adapted to him without implying a duck civilization;
- Mud Dog settlements/paths may privilege quadrupedal movement;
- gorilla-bodied communities may favor stronger vertical loads, broad stairs and robust joinery.

## Metaphysics firewall

The existence of unusual bodies does **not** prove magic, gods, or supernatural theology.

El Niño is a natural weather phenomenon in-world even when unusually narratively consequential.
Austin's singular supernatural condition remains exceptional and does not settle the truth of religion or cosmology.

## Production rule

Every character-bearing scene resolves:
1. representation class;
2. population status;
3. body architecture;
4. civic/social assumptions;
5. architecture/access implications;
6. prohibited species extrapolations.

Unknown population status remains UNKNOWN. Never invent an entire people just to explain one image.
