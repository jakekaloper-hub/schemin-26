## 1. Executive board summary
**Proposed design:** one temperate subcontinent opening south onto a warm Inner Sea. **Great Watershed** gives the continent its causal skeleton; **Fractured Highlands** creates defensible valleys and multiple drainage divides; **Inland Sea** creates navigable estuaries and lower maritime exchange. All three are simultaneous properties of *one* existing Commonwealth, **not** competing maps. Divisions are nonexclusive cultural overlays, never sovereign borders. Owner homelands retain physical-zone identity. Nothing here establishes exact distances, new LOC IDs, named rivers, verified outlets or weather observations.

**Counterweights raised:** Geography rejects cartoonishly straight north–south rivers; Librarian rejects treating dramatic Week 6 relief imagery as measured coordinates; Authors reject a map of twelve isolated mascots; Cinematography rejects camera-dependent relocating of coastlines; Engineering rejects a second spatial authority; Umpire refuses 'physically certified' on existing synthetic tests.

## 3. Continental mountain, water and coast hypothesis
- **Elevations:** high northern/northeastern Spine; fractured secondary ridges descend into Upper Valleys; basin terraces and braided local valleys settle lower; southern wetlands sit below central riverlands. Western tablelands may be high and dry, not necessarily low and flat.
- **Water tree (abstract):** Alpine precipitation / eastern storm rainfall → separate headwaters and upland tributaries → navigable portions of Central Basin → constrained confluences and meanders → low-gradient Southern Wetlands/Delta → Inner Sea. Fractured-highland divides mean **not every creek enters the same river**. Western drainage may be seasonal or locally internally drained, if later evidence supports it. Eastern storm-coast catchments can discharge directly to coastal waters; do not force them across Central Basin.
- **Ridges and passes:** multiple defensible ridge corridors, not a single cliff-wall separating characters. Existing Bridge at the Mountain Lake is an upland route site; no relocation.
- **Sea geometry:** Inner Sea provides navigation and sheltered bays, but is not presumed to penetrate geographically impossible kilometers inland. Deep estuary length and exact coastline remain unresolved.
- **Hydrological acceptance:** Require an explicit source-to-outlet directed graph, validated mountain/valley divides, documented depressions, floodplain inundation under defined discharge, and no unsupported river mouths. Synthetic conditioning alone does not pass.
- **Do not name a new great river** until historical location reconciliation and Commissioner geography approval.

## 4. Climate and seasonal ecology
- Geographic causes: southern/eastern maritime moisture, easterly storm exposure, northern altitude lapse/cold, western partial rain shadow, southern wetland humidity.
- **Late October candidate:** frost and possible snow at Northern Spine elevations; autumn color and cooler nights in Upper Valleys/Basin; dry/open western grazing country; cool rain and strong storms possible on eastern shore; southern delta generally warmer and damp; Inner Sea ports moderated by water temperatures.
- **Winter:** higher snowpack and conditional mountain-pass restrictions; basin frost/cold rain possible; southeastern maritime zones comparatively moderated; no blanket statewide snowfall.
- **Spring:** snowmelt/river rise in uplands, flood tendency downstream, agricultural calendar consequences. Summer brings evaporation and western drought risk, plus humidity/convective storms east and south.
- All numeric weather parameters in PR #226 are **HYPOTHETICAL** and cannot substitute for a calibrated in-world seasonal model. Special El Niño manifestations remain separate from normal weather.

## 8. Central Basin candidate reassessment (PR #226)
Existing **Baseline**, **West Ridge** and **Central Valley** are synthetic 33×33 test terrains. Code tests establish reproducibility, route graph identity, downhill/priority-flood drainage conditioning and routed flats. They do **not** establish correct shape or dimensions of a fictional continent.
- Baseline: simplest comparative reference; locally contains artificial sinks before conditioning. No certification.
- West Ridge: distinct western relief useful for Fractured Highlands hypothesis; does not prove western rain-shadow or historic placement.
- Central Valley: explicit depressed core suggests confluence opportunities; not necessarily a valid naturally navigable valley.
- **Board ruling:** reject choosing a winner by least slope, smallest sink count or beauty. All three remain candidates. Continental outlet and coastline constraints must be approved before local metrics may become history.
