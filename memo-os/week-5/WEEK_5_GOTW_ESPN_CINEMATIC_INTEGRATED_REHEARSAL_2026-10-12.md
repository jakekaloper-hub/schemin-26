## Manuscript — THE PRICE OF THE CENTER
At the neutral grounds, the chessboard rose above the League's ordinary market paths like an argument too large for its own speakers. Stalls stood at the perimeter, shuttered early so their owners could watch. The squares were broad as courtyards, the pieces taller than gatehouses. The Belt Keeper approached from the League Chamber road with no announcement beyond the weight of his armor. His belt was not the prize tonight; it was the history he had brought to the board.

Three Dreaded Snake had come up from the wetland approaches. Three heads shared one body, but they did not share a line of sight. One studied the black king, another watched the pieces nearest the crowd, and the third tracked a lane that no other spectator could yet see.

“You cannot guard every square,” said the first head.

“No,” His Majesty's Blood answered. “Only the ones you will need.”

The opening positions belonged to no one. On the Snake's side, three formations pushed forward with stubborn purpose: the Javonte line gained fifteen points of ground, Emanuel's wing held another fifteen, and Tuten's raiders advanced nearly fourteen. A wall of Bengal-striped shields stiffened the center. For a moment the board looked crowded with threats, and the crowd leaned toward the Snake's half of the field.

But HMB's answering formation had different dimensions. A silver runner marked Smith-Njigba's 23.9-point reach and pierced the long diagonal. The Viking shield wall followed with 19.9 points of force, shutting a corridor the Snake had hoped to exploit. Jameson's 18.7-point cavalry crossed the open files; Boston's 14.25 and Loop's 14 supplied pressure on the flanks. These were not literal NFL plays happening in this order. They were the relative weights of performances made visible at once, as though the whole week's evidence had been cast onto one board.

The Snake's central command piece trembled. Its small brass plate read SHough: minus 0.16. The three heads disagreed about how much protection to spend on it. One demanded a rescue; another urged an attack; the third watched the widening gap between the two. The exposed square was not an insult to a player. It was the visible cost of a fantasy lineup in which useful contributions could not compensate for every weakness.

A bell sounded from the League's perimeter. No final piece moved. The spectators could see that the weight of the board favored the Belt Keeper on the available scoring evidence, but they could not yet claim a certified checkmate. His Majesty's Blood looked toward the Snake's three faces and waited for the last official count. Even the most powerful player at the table had to submit to the rules of the game.
