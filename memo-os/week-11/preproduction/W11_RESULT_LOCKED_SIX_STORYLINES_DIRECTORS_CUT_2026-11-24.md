## G01 · His Majesty’s Blood defeats D0nkey K0ng, 168.20–129.75

**F09 — After you, Austin.** The cart was there before either warrior. Its left wheel stood on the damp stone beside the same working lantern that had burned over the Mountain Lake crossing the week before, while the carter kept the reins close and listened to the lake batter the pier. Across the narrow deck, Wilson Look stepped carefully into the roadway, four equine hooves carrying one immense Arsenal-armored gorilla torso. Wind pulled against the cart’s canopy, the crimson edges of Austin Byars’ cloak and the leaves caught in Wilson’s armored greaves. Wilson moved sideways, gracious enough to be insulting. “After you, Austin.” His smile claimed the bridge as a personal gift. Austin’s hand found the Belt Keeper’s buckle; he looked at the gap Wilson had opened, then past it at the worker who had never been asked. No one laughed when the carter shifted the reins.

**F10 — He was here before us.** “He was here before us,” Austin said. He did not shout above the squall; Wilson heard him anyway. For an instant the champion’s attention was not on his opponent but on the ordinary man and the wagon whose safe passage mattered less to Wilson than making Austin bow. A slick hoof sounded against the worn center stones, caught, and shifted again. The carter saw that too. Austin waited until the cart’s path was visible, then moved toward Wilson. Steel met steel as the wind drove rain between them. The strike that settled their quarrel belonged to Austin, not to the weather. Its force threw Wilson’s weight backward; the footing he had so ostentatiously controlled gave way beneath four skidding hooves. Keep the parapet, lake and cart in the same geographic orientation as F09.

**F11 — The carter listens.** Where Wilson had stood there was a space and a scream of lake spray. His armored bulk went over the intact bridge into the storm-chopped water below. Austin took one step toward the edge, still wearing his belt, and stopped. The carter held his reins so tightly the leather creaked. The moment felt longer than the duel. Then a dark Arsenal head broke through the chop, coughing, alive, fighting toward a plausible bank. Austin’s relief arrived before his pride; the carter saw both. If the existing jester-cap motif appears, it may fall afterward as a small, rueful sign of rivalry, never as a rescue device or victorious punch line during the fear. **HMB won by 38.45 points; Wilson lives.** The bridge remains passable. This scene uses Mountain Lake foam and spray, not invented directional rapids.

*Inklings / Production decision:* The moral reversal is Wilson’s false courtesy becoming genuine concern for a life. Photograph the worker’s waiting hands, the same midspan lantern, the slip-causing contact and the unmistakably **living** four-legged survivor. ## G02 · Slob on my Dobb defeats El Niño, 138.86–134.60

**F12 — Six. I told her six.** The slip beneath Jordan Hollingshead’s visor had curled in the damp, but the one word on it still read SIX. The covered beer load behind the familiar SAME SPEED truck made a dull, rhythmic weight against the straps. Manning Welty stood at the Hearthroad driver’s window watching fine pale gravel move in the first runoff. “What time do they expect it?” he asked. Jordan looked at the slip before he looked at Manning. “Six. I told her six.” He could make a promise in seconds; the weather would take much longer to answer it. The red-cord opener clicked against the dash as Jordan lowered the window and tried to see how deep the first rut had become. Manning was still there when Jordan started the engine. He does **not** later appear magically on a separate swamp road.

**F13 — The radio call.** Some distance after that departure, on a proposed later wetland approach whose loaded-truck access still requires Atlas acceptance, a gust got under the canvas. Jordan had kept the main straps tight; one **secondary** lash, already beating against a metal ring, slipped free. Two heavy kegs left the trailer, hit the wet surface with real mass and rolled toward the shallow roadside flood. The others stayed secured. Jordan watched the nearer keg cross his mirror and caught the washout at the edge of the rear wheel’s track. He braked before the vehicle followed its cargo. The engine shook. So did his hand, very slightly, when he reached for the radio. “I’ve got most of it,” he said after a pause. “Two came off. I’m stopping here and checking the ground before I move.” Whoever was listening at the taproom heard a driver explaining what had happened, not claiming an impossible arrival time. Jordan marked where the two had fallen and arranged a safe recovery or accounting before continuing. **Slob won by 4.26 points**, but the narrowness should live in Jordan’s small recovered margin of control, not in a cliff-hanging truck wreck.

*Inklings / Production decision:* The wager is between a deadline and responsibility. Do not pack the fall, axle danger, recovery and entire phone call into separate poster tiles. F13’s strongest still is Jordan actively stopping a *recoverable* loss. SOURCE HOLD: a loaded truck’s through-route along Delta Causeway has not been verified; staging cannot certify that road.

## G03 · The Chili Cheesers defeat ObiWan Jacoby, 133.52–83.79

**F14 — Off the board.** Rain pressed the public carnival into the foot of the Eastern Ascent, pooling at the microphone stand and running down the edges of Pittsy’s November 19 sheet. Brandon Pryor found the line for The Chili Cheesers and ObiWan Jacoby, then found no number beside it: **OFF BOARD**. Two people nearby were already debating a different House market. “We could’ve argued about who he made the underdog all the way up here,” Brandon told Jake Kaloper. Jake looked past the notice at the uphill crowd. “Listen to them. That could’ve been our game.” He reached for the microphone, and people who had ignored them started to turn. Brandon’s recovered black rodeo hat, lost to mud in Week 10, gathered drops of water along the brim. There was an audience now; Jake had invited it.

**F15 — The safest seat.** Jake climbed onto the hinged seat above the Wolf Brand Chili **NO BEANS** tank as though he had rented the carnival for his own amusement. “Brandon played D5 quarterback in Texas, folks,” he told the microphone—an in-universe heckle, not a verified school biography. “Safest place to stand is right behind the target.” He grinned down at Brandon and asked whether he should run a slant. Brandon turned one ball in his palm. “Sit tight.” In the source-grounded axis of ball, target and hinge, **one accurate throw** found the painted mark. The seat dropped; Jake fell into thick red-brown chili rather than rainwater, and the microphone gave a wet pop. Through the glass his palm met the pane. The crowd became muffled motion above him, and the comedy acquired a full, silent breath of vulnerability.

**F16 — At the ladder.** Jake’s hair broke the surface first, then his face, then his robe and both boots on the lower ladder rungs—all coated in heavy chili while clean rain still struck from above. He tried to wipe his eyes with his sleeve and only spread the mess. Brandon was beside the ladder before the crowd finished cheering. He had the hat in one hand and nothing clever left to say. The board announced The Chili Cheesers **133.52**, ObiWan Jacoby **83.79**. “I thought we were going back,” Jake said, quietly enough to miss the microphone. Brandon stayed where he was. The 49.73-point defeat hurt; it did **not** certify elimination. The refreshed TheFFHub model places ObiWan’s playoff probability at **12.88%** entering the three divisional weeks. The humiliation belongs to this public carnival, not Jake’s separate After Dark shack.

*Inklings / Production decision:* The payoff is not only a splash; it is the crowd he demanded becoming the audience to his own worst performance. Keep Brandon’s one throw, Jake’s underwater face and the final full-length **both-boots** portrait, with rain material visibly different from chili. No ObiWan championship belt, no wind-assisted throw, no PDF-style four-panel collage.

## G04 · Three Dreaded Snake defeats Red Leopards, 125.74–94.80

**F17 — Pick a side.** Kevin Zeek stopped at the break in the vegetation. Rain had erased the easy-looking path, leaving roots slick and mud deep enough to steal a stride. Last week he had watched water undo his own defenses. He was not about to let Phillip see him hesitate. 'Race you to the dry stone.' Phillip Pitts turned all three heads toward the opening. 'You sure you want the short way?' Kevin grinned. 'I usually do.' He took the direct approach; Phillip followed the longer, firmer edge of the same visitor-side clearing.

**F18 — The dry stone.** Kevin had the first few strides. Then the mud caught his foot and he had to steady himself against a root. Across the clearing Phillip kept moving, one head watching the ground, another tracking Kevin, the third fixed on the sheltered stone. When Kevin reached it, Phillip was already waiting. Kevin looked down at his muddy legs. 'You should see me on dry ground.' Phillip glanced at the stone beneath them. 'All three of us would love to.' Kevin laughed despite himself. The Snake had won on his own ground, 125.74 to 94.80.

*Production:* A proposed informal challenge within the existing visitor-side clearing, not a new mapped route or institutional sport. One reptilian body, exactly three post-haircut heads. No hunting costume, bag, or private courtyard. Preserve storm and Week 10 continuity.

## G05 · Mud Dogs defeat Seven Deadly Chins, 151.26–117.66

**F19 — The wrong turn.** The Southern Wetlands flood had risen to the lower supports of the old Gators & Dawgs sign. The empty stands were on the **left**; the little Cancún postcard still held to the **right-hand** post above the water. Bobby Mitchell stood on a raised patch of truly dry ground, one clean paw in plain sight. His season had yielded only two victories before this week, and the card behind him advertised the future he was trying not to picture. Ben Whipple’s truck arrived with water washing over familiar wheel ruts. Ben opened his high window. “Which way to Playoff Island?” he asked. Bobby knew the safe ground; the angle of his paw proved it. He pointed the other way. “That stretch opens up.” The road he named only looked easy from Ben’s height. Bobby kept his eyes steady as the truck turned. He was **lying deliberately**, not issuing the truthful warning of a discarded earlier draft.

**F20 — The road that comes back.** Ben found a curve in the water he thought he remembered. Then the same stand supports, the same bend of the sign and the same half-drowned ruts appeared again. Headlights caught the painted letters from the wrong side. The direction had brought him back toward the place he started, not toward any mapped Playoff Island road. He braked with enough firm track left to turn the truck safely, and understanding altered his expression before anger did. From his small outpost Bobby saw the turn and allowed himself one private smile. Mud Dogs had beaten Seven Deadly Chins **151.26–117.66**, his third win of the season. The smile faded when the Cancún postcard moved in the wind. One successful piece of spite had not turned his year into a championship year. And Ben was not eliminated: Chins remained **6–5**, ESPN’s sixth seed, with an estimated **68.87%** playoff probability and a crucial divisional matchup at El Niño ahead.

*Inklings / Production decision:* Two POVs, one recognizable sign and one causally coherent loop. Bobby’s clean paw vs misleading point is the central irony; give the final postcard a moment of silence. No underwater truck, fictional permanent closure, literal Island panorama, owner-private quotation or confirmed Losers Beach arrival.

## G06 · Dr. Duckhook defeats The LLC, 173.01–127.60

**F21 — The visitor’s chair.** Outside the Country Club of Jackson, November rain raked across the windows and bent the course trees almost to the patio. Inside the executive office it remained warm, dry and intact. The groundskeeper returned the wet working key to its hook. He looked once at David Babb’s open folder, then left, shutting the door **before** the private discussion began. David sat in the visitor’s chair as a man confident of the next purchase. He had beaten ObiWan the previous week, and he believed his LLC investors would back the acquisition of Jackson. “After last week, my investors wanted to know what came next. I told them Jackson.” Zach Wilson glanced at the first page, the purchase offer. He slid it back **unsigned**. “Before you buy my place, maybe ask who’s running yours.” A second sheet crossed the desk, face down. David kept the unused pen in his hand.

**F22 — The second sheet.** David turned over a genuine-looking **fictional LLC board-action notice** with two unmistakable lines: *David Babb removed as corporate CEO. Dr. Duckhook appointed corporate CEO and chairman.* Zach had quietly spoken with members of David’s own executive board; some had asked him to take charge, and he had made further calls. Do not invent a numerical board vote, legal filing or owner-company shareholder list. “You went to my board?” David asked. “Some of them came to me,” Zach said. “I made the calls.” David reread the notice and stood so quickly his chair scraped the floor. The unused pen struck the dry desk beside the unsigned Jackson offer. “You think you can take my company and keep your club?” Zach did not raise his voice. “You never had Jackson.” The storm shook the glass, not the papers. The club was **not sold**. David remained the fantasy manager of **The LLC** even though his fictional corporate CEO role was gone. Only then did the week’s other result enter the room: Duckhook **173.01**, The LLC **127.60**. A cup was set down. David looked at it without sitting. “I take mine black.” Zach reached for the coffee.

*Inklings / Production decision:* Maintain two separate sheets, the key-return exit and the dry/wet contrast; first the offer, then the board revelation, then anger, then the black-coffee coda. The corporate reversal is **fixed owner fiction**, not a fantasy scoring mechanism. Avoid a fake shareholder meeting, comic office destruction or a groundskeeper overhearing the private exchange.
