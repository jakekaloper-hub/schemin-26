## 4. Week 4 candidate surfaces

### A. State of the Realm — PRIMARY
Best default destination.

Eligible examples:
- a meaningful change in playoff probability after Week 4;
- a major separation/compression around the seven-team playoff line;
- unusually difficult/easy remaining schedule;
- exact clinch/elimination later in season;
- a concise leverage fact that explains why an entering Week 5 matchup matters.

The page remains primarily standings + divisional consequence. Analytics is a small supporting notation.

### B. Matchup pages — SECONDARY
A matchup page may receive one compact context note only when the number changes how the result should be understood.

Examples:
- a loss causes a severe odds collapse;
- a win materially revives a weak position;
- a matchup creates the largest conditional playoff swing on the Week 5 slate.

Do not force an odds note into all six matchup pages.

### C. Week 5 GOTW handoff — HIGH-VALUE CANDIDATE
TDS × HMB is already Commissioner-selected as Week 5 GOTW.

After Week 4 finality, conditional Week 5 leverage may strengthen the handoff **if** the model identifies a material postseason swing.

It may not overturn the Commissioner selection. It only adds stakes if supported.

### D. Power Rankings — SEPARATE
Do not merge playoff probability into Power Rankings.

A team can rank highly in current strength while holding weaker playoff odds because record/schedule differ. That tension may be mentioned, but the systems remain separate.

### E. Pittsy's Book — GENERALLY NO
Do not mix model playoff probabilities with Pittsy's betting ledger unless a later explicit design decision creates a clearly labeled editorial comparison.

Pittsy's Book remains its own deterministic betting institution.

### F. Final Word — RARE
May use one consequence-level sentence if it strengthens the forward turn into Week 5. Do not close the issue with a percentage dump.
