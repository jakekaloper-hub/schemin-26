## Story progression

### Beat 1 — Not sounding the alarms yet

- El Niño is at home / inside the Storm Bowl or resolved home environment.
- The first signs of the storm are present.
- Small / miniature El Niño storm manifestations may appear as visual signals of Manning's mounting frustration, provided they read as environmental story shorthand rather than new permanent creatures/canon.
- Seven Deadly Chins sees the warnings but refuses to trigger the storm alarms.

### Beat 2 — Warning signs become impossible to ignore

- Pressure drops.
- Wind and weather escalate.
- The mini El Niño visual signals multiply / intensify as a comic expression of the building system.
- 7DC still fails to respond appropriately.
- The humor comes from delayed recognition, not stupidity for its own sake.

### Beat 3 — Too late

- The storm fully arrives.
- 7DC finally realizes the scale of the problem only after the warning window has effectively passed.
- This is the turn from dry comedy into environmental spectacle.

### Beat 4 — Whipple versus weather

- In a deliberately absurd fantasy-comedy beat, Whipple grabs his shotgun and attempts to shoot at the storm itself.
- The visual should read as futile defiance against weather, not tactical or realistic firearms instruction.
- The storm is unaffected.
- This is the payoff to the existing **Whipple Versus Weather** branch.

### Beat 5 — Washed away

- The storm overwhelms him.
- Whipple / 7DC is swept away by the storm.
- The image should remain stylized / comic / fantastical rather than graphic.
- The payoff is that refusing to sound the alarms left him reacting only after the system had become too large to control.
