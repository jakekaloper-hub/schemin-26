## 1. Board synthesis

Week 4 is not yet a finished story. The correct creative object is a set of six living reservoirs that can absorb scoring, injuries, transactions, wagers, league-chat dialogue, commissioner evidence and real-world personalization without forcing those events into a Wednesday narrative.

The provisional issue motif survives only as an opening-state hypothesis:

> **Ordinary life first; fantasy earns the disruption.**

Late-summer normalcy is useful texture, not a mandatory issue thesis. If live Week 4 evidence produces a stronger organizing idea, the Story Room must replace it.

### Provenance contract

- **COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED_FACT:** Jake directly supplied the owner behavior/event/detail.
- **EDITORIAL_JUDGMENT:** Bullpen/Story Room interpretation of verified or commissioner-supplied material.
- **STORY POSSIBILITY:** provisional branch that may become useful if events support it.
- **FICTIONALIZATION:** invented Schemin-world expression; never promoted into factual league history.
- **LIVE_LEAGUE_FACT:** requires current validated league evidence before publication.

Commissioner personalization is an ingredient, not an obligation.

### Scene rule

Each matchup may earn 2–4 scenes. This is a ceiling/range, not a quota. Every scene must change character, pressure, relationship, knowledge, location state or reader understanding. If it does none of those, cut it.

## 2. D0nkey K0ng × ObiWan Jacoby

**Maturity:** DEVELOPING  
**Entering contrast:** undefeated confidence versus deliberate preparation.  
**Primary POV opportunity:** whichever character experiences the largest change in self-conception during the matchup.

### Personalization inventory
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED_FACT: ObiWan has been training, cooking steaks on a red ceramic kamado-style grill and enjoying late-season waterskiing.
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED_FACT: ObiWan expects victory; a loss would be a significant disappointment.
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED_FACT: D0nkey K0ng enters undefeated and has said, "I'm on a heater."
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED_FACT: D0nkey is a battle-warrior personality and plays Clash of Clans.
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED_FACT / CONDITIONAL DIALOGUE: if D0nkey wins, approved candidate: "And down goes Obiwan Jacoby! The force has given me insight and wisdom."

### Narrative engine A — The Last Easy Day
1. ObiWan at lake/kamado/gym: confidence expressed through ordinary routine, not exposition.
2. Fantasy intrudes: preparation hardens; travel toward the verified matchup environment begins.
3. Live scoring determines whether confidence becomes control, anxiety or humiliation.
4. Result scene only if earned.

**IMAGE TELLS:** leisure narrowing into preparation; physical contrast between water/fire/training and hostile game environment.  
**NARRATION TELLS:** what ObiWan assumed before the game and what the game forces him to reconsider.

### Narrative engine B — The Heater Becomes a Target
1. D0nkey moves through evidence of three victories; "I'm on a heater" is swagger, not prophecy.
2. The undefeated status changes his relationship to the world: everyone now arrives to end it.
3. Live game determines whether pressure validates or punctures the mythology.
4. If D0nkey wins, supplied dialogue may close the chapter; if he loses, the silence after the heater is potentially stronger than a defeat pose.

### Live insertion points
Thursday scoring; early Sunday shock; comeback; lineup mistake; injury; Monday sweat; chat quote; wager; upset-level performance.

### World/continuity guardrail
No automatic battlefield. Venue/travel must resolve through World Atlas/home-away policy. ObiWan has no championship belt. D0nkey remains the Arsenal Gorilla Warrior.

### Abercrombie-method challenge
Do not write "confidence" or "pressure" if behavior can reveal it. Avoid aerial battle summary. Anchor decisive action in what one character notices, fears, dismisses or physically experiences.

### Counterweight
Strong GOTW candidate, not declared GOTW. Kill any treatment whose only idea is "Jedi fights gorilla."

## 3. Mud Dogs × Three Dreaded Snake

**Maturity:** DEVELOPING  
**Entering contrast:** seasonal ease versus restless reconstruction.  
**Primary POV opportunity:** Mud Dogs' indifference to league panic, counterweighted by TDS trying to manufacture change.

### Personalization inventory
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED_FACT: Mud Dogs is quick in group chat and regarded as capable of winning at any moment.
- STORY POSSIBILITY from commissioner context: last warm weather at swamp/boat marina; gearing up for deer season; bird season has opened.
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED_FACT: TDS is coming off a bad loss, training and working phones for trades.

### Narrative engine A — Season's Turn
1. Warm marina/swamp life; summer equipment and hunting equipment overlap.
2. TDS activity intrudes through calls/trade movement/preparation rather than a literal attack.
3. Live scoring changes the metaphor: hunter/prey, patience/panic, or no metaphor at all if the game produces something better.

### Narrative engine B — Three Heads, Three Solutions
Use TDS morphology narratively: one head tracks the matchup, one works trade phones, one obsesses over training/repair. The comedy is not three separate snakes; it is one body trying to solve too many problems at once.

### Win/loss possibilities
- Mud win: victory may feel almost incidental; he returns to the marina/hunting preparation.
- TDS win: frantic reconstruction finally creates leverage; the relaxed swamp becomes uncomfortable.
- Close game: patience versus over-management becomes the chapter.
- Blowout: shorten to two scenes rather than manufacturing drama.

### IMAGE TELLS
Seasonal transition, marina texture, body language, TDS's simultaneous attention.

### NARRATION TELLS
Why each character responds differently to uncertainty.

### Live insertion points
Trade, waiver, group-chat remark, lineup swing, hunting/weekend personalization, late comeback.

### Abercrombie-method challenge
Mud Dogs should not become a generic swamp monster posing in scenery. TDS should not become a generic three-headed villain. Character is revealed by what each does when fantasy becomes uncomfortable.

### Counterweight
Kill generic "snake hunts dog" unless live events make it unavoidable and specific.

## 4. Red Leopards × Dr. Duckhook

**Maturity:** DEVELOPING  
**Entering contrast:** recovery through leisure versus recovery from humiliation.  
**Primary POV opportunity:** Duckhook, because Week 3 consequence gives him the clearest emotional carryover; Red can function as host/counterpoint.

### Personalization inventory
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED FACT: Red Leopards has been recovering from the mountain climb at the beach, building sandcastles and golfing.
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED FACT: Red invites Duckhook down.
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED FACT: Duckhook needs an escape after being Chili'd.

### Narrative engine A — The Vacation They Forgot Was a Matchup
1. Duckhook arrives to escape; Red is already absurdly comfortable.
2. Beach/golf interaction establishes temporary friendship before competition.
3. Someone checks the fantasy score. The atmosphere changes without changing location.
4. Result determines whether vacation becomes rehabilitation, awkwardness or quiet consolation.

### Narrative engine B — Duckhook Rehabilitation
If Duckhook performs well, the story can be about recovering competence rather than humiliating someone else. A clean golf action can parallel a clean fantasy performance without requiring literal score symbolism.

### Win/loss possibilities
- Duckhook win: dignity returns; avoid over-celebration.
- Red win: avoid another humiliation spectacle. A quiet drink, golf gesture or host's consolation may carry more character.
- Chaotic close game: both try to enjoy the outing while increasingly checking phones.

### IMAGE TELLS
Beach/golf ease, Duckhook's lingering discomfort, score-check intrusion.

### NARRATION TELLS
Week 3 consequence and the awkward fact that an escape companion is also this week's opponent.

### Live insertion points
Golf/beach personalization, food joke, chat quote, waiver payoff, Sunday scoring swing.

### Abercrombie-method challenge
Consequences persist. Duckhook cannot emotionally reset because the calendar changed. Humor should arise from his attempts to act normal.

### Counterweight
Kill "Duckhook gets humiliated again" unless Week 4 itself earns it.

### NARRATION TELLS

**Maturity:** DEVELOPING  
**Entering contrast:** ordinary family/work pressure with latent elemental force versus trailer-park fatalism.  
**Primary POV opportunity:** Whipple watching an increasingly strange sky, or El Niño experiencing frustration becoming weather.

### Personalization inventory
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED FACT: El Niño is busy making sales calls and is a father of young boys ("mini El Niños").
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED CHARACTER NOTE: nonchalant entering the matchup.
- STORY POSSIBILITY: frustration may accumulate into an extreme weather system.
- COMMISSIONER_SUPPLIED FACT: Whipple drinks 40-oz beers in a lawn chair outside his trailer.
- STORY POSSIBILITY: Whipple attempts to shoot a storm out of the sky; sufficiently powerful flooding could threaten the trailer park.

### Narrative engine A — Pressure System
1. Mundane parallel life: calls/family versus lawn chair/40-oz.
2. Fantasy pressure changes small environmental details before any spectacle.
3. Live game determines whether weather builds, dissipates or never forms.
4. Only earned evidence permits a major storm consequence.

### Narrative engine B — The Storm That Never Came
If El Niño wins comfortably or remains nonchalant, everyone may prepare for catastrophe that never occurs. Anti-climax can be funnier and more character-specific than destruction.

### Narrative engine C — Whipple Versus Weather
If a genuine El Niño collapse/frustration arc emerges, Whipple's response is not fear but an absurdly practical decision to shoot at the sky. The humor is his behavior, not the storm itself.

### Win/loss possibilities
- 7DC win: storm escalation is possible but not mandatory.
- El Niño win: calm dominance may be stronger than hurricane imagery.
- Wild game: weather can track pressure only if it remains legible and does not overwrite factual scoring.

### IMAGE TELLS
Small atmospheric changes, trailer-park behavior, family/work contrast, physical weather.

### NARRATION TELLS
Why pressure is building and what each character thinks is normal.

### Live insertion points
Sales/family personalization, chat comment, major scoring reversal, weather-like comeback, Monday finish.

### Abercrombie-method challenge
Do not start with the hurricane. Start with the person. Epic scale matters only after the reader understands what changed.

### Counterweight
Flooding or destruction is a branch, never pre-result canon. Do not let Whipple's shotgun gag become the whole character.

## 8. Author Council synthesis

The Council is advisory, not governing. Apply by problem:

- **Tolkien lens:** recurring places and travel should feel inherited, not generated for a page.
- **Martin lens:** six matchups should create ensemble causality and consequences, not six isolated recaps.
- **Rowling lens:** recurring objects can carry accessible setup/payoff without lore exposition.
- **Grisham lens:** every scene must create propulsion; cut atmospheric scenes that do not change pressure.
- **Le Guin lens:** world detail should be filtered through character perception and culture.
- **Sanderson lens:** fantasy/world rules and consequences must remain intelligible and constrained.
- **Abercrombie lens:** epic events must be experienced through differentiated characters; aggressively cut generic spectacle and redundant dialogue.

### Abercrombie-method global notes
1. The best current reservoirs are those where the same event feels different depending on POV.
2. Humiliation must persist when it matters; Duckhook is the clearest Week 3 continuity test.
3. Confidence is behavior, not a label: DK, ObiWan, LLC and Byars need different forms of confidence.
4. Comedy is strongest when nobody exits character to deliver the joke.
5. A huge visual event is weaker than one specific reaction unless the huge event changes the world.
6. Revision should cut any scene whose only purpose is to show a character looking cool.
