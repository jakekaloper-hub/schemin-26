## Executive ruling

Week 4 should expand from the current 27-page spine to a **33-page final publication** by inserting one prose-forward **MATCHUP OVERTURE** before each of the six matchup chapters.

These are not generic transition pages.

Each overture has a specific job:
1. establish the upcoming matchup's immediate story question;
2. place the reader inside the correct Atlas geography/institution;
3. connect prior-week reputation or current-week pressure to the chapter;
4. give the Beat Writer meaningful reader-facing space before the more visual matchup pages;
5. preserve the setup → escalation → payoff rhythm of the existing matchup wave.

The overtures are deliberately prose-heavy and environment-led.

Default ratio:
**65–75% writing / 25–35% environmental art.**

Characters may appear only as restrained silhouettes, distant principals, or environmental presences when useful. The overture does not compete with the cinematic matchup art that follows.

## Week 5 production lesson

For future memos:

**PUBLICATION ORDER ≠ PRODUCTION ORDER.**

Recommended production order:
1. Fact / story / character / world preproduction.
2. Produce matchup waves.
3. Lock each matchup's final payoff/result page.
4. Build scoreboard from those final-page character assets.
5. Finalize global opener copy after the matchup prose is known.
6. Produce issue-level utility pages.
7. Assemble into publication order.

The scoreboard can still appear near the front of the PDF while being one of the last pages actually produced.

## Overture visual doctrine

All six overtures should share a recognizable editorial template without becoming identical:
- fixed Schemin Week 4 masthead;
- large matchup label;
- one overture headline;
- substantial readable prose;
- environment-dominant illustration;
- no scoreboard/result strip;
- no final winner pose;
- no duplicated action from the chapter itself.

Use the Atlas to show the *place the story is about to happen*.

The environment should operate like the opening paragraph of a chapter.

## QA rule

At thumbnail scale:
overture → setup → escalation → payoff

must read as four visibly different pages.

If the overture merely looks like another cinematic matchup page, it fails.
