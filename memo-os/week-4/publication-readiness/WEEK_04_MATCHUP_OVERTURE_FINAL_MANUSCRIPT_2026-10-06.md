# W4-OV01 — D0nkey K0ng vs ObiWan Jacoby
## THE ROAD BACK UP

For three Sundays, everybody in Burgers had been looking at the same silhouette on the ridge.

D0nkey K0ng had taken the highest ground in the division and made the view look permanent. Every road below him seemed to end in somebody else's loss.

ObiWan Jacoby knew that road already.

He had spent the opening month collecting proof that his roster could hurt people in more than one place. Puka. Walker. Henry. Deebo. Enough answers that the question was no longer whether he could climb.

It was what happened when he reached the top.

The highlands did not need another visitor.

They needed somebody willing to make the owner of the hill look down.

# W4-OV02 — Mud Dogs vs Three Dreaded Snake
## SOME PEOPLE CALL IT OVERPREPARED

Pitts had spent the week replacing pieces.

Receipts. Waiver claims. New names. New combinations. Enough roster movement to make Three Dreaded Snake look less like a lineup and more like a construction site that had finally passed inspection.

Mud Dogs arrived with a simpler theory.

Find the swamp. Trust the nose. Figure the rest out when something starts moving.

That was the difference at sunrise.

One side had three sets of eyes, a rebuilt roster and more equipment than anybody wanted to carry.

The other had confidence, plywood and absolutely no reason to believe the plywood was the problem.

By the time the wetlands woke up, both plans were technically still alive.

Only one of them looked like it had been tested beforehand.

# W4-OV03 — Red Leopards vs Dr. Duckhook
## THE COAST WAS SUPPOSED TO HELP

A week after the chili dog, Duckhook wanted distance.

Not a speech. Not revenge. Just somewhere with salt air, a drink and enough ocean between him and the rest of the Wings division to let the joke get old without him.

Red Leopards volunteered to come along.

That part seemed generous.

Red had spent the first month reminding the league that patience could look a lot like mercy right up until the moment it stopped being merciful. On the coast, though, there was no chase. No wager. No mountain.

Just two division rivals, a resort, and an afternoon that might have passed for recovery.

Then Red's golf bag came out of the car.

Duckhook had picked the right place to escape the league.

He had brought the wrong person.

# W4-OV04 — Slob on my Dobb vs The Chili Cheesers
## THE INVITATION

By the time Chili reached Slob's place, everybody in Wings knew what he had done to Duckhook.

The chili dog had become a story before the box score was cold. The Dark Horse had carried an outlaw through another week, and Brandon Pryor arrived at the cave with exactly the kind of reputation that makes a host pour the first drink a little heavier.

Slob did not act threatened.

He opened the door.

Inside were too many televisions, too much memorabilia, enough neon to turn midnight into a commercial break, and one homemade championship belt sitting where Chili could definitely see it.

Nobody explained the belt.

Nobody needed to.

The invitation had sounded social.

The room had other plans.

# W4-OV05 — The LLC vs His Majesty's Blood
## TWO TICKETS, ONE WINDOW

Pittsy's Book is neutral ground only if nobody asks who the house is rooting for.

The LLC arrived with a model, a cigar and the kind of confidence that makes a man buy his own number.

His Majesty's Blood arrived wearing the belt and bought himself too.

Two customers. Two self-tickets. One counter.

That was the clean version.

The less clean version was that both men had spent the season learning different lessons about control. The LLC trusted numbers because numbers usually behaved. The Belt Keeper had already learned that a win could arrive with a season-altering loss attached to it.

Neither came to the Waterfall Temple looking for philosophy.

They came for receipts.

By kickoff, Pittsy had both.

# W4-OV06 — El Niño vs Seven Deadly Chins
## THE BAROMETER MOVED FIRST

Before the storm reached Whipple's trailer park, it started in a house across Pizza.

Nothing dramatic.

A toy underfoot. Food on the floor. Somebody yelling from the wrong room. Another small disaster arriving before the last one had been cleaned up.

El Niño kept absorbing it.

Across the region, Seven Deadly Chins had a quieter evening. An RV. A lawn chair. A drink. The kind of stillness that makes weather feel like somebody else's problem.

For a while, it was.

Then the air changed.

Not enough to send anyone running. Just enough to move an awning, turn a few leaves over and make the clouds look like they had somewhere specific to be.

Whipple did not know what was coming.

El Niño did.

He was already living in it.
