## 1. Mission ruling

The transition system should be **subtractive, not additive**.

Week 4 does not need a full-page transition after every chapter. It needs a small number of transitions that materially move story, place, emotional tone, fantasy evidence, institutional state or Week 4 → Week 5 momentum.

The board adopts this working rule for Week 4 testing:

**PAGE_FROM_STATE → TRANSITION_CARRIER → PAGE_TO_STATE**

This is a Week 4 production experiment only. It is not yet promoted into Memo OS doctrine.

## 3. Domain-authority synthesis

### Beat Writer
Transitions should carry **meaning**, not just geography.

### Groundskeeper
Default form should be the lightest form that works: page band, inset, short prose bridge, environmental cut, or merged module before adding a full page.

### Scout
Live score shape and roster pressure are legitimate transition inputs, but final result language remains gated.

### Librarian
Week 1–3 locations / objects can return only when their meaning has changed. Repetition without changed meaning is decorative callback.

### Architect
Travel must remain route-valid. The Atlas supports transitions; it is not itself a transition.

### Analyst
Not every transaction deserves story space. Use only signals that materially explain pressure, preparedness, missed opportunity or roster stress.

### Umpire
Transitions must not smuggle in final winners, medical claims or permanent geography.

### Closer
The issue should read as one journey through three divisional story systems without becoming a map book.

## 4. Author Council advisory synthesis

### Tolkien
Favor inherited roads, remembered places and visible scars over explanatory lore.

### Martin
Use transitions to show local power changing across a division; one matchup can alter how the next is perceived without inventing causal fantasy effects.

### Rowling
Recurring objects / institutions work best when their meaning changes on return.

### Grisham
Kill any transition that does not increase propulsion or comprehension.

### Le Guin
Show geography through behavior and social practice, not through exposition boxes.

### Sanderson
Travel, weather and institutional transitions must obey the world's established rules.

### Abercrombie
Human reaction / aftermath often beats another spectacle page.

### Preserved disagreement
Tolkien / Martin favor slightly more visible regional continuity. Grisham / Abercrombie favor fewer dedicated transition pages. The compromise is **regional continuity through compact carriers**, with only one or two transition experiences potentially earning full-page status.

## 23. Consultant disagreements

### Tolkien vs Grisham
Tolkien would expose more roads / regional texture. Grisham would cut anything not moving the reader.  
**Ruling:** preserve geography through compact carriers, not dedicated lore pages.

### Martin vs Groundskeeper
Martin benefits from regional ensemble context; Groundskeeper warns against excessive division modules.  
**Ruling:** one opening / bridge treatment per division maximum, with State of Realm absorbing summaries.

### Rowling vs Abercrombie
Rowling supports recurring objects. Abercrombie warns objects can become gimmicks.  
**Ruling:** one primary carrier object per transition maximum and only when its meaning changes.

### Sanderson vs poetic transition
Sanderson requires world logic even when transition is metaphorical.  
**Ruling:** label thematic transition internally; do not imply false physical causality.
