## Controlling rule: show the Commonwealth, do not annotate it
World and Atlas presentations should feel like real cinematic landscapes and lived environments, not game maps or explanatory diagrams. Default prohibitions in narrative visuals: prominent place labels, legends, color-coded territorial overlays, map pins, symbolic keys, arbitrary colored boundaries, ornamental compass clutter, and excessive explanatory banners. These may remain in technical internal references and precise factual/statistical pages where needed, but not as the primary landscape visual language.

## Cinematography and world discovery
- Discover geography through plausible camera positions and natural progression: road, shoreline, valley, doorway, ridge, watercourse, settlement, interior.
- Use local material culture, scale, weather, season, architecture, habitation, travel and persistent scars to communicate place.
- Favor photographic realism and coherent depth/lighting; do not use generic fantasy backdrops, generic horror fonts, or artificially saturated territory colors.
- Halloween weekend: restrained atmospheric Easter eggs, fog, long shadows, autumn light, distant lanterns and occasional unsettling detail. Never let Halloween become the issue's dominant plot or obscure character visibility.
- Shared POV overtures: equal character treatment embedded within one recognizable geography; no compulsory literal 50/50 portrait split if it harms environmental discovery, but preserve equivalent narrative attention. Existing two-half-page concept remains a compositional starting point.
- Frank Masi camera-work perspective is an editorial methodology; do not represent him as personally consulted or approving without actual evidence.

## Required acceptance questions for every narrative visual
1. Can the location be recognized without text labels or a legend?
2. Could a camera plausibly occupy the depicted position?
3. Are roads, water, elevations, buildings and character movement spatially consistent?
4. Does the environment reveal local culture or history beyond the two principal characters?
5. Does the scene communicate its specific fantasy-football consequence without invented facts?
6. Are dark lighting and fog atmospheric while maintaining mobile-scale readability?
7. Does the image belong to the same Commonwealth as the rest of the issue?
8. Does the page improve the whole publication rather than just look impressive alone?
