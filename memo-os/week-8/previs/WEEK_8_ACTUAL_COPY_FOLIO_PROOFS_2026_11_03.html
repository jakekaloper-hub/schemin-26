Across the channel, Dr. Duckhook's white feathers and unlikely golf attire marked a figure who seemed spectacularly out of place. The water between them was quiet, but not empty.

The first disturbance could have been mistaken for a branch falling into the water. Then another mark appeared along the wet edge of the channel, and the hunt became specific. Mud Dogs followed what the ground would tell him. Duckhook advanced with the awkward determination of someone whose equipment had been designed for an entirely different kind of course.

The crossing lay beneath a low ceiling of autumn mist. One road came down from the dry country; another followed the water, carrying the marks of wheels and years. The three heads of the Serpent watched the approaches from one broad-shouldered body. Beyond the worn track, Chili came with Dark Horse. For a moment neither moved. They had never walked this road together. Still, each knew the weight of the other waiting at its end.

Before there were kingdoms to defend and reputations to carry, there had been a corner bedroom in the Kappa Sigma house at Ole Miss. Two roommates, ordinary evenings, a world that had not yet learned to measure their friendship in points. Memory returned in fragments: lamplight, a doorway, the unexamined certainty that the other would be there. It offered no instruction for what to say now.

The edge of the remembered doorway gave way to an upright post at the Shared March. The resemblance lasted only an instant. Outside the memory, the air was colder, the crossing wider, and the years impossible to ignore. Nothing in the settlement belonged to Oxford. Yet both arrivals carried a piece of that room into the same place.
