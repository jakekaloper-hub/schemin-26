## W08-06-01 — The Place We Once Knew: Two arrivals — shared POV

Two roads reached the crossing from opposite sides. The first carried the three-headed Serpent, one powerful reptilian body moving beneath a canopy of late-autumn mist. The second carried the Chili Outlaw and the separate black form of Dark Horse. Neither arrival was announced. At the edge of the settlement, a wagon stood quiet beside a worn track and the water kept moving as though the meeting were of no particular interest. The three serpent heads turned at slightly different moments. Chili paused with a hand near the horse's reins. They knew each other from a place that had no business existing in this landscape. The old familiarity was immediate. So was the awkwardness of having to compete.

## W08-06-02 — The Place We Once Knew: Oxford corner bedroom — memory only

Oxford came back to them not as a perfect scene but as a scale of life that now seemed almost impossibly small. A corner bedroom in the Kappa Sigma house at Ole Miss. Two roommates who could cross a room without having to explain what brought them there. No kingdoms, no rival territories, no week measured to hundredths of a point. Memory did not supply a speech or a miraculous detail. It supplied something worse: the ease of being known before either man had made himself into a figure the Commonwealth could recognize from a distance. That was what made the next meeting difficult. There was no stranger to blame for it.

*Editorial: The intimacy of former roommates becomes the emotional counterweight. Camera: Still interior perspective with window light and two implied personal presences; no fantasy anatomy inserted into literal college memory.*

## W08-06-03 — The Place We Once Knew: Architectural match cut — Oxford to Shared March

The remembered corner gave way to a post beside the crossing. For an instant the shape of the doorway and the shape of the road seemed to occupy the same place in the eye; then the illusion vanished. This was the Shared March, not Oxford. The cold water, wheel-worn approaches, and stranger traffic belonged to a different world. Chili stepped down beside Dark Horse. Across the track, the Serpent's three heads studied the narrow approach from three angles. Their history had shortened the distance between them without making the present encounter easier. Knowing exactly how someone used to stand did not tell you what he would refuse to surrender now.

*Editorial: Contrast time's distance with the unchanged act of meeting. Camera: Match-cut on a vertical doorway/post and consistent eye line; Oxford is not an Atlas location.*

For a moment they simply occupied the same view, close enough to register what time had made of each of them. The point of recognition was not tenderness. It was information. ## W08-06-05 — The Place We Once Knew: The first strike

The reptilian warrior did not take the invitation. One head watched the outlaw's approach, another tracked the stable path, and the third kept its attention on the distance Chili was trying to close. Dark Horse shifted at the edge of the encounter. This was not a fight invented to destroy an old friendship. It was the kind of contest made sharper by familiarity: each man could recognize the other's bluff and still be wrong about what came next.

*Editorial: Familiarity no longer grants advantage. Camera: Two-shot across a navigable width, alternating grounded eyelines.*

## W08-06-06 — The Place We Once Knew: Familiarity fails

The outlaw stepped back toward the stable path, unwilling to grant anything resembling surrender; the Serpent pressed toward the center without turning the result into a personal humiliation. There was no final remark that could carry twelve years or more of remembered familiarity. The place was full of practical noises—water, hoof movement, a wagon board settling—and neither competitor could pretend the contest was only a metaphor. One of them was going to leave at 4–4. So was the other. Only one would leave with the win.

*Editorial: A friend becomes an opponent without becoming an enemy. Camera: Converging perspectives with unchanged three-head anatomy and horse scale.*

## W08-02-01 — Hostile Ground: Predator / intruder overture

The Red Leopard did not patrol the ruins. He possessed them. From the highest surviving terrace, his amber eyes tracked rain over the storm coast and into every passage he believed an intruder could use. Far below, The LLC stepped across a flooded seam in the masonry without lifting the hem of his dark coat. He carried his briefcase as if the world were obliged to provide a dry boardroom at the end of every appointment. The predator saw a man walking into a trap. The visitor looked up at the terrace, took the measure of the distance, and behaved like someone checking whether the building still belonged to its current owner.

*Editorial: Both masterminds believe they control the encounter. Camera: Coastal master, then two matched height reveals.*

## W08-02-02 — Hostile Ground: Coastal infiltration

The LLC let the lower passage narrow around him. Water struck the walls; above, claws scraped stone as the Leopard changed position to keep him in sight. The businessman paused at a junction, opened his briefcase only enough to consult what was inside, and calmly chose the route that appeared least promising. That was the insult: the intruder was making decisions in a place where every decision should have been dictated by the hunter. From the upper ledge, the Leopard shifted to intercept. For the first time, he could not watch the approach and guard the exit from the same vantage. He was still faster. The question was whether he could be everywhere at once.

*Editorial: Confidence and suspicion rise together. Camera: Low tracking against worn stone, upper ledge in same axis.*

## W08-02-03 — Hostile Ground: Reversal in the ruins

The Leopard reached the expected opening and found empty wet stone. Below him, a sound carried through a broken arch where the surf disguised footsteps. The LLC had not discovered a magical escape route. He had merely noticed that the ruin's old lower passage turned behind the wall the predator used as his lookout. The hunter doubled back, claws throwing water from the ledge. The suited visitor emerged into an exposed patch of light with nowhere comfortable left to go. Both stopped. The man had bought only seconds, and the Leopard had the height. It was suddenly possible that both of their plans were correct—and that one of them would still lose.

*Editorial: The Leopard discovers an assumption was wrong. Camera: Cross-cut upper/lower eyelines with consistent height.*

## W08-02-04 — Hostile Ground: Exposed-height confrontation

He had not conquered the ruins or taken the predator's territory. ## W08-01-01 — The Storm That Wouldn't Leave: Order / storm overture

ObiWan Jacoby had prepared for weather. The Trade Jedi had secured the loose gear, checked the exposed approach, and made sure the maps stayed where maps were supposed to stay: dry, flat, and useful. The mountain base looked disciplined from the upper terrace. Below, a blue-white shape moved through the gathering cloud. El Niño rose with the rain as if the ridge were a place he had been invited to inspect. ObiWan watched the weather elemental climb. There was nothing particularly alarming about the first gust. That was the problem with the first gust. It gave a reasonable man every opportunity to make a very reasonable mistake.

*Editorial: Order meets an unwelcome weather system. Camera: Valley-to-ridge tracking, maintain human and elemental silhouettes.*

## W08-01-02 — The Storm That Wouldn't Leave: First failed defense

The first measure worked beautifully for almost a minute. ObiWan drew an awning over the exposed worktable. The awning kept the rain off the maps, tipped the runoff onto the supply path, and sent a sheet of cold water straight across his boots. He moved the table. The wind moved the awning. He secured the awning; a second gust blew the door closed against the rope he needed on the other side. Far below, El Niño continued his climb without appearing to exert himself. ObiWan stared at the soaked table, then at the mountain, as though one of them had failed to follow a clear set of instructions.

*Editorial: Irritation replaces confidence. Camera: Medium blocking, mountain drainage follows gravity.*

## W08-01-03 — The Storm That Wouldn't Leave: The mountain siege

By dusk the ridge had become a chain of sensible decisions with ridiculous consequences. Water streamed downhill as water always had; the paths were not magically flooding against gravity. But the wind blew through the very gaps ObiWan had left for ventilation, and the sheltered stair became a funnel for spray. El Niño moved across the opposite ledge with lightning running faintly through his storm-water form. His presence turned every small weakness into a fresh argument. ObiWan reached for his energy blade, looked at the rain falling through it, and reconsidered what exactly a sword was supposed to accomplish against a visitor made largely of precipitation.

*Editorial: The nuisance becomes a siege. Camera: Wide storm master with readable blue eyes/staff and no false flooding.*

## W08-01-04 — The Storm That Wouldn't Leave: Escalating setbacks

He had stopped solving problems and started negotiating with them. Keep the maps dry or keep the path clear. Secure the door or reach the terrace. Guard the ridge or spend another minute retrieving something the wind had claimed. The Trade Jedi chose each compromise with growing irritation. El Niño offered none. The weather elemental simply occupied the mountain's air until it felt as though the whole stronghold had been reassigned to him. For a fleeting moment the absurdity nearly won a smile from ObiWan. Then he looked down the exposed route and remembered that the season did not grade effort. It graded outcomes.

*Editorial: Comedy hardens into fear of a 4–4 season. Camera: Close-up and wider countershot; no copy of Seinfeld dialogue.*

## W08-01-05 — The Storm That Wouldn't Leave: The final passage

By the time the clouds began to move on, the result had settled: **El Niño 164.38, ObiWan Jacoby 142.30**. The storm had won by **22.08**, leaving ObiWan at **4–4**. His base still stood. No imaginary tower had collapsed, and no championship Belt hung from his shoulders to make the loss more theatrical. A damp map lifted at one corner of the worktable. He pressed it flat with his palm and watched the retreating weather across the valley. He had defended the mountain from the storm's damage. He had not defended his record from the storm itself. That distinction was going to take some time to dry out.

## W08-10-01 — The Last Light: Hunter / hunted overture

The first thing the swamp surrendered was the sky. A thin copper seam held in the water between cypress roots; above it, the last daylight was already being swallowed by the trees. Mud Dogs watched from a bank of black earth, a chain hanging from his massive, mud-caked body. Across the channel, a white duck in golf gear picked his way along a strip of firmer ground, studying the terrain with an expression that belonged on a manicured course. He had found neither a fairway nor a welcome committee. The beast had found something that looked like prey. Neither had yet noticed how badly the fading light favored the visitor who kept looking for a line instead of an escape.

*Editorial: Predatory certainty and misplaced confidence. Camera: Waterline tracking, bank-height reveal, equal POV attention.*

## W08-10-02 — The Last Light: The disturbed trail

The ground offered several tracks, but none arranged themselves into the straight pursuit he wanted. On the opposite side, Duckhook raised his golf-club arm, measured the distance between exposed roots, and sent a small piece of loose debris skipping through the shallows. One disturbance became three. To Mud Dogs it sounded like an animal moving carelessly through his territory. To Duckhook it was a line he had chosen. The swamp had become a course, and the course was being played by the least likely creature in it.

## W08-10-03 — The Last Light: The narrowing channel

The beast knew every smell of this place, but scent was useless when the only route wide enough for his weight was behind him. Duckhook did not hurry. He tested the ground with his club, chose a dry step, and moved again. A golf course would have called the water a hazard. Here, the hazard was coming after him, and the duck had the extraordinary composure to keep playing.

At the final opening between the trees, the last copper reflection broke apart. Duckhook emerged on firm ground with the enormous swamp still behind him. ## W08-05-01 — The Fallen Giant: Challenger / giant overture

The highland road curved upward until its stones seemed to disappear beneath the shadow of the stronghold. Across the ridge, the Arsenal Centaur stood with four equine legs planted wide, his black-furred gorilla torso wrapped in Arsenal-red armor. He needed no herald to announce him. A raccoon kept to his side, sniffed the road, and promptly lost interest in the giant. Chins noticed. He adjusted the hammer at his shoulder and kept walking. If there was supposed to be a ceremony before challenging a legend, no one had bothered to tell him.

For a moment the field seemed arranged to demonstrate the difference between a legendary warlord and a man who looked as if he had come from the back of a truck-stop cookout. ## W08-03-01 — The Weight of a Crown: Champion / contender overture

HMB's pale face caught the last autumn light; the black runes of his armor and the championship Belt made an argument for respect before he had spoken. Slob waited near the center, shaggy and horned, bat in hand, his pit bull alert beside him. HMB halted and allowed a moment for the welcome. Slob looked at the Belt, looked at the visitor, and shifted just enough to make clear that he was not going to fetch a chair.

*Editorial: Inherited prestige meets indifference. Camera: Human-height camp approach, HMB Belt and Slob horns visible.*

## W08-03-02 — The Weight of a Crown: The challenge

HMB moved forward with the deliberate care of someone who expected the next gesture to become part of a legend. Slob responded by dragging the end of his bat through the dirt, indicating the ground between them. It was neither a throne room nor a dueling court. It was a camp yard with uneven footing, scattered wood, and a dog that had better judgment than most witnesses. The Belt Keeper glanced down at his boots. Slob watched him notice the mud. There, before a sword was lifted, the first advantage changed hands. The immortal had arrived expecting a challenge worthy of his title. Slob had made him fight where the title meant very little.

*Editorial: Respect becomes contested rather than granted. Camera: Grounded two-shot, companions and props only where plausible.*

## W08-03-03 — The Weight of a Crown: Ceremonial confidence breaks

The first exchanges were ugly in precisely the way Slob preferred. A polished turn became a scramble for balance. HMB's rune-dark blade met the metal bat with a sound that sent the pit bull backing away. Neither man could make the other retreat far enough to claim control. The Belt Keeper adjusted, stopped posing, and began taking each step with the care of a fighter instead of a monument. Across from him, Slob's grin widened. This was the point of the invitation. The champion could keep his history. The camp wanted to know whether he could survive a present-tense problem that refused to show proper reverence.

*Editorial: Confidence becomes vulnerability. Camera: Tight physical beats with readable body/prop continuity.*

## W08-03-04 — The Weight of a Crown: The reckoning

HMB survived. **His Majesty's Blood 162.62, Slob on my Dobb 158.26**—only **4.36 points** between the Belt Keeper and a loss that would have traveled quickly through the Commonwealth. He stood at the edge of the camp, title secure, armor marked by the effort of keeping it that way. Slob remained in the yard, breathing hard and looking notably unconvinced that he had met an untouchable immortal. The pit bull settled beside him. HMB turned for the road with the championship Belt still his. He had arrived expecting admiration. He departed with something less comfortable and arguably more useful: proof that the Belt could not do the fighting for him.

*Editorial: Survival rather than effortless supremacy. Camera: Aftermath medium-wide, belt with HMB only, camp still inhabited.*
