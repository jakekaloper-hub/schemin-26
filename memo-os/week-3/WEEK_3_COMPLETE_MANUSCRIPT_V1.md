## P2 — WHEN STORIES BECOME REPUTATIONS
Three weeks is not a season. It is, however, long enough for accidents to begin looking like habits.

By Sunday morning, the world of Schemin' had already begun separating itself into those who were merely carrying records and those who were beginning to carry reputations. Two unbeaten armies walked toward the same ruined field. A Jedi and a Snake found themselves staring over cold water. One hundred dollars waited at the top of a mountain. An immortal champion, already wounded by the loss of one of his keepers, drove toward the possibility of 0-3. A corporate fortress watched weather gather on its horizon. And at a country club in Mississippi, a chili dog that had arrived as a joke waited patiently to become prophecy.

Week Three did not settle the league. It did something more useful: it gave the league evidence.

By the time Monday night went quiet, there was one unbeaten team left. There were contenders with bruises, losing teams with dangerous point totals, a former champion finally breathing again, and managers already trading pieces before the smoke had cleared.

The season is still young. The stories no longer are.

## P3 — DK/MUD — WHEN THE UNBEATEN MET
They entered from opposite ends of the world with the same record and entirely different ideas of how a kingdom should be crossed.

D0nkey K0ng came armed like a campaign: steel, discipline, speed and the confidence of an Arsenal Centaur that had not yet been forced backward. Mud Dogs came out of the mire carrying no interest in elegance. The swamp-born menace had survived two weeks by making every road ugly enough to become familiar.

At 2-0 apiece, there was nowhere for either to hide. Week Three's Game of the Week was not a coronation. It was a narrowing. Two unbeaten paths had reached the same valley. Only one would continue.

Across the broken ground, the standards rose. Then both sides moved.

## P4 — DK/MUD — THE LAST STANDARD STANDING
The first collision did not decide anything.

Mud Dogs dragged the fight into churned earth, where footing disappeared and clean plans became useless. D0nkey K0ng answered by refusing to fight clean. The Centaur drove forward through the mire, axe and armor flashing through a battlefield that became less recognizable with every exchange.

The score widened. Mud Dogs kept coming.

That mattered.

A rout would have made the afternoon forgettable. Resistance made it a battle. Every yard of separation had to be taken from something that looked built to survive being buried.

Eventually the field ran out of surprises. The Arsenal Centaur did not.

## P5 — DK/MUD — THE YIELD
When it ended, there was no crown waiting on the hill.

There was only a battlefield, one exhausted victor and an opponent that had finally reached the limit of what it could absorb.

Mud Dogs yielded. D0nkey K0ng lowered the weapon.

The final margin — 172.48 to 126.86 — made the result look cleaner than the world around them. DK had entered beside another unbeaten team and left at 3-0, alone in that distinction. Mud Dogs fell to 2-1, wounded but hardly erased.

Respect is easier to believe after somebody has made you earn it.

The hill belonged to D0nkey K0ng.

The kingdom did not.

## P6 — OBIWAN/TDS — THE BRIDGE
Some decisions become difficult only after you have looked down.

Above the lake, the bridge seemed narrower than it had from the boat. ObiWan Jacoby had already committed. The Trade Jedi went over the edge and disappeared toward the cold water below.

Three Dreaded Snake reached the same point and stopped.

This was not a question of talent. TDS had spent the week reshaping a roster and had enough fight to remain dangerous. But the bridge did not care about waiver bids, projections or intentions. It asked a simpler question.

Are you going?

For a long moment, the Snake stared at the answer.

## P7 — OBIWAN/TDS — THE SWIM OF SHAME
He was not.

There are dignified ways to lose. Swimming back to the boat beneath the bridge you refused to jump from is not among them.

ObiWan's 136.73 carried him to 2-1. TDS finished at 114.50 and 1-2, then discovered that the long route home also happens to be the wettest.

From the boat, the Jedi laughed.

Behind the Snake, the bridge remained exactly where it had been all afternoon — enormous, silent and suddenly much funnier.

There are shorter ways home.

Most require jumping first.

## P10 — RED LEOPARDS/SLOB — EASY MONEY
The wager was simple enough to understand.

Red Leopards put one hundred dollars on himself.

Slob on my Dobb accepted.

At the foot of the mountain, the arrangement looked particularly attractive to Slob. Strength was visible. Strength had weight. Strength could carry a bat, a beer and enough confidence to make a long climb seem like somebody else's problem.

Red Leopards offered no argument.

He simply started climbing.

For a while, Slob stayed close. Then the mountain began collecting interest.

## P11 — RED LEOPARDS/SLOB — THE LONG CLIMB
Altitude is an unforgiving accountant.

By the upper trail, Slob's early swagger had been reduced to survival. The legs that had powered through the opening ascent grew heavy. The mountain kept rising anyway.

Red Leopards kept moving.

At the summit, the Predator Board reached the top with 158.44 points behind him and a 2-1 record ahead. Slob finished at 141.86, fell to 1-2 and finally produced the only document the mountain required: a white flag.

The hundred-dollar wager had begun as easy money.

By the end, it looked more like a receipt.

## P12 — HMB/7DC — SOUTHERN SPEEDWAY
His Majesty's Blood did not arrive at the speedway looking for style points.

He arrived 0-2.

One of his two keepers was gone for the season. The Belt Keeper, accustomed to carrying the visual language of a champion, suddenly had a much smaller ambition: reach the finish line before the season turned three losses old.

Seven Deadly Chins had no intention of making that graceful.

The People's Champ rolled onto the track in a machine that looked assembled from horsepower, bad judgment and whatever remained behind the garage. HMB answered in black steel and runes.

The lights went out.

Subtlety went with them.

## P13 — HMB/7DC — SHAKE, RATTLE, SURVIVE
The final laps became less race than argument.

Seven Deadly Chins refused to disappear. HMB could not create clean air. They reached the late turn side by side, two incompatible machines occupying one piece of asphalt.

Then came contact.

Seven Deadly Chins spun into a storm of tire smoke. HMB found daylight.

The checkered line arrived before the chaos could reorganize itself.

136.00 to 125.54.

For the first time this season, His Majesty's Blood had a win.

It was not domination. It was not redemption. It did not restore what had been lost.

It was a breath.

At 1-2, that was enough.

## P14 — LLC/EL NIÑO — THE WEATHER EVENT
The forecast was hostile.

El Niño arrived over the corporate district without any interest in quarterly guidance. Water climbed the streets. Lightning divided the skyline. Wind turned carefully prepared infrastructure into suggestions.

Inside The LLC, the response was immediate.

Barriers rose.

Pumps activated.

Emergency systems came online.

Somewhere, a binder titled BUSINESS CONTINUITY was opened with tremendous seriousness.

Outside, the Weather System kept coming.

## P15 — LLC/EL NIÑO — WEATHER PROTECTION PROTOCOL
By the end of the storm, nearly everything around headquarters was wet.

Headquarters was not.

The LLC stood above the flood line in a dark suit and sunglasses, surveying the water from a dry executive terrace while El Niño raged beyond the perimeter.

The scoreboard was just as clinical: LLC 147.43, El Niño 114.62.

Both left Week Three at 1-2, but only one got to invoice the disaster.

The LLC had not defeated the weather.

It had found a way to expense it.

## P16 — THE THREE KINGDOMS
BURGERS. WINGS. PIZZA.

For three weeks the league had been experienced as six separate battles at a time. Here, the camera finally pulls high enough to see the borders.

Burgers carries the league's lone unbeaten standard in D0nkey K0ng, with ObiWan Jacoby and Mud Dogs both one game behind and TDS searching for footing.

Wings contains no unbeaten team and no easy territory: Red Leopards and Chili sit at 2-1 while Slob and Duckhook remain dangerous at 1-2.

Pizza is compressed into a four-way 1-2 knot. HMB finally escaped the winless column; LLC did the same; El Niño and Seven Deadly Chins now share the same record.

The map has borders.

The season has not respected them.
