## FOLIO 02 — OPENING DRIVE
**Source section:** OPENING DRIVE · **Word count:** 170 · **Gate:** EDITORIAL_COPYFIT_REVIEW

In most weeks, winning is easy to recognize. Someone towers over a scoreboard, someone falls out of the frame, and the Commonwealth collects the debris by sunrise. Week Six did not offer that kindness.

At the swamp, His Majesty's Blood survived Mud Dogs by twelve hundredths of a point, the difference between a deed waved in triumph and a deed dragged back into the muck. At the falls, Three Dreaded Snake edged The LLC by thirty-seven hundredths, a margin too narrow to bridge the distance between a promise and an actual bargain. Elsewhere, Slob on my Dobb did not bother with subtlety. The week's largest score—143.70—sent ObiWan Jacoby home from the dodgeball court with questions for the officials, and possibly for the scoreboard operator.

D0nkey K0ng invaded the Theatre of Ruins and stole the football afternoon. Duckhook held the clubhouse against El Niño. Seven Deadly Chins rode out with the final word in the Chili Stable. Six contests; six winners; not a single permission slip for the league to relax.

## FOLIO 04 — THEATRE 1
**Source section:** THEATRE OF RUINS · **Word count:** 180 · **Gate:** EDITORIAL_COPYFIT_REVIEW

Rain threaded the terraces of the Red Leopard Ruins before the visiting supporters could be heard. When they came, it was with a chorus of Arsenal-colored banners, rolling up the escarpment like a second front. The Red Leopards had built an afternoon around rivalry: Manchester United colors, a football field drawn into the ruin's available floor, and a tunnel entrance grand enough to make a lion of any ordinary arrival.

The visitor was not ordinary. D0nkey K0ng stepped into view as one vast integrated figure: gorilla above, four equine legs below, a single body carrying its own momentum. A scarf swung from one shoulder. Every attempt at a gracious arrival became a minor argument with a stairway.

The Leopard's tail lashed beneath his kit. “You brought a cavalry unit to a football match?”

DK looked down at the field. “You built me four lanes.”

The crowd divided the rain with noise. For a moment the ancient ruins seemed to remember they were supposed to be a fortress. Then the whistle blew, and the fortress discovered it was hosting a derby.

The Leopard played as if the home terraces could close the distance for him. DK answered with the stubborn physics of four legs: no effortless human footwork, no mistaken two-legged mascot pose, just a tremendous creature learning to turn force into angles.

Behind the terraces, supporters argued over which famous football memory the ruin was paying tribute to. On the field, the rivalry had become its own memory. The home crowd roared for an equalizer; across the terraces, visitors beat their banners against ancient stone until the ruin itself seemed to echo their answer.

The Leopard made a final run. DK met him beneath the shadow of a ruined arch, and the crowd's collective breath seemed to hang there, suspended among the hanging banners.

## FOLIO 07 — JACKSON I / A QUESTION OF ETIQUETTE
**Source section:** JACKSON INVITATIONAL Part I · **Gate:** EDITORIAL_COPYFIT_REVIEW · **PAGE MASTER:** 1024 × 1536 PNG, portrait 2:3

The Country Club of Jackson believed in a hierarchy of disasters. A guest arriving late was vulgar. A cart leaving tracks across the wrong approach was unforgivable. A storm following El Niño through the front gates was neither of those things; it was a scheduling matter to be discussed after lunch.

Dr. Duckhook's guests arrived in polished layers, ready to treat the tournament as a meeting of civilized minds. El Niño surveyed the green like a man who had brought his own weather but forgotten where to check it. The caddies watched the edge of the sky. The staff quietly identified the driest route back to the veranda.

“I ordered a fairway,” Duckhook said.

“You got one,” El Niño answered. “You didn't specify how long it would stay fair.”

The event began beneath the clubhouse's unwavering rules of etiquette. Beyond the windows, a caddy steadied the club towel as the wind lifted it sideways. Duckhook watched the green; El Niño watched the clouds.

The first golf cart lurched away from the clubhouse approach, chased by a caddy carrying a club towel like a surrender flag. The spectators disagreed without daring to say so aloud.

Duckhook reached the terrace as the rain arrived in earnest. The white tablecloths fluttered; the bunker acquired its own little weather system. El Niño, unhurried, surveyed the soaked fairway.

The club survived the spectacle in the only way it knew how: by refusing to admit anything unusual had happened.

Dr. Duckhook took the fantasy contest **132.40–119.86**, then transformed his victory into a dignified toast. El Niño raised an empty glass toward the ceiling, listening to the rain above it. The club's staff began restoring the course before anyone could suggest that the tournament's disorder had become a new permanent feature.

## FOLIO 11 — CHILI
**Source section:** CHILI DOG RODEO · **Word count:** 200 · **Gate:** EDITORIAL_COPYFIT_REVIEW

The Chili Stable had prepared for a rodeo. It had not prepared for a chili dog to become the most pursued cargo in the western range.

The first wagon arrived with a banner, a flourish, and an obvious lack of control. A raccoon slipped between the crates and emerged carrying something the cooks insisted had been reserved for a paying guest. Seven Deadly Chins saw the escape from across the arena and turned a contest into a pursuit. The Chili Cheesers attempted to restore order without admitting that order had left several minutes earlier.

A dark horse passed across a fenced lane at the worst possible time, throwing dust over the evidence. The raccoon doubled back. A spectator shouted the wrong instructions. Half the stable believed the lunch was heading east; the other half had already begun composing songs about its disappearance.

The final ledger favored Seven Deadly Chins, 131.78 to 125.24. The victor accepted the day's applause as if he had organized the entire disaster. The Chili Cheesers stared at the emptied wagon.

“Was that even our order?” someone asked.

No one could identify the sender with certainty, which preserved the mystery and made the story much funnier.

## FOLIO 12 — BLACKWATER 1
**Source section:** BLACKWATER DEED JOB · **Word count:** 150 · **Gate:** EDITORIAL_COPYFIT_REVIEW

The Mud Dogs had arranged a meeting where the river made promises hard to enforce: a low barge, a chest of damp papers, and marsh banks that looked identical from every angle. His Majesty's Blood arrived hooded and armored, the red eyes of his approved form holding a steady place within the dark. The Belt remained his, independent of the week's fantasy result.

Mud spread a wrinkled diagram over a crate. It claimed to show a piece of the swamp worth more than any reasonable creature should wish to own.

“You've asked me here to sign a map,” Blood said.

“A map,” Mud replied, “is a deed that hasn't learned confidence.”

The channel offered no opinion. It did, however, move the barge an inconvenient distance while both parties leaned over the ink. A latch snapped shut on the chest. The papers were suddenly more important than the people holding them.

## FOLIO 13 — BLACKWATER 2
**Source section:** BLACKWATER DEED JOB · **Word count:** 150 · **Gate:** EDITORIAL_COPYFIT_REVIEW

The account would later be simplified into a contest for ownership. That was never settled. The barge was a temporary office, the chest held documents of unverified force, and neither participant had produced a lawful land transfer recognized by the Commonwealth.

What was settled was a football result: His Majesty's Blood 136.92, Mud Dogs 136.80. Twelve hundredths. A margin so narrow Mud tried to make the number stand still by writing it twice.

Blood did not celebrate extravagantly. He simply closed the lid of the chest, watched the barge rotate in the slow current, and took his leave with the Belt exactly where it belonged.

Mud stared at the damp page in his hand. “You didn't even buy anything.”

“No,” Blood said. “But I won.”

The river carried the words downstream. The paperwork remained what it had been at the start: suspicious, unsigned in any authoritative sense, and dramatically inconvenient.

## FOLIO 14 — FALLS 1
**Source section:** FALLS PROTOCOL · **Word count:** 153 · **Gate:** EDITORIAL_COPYFIT_REVIEW

Mist covered the Waterfall Temple stairs so completely that The LLC could hear Three Dreaded Snake before seeing him. Three heads appeared above one coiled body, each considering a different aspect of the negotiation. One asked about cost. One asked about liability. The third wanted to know why the visitor had brought so many papers to a place made mostly of water.

The LLC unfolded a plan across a stone ledge. It contained a crossing, possible future access, and a quantity of fine print that the waterfall attempted to erase.

“This is a framework,” LLC explained.

“Then where is the frame?” asked the first head.

“Where is the bridge?” asked the second.

“Where is the exit?” asked the third.

Their arguments were interrupted by a gust that lifted a corner of the plan over the spray. Neither a bridge nor a vault was built. The conversation was a negotiation, not a binding contract.

For all the paper placed between them, the two parties were finally divided by something very small: Three Dreaded Snake scored 138.06 to The LLC's 137.69, a victory of thirty-seven hundredths.

**Source section:** FINAL WORD · **Word count:** 151 · **Gate:** EDITORIAL_COPYFIT_REVIEW

There are two ways to judge a week in the Commonwealth. One is to ask who won. The other is to look at what the winners left behind.

At the ruins, a visiting song that would not stop. At the country club, a toast delivered with the windows shut against the rain. At Foothill Camp, chalk lines nobody trusted. At the Chili Stable, a missing meal that might yet become an institution. In the swamp, a deed without title. At the waterfall, a bridge without a bridge.

The scores are final for now; the arguments are not. Next week, Red Leopards welcomes His Majesty's Blood in the officially chosen Game of the Week. The house is already preparing for company. Whether the guest enjoys the welcome remains a story for another edition.
