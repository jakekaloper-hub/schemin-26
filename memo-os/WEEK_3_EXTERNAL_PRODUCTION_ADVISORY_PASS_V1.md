# WEEK 3 — EXTERNAL PRODUCTION ADVISORY PASS V1

**Purpose:** strengthen preproduction using documented methods from accomplished real-world filmmakers, sports-documentary producers, cinematographers and world-building studios. This is research synthesis, not a claim that these people personally reviewed or endorsed Schemin '26.

## Advisory Room

### Sports Documentary / Executive Producer Desk — ESPN Films / 30 for 30
Documented principle: story first; director-driven point of view; specific sports moments and characters should reveal larger themes. Strong long-form work earns additional canvas when context and character depth require it.

**Week 3 implementation**
- Do not treat six matchups as six equal recap cards.
- Find the human/league question inside each matchup before writing.
- Require every chapter to answer: why does this result matter beyond the score?
- Permit the strongest Week 3 story to occupy more pages.
- Use the opening to establish context that cannot be compressed into scoreboard copy.
- The final memo should have a point of view without falsifying evidence.

### Documentary Visualization Desk — 30 for 30 production practice
Documented principle: when archive material cannot adequately visualize a story, another visual grammar such as animation can carry memory/anecdote while the factual spine remains intact.

**Week 3 implementation**
- Jake's lake “swim of shame,” Sonic chili-dog incident and other league lore may become illustrated memory sequences.
- Visually distinguish memory/lore from verified live game chronology.
- Never fabricate photographic-looking “evidence” for an anecdote.
- Illustration may express how an event is remembered; deterministic text identifies what is verified.

### Director / Continuity Desk — Peter Jackson production practice
Documented principle: visual continuity across multiple units required meetings, shared review of daily work and a central cinematographic hand maintaining the pulse. Jackson also described the camera as another character in the scene.

**Week 3 implementation**
- Treat the six matchup art teams as parallel units under one Week 3 visual director.
- Establish shared Week 3 lighting/weather/materiality before image production.
- Run a “dailies” review after every first-pass matchup image.
- Compare adjacent pages, not images in isolation.
- Assign an implied camera motivation to each illustration: witness, pursue, reveal, isolate, overwhelm or depart.
- The Closer owns cross-page continuity, not merely per-page quality.

### World-Building Desk — Wētā Workshop
Documented principle: character, creature, armour, prop, miniature, environment and world-building design operate as one system; concept work can preserve a world's ethos even when translated into another medium.

**Week 3 implementation**
- Build reusable environment/object packets, not disposable backgrounds.
- Every recurring object receives continuity metadata: owner, meaning, first appearance, current condition.
- The Dark Horse, championship belt, chili dog, bridge/water motif, golf equipment, corporate artifacts and recurring league geography become trackable world assets.
- Environments must imply history and use, not look like generic fantasy backdrops.
- Art briefs specify material, wear, weather, scale and cultural origin.

### Cinematography Desk — Roger Deakins / visual-storytelling practice
Documented principle: framing, light and composition should convey the story's feeling; simplicity and motivated light often outperform decorative complexity. Preparation matters, but story/performer discoveries can justify deviation from storyboards.

**Week 3 implementation**
- Every hero image gets a one-sentence emotional objective before a visual prompt.
- Light must be motivated by environment/story.
- Avoid filling every inch of the page with spectacle.
- Use negative space and wide framing when isolation, defeat or scale is the point.
- Close framing is earned by confrontation/intimacy.
- Previsualization is a hypothesis, not a prison: final verified events may change composition.

## New Preproduction Deliverables

Before Monday close, Bullpen should maintain:

1. **W3 STORY QUESTION REGISTER**
   - one central dramatic question per matchup
   - one larger league theme each matchup may illuminate
   - evidence supporting that interpretation
   - counter-reading so writers do not force a predetermined meaning

2. **W3 VISUAL CONTINUITY BOARD**
   - character packet
   - environment
   - time of day
   - weather
   - dominant scale
   - camera role
   - motivated light source
   - recurring objects
   - condition/damage carried from prior pages
   - prohibited drift

3. **W3 WORLD ASSET REGISTER**
   - recurring locations
   - props
   - costumes/armour
   - mounts/companions
   - trophies
   - food/joke artifacts
   - symbols
   - first appearance
   - continuity state
   - future reuse potential

4. **W3 DAILIES BOARD**
   For each generated composition:
   - factual accuracy
   - character accuracy
   - story readability without caption
   - emotional objective achieved?
   - composition hierarchy
   - visual redundancy with prose?
   - continuity with preceding/following page
   - approve / revise / reject

5. **W3 PAGE RHYTHM MAP**
   Plan the whole memo as a sequence of visual pressure:
   quiet → scale → conflict → compression → release → escalation → aftermath.
   Do not alternate mechanically. Use page turns intentionally.

6. **W3 MEMORY / LORE TREATMENT RULE**
   Personal league anecdotes are allowed as memory, folklore or dramatization but are visually and editorially distinct from verified game telemetry.

7. **W3 SOUNDLESS FILM TEST**
   Before typography, review the planned illustrations in sequence. A reader should perceive changing emotional states and basic narrative movement without reading paragraphs.

8. **W3 TEXT-ONLY TEST**
   Remove illustrations. Manuscript must still tell a coherent Week 3 story with factual integrity.

9. **W3 REDUNDANCY TEST**
   Recombine text + illustration. If both communicate the same information, rewrite one.

10. **W3 FINAL-DAY CHANGE BUDGET**
   Prebuild environments, layouts and reference packets now; delay outcome-dependent hero poses, final copy, score typography and consequence language until Fact Lock. This reduces Monday rework.

## Six Preproduction Story Questions

These are provisional questions, not conclusions.

**ObiWan vs TDS:** Can aggressive roster engineering overcome structural scarcity, or does the crossing expose the cost of having too little room for error?

**D0nkey K0ng vs Mud Dogs:** What happens when two competent managers with different forms of intelligence meet in terrain neither fully owns?

**Chili vs Duckhook:** Was Wednesday's chili dog merely a joke, or will the league remember it as an omen because of what Sunday ultimately does?

**Slob vs Red Leopards:** Which matters more in this chapter — accumulated football certainty or the endurance to keep climbing after years without the summit?

**HMB vs Seven Deadly Chins:** How does the psychology of possessing championships differ from pursuing the first one?

**El Niño vs LLC:** Can accumulated capital and expectation become actual power, or does the storm expose the gap between assets and results?

## Visual Previs Rules

For each matchup prepare three composition hypotheses before final result:
- **A — victor-state composition**
- **B — reversal/close-finish composition**
- **C — aftermath composition**

Do not generate final result-specific art until Fact Lock. Environment and character studies may proceed.

Each visual brief must include:
EMOTIONAL OBJECTIVE / CAMERA ROLE / SCALE / FOREGROUND / MIDGROUND / BACKGROUND / MOTIVATED LIGHT / WEATHER / CHARACTER LOCKS / OBJECT LOCKS / NEGATIVE SPACE / TEXT-SAFE AREA / CONTINUITY-IN / CONTINUITY-OUT.

## Monday Production Room

At Fact Lock:
1. Data Director reports only verified changes.
2. Story Editor identifies what changed in each dramatic question.
3. Historian checks callbacks.
4. Showrunner selects macro-story.
5. Manuscript desk writes complete prose.
6. Art Director converts manuscript beats into visual briefs.
7. Cinematography desk selects framing/light/camera role.
8. Worldbuilding desk validates environments/objects.
9. Character desk validates packets.
10. Layout desk composes pages and controls rhythm.
11. Dailies board reviews the entire sequence.
12. Fact/continuity QA audits.
13. The Closer either releases or blocks.

## Governing Principle

The outside-production research changes our process, not our facts.

**Story first. Evidence underneath it. Worldbuilding around it. Composition in service of it. Continuity across all of it.**
