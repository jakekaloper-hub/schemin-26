## 01 — THE MIRACLE PUTT: Three Dreaded Snake (HOME) / Dr. Duckhook (AWAY)
### Overture — A Friendly Round
The waterfall made a sound so steady that Duckhook mistook it for silence. He stepped out of the damp jungle path and found his host beside a compact strip of grass, three heads peering at the same flag from three different angles. TDS had cleared a little room for the game, though the jungle had already begun claiming it back.

“Friendly round,” Duckhook said.

“Of course,” answered one head. Another studied his grip. The third watched the little cup at the end of the green.

The visitor set down his bag with the care of a professional preparing for an afternoon nobody would remember. TDS indicated the first tee. The two exchanged compliments about the course, each careful to make the compliments sound sincere. Neither asked what the other considered a friendly score.

### Escalation — The Courtesies End
By the next stretch of play, every compliment had acquired a sharp edge. Duckhook took an unnecessary practice swing after a perfectly acceptable shot. TDS celebrated a short putt as if the temple had survived a siege. One head called the line straight; another insisted it broke left; the third accused the first two of standing where the light was best.

They began to invent ceremonies for their own successes. Duckhook removed his cap after each good stroke. TDS's heads developed three incompatible victory nods. When Duckhook conceded a harmless putt, TDS demanded the ball be struck anyway. When TDS suggested a break, Duckhook asked whether the course had always been so difficult after sundown.

The waterfall kept its own counsel. Beneath the humor, both had begun to count the distance between friendship and defeat.

### Payoff — One Last Roll
At the final green, Duckhook faced a putt no reasonable guest should have attempted with confidence. Waterfall mist softened the far edge. The ground seemed to lean away from the cup. TDS stood motionless except for three pairs of eyes following the club.

Duckhook struck. The ball crossed the wet grass, slowed, hesitated on the lip and fell.

For a heartbeat the three heads gave three different verdicts. Then all three went quiet.

Duckhook raised his cap, not quite able to stop himself from smiling. TDS looked at the empty green and offered a towel as though the entire afternoon had been an exercise in hospitality.

The Commonwealth ledger would show Duckhook ahead by **0.32**. The little course could offer no such precision. It had only a cup, a ball and a host who would remember that sound for a long time.

**Inklings craft disposition (simulated):** Keep the hospitality motif, give TDS dignity, avoid equating fantasy points with literal strokes. **Atlas HOLD:** compact course not yet approved.

## 02 — THE HAUNTED STRONGHOLD: D0nkey K0ng (HOME) / El Niño (AWAY)
### Overture — The First Knock
DK had returned to a stronghold that seemed unwilling to welcome him. A shutter clicked twice against stone. Somewhere above the Arsenal hall, a lantern guttered and recovered. The banner at the stairhead moved though the inner doors were closed.

He inspected the latch, declared it sound and went back to arranging the training gear. A second knock came from the tower.

There were ordinary explanations for all of it. DK knew that. He also knew the sound had come from above him, and that no one had passed on the stairs. He stood in the doorway with his four equine legs planted squarely, the gorilla-warrior above them listening for a third knock.

It came immediately.

### Escalation — The Wrong Enemy
The first precaution was sensible: close the shutters. The second was less so: wedge a rack of equipment against a door that had not opened. By the third, DK had begun to address the empty hall in the voice he reserved for enemies who needed warning.

The tower answered with a long, thin whistle. Loose fabric snapped against a narrow window. DK swung around, catching sight of his own silhouette on a stone wall and recoiling before he recognized it.

He laughed at himself. Too loudly.

Then the lantern went out. He relit it and watched the flame bend toward the tower opening. The draft had a direction. The knocking had a rhythm. Every sign pointed toward the weather, but fear had already supplied a more exciting explanation.

DK climbed to settle the matter.

### Payoff — The Window
The tower's upper room held no ghost. It held a battered shutter, a loose fastening and a gust gathering strength beyond the opening. DK stepped forward to inspect the frame. Behind him, another shutter struck stone with the violence of a slammed door.

He startled. At that instant El Niño's wind drove through the tower.

The gust caught his great integrated body broadside. Four hooves lost their purchase. His arms reached for the sill too late, and the next image belonged to the outside of the stronghold: DK tumbling uncontrollably through the tower opening, banners whipping in the same direction as his fall.

The tower remained. The haunting did not.

On the league's observed scoreboard, El Niño stood **11.08 points** ahead. At the stronghold, DK had finally identified his opponent. Unfortunately, the explanation had arrived after he left the room.

**Inklings craft disposition (simulated):** Physical clues must precede the reveal; no sentient ghost or El Niño dialogue. **Hard HOLD:** approved tower geometry, landing/staging feasibility, DK reference conflict, environmental propagation.

## 03 — THE UNARMED LESSON: ObiWan Jacoby (HOME) / Seven Deadly Chins (AWAY)
### Overture — A Weapon Left Sleeping
ObiWan practiced alone on the mountain training ground when Chins appeared at the edge of the courtyard. The visitor watched the last movement, then stepped forward with a challenge that sounded almost courteous.

The Trade Jedi glanced at the green lightsaber clipped to his belt. It remained dark.

“Not necessary,” he said.

Chins looked from the weapon to ObiWan's empty hands. He had traveled too far for an exhibition, but he had no intention of insisting that an opponent draw a blade. The two took their places on firm ground, the mountain air moving through the courtyard without favor.

ObiWan smiled as though the outcome had already been explained.

### Escalation — Weight and Timing
ObiWan moved first, light on his feet, turning his opponent's momentum into a series of near misses. Chins absorbed the feints and adjusted his stance. The Jedi's hands moved with precision, his feet drawing circles that seemed to keep him safely outside the larger man's reach.

Then Chins caught an arm.

For an instant the balance changed. ObiWan twisted free and made a show of brushing dust from his sleeve. His lightsaber remained unlit against his belt. The gesture earned no applause. Chins merely stepped closer.

ObiWan attempted a more elaborate unarmed maneuver, confident that skill would replace leverage. Chins recognized the opening before the motion was complete.

### Payoff — The Sky Between
The counter began at ground level: a planted foot, a firm grip, a turn of the hips. ObiWan discovered that confidence could not change where his weight had gone.

Then the courtyard fell away beneath him.

For one impossible-looking instant the Trade Jedi crossed the open air, robes and belt following the arc. His lightsaber remained secured, unlit and unused. Below, Chins completed the throw and kept his footing.

The landing was decisive. Chins did not need a speech. He offered ObiWan room to rise, and the Jedi took it without looking at the weapon that had never left his belt.

The observed fantasy margin was **21.85 points** in Chins's favor. The training ground had offered a simpler lesson: the opponent ObiWan had underestimated was standing right in front of him.

**Inklings craft disposition (simulated):** Preserve both characters' agency; a consequence of pride, not a personality diagnosis of the real owner. **Atlas HOLD:** approved court, travel and landing space.

## 04 — THE CYPRESS CLEARING: Mud Dogs (HOME) / The Chili Cheesers (AWAY)
### Overture — Tracks Too Large
The first print lay in soft mud where the reeds thinned. Mud Dogs stopped beside it and said nothing. Chili knelt, measured the shape with his eyes and followed the trail toward the darker trees.

The tracks were unusually large. Neither hunter wanted to be first to say what that might mean.

Mud knew which patches would hold a boot and which would swallow one. Chili had his own method of reading broken stems and disturbed leaves. They moved through the wetland in an uneasy partnership, each pretending not to notice when the other found the better sign.

Ahead, something shifted beyond the cypress roots.

### Escalation — The Buck in the Reeds
The buck appeared in a brief corridor of light, magnificent and almost impossibly still. Chili saw it first; Mud saw the obstruction between them. Neither had a clean shot. The animal turned and vanished before either man could settle the question.

They followed without speaking. A splash farther down the channel sent both toward the wrong bank. Mud corrected the route with a gesture. Chili hesitated, then accepted the local knowledge he had been reluctant to ask for.

At the clearing, the wind moved through the cypress crowns. The tracks crossed a patch of firmer ground. This time they waited rather than rushed.

### Payoff — The Photograph
The buck stepped into the open. Chili had the position, the clear line and the patience to wait for it. The hunt ended there.

Later, in the cypress clearing, Chili stood beside the trophy for a photograph. Mud remained close enough to be part of the memory but not the center of it. The visitor's triumph was plain; so was the host's knowledge of the ground that had made the moment possible.

The league ledger showed Chili ahead by **26.20**. The swamp kept a more complicated account: one hunter had claimed the buck, but neither had reached it alone.

**Inklings craft disposition (simulated):** Avoid a trophy-only story; retain the host's competence. **World HOLD:** species/antler portrayal, permitted fictional hunting depiction, cypress site approval.

## 05 — THE FORGOTTEN COURT: Red Leopards (HOME) / His Majesty's Blood (AWAY)
### Overture — A Game Without Witnesses
The court was quiet when they arrived. No stands, no announcer, no procession. The basket stood over worn ground, and the only sound was the ball striking the surface between two rivals who had met before under very different names and circumstances.

Red Leopards had won one documented encounter in 2024. The owner lineage now known as His Majesty's Blood had answered in 2025. Neither result settled an all-time record. Both supplied enough memory for a private game.

The Belt Keeper arrived in his approved dark presentation, the Belt retained and set safely away from play. Red took the ball. First to eleven, they agreed. Nothing else needed to be said.

### Escalation — Every Inch Contested
The first drives were measured. Then the defense tightened. HMB used his reach to force Red wide; Red answered with quicker changes of direction. Each basket acquired a comment. Each missed attempt became evidence in an argument neither intended to concede.

There was no referee to appeal to, and no crowd to reward a flourish. When a possession ended awkwardly, the two settled it themselves and resumed.

Their rivalry had shrunk from the Commonwealth's history to the width of a half-court. That made every step more personal. Red dribbled at the perimeter and watched HMB set his feet.

### Payoff — The Crossover
Red moved left. HMB followed. The Leopard planted, crossed the ball sharply back and changed direction before the Belt Keeper could recover. HMB's balance broke for a step, then another.

Red rose into the open space and released the jumper.

The ball fell cleanly through the basket. For a moment neither moved. Red had the court. HMB still had the Belt. The distinction mattered.

The observed Week 7 fantasy score favored Red by **11.51 points**. The private basketball ending dramatized that result without pretending the game transferred any title.

**Inklings craft disposition (simulated):** History is backdrop, not invented all-time record; preserve HMB dignity. **Hard HOLD:** actual forgotten court, neutral GOTW eligibility/exception, stable playing surface.

## 06 — THE GENTLEMAN'S FLUSH: Slob on my Dobb (HOME) / The LLC (AWAY)
### Overture — The Invitation
The LLC arrived dressed for a negotiation and found Slob waiting beside a card table in a foothill room that had never aspired to elegance. The host had made an effort: the chairs matched almost well enough, the cards were clean and the refreshments occupied a tray instead of the floor.

“Gentleman's game,” Slob announced.

The LLC inspected the table, the light and the uneven stack of chips. He accepted a chair. For a while the evening proceeded with surprising courtesy. Slob dealt carefully. The visitor smiled as though the room had confirmed everything he expected.

### Escalation — A Tell Too Easy
The LLC began to notice a pattern. Slob seemed to pause before strong hands, speak too quickly before weak ones and stare at the same corner whenever he had something worth protecting.

The visitor tested his theory. Once it worked. Then again. Confidence arrived wearing the disguise of calculation.

Slob remained polite. He asked whether the cards were comfortable to hold. He offered to move a lamp. The LLC mistook every small courtesy for a distraction and every distraction for proof that his reading was correct.

On the final hand, the visitor studied Slob's face rather than the table. That was his mistake.

### Payoff — Five Cards
Slob laid his cards down in a neat row.

Ten. Jack. Queen. King. Ace. All one suit.

The royal flush occupied only a few inches of felt. It was enough to silence the room.

The LLC looked first at the cards, then at Slob, then at the little domestic arrangements he had dismissed when he arrived. Slob gathered the tray and asked whether his guest wanted another drink.

The observed fantasy scoreboard gave Slob the week's largest total, **155.92**, and a **44.71-point** margin. The poker room supplied the visual equivalent: a winning hand so complete that no argument improved it.

**Inklings craft disposition (simulated):** The visitor must lose through a believable inference, not magical luck or fake card mechanics. **QA HOLD:** exact five-card same-suit sequence, camera, approved Slob interior.
