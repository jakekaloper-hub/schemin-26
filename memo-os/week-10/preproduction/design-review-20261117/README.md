# Week 10 — six-sheet art-direction review

**Status: design discussion and proposed enhancements; no new artwork, final-layout approval, merge or publication.**

This review answers Jake's November 17 feedback on the actual six generated review sheets. The diagnosis is specific: the repeated top-to-bottom **header → illustration → written material** is clean and clear, but it separates the page into predictable compartments. The requested improvement is not more decoration. It is a folio in which composition, setting, action, typography and information are designed together.

## What is working

- The reader can quickly identify departments, matchup participants and sequence order.
- The six sheets expose the dramatic beats clearly enough to review causality before final artwork.
- Canonical silhouettes, recurring architecture and important props are substantially more recognizable than the rejected early attempts.
- The Opening Drive now draws from the established briefing-table, map, banners and sealed-folio language rather than an unrelated travel scene.
- The birthday gift, separate burger and cake, the ordinary beer delivery, and the surviving Imperial battery give individual sequences recognizable physical logic.

## What is limiting the design

- Most folios use the same horizontal bands and put exposition below the picture. A reader can predict the page shape before seeing the event.
- Very large repeated title treatments compete with action and make supporting departments feel like interchangeable poster cards.
- Adding a parchment board inside a picture is only partial integration when a second block below simply explains that board.
- The narrow contact-sheet scale hides whether complete copy really fits at the intended page size. A good-looking composite is not a substitute for a legible native folio.
- Tables and labels require exact composition rather than generative lettering. Several of the provisional images are now historical snapshots and must not be treated as current final sports records.
- A blanket command to make everything immersive would create the opposite failure: decorative typography, crooked tables and hard-to-read text. Integration must serve comprehension.

## Recommended direction — one visual system, several composition families

Keep a recognizable typographic family, restrained ornaments, full canonical names, department identity and folio numbering. Do not keep an identical location or size for every heading, identical art/text split, or identical bottom text box.

Use a small, purposeful family of page structures: **character-led cover; institutional briefing; kinetic action; vertical conflict; quiet aftermath; record-led intelligence; scenic transition; fixture-and-stakes dispatch.** These are proposed tools, not new rigid templates. The event decides the composition. Text occupies planned clear space in the artwork; not every paragraph needs to be literally engraved on a prop.

Alternative approaches considered: retaining the uniform frame is safest for scanning but fails the specific creativity request; unique maximalist layouts on every page would lose cohesion and phone readability. The recommended middle course keeps a common identity with scene-led variation.

## Ten review questions

### 1. What stays recognizable across the issue without forcing every page into the same layout?
Keep type families, restrained ornaments, department labels and folio numbering. Vary their placement, image scale and reading path. The benefit is continuity; the risk is mistaking a common visual identity for a mandatory grid.

### 2. Can each folio be designed around one dominant story action instead of a header–picture–paragraph stack?
Choose pursuit, flood, noise, delivery, reveal, resistance or decision first, then organize the page around that action. Avoid using one diagonal merely because it looks more dynamic.

### 3. Where can narration live in the scene without becoming tiny text painted onto props?
Plan quiet sky, smoke, walls, water or shadow as reading space. Use actual signs and ledgers for short labels and structured facts. Long narration remains editorial typography, not an implausible wall inscription.

### 4. Which departments should preserve their published visual language, and which can change substantially?
Opening Drive should remain unmistakably Opening Drive. Its table, map, banners and sealed folio can be recomposed, not discarded. Other departments should express their own function rather than copy that page.

### 5. How should three-page matchup sequences change scale and viewpoint rather than repeat a poster?
Use establishing, action and consequence views; protect landmarks, screen direction, character anatomy and causality. Camera variety is useful only when the reader still understands where everyone is.

### 6. Can text and image overlap meaningfully without losing names, numbers or phone readability?
A silhouette may cross a decorative rule. A lasso may lead the eye around a clear text area. Neither should cross body copy or a score. Review the real page at phone width before approving the composition.

### 7. How can the data-heavy fifth sheet feel like the Commonwealth rather than six reports?
Give each data function a different spatial setting and hierarchy, but typeset every name and number exactly. A dramatic perspective should never make a standings column harder to compare.

### 8. Should The Next Six communicate why each Week 11 matchup matters, not only who plays whom?
Yes: place concise stakes with the six fixtures. Top-seed pressure, possible elimination stakes and mathematically established elimination are different claims. Preserve those distinctions and all full team names.

### 9. How do we create rhythm across all 34 pages instead of simply adding detail?
Alternate density, distance, motion and silence. The Roads Between and the Final Word should give breathing room. Not every page should be a title-heavy climax.

### 10. What must the next proof demonstrate before production approval?
Correct 1080×1920 geometry, canonical reference use, complete readable copy, accurate source-bound data, purposeful composition variation and a contact sheet that really is scaled from the independent page masters. Do not claim those properties from the current composite images alone.
