## What the Week 10 briefs now require

- Start with the dramatic moment and camera: bodies, force, eyelines, distance, weather, witnesses and consequence determine the image.
- Compose the headline, narration and factual lettering with that image from the outset. Natural shadow, cloud, fog, stone, wood, cloth or an appropriate physical receipt can carry words. A surface is chosen because the scene supports it, not because every page must have the same box.
- Keep image depth and light continuous behind the words. Avoid a picture-above-paragraphs construction, detached generic score cards, standard parchment rectangles, fixed columns, HTML/CSS page production, report tables floating over art and uniform page geometry.
- Preserve exact wording and facts from the source fields. Verify all lettering on the actual raster. Any factual repair must retain the page's established material, light and composition.
- Use current exact character references. Historical pages guide cinematic craft, not current anatomy, costumes, companions, scores or location canon.
- Commission complete native **1080 × 1920 PNGs, 9:16**, and inspect the actual image. Historical 2:3 images remain historical; they are not stretched into new masters.
- Review the complete illustrated page at full size and phone scale. Judge the action, emotion, identity and legibility together. A font calculation or word-count pass cannot substitute for looking at the artwork.
