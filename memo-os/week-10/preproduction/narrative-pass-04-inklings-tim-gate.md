### Opening Drive — proposed replacement for W10-P02
By Sunday night, the Commonwealth had learned that a number could become a weapon without anyone lifting a blade. Two teams had crossed two hundred. One sent a hamburger to a birthday party; another brought an army to a mountain gate. A centaur was chasing an outlaw through Highland mud. In the eastern ruins, a river quietly made a mockery of Red's sandbags, while a second gust carried the same storm's reach to a court farther inland. Chins found the one household that needed quiet and arrived with a jackhammer. Slob made a beer delivery through a swamp whose owner could no longer command the road. The front belonged to Niño; the humiliations belonged to the men who chose them. With four regular-season games remaining after this one, the question was no longer simply who won. It was what each result would leave behind.

### Commissioner’s Note — proposed replacement for W10-P03
There is a difference between being beaten and having to live with the evidence. A score is entered once. A birthday photograph is shown forever. A broken hoop stays broken until someone fixes it. The owner of a swamp still has to watch the next truck drive through. That is the Commonwealth we have spent seasons building, and it is why this league is more than six boxes on a scoreboard. But our stories do not outrank the standings. Nobody is going to Cancún by mathematical decree until the last route is closed. Nobody is healed because a reserve slot looks full. Tonight's remaining starters can still move the board. When those numbers settle, the next week will ask each of us what we have left to field.

### Chili–DK: three-page emotional spine (W10-P05–07)
**P05 — Everybody Gets a View:** Chili has always understood the value of a crowd. At last week's cookout he chose the victim, the serving and the moment everyone laughed. Now he circles the Arsenal's Highland ring with the crowd above him, searching for an exit while the four-legged centaur closes behind, lasso turning overhead. Every open gate seems to belong to DK. The same storm that wet the rails has made the ground a poor place to sprint in expensive boots.
**P06 — The Ring Runs Out:** Chili cuts hard for the service gate. The lasso reaches him before he reaches it. He keeps running for a fraction too long, turning the capture into a skid through the muddy groove he made himself. The crowd's reaction is no longer the sound he came to produce. DK has not needed the axe. The rope, the arena and Chili's own decision to flee have done the work.
**P07 — No Private Exit:** DK finishes the binding in the center ring, with all four hooves planted and the rope's path plain to see. Chili lies hogtied, hat just out of reach, looking back at the entrance where he had expected to be the attraction. Last week he had served humiliation to a champion. This week the Commonwealth has found a new course for the menu.

### Red–Niño: three-page emotional spine (W10-P08–10)
**P08 — One More Bag:** The river did not arrive as a wall. It climbed a little, found a gap, and waited for Red to believe the last sandbag had solved it. The old goal leaned over a pitch still carrying last week's damage. Red shifted another bag into place. The water went around it. In 2022, he had beaten Niño twice and carried thirteen wins into the semifinal. It was not the strength of the old season he remembered now, but how little of it had mattered on the night it ended.
**P09 — The Forgotten Court:** Later, in the Central Basin—not a magically adjacent corner of the eastern ruins—Red reaches the old basketball court, a place associated with a different recent victory. The same expanding front has arrived by a different road. A gust twists the branches above the familiar rim. He has barely looked up when the old limb comes down across the backboard. One storm, two places, and no time to make either one safe.
**P10 — That Cold Again:** The hoop hangs wrong. Red sits beneath it with the mud of his failed sandbag line still on him. Niño laughs somewhere inside the moving cloud, and the old semifinal comes back in full: 116.32 to 97.96, a 13–1 favorite sent home, and Niño eventually lifting that season's title. Red is not eliminated today. That is almost the cruelest part. He still has to decide whether to get up and repair another thing.

### TDS–Chins: three-page emotional spine (W10-P12–14)
**P12 — The Work Begins:** There had been a time when the temple's waterfall was the loudest thing the family heard. Now a jackhammer bites the courtyard paving, sending pale chips against steps worn smooth over years. Behind the upstairs door the newborn starts crying. TDS's partner calls for help. Three heads reach the balcony at once, none of them prepared for the sight of Chins enjoying himself below.
**P13 — He Heard You:** TDS shouts down to stop. Chins lets the hammer go quiet long enough to look up. For one hopeful breath the waterfall fills the court again. TDS turns toward the crying child. Then Chins laughs, turns the music higher and plants the bit in the next square of paving. The insult is not that he could not hear. It is that he listened and chose the encore.
**P14 — No Quiet Hours:** Broken concrete lies in a widening crescent around the same jackhammer. Chins dances badly to his own music, delighted by an audience of three furious heads. Upstairs, the father has a crying baby and an angry partner; downstairs, his visitor has a fresh piece of ground to destroy. Last season TDS beat Chins twice. This week Chins has discovered a form of revenge that needs neither a trophy nor permission.

### Mud–Slob: three-page emotional spine (W10-P15–17)
**P15 — Road Closed:** Mud plants himself beside the old swamp entrance marker, blocking the raised service strip with the confidence of a landlord. Beyond him comes a long flatbed of beer, driven by Slob with the ease of a man on a familiar delivery run. The swamp is wet, the old racing ruts are full, and the cargo straps are tight. Mud barks a warning. Slob checks the mirror.
**P16 — Same Speed:** The truck does not stop. Slob does not even make a show of defiance. He keeps the wheels on the firm strip, guides the kegs around the bend and rolls past at ordinary speed. Mud follows on the soft shoulder, barking harder as the trailer grows longer and less interested in him. The joke is not that Slob has conquered the swamp. It is that the swamp has ceased to inconvenience his schedule.
**P17 — Cancún Is Calling:** The last tail lamp disappears into reeds. Mud stands under his own sign beside empty stands and flooded racing scars, watching a postcard for Cancún flutter against the post. It is a taunt, not a ticket: the seventh playoff place is still mathematically reachable under the current scenario. But another loss next week could make the joke look less like a joke. For now, Slob has delivered the beer, and Mud has been left with the silence after a truck.

### HMB–Duck: three-page emotional spine (W10-P19–21)
**P19 — A Very Generous Guest:** The dining hall in Jackson is full enough that the guests must turn sideways between the streamers and the cakes. Duckhook is the birthday boy, a host in his own kingdom of polished tables and familiar club service. HMB arrives beneath the storm-dark windows carrying an enormous present, belt visible and sword irrelevant. Last year Duck came within four tenths of stopping this rival's championship path. Tonight he opens the door to him.
**P20 — The 200 Burger:** Duck pulls the ribbon, raises the lid and discovers a double-decker hamburger big enough to have a title: 200 BURGER. For a moment he tries to smile like a gracious host. Then the guests understand what the number means. HMB has given him a celebration he can neither refuse nor explain away. The separate birthday cake waits on the sideboard.
**P21 — Many Unhappy Returns:** HMB presses that cake squarely into Duck's face. Frosting coats the orange bill and the front of the green vest while the warm dining hall erupts. The burger remains magnificent in its box, untouched. Last week Chili made HMB the dish at a public cookout. This week the champion has found a new place setting, and the birthday boy is wearing dessert.

### Obi–LLC: three-page emotional spine (W10-P22–24)
**P22 — The Empire Opens Fire:** The LLC had beaten Obi by eight tenths of a point in 2024. A margin that narrow teaches a rival to fear one last play. This time the executive has brought an army. Red fire crosses the mountain causeway, and Obi answers with a green blade, destroying the first battery. The home gate is behind him, warm and small against the cold storm.
**P23 — The Higher Route:** Obi takes the ridge because it is the path an army would overlook and because he refuses to let the first success be his last. For a moment the second advance works. The LLC does not need to move from the command shelf to answer. One gesture brings the surviving battery around. The battle is not quiet; neither is the courage required to keep going.
**P24 — Unlimited Firepower:** Obi holds the inner arch as the remaining guns converge. Smoke from the destroyed emplacement blows in the same direction as the flags; the one surviving battery still fires. He has made two choices, earned two real moments, and still faces an enemy whose resources outlast them. In the current leading-score branch, that is the cruel difference between a heroic scene and a winning one. The final sports outcome remains unposted.
