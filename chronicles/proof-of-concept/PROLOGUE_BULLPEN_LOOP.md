# QUESTION

Should the 2026 Schemin Chronicle begin directly with Chapter I / Week 1, or should it contain a prologue?

The board is instructed to optimize for the long-lived Chronicle, not merely Week 1.

---

# LOOP 1 — ARGUMENT FOR A PROLOGUE

## Librarian
A prologue solves a temporal problem.

Week 1 is the first week of the 2026 volume, not the birth of the League. Existing evidence already supports:
- a League with prior seasons;
- a Championship Belt already in circulation;
- established inhabitants with visual identities;
- recurring institutional concepts such as books, wagers, standings and reputation;
- historical records that predate 2026.

Opening without acknowledging prior time risks making 2026 feel like creation ex nihilo.

## Beat Writer
“The First Receipts” becomes stronger if the reader understands it means the first receipts **of this book**, not the first records in existence.

A prologue can establish one governing literary promise:

> This world remembers more than this volume can immediately explain.

That creates depth without requiring an encyclopedia.

## Visual Development
The prologue gives the publication a distinct visual overture:
- aged paper;
- layered handwriting;
- partial portraits;
- scratched-out names;
- old marks;
- Belt fragments;
- maps whose authority is uncertain;
- records from different eras;
- damaged or incomplete artifacts.

Then the first pristine 2026 page creates a powerful material contrast.

---

# LOOP 2 — ARGUMENT AGAINST A PROLOGUE

## Analyst
A bad prologue delays the actual characters.

The twelve are the product. If the reader spends six pages on unidentified history before meeting them, the Chronicle may feel self-important rather than immersive.

## Clubhouse Manager
The world should not become more important than its inhabitants.

A mythology montage that turns owners into tiny pieces of “lore” would undermine the character-first system.

## Umpire
Historical archaeology is incomplete.

A conventional historical prologue creates strong temptation to fill blank years with invented champions, rivalries, kingdoms, wars or dynasties and later mistake those inventions for recovered history.

The prologue cannot outrun the evidence ledger.

---

# LOOP 3 — SYNTHESIS

The problem is not whether a prologue exists.

The problem is **what kind of prologue** exists.

Reject:
- encyclopedic history;
- omniscient timeline;
- “thousands of years ago” fantasy exposition;
- invented ancient wars;
- invented championship lineage;
- invented geography;
- a narrator explaining the rules of the universe;
- six pages of anonymous lore before recognizable characters appear.

Approve:
## THE ARCHIVE PROLOGUE

The prologue is an encounter with incomplete memory.

It shows that records existed before 2026 without pretending we have recovered all of them.

Its subject is not ancient history.

Its subject is **the existence of history**.

---

# BOARD DECISION

## APPROVED FORMAT

**PROLOGUE — BEFORE THIS BOOK**

Target: **4 pages**, not 6.

The prologue is part of the physical Chronicle but is not “Week 0.”

It does not contain a matchup story.

It establishes:
1. the League predates 2026;
2. names can change while inhabitants persist;
3. records survive imperfectly;
4. the Belt already has history;
5. evidence, rumor and memory are not identical;
6. this volume begins with a clean 2026 ledger.

The prologue ends by handing the reader directly into:

**CHAPTER I — THE FIRST RECEIPTS**

---

# FOUR-PAGE PROLOGUE ARCHITECTURE

## P0.1 — THE ARCHIVE

**Narrative purpose:** Age without chronology dump.

**Visual:** An archive/table/chamber viewed as a physical accumulation of seasons: bound books, loose score sheets, receipts, marginalia, old brackets, sealed envelopes, damaged paper, photographs/paintings whose details are partially obscured.

**Text:** Very little.

Working line:

> Before this book, there were other books.

No unsupported historical result appears.

### Umpire gate
Every legible factual artifact must either be verified or deliberately non-specific.

---

## P0.2 — NAMES DO NOT HOLD STILL

**Narrative purpose:** Teach rename continuity before it becomes confusing.

**Visual:** layered labels/nameplates/ledger entries where old and newer team names occupy different eras; inhabitants remain visually consistent where evidence permits.

**Core concept:**
Owners own characters. Teams own names.

Do not literally print internal governance language unless it works artistically.

**Potential verified examples only after provenance lock:**
- Belt Keeper's changing team labels;
- Arsenal Centaur's prior/current labels.

### Umpire gate
No alias enters final art without temporal verification.

---

## P0.3 — WHAT SURVIVES

**Narrative purpose:** Establish competing forms of memory.

**Visual:** Championship Belt detail; Pittsy ledger; damaged receipt; map fragment; witness sketch; old score notation.

**Narrative idea:**

> Some things survived because someone kept them. Others survived because nobody stopped telling the story.

This introduces the Chronicle's three historical layers without explaining them:
- Recorded History;
- Chronicle History;
- Legend/Folklore.

### Umpire gate
Do not imply the Belt is magical.
Do not identify an unverified former champion.

---

## P0.4 — THE CLEAN PAGE

**Narrative purpose:** Hard transition into 2026.

**Visual:** New ledger/book opened to the 2026 season. Clean standings. Twelve entry lines. Fresh paper against the aged archive.

The surrounding archive remains visible at the edge of frame.

**Final movement:**

> History had already happened.
>
> None of it could fill the empty columns.
>
> The new book required new evidence.

Page turn:

# CHAPTER I — THE FIRST RECEIPTS

Opening:

> There were twelve of them when the books opened.

---

# LOOP 4 — EFFECT ON CHAPTER I

The prologue changes Chapter I in four useful ways.

### 1. Remove burden
Chapter I no longer has to imply it is explaining the League's origin.

### 2. Increase mystery
Established objects can appear without immediate exposition because the reader already understands that history precedes them.

### 3. Clarify names
Rename continuity becomes a feature of the world rather than an editorial inconvenience.

### 4. Strengthen receipts
Week 1's records are not the invention of recordkeeping; they are the first entries in the 2026 volume.

---

# LOOP 5 — EFFECT ON HISTORICAL ARCHAEOLOGY

Historical recovery now has a publication destination.

As older seasons become verified, the archive can become more specific over time.

This creates an important rule:

## RETROACTIVE DETAIL, NOT RETROACTIVE CONTRADICTION

Future recovered history may sharpen previously ambiguous prologue artifacts.

It may not contradict a factual statement already canon-committed.

Therefore the first prologue should intentionally use:
- obscured dates;
- cropped records;
- partial inscriptions;
- unlabeled images;
- verified objects;
- verified identity continuity;
- atmosphere.

Ambiguity is not a weakness here. It is a preservation mechanism.

---

# LOOP 6 — SHOULD THE PROLOGUE BE REWRITTEN EVERY WEEK?

**No.**

The prologue belongs to **Volume I / 2026**, not every weekly release.

Weekly/Chapter PDFs may optionally include a compact “Previously in the Chronicle” device later, but that is a separate system.

The complete Volume I book eventually contains:
Prologue → Chapter I → Chapter II → Chapter III ... → postseason/end matter.

Standalone weekly publication can begin with its own cover and chapter opening without repeating the four-page prologue.

---

# LOOP 7 — PRODUCTION CONSEQUENCE

The previous instruction to render Chapter I Pages 10–11 first is modified.

Before final rendering, produce one **opening-system prototype**:

**P0.4 Clean Page → Chapter I Page 1**

Why:
This tests whether the prologue actually improves the page turn and whether the visual language can move from old archive material into living 2026 narrative.

Then retain Pages 10–11 as the primary character-continuity stress test.

Production test order:
1. P0.4 + Chapter I Page 1 — book-opening transition.
2. Chapter I Pages 10–11 — character/canon stress test.
3. Chapter I Pages 12–14 — artifact/material storytelling stress test.
4. Umpire comparison.
5. Expand to remaining pages only after the three systems pass.

---

# CLOSER RULING

**ADD THE PROLOGUE.**

But keep it short, physical, incomplete and evidence-disciplined.

The prologue should make the world feel older.

Chapter I should make it feel alive.

Chapter II should prove it remembers.

Chapter III should prove it can continue without us reinventing the machine.

This becomes the working opening architecture for Schemin Chronicle — Volume I / 2026.
