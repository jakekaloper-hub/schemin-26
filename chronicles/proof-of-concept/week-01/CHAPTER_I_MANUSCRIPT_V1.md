# SCHEMIN CHRONICLE — 2026
# CHAPTER I — THE FIRST RECEIPTS

**Status:** MANUSCRIPT V1 — STORY, NOT YET CANON-COMMITTED  
**Production:** Week 1 Proof-of-Concept  
**Truth rule:** Locked scores/results are FACT. Established identities/objects are CANON. Narrative connective tissue below is STORY until accepted.

---

There were twelve of them when the books opened.

Not twelve kings. Not twelve armies. Not twelve banners planted neatly around a map whose borders everyone agreed upon.

Twelve inhabitants arrived from places that did not yet seem interested in fitting together.

A red cat of impossible size came out of old green stone and wet leaves. A thing made of storm moved beneath a sky that appeared to have followed him. Somewhere farther off, iron dragged through swamp water behind a mud-caked beast. A white duck smoked a cigar beside golf clubs. A centaur carried an axe and a tankard. A horned brute carried a bat.

The corporate man brought paperwork.

The outlaw brought chili.

And somewhere above the lower roads, where the country lifted into the North Georgia mountains, a blond strategist stood beside a golden retriever and watched the first notices arrive.

Nobody yet knew what any of it meant.

That was the privilege of the first week.

There was no evidence before the evidence.

---

## I. THE BOOK OPENS

Pittsy's book was already taking marks.

Ink accumulated beside names. Numbers were crossed out, rewritten, circled, argued over. Someone had left the impression of a wet glass on one corner. Another hand had written something in the margin that would have been more useful if it had been legible.

The important thing was not that people wagered.

The important thing was that somebody remembered.

Elsewhere, another ledger waited. Receipts accumulated. Standings had empty columns. The spaces were clean enough to be threatening.

By the end of the week, they would not be.

The first encounters began before anyone had agreed on what to call them.

---

## II. WEATHER AND SCALE

Three Dreaded Snake entered beneath weather that would have sent ordinary travelers looking for a roof.

El Niño did not arrive beneath the storm.

He arrived as part of it.

Water gathered where there had been none. Wind folded vegetation flat and then released it. Light moved inside cloud. The elemental shape at the center of it carried a staff and looked less like a man commanding weather than weather temporarily willing to resemble a man.

Across from him, green-gold scales caught the flashes.

The dreadlocks stayed loose.

The serpent did not attempt to command the storm. He endured it. Advanced through it. Disappeared behind sheets of water and returned somewhere the eye did not expect him to be.

When the accounting was finished, the mark beside Three Dreaded Snake read **175.56**.

Beside El Niño: **144.42**.

The margin was not small.

But neither did the storm cease because a book said it had lost.

It moved on.

TDS remained behind with wet dreadlocks, a 1–0 mark, and the second-largest number anyone would put on paper that week.

For now, that was enough.

---

## III. A MATTER OF MEASURE

The white duck studied the line.

Seven Deadly Chins studied the duck.

The raccoon studied everybody.

Dr. Duckhook adjusted his bucket hat, shifted the cigar to the other side of his bill, and considered whatever impossible geometry existed between where he stood and where he intended something to finish.

Chins had brought a hammer.

This seemed, initially, like an argument against geometry.

It became a long afternoon.

Precision gained ground. Force took it back. A mark was made, disputed, inspected, made again. The sort of crowd that gathers when two strangers begin arguing over an inch slowly became the sort of crowd that understands an inch may be the entire point.

By the final measure, neither participant looked defeated.

The ledger was less diplomatic.

**Dr. Duckhook — 147.32.**

**Seven Deadly Chins — 145.82.**

One and a half.

Chins stared at the notation.

Duckhook drew on the cigar.

The raccoon appeared to have formed an opinion.

Someone asked whether the measure should be taken again.

Nobody volunteered to ask Chins.

The receipt survived.

That was what mattered.

---

## IV. HORSEFLESH, HORNS, AND A SPILLED DRINK

The Arsenal Centaur was difficult to ignore even before he moved.

Red cloth. Muscle. Steel. The heavy geometry of a creature built for two different kinds of violence and apparently dissatisfied with both. His axe announced one set of intentions. His tankard suggested another.

Slob on my Dobb answered with a metal bat and the expression of a man-beast who considered introductions a formality invented by smaller people.

There was noise.

There was boasting.

There was at least one drink that did not survive the opening exchange.

What followed lacked the elegance of Duckhook's measure and the elemental scale of the storm. It was closer to a disagreement that had become too large for the furniture.

The Centaur held the advantage when it mattered.

**142.70 to 134.96.**

He raised the tankard afterward.

Slob did not look diminished.

He looked inconvenienced.

The distinction would matter.

---

## V. THE SWAMP KEEPS ITS OWN ACCOUNT

Mud Dogs came out of the water wearing the swamp.

Mud in the coat. Mud on the chain. Mud where a cleaner creature might have possessed a recognizable boundary between itself and the ground.

The Red Leopard entered differently.

Black rosettes broke the red hide. Old armor sat against a body already equipped with claws sufficient for most disagreements. Behind him—or perhaps merely associated with him in the mind of whoever later painted the scene—were suggestions of wet stone and ruined green places.

Predator met predator.

No crown waited for either of them.

No grand proclamation followed.

The difference was **4.50**.

Mud Dogs: **125.36**.

Red Leopards: **120.86**.

Close enough that the losing side could leave believing the next accounting might reverse everything.

Close enough that the winning side could leave knowing it had not.

The chain disappeared back through brown water.

The Red Leopard went his own way.

The League kept the receipt.

---

## VI. THE MAN WITH THE BELT

The Belt Keeper did not need an introduction.

The object did that work.

It sat against black rune armor with the vulgar confidence of something that had been seen, coveted, discussed, photographed, remembered. Whether the Belt possessed any power beyond the attention it commanded was a question nobody needed answered that afternoon.

Attention was power enough.

Across from him stood the Trade Jedi.

Young. Blond. Earth tones instead of black armor. A green blade. A golden retriever nearby, watching with the patient concern of an animal accustomed to human beings making complicated things unnecessarily serious.

The strategist did not wear a belt.

He did not arrive to claim one.

He arrived prepared.

The encounter unfolded less like conquest than calculation. Position. Patience. Refusal to spend movement where none was required. Green light against rune-dark steel. The Belt Keeper remained what he had been when it began: imposing, pale, armed, visibly carrying the object that caused strangers to turn their heads.

But the first Week 1 accounting did not belong to him.

**ObiWan Jacoby — 141.70.**

**The Immortal — 131.26.**

The name written beside the dark champion would change in time.

The inhabitant would not.

When it was over, the Belt Keeper left with the Championship Belt still in his possession.

The Trade Jedi left with something less tangible and, for the moment, more useful.

A receipt.

The retriever followed.

---

## VII. THE OUTLAW AND THE LEDGER

The LLC believed in surfaces.

Dark tailoring. Gold. Leather. Glass. Paper arranged into stacks with squared corners. A briefcase placed where a briefcase ought to be placed. A cigar allowed to burn at the speed appropriate for a man who expected other people to wait.

Numbers were not enemies.

Numbers were the mechanism by which enemies became manageable.

Then the Chili Outlaw arrived.

He came carrying the visual vocabulary of heat: weathered red, metal, steam, peppers, smoke. The Dark Horse remained close enough to make the entire proceeding feel less like a meeting than a temporary occupation.

The corporate man's world could price risk.

It had more difficulty pricing excess.

By the time the encounter reached its crescendo, the neat distinction between ledger and stain had become harder to maintain. Chili oil marked paper. Steam crossed polished surfaces. Ash found expensive cloth. Somewhere in the confusion, somebody tried to preserve a document by lifting it above the mess and succeeded only in making it more visible.

Then came the number.

**198.00.**

The largest mark of the opening week.

Across from it:

**122.01.**

A difference of **75.99**.

Nobody needed a chronicler to explain that.

The LLC adjusted himself amid the evidence.

He remained a corporate raider. He remained composed enough to understand that one terrible accounting was still only one accounting.

The Chili Outlaw did not become king of anything.

There was no throne waiting.

There was, however, a receipt.

And by nightfall everyone had heard about it.

---

## VIII. WHAT THE FIRST WEEK KNEW

Six marks entered the winning column.

Six entered the other.

Chili Cheesers: 1–0.

Three Dreaded Snake: 1–0.

Dr. Duckhook: 1–0.

Donkey Kong, inhabited by the Arsenal Centaur: 1–0.

ObiWan Jacoby: 1–0.

Mud Dogs: 1–0.

Across from them, The LLC, El Niño, Seven Deadly Chins, Slob on my Dobb, the Belt Keeper's then-current Immortal name, and Red Leopards carried the first losses of the year.

Nothing else had been decided.

That was the mistake the first week always invited: believing the first evidence was the final evidence.

Pittsy's pages were no longer clean.

The standings had begun.

Wagers had become settlements.

Reputation, that most unreliable form of currency, had acquired its opening prices.

Somewhere, six winners were being discussed as if victory had revealed their nature.

Somewhere else, six losers were discovering how quickly a single result could become a story told by somebody who had not been there.

The League collected its remnants.

A marked page.

A measure.

A stain.

A score.

A witness.

A receipt.

Then the roads opened again.

The storm moved.

The swamp settled.

The Belt remained where it had been.

The Chili Outlaw rode away from the loudest number of the week.

And twelve inhabitants carried the first evidence of the season toward whatever came next.

None of them knew Week 2.

That was the privilege of the reader who would eventually turn the page.
