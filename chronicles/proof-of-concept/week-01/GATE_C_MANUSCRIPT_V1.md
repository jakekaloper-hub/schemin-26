# GATE C — MANUSCRIPT V1
## Trade Jedi vs. His Majesty's Blood

**Status:** STORY DRAFT — NOT CANON COMMIT
**Placement:** Chapter I — The First Receipts
**Temporal boundary:** Week 1 only

## THE ROAD TO THE KEEPER

The road narrowed as it climbed.

Below it, the country fell away in long folds of pine and stone, the last warm light catching on ridges that seemed, from a distance, almost gentle. Up close there was nothing gentle about them. The trail had been cut into the mountain wherever the mountain had permitted it, and nowhere else.

The Trade Jedi walked without hurry.

His coat had collected dust at the hem. A leather case knocked softly against his hip with every third step. Inside it were pages, figures, names, possibilities—things that mattered greatly in rooms with tables and very little out here.

The retriever ranged ahead of him, stopped, looked back, and waited.

“I know,” he said.

The dog turned uphill again.

By then the towers had appeared.

They did not rise from the mountain so much as darken it.

Black stone occupied the higher ridge beneath a bank of weather, its walls disappearing at intervals into cloud. No welcoming fire showed in the windows. No herald rode down to meet him. A single banner moved above the gate, heavy enough that the wind seemed to struggle with it.

The Trade Jedi stopped where the road gave him his first clear view.

Something metallic caught what little light remained.

Not a crown.

Not a sword.

The Belt.

Then cloud crossed the ridge and the light was gone.

## THE MAN WHO CARRIED HISTORY

His Majesty's Blood received visitors standing.

There was a chair behind him large enough to be mistaken for a throne, but he had not used it.

The Belt Keeper was taller at close range than distance had suggested and leaner than the stories made him. Black armor lay over him in overlapping scales and plates, each surface scratched, darkened or marked by narrow runes. His face was almost without color. Long black hair hung loose around it. Pale eyes regarded the newcomer without warmth and without surprise.

A great sword rested point-down beside him.

The Belt sat at his waist.

That was the difficulty of looking at him: everything else eventually led the eye back to it.

The Trade Jedi's hand remained away from his own weapon.

The retriever sat.

For a while neither man spoke.

At last the Keeper said, “You came a long way to see something you cannot take.”

The Trade Jedi looked at the Belt, then at him.

“I didn't come for it.”

That earned the smallest change in the Keeper's expression.

Not amusement.

Interest, perhaps.

“Everyone comes for it.”

“Then everyone makes the same mistake.”

The wind moved through the open stonework above them. Somewhere deeper in the fortress, iron struck iron once and stopped.

The Keeper lifted the great sword from the floor.

“Show me yours.”

Only then did the green blade come alive.

## THE CONTEST

Men who had never seen the Keeper move described him badly.

They mistook stillness for slowness.

The first step corrected that.

Black steel crossed the space between them with such economy that the Trade Jedi abandoned the ground he had chosen before he had properly occupied it. The green blade turned the strike aside. The force of it traveled through his wrists and into his shoulders.

The second strike arrived where his retreat wanted to go.

He changed direction.

The Keeper did not chase.

That was worse.

The courtyard had been built in uneven levels around the mountain rock. Stairs rose where a flat floor would have been easier. Pillars interrupted obvious lines. Water from the high weather crossed the stone in narrow silver threads.

The Trade Jedi began to understand the room.

The Keeper already did.

So the younger man stopped trying to win the ground.

He gave it away.

A step.

Another.

The Keeper advanced only when the advantage was real.

Good, the Trade Jedi thought.

The green light moved lower.

A feint opened the left side. The Keeper ignored it.

A second invited the high line. Ignored again.

The Trade Jedi retreated toward the broken arcade.

The Keeper's pale eyes narrowed.

There.

Not a weakness.

A decision.

The Trade Jedi had not spent the climb imagining how to strike the man in front of him. He had spent it imagining what the man in front of him would refuse to do.

The next exchange sounded different.

Green light met rune-dark steel once, twice, then disappeared as the Trade Jedi killed the blade.

For half a heartbeat the courtyard went dark.

The Keeper's sword continued along the line it had already chosen.

The Trade Jedi was no longer on it.

He moved inside the arc, crossed behind a pillar and forced the Keeper to turn through the narrow side of the broken stone. When the green blade returned, it was not at the Keeper's throat.

It rested against the flat of the great sword below his hands.

Position, not blood.

The two men stood there.

Rain ticked against armor.

The retriever watched from beneath the arch.

The Keeper looked down at the crossed weapons.

Then he looked at the Trade Jedi.

The green blade withdrew.

The Keeper lowered his sword.

No one cheered.

There was no one there who needed to.

## THE RECEIPT

The League did not record the rain.

It did not record the broken arcade, the darkened blade or the moment the Keeper chose not to contest what had already been decided.

The League recorded numbers.

**OBIWAN JACOBY — 141.70**

**HIS MAJESTY'S BLOOD — 131.26**

One line moved to **1–0**.

The other moved to **0–1**.

Ink dried faster than reputation.

## THE BELT STAYS

The Trade Jedi closed the ledger.

Across the courtyard, the Keeper fastened the great sword against his back.

The Belt had not moved.

That, more than anything, clarified what had happened.

A victory could be recorded immediately. History was less obedient.

The Trade Jedi had beaten the man carrying the Belt.

He had not become the man carrying the Belt.

The distinction did not diminish the result. It made the result stranger.

The Keeper stepped past him toward the inner gate.

“You'll hear them talk now,” he said.

“About the score?”

“About what they think it means.”

The Trade Jedi looked toward the road down the mountain.

“And what does it mean?”

The Keeper stopped.

For the first time, the pale eyes moved toward the dog rather than the man.

“Ask me when they stop talking.”

Then he disappeared beneath the stone.

---

## WHAT A WIN DOES NOT BUY

The Trade Jedi remained in the courtyard until the rain softened.

He could have imagined the Belt around his own waist. Men had imagined more for doing less.

Instead he knelt beside the retriever and tightened the strap on the leather case.

The receipt went inside.

That was enough.

For now.

The mountain behind him still belonged to the Keeper. The Belt still belonged to the Keeper. Whatever stories had accumulated around both of them had survived one line of new ink.

But the line existed.

That mattered too.

He had not come down from the mountain with a title.

He came down with evidence.

---

## RECEIPTS TRAVEL

By morning, a copy of the result had left the mountain.

By noon, another had been folded into a ledger elsewhere.

Someone underlined **141.70**.

Someone circled **131.26**.

Someone wrote a question in the margin and did not sign it.

The League was beginning to do what leagues do.

It was turning evidence into argument.

And there were still ten inhabitants whose first receipts had not yet finished traveling.
