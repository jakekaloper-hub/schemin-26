# PROLOGUE — PRODUCTION ARCHITECTURE V2
## BEFORE THIS BOOK

**Status:** ACTIVE
**Maximum length:** 12 finished pages
**Bullpen target:** 8 pages
**Expansion rule:** pages 9–12 must be earned by evidence or reader experience; never added to hit a quota.

# PURPOSE

The Prologue must make the reader feel three things before Week 1 begins:

1. this world existed before the 2026 volume;
2. its history survives imperfectly through objects, records, names and memory;
3. none of that history can decide what happens when the new ledger opens.

It is not a franchise encyclopedia.
It is not a championship timeline.
It is not “Week 0.”
It is an invitation into an old world at the moment a new record begins.

# LITERARY STANDARD

The finished pages carry the actual literature.

Avoid:
- caption-heavy magazine writing;
- lore dumps;
- lists of prior champions without narrative purpose;
- invented dynasties;
- fake certainty;
- omniscient historical exposition.

Use:
- physical evidence;
- partial records;
- tactile objects;
- restrained narrator;
- unanswered implications;
- material age;
- specific verified identity continuity;
- quiet transitions;
- prose/art ratios that change by page.

# TARGET EIGHT-PAGE ARC

## P0.1 — BEFORE THIS BOOK
**Mode:** opening painting + literary prose
**Function:** age.

Open inside the Archive.

Books, damaged paper, old score sheets, receipts, folded notices, worn bindings, photographs/images whose factual details are obscured unless verified.

Core movement:
The League did not begin with this volume.

Do not explain when it began.

Potential opening:
“Before this book, there were other books.”

Target emotion: discovery.

---

## P0.2 — WHAT THE PAPER REMEMBERS
**Mode:** prose-led artifact page
**Function:** evidence.

Records preserve outcomes more faithfully than motives.
Scores survive.
Names survive sometimes.
Context decays.

Introduce the Chronicle epistemology without naming it as a system:
- what was written;
- what was remembered;
- what was repeated until nobody remembered who said it first.

Target emotion: curiosity.

---

## P0.3 — NAMES DO NOT HOLD STILL
**Mode:** identity/rename continuity page
**Function:** teach reader how identity works.

Owners/inhabitants persist while team names may change.

Use only provenance-locked examples.

Primary verified contemporary example:
Austin Byars → The Belt Keeper → His Majesty's Blood.

Historical aliases may appear only where provenance is sufficient and typography is QA'd.

Kevin Zeek → Red Leopards remains current identity resolution.

Do not make this a database diagram. Translate continuity into literature and artifacts: crossed-out headings, rewritten labels, same figure in different records.

Core doctrine:
**Owners own characters. Teams own names.**

The sentence itself need not appear verbatim if literature communicates it better.

Target emotion: recognition.

---

## P0.4 — THE BELT
**Mode:** object portrait / sparse prose
**Function:** continuity object.

The Championship Belt is shown as a surviving object with wear, scratches, leather, weight and accumulated attention.

Allowed:
- it has history;
- it has been carried;
- people attach meaning to it;
- current custody with Belt Keeper where temporally appropriate.

Not allowed without evidence:
- magical powers;
- fabricated lineage;
- fabricated holders;
- fabricated dates;
- invented origin.

Target emotion: weight.

---

## P0.5 — WHAT DOES NOT SURVIVE CLEANLY
**Mode:** fragmented page / damaged evidence
**Function:** uncertainty.

A receipt missing a corner.
An image without a date.
A score with context absent.
A map fragment whose borders are not treated as political fact.
A handwritten claim that is clearly presented as a claim rather than narrator truth.

This is where the reader learns that the Chronicle can contain ambiguity without becoming unreliable.

Target emotion: mystery.

---

## P0.6 — TWELVE
**Mode:** widening-world page
**Function:** scale.

The world is inhabited by twelve principal competitors in the 2026 League.

Do not turn this into twelve character cards.

Use silhouettes, belongings, traces, environments, arrivals or twelve spaces awaiting names.

The characters themselves will be properly introduced through Chapter I.

Target emotion: anticipation.

---

## P0.7 — THE NEW VOLUME
**Mode:** transition / physical book page
**Function:** reset.

Old archive recedes.

A new 2026 volume is removed/opened.
Fresh paper contrasts with damaged archive material.

The past exists.
The current season remains unwritten.

Target emotion: silence.

---

## P0.8 — THE CLEAN PAGE
**Mode:** minimal, high-whitespace page
**Function:** threshold.

Fresh 2026 ledger.
Twelve empty entry spaces.
No wins.
No losses.
No Week 1 scores.

Primary prose:

“History had already happened.

None of it could fill the empty columns.

The new book required new evidence.”

Page turn:
**CHAPTER I — THE FIRST RECEIPTS**

Opening:
“There were twelve of them when the books opened.”

Target emotion: beginning.

# OPTIONAL EXPANSION PAGES 9–12

Only add if earned.

Potential uses:
- a verified prior-season fragment that materially deepens 2026;
- an artifact spread requiring physical space;
- a silent two-page Archive panorama;
- a verified rivalry/history fragment whose omission weakens Chapter I;
- an intentional blank/near-blank transition.

Do NOT use optional pages for generic lore.

# VISUAL RHYTHM

Recommended:
P1 painting/prose
→ P2 artifact/prose
→ P3 identity
→ P4 object portrait
→ P5 fragmented evidence
→ P6 world widening
→ P7 quiet transition
→ P8 near-silent clean page

The Prologue should become quieter as it approaches Chapter I.

# TRUTH GATE

Every legible:
- name;
- year;
- championship count;
- score;
- record;
- alias;
- holder;
- inscription presented as factual

must be provenance-supported or removed/obscured.

Atmosphere may be invented.
Historical claims may not.

# FINAL TEST

Remove every proper noun.

If the Prologue still creates:
- age;
- evidence;
- imperfect memory;
- identity continuity;
- anticipation;
- and the sensation of opening a new volume,

the structure works.

Then restore only the proper nouns the evidence can carry.
