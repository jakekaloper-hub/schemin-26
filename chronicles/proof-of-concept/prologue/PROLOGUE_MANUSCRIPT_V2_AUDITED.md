## I. BEFORE THIS BOOK

Before this book, there were other books.

Some survived cleanly.

Most did not.

They lay where men had left them: stacked beneath newer ledgers, folded into drawers, tied with cord gone stiff with age. Loose pages protruded from cracked bindings. Receipts slept between records that had once seemed important enough to preserve properly. Ink had browned. Corners had vanished.

There were photographs without dates.

Dates without photographs.

Scores without the arguments that followed them.

Arguments without surviving proof of what began them.

And everywhere there were names.

The evidence did not survive because the League was orderly.

It survived because someone, somewhere, refused to throw something away.

---

## II. WHAT THE PAPER REMEMBERS

Paper remembers what was written upon it.

It is less reliable about everything else.

A number can remain after the room that argued over it has emptied.

A mark can survive the hand that made it.

A receipt can outlive the joke, insult, wager or accusation that caused somebody to keep it.

In the margins, other voices waited.

**Should have known.**

**Never again.**

**He got lucky.**

**Ask him what happened.**

No signatures.

No explanations.

The Archive did not correct them.

It kept the claims beside the cleaner records and allowed the disagreement to survive.

First there was what happened.

Then what was written.

Then what people said the writing meant.

Given enough time, all three could occupy the same drawer.

---

## III. NAMES DO NOT HOLD STILL

Names were less durable than inhabitants.

A line could be struck.

Another could be written.

The figure beneath it remained.

By the opening of this volume, Austin Byars was entered under **His Majesty's Blood**.

The inhabitant was the Belt Keeper.

The distinction mattered.

A changed heading could not erase what the same figure had carried into the next page: reputation, possessions, relationships, consequences.

Elsewhere, Kevin Zeek remained beneath the name **Red Leopards**, requiring no such correction from the clerk.

The Archive learned to follow the inhabitant first and the heading second.

Names could move.

Consequences were more stubborn.

---

## IV. THE BELT

Some objects were easier to follow than names.

The Belt was one of them.

Its metal carried scratches. Its leather remembered use. Light caught differently on the raised edges than in the worn places between them.

Men had looked at it often enough to make the object larger in conversation than it was on a table.

The surviving evidence could answer some questions about it.

Not all.

It did not explain an origin.

It did not require magic.

It did not settle every argument people attached to it.

The object survived the explanations.

At the opening of the 2026 volume, the Belt remained with the Belt Keeper.

For the new book, that was enough.

---

## V. WHAT DOES NOT SURVIVE CLEANLY

One receipt had lost its lower corner.

An image had no date.

A page carried figures without the conversation that followed them.

A hand-drawn map showed a road reaching a mountain and nothing certain beyond the edge of the paper.

Someone had written **LIAR** across a note in darker ink.

Someone else had underlined the word twice.

The Archive kept both marks.

There was no virtue in pretending damaged evidence was whole.

A blank could be more truthful than an invention.

A question mark could preserve more history than an answer supplied too late.

Where the record spoke clearly, the Chronicle could speak clearly.

Where memory and record disagreed, the disagreement belonged to the story.

Where neither survived—

the page would remain open.

---

## VI. TWELVE

The new volume had twelve places waiting inside it.

Twelve inhabitants would carry whatever already belonged to them into encounters that had not happened yet.

Some could be recognized by what traveled with them.

A green blade.

A great rune-marked sword.

A briefcase.

A hammer.

Golf equipment.

A chain.

An axe and a tankard.

A steaming bowl.

Claws.

Dreadlocks.

Weather given shape.

A golden retriever who cared very little about records.

The Archive could preserve what had already been established.

It could not tell any of them what would happen next.

That belonged to the empty book.

---

## VII. THE NEW VOLUME

The new book made almost no sound when it was placed on the table.

After the cracked leather and swollen paper around it, the cover looked indecently clean.

No spilled drink had reached it.

No angry hand had folded a corner.

No score had been corrected in the margin.

No one had written **never again** and then done it again the following week.

The clasp opened.

Fresh paper waited beneath it.

The old books remained on the shelves.

They did not disappear because a new one had arrived.

Neither did they get to write in it.

A pen was placed beside the first page.

For a while, nobody touched it.

---

## VIII. THE CLEAN PAGE

At the front of the volume:

**2026**

Below it waited twelve empty entries.

No victories.

No defeats.

No receipts.

History had already happened.

None of it could fill the empty columns.

The new book required new evidence.

The page turned.

**CHAPTER I — THE FIRST RECEIPTS**

There were twelve of them when the books opened.
