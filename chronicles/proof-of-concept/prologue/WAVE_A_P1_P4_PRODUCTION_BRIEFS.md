## Opening Grammar Gate

# GLOBAL MATERIAL LANGUAGE

- premium illustrated fantasy chronicle;
- tactile uncoated paper / aged archive materiality;
- painterly cinematic environments;
- physical evidence integrated into composition;
- serif literary typography applied in composition, not trusted to image generation;
- no black UI panels;
- no stat-card design;
- no generic fantasy parchment overload;
- visual hierarchy must remain legible on phone.

The opening should feel discovered rather than designed.

---

# P1–P2 — BEFORE THIS BOOK

## Narrative job
Convince the reader that the Chronicle begins inside a history older than the volume they are holding.

## Composition
Two-page cinematic archive spread.

Foreground:
- cracked ledger corner;
- loose receipt edges;
- cord;
- dust;
- damaged photograph back;
- page fibers.

Midground:
- open old volume catching low lamplight;
- shelves with inconsistent bindings;
- evidence stored imperfectly.

Background:
- archive recedes into darkness;
- hint of architecture beyond, but no character silhouette commanding frame.

## Text allocation
Left/opening:
**BEFORE THIS BOOK, THERE WERE OTHER BOOKS.**

Then selected opening manuscript paragraphs in controlled blocks.

Do not force entire Section I onto spread. Remaining Archive prose can flow P3.

## Image-generation rule
No legible factual text required inside generated art.
Books/receipts may contain marks but not invented readable scores/names.

## Emotional target
Reverence + uncertainty, not horror.

## Reject if
- a random wizard/archivist becomes protagonist;
- looks like generic Hogwarts library;
- obvious UI/card framing;
- every surface is sepia parchment;
- fake readable league facts appear;
- text area has insufficient contrast.

# P3 — WHAT PAPER KEEPS

## Narrative job
Teach evidence discipline through objects.

## Composition
Literary page with artifact constellation:
- bent photograph;
- receipt with stained total area;
- bookmaker notation;
- torn map corner;
- crossed-out name layer;
- underlined score shape without invented readable digits.

Objects overlap naturally but leave a stable prose column.

## Text
Continue Archive section through:
“History did not survive because the League was orderly.
It survived because someone, somewhere, refused to throw something away.”

Then transition into the rule about names.

## Typography
All meaningful text typeset externally.
Generated object marks decorative only.

## Emotional target
The reader starts examining the page rather than merely viewing it.

## Reject if
- scrapbook/collage aesthetic feels modern-craft rather than historical;
- objects are too clean;
- artifacts imply unsupported results;
- prose becomes illegible at mobile size.

---

# P4 — NAMES MOVE

## Narrative job
Establish the identity law before characters appear:
**owners/inhabitants persist; team names may change.**

## Composition
Quiet page.

A single archival record has layers of names struck/replaced over time, but exact historical aliases are added only through verified typesetting.

Behind/through paper:
subtle ghost of an unchanged silhouette/object—not a full identifiable character portrait.

Possible physical device:
same seal/owner mark recurring beside different name strips.

## Primary text
**NAMES MOVED.**
**CONSEQUENCES DID NOT MOVE SO EASILY.**

Supporting manuscript prose explains:
- books need labels;
- inhabitant remains;
- archive follows inhabitant first.

## Critical restraint
Do not use Byars or Arsenal rename examples visually unless exact temporal alias strings are verified for the composition.
The law can be established before examples.

## Emotional target
The world has rules of memory.

## Reject if
- it reads like database documentation;
- rename doctrine is explained as fantasy-football administration;
- visual implies a character physically transforms when name changes;
- decorative text becomes mistaken for authoritative names.

# WAVE A QA

Critic asks:
- Does this feel like the beginning of a real book?
- Does each page reveal something rather than repeat atmosphere?
- Is the Archive intriguing enough to support later returns?

Umpire asks:
- Any invented legible facts?
- Any premature character/event?
- Any unsupported map borders?
- Any generated typography being treated as authoritative?

Render QA asks:
- phone readability;
- gutter safety;
- text contrast;
- no critical detail lost at crop;
- consistent paper/material identity.

Closer passes Wave A only if P1–P4 establish a visual system strong enough that P5 can introduce real 2025 history without changing design languages.
