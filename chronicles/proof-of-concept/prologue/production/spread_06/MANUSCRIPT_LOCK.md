## Exact compositor manuscript

The richest starting hand in the League lived in a room that looked incapable of keeping inventory.

Jordan Hollingshead's place appeared to have survived a celebration, an equipment failure, and an argument over who had promised to clean up, all without deciding which event had happened first.

A cooler stood open.

A metal bat leaned against a scarred table.

Battered football armor occupied a chair that had probably not been intended to hold armor.

The pit bull slept beneath everything with the confidence of an animal that knew falling objects had somehow always missed him.

The enormous horned figure moved through the disorder without disturbing it.

That was the first clue.

The second was that nothing useful was ever more than an arm's reach away.

Draft night had been generous to Slob on my Dobb.

Two first-round selections.

Additional premium capital.

Quinshon Judkins retained at the eighth.

Bucky Irving at the fourteenth.

On the Archive's copied board, more early seals bore Slob's mark than any other hand could ignore.

The room called it normal.

Slob lifted the bat, checked its weight, set it back in precisely the same place and reached past three empty cans to retrieve the one paper he wanted without looking.

Chaos, it turned out, could have an address system.

The League had another word for abundance.

Expectation.

A poor hand could explain itself.

A rich one eventually had to answer questions.

Across Schemin, another inhabitant understood that problem in a cleaner room.

## VIII. THE ACQUISITION

In the dark boardroom of The LLC, the chairs were empty and the screens had gone black.

David Babb remained.

His suit was still exact. The cigar had burned down far enough to make the hour obvious. Gold caught the low light at his wrist while the open LLC briefcase sat beside a sequence of papers marked, signed, rearranged and marked again.

The draft had given him no shortage of opportunity.

Omarion Hampton had already been controlled at the second.

Colston Loveland at the ninth.

Multiple early selections gave The LLC one of the deeper premium inventories in the room, and Jonathan Taylor joined the holdings near the beginning of the draft.

By the time the room emptied, the early rights had become names and the names had become holdings.

David closed one folder.

Then another.

The League sometimes mistook abundance for comfort.

Men who had never controlled much imagined that having options made decisions easier.

The LLC understood the opposite.

Every additional option created another version of the future against which the chosen one could be audited.

Every acquisition created an opportunity cost.

Every closed deal became evidence.

The briefcase shut with a small, expensive sound.

There was no applause.

There was only the knowledge that capital had been converted and would eventually be judged by consequences rather than intent.

Slob's abundance looked like disorder.

The LLC's abundance looked like control.

The new ledger would not care about the difference.
