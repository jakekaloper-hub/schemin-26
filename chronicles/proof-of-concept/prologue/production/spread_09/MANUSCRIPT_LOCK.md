## Exact compositor manuscript

The Arsenal Centaur sharpened his axe while the ale went warm.

Wilson Look had noticed the ale going warm.

He simply considered the axe more important.

His equine body filled much of the mountain shelter, Arsenal-red cloth and armor catching the afternoon. One hand worked the stone down the ornate edge. Nearby, a route had been marked across a piece of hide and weighted flat beneath the tankard he was neglecting.

Bijan Robinson had been retained at the first.

Breece Hall at the fourth.

The rest of the draft had not begun with the extravagant advantages held elsewhere.

By night's end, the hand on the table looked fuller than the one with which he had entered.

That interested him.

A philosophy was easy to admire before contact.

The useful question was whether it survived contact.

He lifted the axe, examined the edge, and returned to the stone.

The ale surrendered.

Elsewhere, the archive had already corrected his name in more than one place.

Names moved.

The body did not.

Whatever banner appeared above Wilson Look's line, the inhabitant beneath it remained the Arsenal Centaur.

The rule sounded obvious once written.

History had taught the clerks not to trust obvious things until they were written down.

## XIV. THE MIDDLE OF THE ROAD

Before dawn, at a truck stop whose sign had lost two letters, Ben Whipple repaired a table.

This was not metaphor.

The table was broken.

He had a hammer.

The problem therefore possessed an obvious solution.

A raccoon watched from the tailgate as Ben drove the last nail, his dirty white tank already carrying evidence of breakfast smoke. The CHIN UP SHUT UP cap sat low over his brow. A forty-ounce bottle waited nearby with no urgency at all.

No surviving sheet on the Archive table gave the clerk permission to make Ben richer or poorer than the evidence allowed.

So the space beside his name remained partly blank.

The raccoon did not seem troubled by this.

Ben struck the nail once more than necessary.

The table held.

Grand histories had a habit of overlooking men in the middle until the middle moved.

A throne announced itself.

A disaster announced itself.

A man fixing a table before sunrise could disappear between them if the chronicler believed significance always arrived dressed for ceremony.

The raccoon climbed onto the repaired table.

It held him too.

Ben grunted.

This seemed to satisfy everyone involved.
