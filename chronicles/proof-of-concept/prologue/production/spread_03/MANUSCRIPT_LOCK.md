## Exact compositor manuscript

Austin Byars did not sit upon a throne.

There was no crown.

The distinction mattered.

Stories had a habit of decorating champions until the decoration replaced the man. The archive had done it before. A victory became a coronation. A custodian became a king. A reputation acquired ornaments nobody had actually worn.

The Belt Keeper required none of them.

He stood beside a narrow table in black rune-worked armor while a cloth moved slowly over the metal at his waist. Long black hair fell around an ashen face. Pale eyes reflected the fire without seeming warmed by it. Against the wall rested a sword large enough to make ceremony unnecessary, its runes catching the light one mark at a time.

He polished the Belt.

It did not need polishing.

That was not the point.

Possession created maintenance.

Across the room, another table carried the remains of draft night: names, rounds, selections, keeper costs, and the evidence of opportunities already spent before the first competitive record of 2026 existed.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba had been retained at the cost of the second.

De'Von Achane at the fifteenth.

One cost looked ordinary beside the market of the room. The other looked like theft committed politely enough to remain legal.

Yet the champion had entered draft night without a live first-round selection.

That fact sat beside the Belt without contradiction.

The League's economy was full of such things.

A man could be richest in history and poorer in immediate opportunity.

He could carry the only object everyone recognized as proof of the previous year and still watch others choose before he did.

He could own a championship and possess no lead.

That was the insult built into every new season.

The Belt Keeper fastened the buckle.

Outside, something struck the fortress bell once.

Not for him.

For the opening of the year.

The sound did not need to cross the whole of Schemin. The Archive had already sent the same black-edged notice along every road it knew: the choosing would begin at the appointed hour. Copies lay beneath a tankard, beside a golf bag, under a corporate seal, pinned near three empty hooks, and folded into the Trade Jedi's mountain papers.

Across Schemin, twelve different preparations were ending at roughly the same time, and history had arranged them unequally.

The champion carried metal.

The runner-up carried proximity.

Third place carried scarcity.

A duck carried four-tenths.

Others carried abundance, expectation, expensive commitments, visible strength, uncertain direction, doctrine, work, fire.

None of them carried a 2026 victory.

That would have to be earned after the book opened.
