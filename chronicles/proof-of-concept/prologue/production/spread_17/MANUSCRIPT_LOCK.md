This was not a criticism.

Without such reduction, the shelves would have become useless.

But the clerk who prepared the 2026 volume had spent enough nights among those shelves to know what the rows left out.

A spreadsheet could show that one inhabitant had two first-round opportunities and another had none.

It could not show the difference between abundance and waiting.

A keeper ledger could show Achane in the fifteenth.

It could not show the Belt above the same table.

A postseason line could show four-tenths.

It could not show Duckhook's club hovering behind a ball trapped beside black water.

A final standing could show second.

It could not show a strategist moving a marker and moving it back.

The clerk closed the narrow ledger and looked down the aisle at everything that would never fit inside it: the dented cabinet, the red thread, the obsolete forms, the water-swollen books.

Beside the ledger waited the larger volume.

The Chronicle.

The clerk laid the draft record beside the new book.

For the first time, the blank pages no longer looked empty.

They looked defended.

Twelve histories stood behind them.

That was different.
