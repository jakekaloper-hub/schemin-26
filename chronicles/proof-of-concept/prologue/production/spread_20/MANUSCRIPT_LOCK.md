The knowledge that every choice one inhabitant made could eventually become another inhabitant's problem.

The page knew none of their endings.

That was its great advantage.

It did not care who had been forecast first.

It did not care who had been forecast last.

It did not care that one man carried the Belt or another carried the richest board or another had waited through three premium rounds.

The page had no favorites.

It had columns.

At the top, in fresh ink, the Chronicle wrote:

CHAPTER I

THE FIRST RECEIPTS

Then the clerk set down the pen.

Somewhere beyond the archive, twelve roads were already moving toward one another.

The first evidence had not yet arrived.

But it was coming.
