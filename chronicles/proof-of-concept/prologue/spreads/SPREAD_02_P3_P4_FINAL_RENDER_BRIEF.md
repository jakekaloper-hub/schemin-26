# Spread story

The reader has just seen the broad Chronicle world. Now the camera moves physically inward.

P3 examines what survives on paper.
P4 teaches the first law of Chronicle identity: names can change; the inhabitant does not.

The spread must feel like the **next turn of the exact same book** as approved P1–P2.

# P3 — WHAT PAPER KEEPS

Visual:
- archive table or slanted desk in the same mountain/archive civilization;
- candle/lamplight and cool exterior atmosphere inherited from benchmark;
- bent photograph backs;
- weathered receipt edges;
- bound ledgers;
- bookmaker-like notation marks that remain illegible;
- damaged page corners;
- seal/wax/cord;
- partial old map fragment without readable unsupported borders;
- depth beyond desk into archive shelves/window/world.

Literary text is NOT generated into art.
Reserve a stable high-contrast prose field.

Authoritative heading, applied later:
**WHAT PAPER KEEPS**

Core manuscript passage for composition:
“Some survived cleanly. Most did not.

They lay beneath newer ledgers and inside drawers whose keys had changed hands too many times. Loose pages leaned from cracked bindings. Receipts slept between records that had once seemed important enough to preserve properly. Ink browned. Corners disappeared.

There were photographs without dates.
Dates without photographs.
Scores without the arguments that followed them.
Arguments without any surviving proof of what had begun them.

History did not survive because the League was orderly.
It survived because someone, somewhere, refused to throw something away.”

# P4 — NAMES MOVE

- subtle unchanged silhouette motif behind layers, deliberately NON-IDENTIFIABLE;
- same gold/black/blue/orange material language as benchmark;
- visual exit toward darker archive corridor/page-turn edge.

Authoritative heading:
**NAMES MOVE**

Primary line:
**Names moved. Consequences did not move so easily.**

Supporting prose:
“A man might return beneath another banner. A joke might become a title. A title might become an embarrassment and disappear before the next winter. The books obeyed the changes because books had to call a thing something.

But the inhabitant remained.

The archive learned to follow the inhabitant first.

That was how old debts found new doors.

And how old victories did too.”
