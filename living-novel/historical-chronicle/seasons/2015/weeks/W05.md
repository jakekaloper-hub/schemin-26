# 2015 — Week 5: What the Victors Remember

Historical principal-POV owner bindings and Atlas locations are not yet certified. These six prose treatments are preliminary chronicle writing, not approved novel chapters.

## 1. The Cost of Rebuilding
*Scruffy Looking Nerf Herders 69 — Kunt Liquors 87. Verified victor: Kunt Liquors.*

Kunt Liquors defeated Scruffy Looking Nerf Herders by eighteen. The victory followed its crushing Week Four loss, a reminder that the chronicle is not a smooth ascent for any contender. For the Scruffy Looking Nerf Herders, a second consecutive defeat cuts against the recovery they had briefly achieved. What would they do differently if they could not dismiss this as a single poor day? Fiction earns its weight when a character chooses something irreversible in response, but the choice must come from verified characterization rather than the writer's convenience.

## 2. A Line Pulled Tight
*Bavid Dabbs 78 — Hook, Line, and Zeeker 95.5. Verified victor: Hook, Line, and Zeeker.*

Hook, Line, and Zeeker overcame Bavid Dabbs by seventeen and a half. The two histories had already oscillated between proud claims and discouraging replies. The encounter's meaningful dramatic question is whether a victorious side can resist translating its success into a story of permanent superiority. Bavid Dabbs must continue after another setback. Beyond the Contest, one could explore how the same setback affects a public oath or a household expectation, once institutional and territorial canon can bear such invention.

## 3. The Advantage Continues
*I'M HERE SO I WON'T GET FINED 59 — Here, Ok I took L's 75.5. Verified victor: Here, Ok I took L's.*

Here, Ok I took L's defeated I'M HERE SO I WON'T GET FINED by sixteen and a half. Five opening victories form the beginnings of a public story, but not the end of one. The author should show admiration turning slowly toward resentment, or confidence turning into isolation, if character evidence supports those trajectories. The defeated side has a different problem: repeated losses can make every new confrontation feel like a verdict delivered in advance. The result settles this week's contest, not its contestants' worth.

## 4. The Crown's First Shadow
*Cypress Gunslingers 117.5 — Discount Belichick 103. Verified victor: Cypress Gunslingers.*

Cypress Gunslingers defeated Discount Belichick by fourteen and a half. After the fourth week's narrow return, the future champion fell again. That sequence resists triumphalist history. It asks for a character whose eventual victory cannot simply be announced, and an opponent whose success must be taken seriously on its own terms. A useful fictional scene might end not with the defeat itself but with the choice that follows it. That choice has not been established by these records, and should remain open until the Author Room has the historical principal's continuity packet.

## 5. A Contender Makes Distance
*Austin's Hyena's 135.5 — Pittsy's Rhino's 103.5. Verified victor: Austin's Hyena's.*

Austin's Hyena's prevailed over Pittsy's Rhino's by thirty-two. The future finalist now has a more emphatic success after a difficult stretch. The danger for the prose is to treat this week's defeated party as disposable. Pittsy's Rhino's had previously dealt the largest blow of Week Three; its history deserves texture, not a simple downward turn. The winner's accomplishment can stand while the other's agency survives. Epic fiction depends on a world in which twelve lives keep moving even when the current chapter belongs elsewhere.

## 6. The Storm Has Not Been Named
*Team Hollingshead 80 — Calvin's Johnson 98. Verified victor: Calvin's Johnson.*

Calvin's Johnson defeated Team Hollingshead by eighteen. It was another result in a sequence of changing fortunes, and the closing encounter of a week in which the future champion had lost yet again. The author might be tempted to invoke distant omens or personify disasters in order to manufacture cohesion. Better cohesion comes from the living world: what different peoples believe power means, whom they trust, what memory makes them afraid to risk. Those relationships are to be documented, not invented as settled canon. The result belongs to Calvin's Johnson; the history belongs to everyone.

## The Week Beyond the Contest

The six judgments did not reach the Commonwealth as one neatly arranged revelation. Each would be known first by those nearest its consequence, and only later by others whose memories and interests made its meaning different. A winner would be celebrated somewhere and distrusted elsewhere; a defeat might draw compassion from a stranger and ambition from a neighbor. Those social relationships cannot be minted from a results table. The next creative revision must establish whose knowledge crossed what border, what pressure moved between the six independent encounters, and which of the twelve canonical perspectives could carry that change without foreknowledge. Until then, this passage preserves the shared consequence: reputations have changed, but no power has learned the whole truth of the year.
