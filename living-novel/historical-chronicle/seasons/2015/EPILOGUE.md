# 2015 — The Crown and Its Shadow
*Commonwealth Historical Chronicle — Season epilogue, first literary draft*

A victory is most dangerous on the morning after it.

Before that morning, everyone understands the nature of the contest. There is an adversary to resist, a result not yet settled, an argument that can be answered by endurance. The season has asked its question, again and again, until it has narrowed the field and brought two surviving contenders toward a final reckoning.

Discount Belichick was one. Austin's Hyena's was the other.

The ending is beyond dispute in the surviving record: two hundred eleven and a half to one hundred eighty-seven. Yet the ending of a contest is not the ending of a history. It may not even be the ending of a quarrel. What changes when one claimant has prevailed is the language available to everybody else. Old decisions may be called necessary; a lucky escape can be retold as discipline; an opponent who once seemed frightening becomes, in hindsight, an obstacle overcome. The chronicle must resist the temptation to believe those explanations merely because the outcome has made them convenient.

The victorious name would become the first championship name in the recoverable history of the League of Thrones. That is a claim of evidence, not of cosmic precedence. The Commonwealth had already been inhabited, governed, fought over, repaired and remembered before this surviving archive opened. The champion did not create its people, rivers, trade, or old griefs.

In any living polity, authority meets its test after the heralds fall silent. A rival defeated is still a rival with memories. A follower rewarded may become a follower dissatisfied. A decision which looked simple while pressure was mounting can look cruel when somebody must pay its price in daylight.

Those are the questions that belong to the next telling, once the canonical identities of the historical participants and the actual territory of their stories are established. The surviving scores cannot answer them, and an author must not smuggle invented answers into the evidence file.

There would be a next season. The record already holds another champion's name for 2016, but none of the figures living inside the last day of 2015 could know what that later history would choose to preserve. What they had was a result, the exhaustion of reaching it, and an entire world still demanding to be lived in.

The crown was won.

The living story, unlike the contest, did not end.
