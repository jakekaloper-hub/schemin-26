## A place for the table

The registrar set a little book in front of Jake and told him not to open it.

There were two books now. The larger one accounted for accepted supplies, debts and the names of people who had explicitly permitted some public record. The smaller held directions given privately by those trying to find safe places to sleep away from the dangerous eastern side of the shelter. The latter was held shut by a belt that had belonged to the registrar long before the shelter needed it.

Jake had helped make the first book. He had not known the second existed.

"We cannot keep telling anyone who asks where the others went," the registrar said. "Leaving us is not giving us ownership of their destination."

He wanted to protest that names kept in darkness could be lost. She showed him the first blank entry: an individual had departed, a possible lodging had been mentioned, but no arrival was confirmed and no permission to disclose an address had been given. Turning that into a finished story would be another way of losing the person.

Beyond the window the carpenter was examining the marked post. The wider cordon remained. The wall had not collapsed, but nothing had certified it safe. A separate market awning had been returned after its bounded loan; the earlier three-night canvas had long since gone home. The grain stood on tested supports outside the damp room, and the door removed from the eastern room lay on blocks where no one could mistake it for a brace.

The girl with the leaf-shaped key came in carrying a panel from a broken cupboard. She wished to use its back for a new path notice. Her old drawing had included circles for people who had left. She now wanted to remove those circles because strangers had begun asking whose departures they recorded.

"I drew a way to walk," she said, "not a way to find people who don't want to be found."

Jake heard the registrar give a short laugh. She asked the girl to obtain the carpenter's approval before displaying another path. It was not an official map or a certificate of safety.

They carried the long table into the daylight yard. Its feet would not stand level until the woman with the wrapped hand chose a better patch and the carpenter checked the approach. Jake cut shims from a scrap board. He took care to mark each piece so the children would not mistake it for firewood.

The pot sat straight at last. Nobody cheered. They had made a table useful without claiming to have rebuilt a house.

## I — A gift with a condition

*Fixture A — archived result: Hook, Line, and Zeeker (ID 2) 120.06 — The Real Austin's Hyenas (ID 12) 129.18. Winner: The Real Austin's Hyenas (ID 12). Margin: 9.12.*

A traveler brought seven straight nails and two pieces of wood to the yard. He offered them for the new path notice, then asked whether the shelter had a public register of those who had supplied its needs.

The woman with the wrapped hand counted the nails. The carpenter inspected the wood and rejected one board because its split ran the full length of the grain. The other would carry the path notice, but only if the traveler understood that an offered board did not buy a permanent place for his name above the gate.

"Must kindness disappear?" the traveler asked.

The registrar gave him back the nails. "Not at all. But you decide whether your gift has that condition. We should not accept it and pretend afterward that it was unconditional."

Jake watched the man pick the nails up again. He said he had given timber in other places and could no longer find anyone who remembered. His voice was not loud, but he spoke to the registrar rather than the spectators. The woman with the wrapped hand asked him whether being remembered by a person would suffice in place of a public plaque. He seemed surprised by the distinction.

On the nearby board hung the week's first competitive result: The Real Austin's Hyenas defeated Hook, Line, and Zeeker 129.18 to 120.06. The margin was 9.12. Somebody thought the team name was a register of the people who kept the yard. Another recalled a team called Austin's Hyena's. Jake corrected the narrow factual error: historical ID 12, **The Real Austin's Hyenas**, and historical ID 8, **Austin's Hyena's**, were different scoring slots. The names did not establish ownership of the wood, the land, or either actual human participant.

The traveler gave the usable board and nails without a condition and asked the registrar to record that decision accurately. She did. Before leaving he told the girl his name. She repeated it once, carefully, to show that she had heard. Jake could not know whether she would remember it years afterward. The man seemed content not to demand the future from her.

The girl mounted her revised path notice where visitors could read it without standing under the unsafe wall. She had drawn no houses, no private addresses and no circles naming those absent. There was an arrow for the permitted path and a warning that the carpenter might move the cordon. The traveler looked at the board on his way out, then carried the rejected split piece of wood away.

What the yard had gained could be counted. The name they had not displayed remained the man's to give or keep.

## II — The wheelwright's other work

*Fixture B — archived result: Here, Ok I took L's (ID 5) 73.24 — Pittsy's Rhino's (ID 6) 95.88. Winner: Pittsy's Rhino's (ID 6). Margin: 22.64.*

Jake visited the wheelwright's yard with a rejected transcription of the young worker's old timber claim folded inside his coat. He had brought it back because the worker had objected to a word that implied agreement. The correction was not a favor; the registrar ought never to have recorded consent she could not prove.

The young worker was kneeling beside a low timber stand. A wooden axle lay across it while the wheelwright's daughter inspected a bearing. There was a mark on one support, and the worker checked it with a straightedge twice before lifting his tool.

"You've come about the frame," he said.

"Not this time."

Jake handed over the transcription. The young man read it, put one finger over the disputed word, then struck that word through. He said he would write his own response when the registrar returned the corrected copy. He did not waive the old claim. He had been paid for newer work, but money earned elsewhere could not settle labor done for the shelter.

The daughter examined the carpenter's proposed freestanding retrieval frame, which Jake had carried only as a drawing. She shook her head. The shop could quote for measuring suitable timber, but could not promise a safe design on ground the shelter had not surveyed. The low stand used inside her yard was not a model for lifting a chest through an unsafe doorway. Its supports rested on known ground and carried a known object; the other structure had neither advantage.

The second contest notice hung by the shop accounts: Pittsy's Rhino's had beaten Here, Ok I took L's 95.88 to 73.24, a margin of 22.64. Nobody in the shop argued about the winner. The worker did not turn the number into a metaphor for his unpaid history. He had an axle to finish and a wage attached to the task.

The daughter wrote the proposed price for measurement once more, specifying that it was an **unaccepted quotation**, not an invoice, construction authorization or safe-load certificate. Jake folded it separately from the worker's corrected claim. He thanked her without requesting free engineering time.

As he left he saw the great wheel behind the yard, still motionless after its failed lifting experiment. The intended channel remained unexcavated. Yet around that stopped machine people repaired boots, measured joints and made useful small things. The younger worker went back to his bench without asking the shelter's leave.

Jake had crossed town to return a word to its owner. He had not returned with permission to use the worker's hands.

## III — A returned person is not a destination

*Fixture C — archived result: I'M HERE SO I WON'T GET FINED (ID 11) 132.60 — All ACL Squad (ID 9) 86.70. Winner: I'M HERE SO I WON'T GET FINED (ID 11). Margin: 45.90.*

The woman who had carried two wet blankets out of the yard when the cordon widened came back before midday. She wanted her cooking pot. The kitchen had moved it to a dry shelf, and nobody had used it since she left.

Jake recognized the knot in the cloth around the blankets. He almost told the registrar that one of the missing names could now be marked safely arrived. Then the woman said she had slept beneath a roof elsewhere but would not say whose. She had returned for property, not to provide an address.

The registrar asked whether the departure entry might note the woman's return in person. She agreed. She would not permit the destination to be named or inferred.

The girl heard a man at the gate asking which circle on her old drawing belonged to the woman. She took the drawing down and replaced it with the path-only version. The carpenter confirmed its permitted route, and the registrar kept arrival observations in her private book. No public mark linked a person's movements to a specific lodging.

A runner arrived with the third competitive sheet: I'M HERE SO I WON'T GET FINED 132.60, All ACL Squad 86.70, margin 45.90. Someone called out the first name as though a person bearing it should present himself. Jake told him the name identified a team in an archived scoring record, not a traveler seeking a roof.

The woman accepted a short piece of cord to repair her pot handle. She offered to bring a little food if she had any to spare on another day. The woman with the wrapped hand thanked her without entering an amount or a due date. They had heard a possibility, not received a commitment.

When the woman departed again, she used the safe path. Jake watched only until she passed the gate. She had left the shelter twice, returned once, and given no authority for the public to learn where she went. The registrar's book could say precisely that much.

The girl asked whether there was a symbol for a person who had been seen again but had not disclosed an address. The registrar said the private book needed words, not an invented house. The girl thought a while, then said she would leave the public sign alone.

Jake was struck by how easily the new rule had survived its first test. Not because everyone agreed, but because the people keeping the records knew which question they had the right to answer.

## IV — The visitor who refused to certify

*Fixture D — archived result: Bavid Dabbs (ID 4) 75.84 — Team Hollingshead (ID 10) 132.26. Winner: Team Hollingshead (ID 10). Margin: 56.42.*

A visitor arrived with the carpenter, carrying an instrument wrapped in cloth. The carpenter said the person was experienced in inspecting structures but did not assert that any official credentials had been authenticated. The visitor offered a bounded observation from outside the marked danger. She would not cross the cord, inspect the buried footing by force, or certify the building safe merely because its wall still stood.

Jake hoped she might at least approve the proposed retrieval frame. She told him that a drawing could not support the weight of an unknown floor.

The carpenter showed her the measurements he had taken before and after the rain. His marks described the slight movement of an exterior post, but nobody had directly examined the hidden footing. The visitor compared the visible alignment with her straightedge, then asked how the earlier readings had been made. She listened to the carpenter's answer and wrote down which observations were his rather than hers.

A boy brought the fourth result while they worked: Team Hollingshead 132.26 over Bavid Dabbs 75.84, margin 56.42. Someone called it a collapse. The visitor looked sharply toward the wall until she understood the shout concerned a contest. She asked the boy not to use the same alarm near people working behind a safety line.

The carpenter repeated the warning. A scored defeat was not a falling beam. The public could celebrate or mourn the result without making those near the cord fear an accident had occurred.

After examining the visible joint, the visitor recommended that the wider exclusion remain. She offered a signed note of **limited visual observation**, not a safety certificate. Further footing investigation would require safe methods and suitable authority; no such investigation happened that day.

The woman whose chest remained inside the closed eastern room came to hear the visitor's findings. She read the note herself and said her earlier instruction against unauthorized retrieval still stood. The carpenter acknowledged her decision. No one suggested the newly arrived observer had conferred permission to move the chest or open its contents.

Jake felt his hope of a quick return diminish. The feeling was his; he did not assign it to the woman. She folded the note, made a copy with the registrar, and left with her key. The original chest key was still in her keeping, distinct from the leaf-shaped key the girl had found.

The visitor went away before dusk. The wall had not collapsed. Neither had it been made safe by the fact that someone competent had looked at it.

## V — The use of borrowed ground

*Fixture E — archived result: Scruffy Looking Nerf Herders (ID 1) 81.44 — Austin's Hyena's (ID 8) 103.86. Winner: Austin's Hyena's (ID 8). Margin: 22.42.*

The fifth result belonged to the provisional fictional Jake scoring lane. Scruffy Looking Nerf Herders, historical ID 1, scored 81.44 against Austin's Hyena's, ID 8, at 103.86. ID 8 won by 22.42. This archival result did not provider-authenticate the actual 2016 human owner of ID 1.

Jake read it while a landholder explained that the long table must be moved before evening. The table could stand on the level patch during daylight, she said, but it obstructed the cart passage after dark. She had permitted walking, not an indefinite camp or a claim to her ground.

He began to list the people who needed shelter. The woman with the wrapped hand interrupted. She had not asked him to bargain for a right to sleep there, and the landholder had not offered one. The day kitchen could be managed within the limit if the path remained clear and the pot was carried back in time.

The girl showed the landholder her revised path notice. It contained no houses or private names. The carpenter had confirmed a safe approach from the correct side of the cord. The landholder agreed to the daytime placement on those terms and made no promise of permanence.

A man who had hoped to sleep near the table protested. Jake could not give him a bed by declaring a losing contest unjust, nor could he turn a temporary permission into a law of property. The registrar offered to check whether the man had authorized contacts elsewhere. He refused and went out by the safe path. Nobody entered a false destination for him.

Jake took one end of the table when the light began to fail. The woman with the wrapped hand directed the movement; the girl gathered the shims. A competitive result could announce who had won without pretending anyone had won the right to another person's ground.

For a moment Jake resented carrying the table back. Then he understood that they had gained a workable daylight arrangement instead of a contested permanent occupation. Tomorrow they might set it out again, if they continued honoring the terms. It was not enough to house everyone. It was enough to keep one honest agreement alive.

## VI — The difference that did not vanish

*Fixture F — archived result: Kunt Liquors (ID 3) 100.48 — The Gunslingers (ID 7) 102.36. Winner: The Gunslingers (ID 7). Margin: 1.88.*

The final competitive notice came damp from the runner's hands. The registrar would not copy the blurred figures until he produced the clean original. Kunt Liquors had scored 100.48. The Gunslingers had scored 102.36. The margin was only 1.88, but the winner was definite within that contest. The registrar made the copy legible before placing it beside the other five.

Then the messenger who had carried the rumor of a hidden claimant returned. He asked whether the original sealed message had been delivered. It had not. The intended recipient had not been authenticated; nobody knew where the unidentified claimant was; the accusation that a person had been concealed in the shelter remained unsupported.

The messenger said his sender wanted a reply before the season's end. He could show no new authorization to receive the unopened message or any private address. The registrar offered a separate custody statement: she could attest that the message remained unopened and undelivered, but she would not name a recipient she had not found.

He agreed to carry that statement if she supplied a copy. She wrote it and asked him to sign for receiving **only the new statement**, not for custody of the original message. He signed, kept his copy, and left. The original remained locked away in the registrar's private box.

Jake watched the registrar put the duplicate receipt beneath the smaller book's belt. Nothing about the missing claimant had been resolved. Yet the messenger's return had produced a truthful communication that did not counterfeit an identification or surrender another person's privacy.

The girl mounted the corrected last result on the public board. It hung beside the carpenter's safety warning and the new path drawing. Jake looked from one to the next: a complete contest, a continuing hazard, and a route approved for present conditions. Three documents could all be true without any one authorizing the other.

The woman with the wrapped hand came to collect the lantern. She had kept a little broth for the worker who might come from the wheelwright's yard. Jake asked whether it should be recorded as a debt if he failed to appear.

She took the lantern from his hand.

"No," she said. "It's supper."

He stood watching the last light move across the long table's shims, now collected in a small cloth bag. She could offer a bowl without drafting a contract; the worker could refuse without making her gift invalid. Jake found that freedom harder to copy than any contest score.

## What a regular season could not finish

All **78 of 78** 2016 regular-season matchups now had a Chronicle narrative: thirteen weeks, six recorded contests each. The season's scoring record could be complete while the Commonwealth fiction remained open. The year had not ended, and neither had the stakes of the people living beyond the contest sheets.

The eastern room was still closed and the wider exterior-wall cordon still stood. A visiting observer had made a limited note, not a safety certification. The removed door still lay on blocks. The chest and its contents remained inside; its owner retained her separate key and her written refusal of unauthorized retrieval. The girl's unrelated found leaf-shaped key had not been claimed. The freestanding retrieval frame remained unbuilt and the wheelwright's quotation unaccepted.

The original canvas loan was long expired. The separate market awning had been returned. The displaced people had no invented guarantee of housing. The daytime table stood on revocable permission and was brought back before night. The registrar kept private destinations private. A woman had returned for a pot, not granted her whereabouts to public view.

The accepted-material invoice remained unpaid. The younger worker's older timber claim was still unresolved, though his separate paid employment continued. The great wheel remained still, its proposed channel unexcavated. The unverified witness had supplied no sworn proof. The unidentified claimant had not been found; the first sealed message remained unopened and undelivered. A **separate** custody statement had gone with the messenger, without proof that an intended recipient had received it.

Jake helped carry the table back to the dry space, careful not to let its uneven feet drag through mud. The girl fetched the shims. The carpenter checked the path. The woman with the wrapped hand set the last empty bowl on a shelf.

"Where tomorrow?" Jake asked.

She pointed toward the permitted daylight patch. Then she went to wash the bowl.

Tomorrow would require carrying the table again. He had once thought such repetition meant a promise had failed. Now he could see how much labor lay inside a promise small enough to keep.
