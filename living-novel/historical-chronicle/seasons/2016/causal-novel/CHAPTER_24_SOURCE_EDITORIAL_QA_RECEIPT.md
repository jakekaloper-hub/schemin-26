## Specific fictional changes

1. **Sola returns through her own initiative.** She does not ask Jake to rescue her or reinstate her residence; she offers paid reed-panel work after hearing that the household needs a privacy screen. She presents both original clogs still in her possession, visibly different from the unidentified object left by the previous night's stranger. That specific speculative attack inference is rejected without establishing the stranger's identity or the third clog's ownership.
2. **Paid craft is a transaction, not a sentimental cure.** Sola negotiates hours, material, money and the right not to sleep at the shelter. The carpenter, Tern, mother, cook and Esi each decide their own contribution, cost and limits. A **two-leaf movable privacy screen** is completed and paid for; the third leaf remains unpurchased. It affords practical screening without repairing the data breach or guaranteeing safety.
3. **Jake makes a future-facing decision.** One of Deren's stoneworks crew delivers an oral, incidental reminder of the previously offered seasonal work. Jake accepts the prospective work and commits to meet a freight cart in three mornings. The crew does not promise wages for idle days. Jake actually reallocates night-watch and serving responsibilities, and the remaining old pledge is neither forgiven nor pretended paid. Neither departure nor employment has occurred by the close of the chapter.
4. **Narrative counterpoint.** The shelter's children joke, apprentice badly and exercise curiosity; the cook defends kitchen resources; Len retains an imperfect carved bird and a serious impaired eye. Material life is not just a pretext for a moral ruling.
