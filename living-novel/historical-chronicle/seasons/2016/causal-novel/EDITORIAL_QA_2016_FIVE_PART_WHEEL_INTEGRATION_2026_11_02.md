## What has materially changed in the story

The shelter's doubtful grain was left isolated on temporary boards when the shed owner reclaimed the bay. In the new evening scene a visitor asks who has actual custody of the grain after people leave, exposing that Jake's sign “Kept at the wheelwright's yard” implies an authority or storage undertaking nobody gave. The woman conducting inspection retains responsibility only for the record, not food certification or storage. The wheelwright's daughter confirms only the overnight board loan and the next-dawn return. The visitor refuses to treat wording on a sign as proof the food is fit. Jake corrects the notice, becomes personally accountable for returning the boards, but acquires no authority to distribute the sacks. This forms a causal bridge from the earlier unauthorized singer broadside to the following morning's father/daughter authorship dispute: a name, a mark, or a drawing is not a reliable instrument merely by appearing official.

The reader sees at least three distinct refusals with genuine costs: the worker refuses to let the great wheel haul a load, the shed owner retains her space, and the daughter refuses to authorize unmeasured work under the family shop's name. Jake's contribution narrows from issuing solutions to performing limited tasks under another person's instructions. That distinction is the chapter's dramatic spine.
