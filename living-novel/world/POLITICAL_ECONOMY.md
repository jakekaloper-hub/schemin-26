# Political Economy — Foundation v1

Status: INTERPRETIVE CANON / STEP 3

## Foundational condition
No single ruler controls the principal subcontinent. Geography favors a plural political order: river powers, upland jurisdictions, chartered cities, marcher territories and maritime interests coexist and compete.

## Food and legitimacy
The central basin produces much of the transferable grain surplus. Political authority therefore depends not only on soldiers but on:
- harvest reliability;
- river access;
- storage;
- toll policy;
- merchant credit;
- ability to keep roads/bridges/passages open.

A powerful warlord can win campaigns and still fail to govern if food and credit collapse.

## Trade
Primary strategic flows:
- mountain metals/timber downward;
- basin grain outward;
- western livestock/horses toward cities and armies;
- eastern timber/fish/coastal goods;
- delta salt/fish/reeds and customs traffic;
- sea-borne luxury goods, dyes, glass, spices, manuscripts and specialty craft.

## Money and credit
Coin exists but large undertakings also depend on merchant credit, obligations, tax farming, stored grain and negotiated rights.
This permits characters such as powerful merchants, lenders, guild officers and administrators to matter without requiring magical wealth.

## Political forms
The setting supports several forms simultaneously:
- hereditary lordships;
- chartered/free cities;
- councils;
- landed estates;
- marcher commands;
- temple or memorial trusts;
- merchant leagues;
- military offices;
- crown-like dignities without universal sovereignty.

## Central tension
Power has at least four distinct currencies:
1. coercive force;
2. economic capacity;
3. institutional legitimacy;
4. reputation/allegiance.

A character can dominate one and remain weak in another.

This creates the conceptual space needed to translate the league's repeated distinction between regular-season dominance and championship legitimacy without copying sports mechanics literally.

## The Twelve
The Twelve will eventually occupy different combinations of these power currencies. Their current characters should influence how they exercise power, not determine why the economy exists.
