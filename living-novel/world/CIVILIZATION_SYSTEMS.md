# Civilization Systems — Foundation v1

Status: INTERPRETIVE CANON

## Social order
Status is layered rather than purely feudal. Birth matters, but so do land, military service, trade wealth, institutional office, scholarship, religious standing, craft mastery and reputation earned through the Contest.

This creates social mobility without making the society modern.

## Settlement hierarchy
- hamlets/villages: food production and local craft;
- market towns: weekly exchange, minor courts, storage;
- fortified towns: chokepoints, garrisons, regional administration;
- river cities: grain, tolls, archives, craft specialization;
- sea ports: long-distance trade and cosmopolitan exchange;
- sacred/memorial sites: authority disproportionate to population;
- frontier keeps: military/security functions.

## Law
Law is plural.
Local custom, lordly decree, city charter, merchant contract, institutional compact and religious obligation can overlap.
Jurisdictional conflict is therefore a normal political weapon.

## Information
No instant communication.
Information travels by:
- riders;
- river courier;
- coastal vessel;
- herald;
- merchant networks;
- institutional messengers;
- written correspondence;
- rumor.

False or delayed information can have real consequences.

## Literacy
Uneven but not rare among elites, clergy, merchants, officials and specialized crafts.
Archives matter because records are expensive to create, preserve and authenticate.

## War
War is costly and politically dangerous.
Forces can include household retainers, levies, professional companies, mounted troops, scouts, engineers, river/coastal forces and contracted specialists.
Campaigns are constrained by food, weather, roads, rivers and disease.

The Contest is not synonymous with war. Its sanctioned Encounters can prevent some disputes from escalating while creating new rivalries.

## Sport, feast and wager
Competitive culture exists beyond the Contest: hunts, races, martial games, strength contests, drinking games, marksmanship, riding and regionally specific sports.
Wagering is culturally important enough to create humor, debt, status and scandal without turning the world into a sportsbook parody.

## Ordinary life
The world must regularly show labor, meals, weather, repair, travel, markets, taverns, worship, harvest, animal care, bureaucracy, boredom and celebration.
Epic stakes gain weight only if ordinary life exists to be disrupted.
