# Belief and Myth — Foundation v1

Status: INTERPRETIVE CANON
Founder doctrine: original secondary-world religion/mythology; metaphysical truth remains uncertain.

## Core design
There is no universally verified theology.
Different cultures interpret the same world through overlapping traditions, local saints/heroes/ancestors, natural signs, philosophical schools, institutional rites and disputed sacred histories.

## Shared religious problem
Mortality, memory, fortune, obligation and legitimacy are central concerns.
People ask:
- What survives a person?
- Does fortune reveal favor or merely chance?
- Does victory prove worth?
- What do the living owe the dead?
- Can an oath bind more strongly than blood?
- Is memory a form of immortality?

These questions arise naturally from the memorial/championship culture without turning Michael Farrell into a deity.

## Memorial tradition
The dead may be publicly remembered through names, objects, recitations, preserved deeds and communal rites.
The Farrell Trophy can therefore sit naturally inside a broader culture of memorial stewardship while remaining unique.

Michael Farrell is NOT fictionalized as a god, saint, ghost or supernatural agent.

## Religious plurality
Possible traditions include:
- household/ancestor remembrance;
- river and harvest rites;
- mountain oath traditions;
- maritime votive customs;
- philosophical schools skeptical of divine intervention;
- institutional memorial orders;
- local cults/saints/heroes whose historical truth is disputed.

Specific denominations and names remain soft until regional cultures are finalized.

## Supernatural status
Ambiguous.
Coincidence, weather, dreams, unusual natural phenomena, prophecy claims and relic traditions may occur.
Narration should not casually certify divine causation.
If true magic is later introduced, it must pass the source/capability/limit/cost/knowledge/social/political test.

## Narrative rule
Characters may believe intensely. The novel need not confirm that they are correct.
