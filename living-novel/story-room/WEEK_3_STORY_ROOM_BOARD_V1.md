## Governing question
What changed in the world during Week 3?

## Thesis A — THE COST OF PROOF
**Argument:** By Week3, reputation is no longer free. Every claim now requires payment: DK proves unbeaten authority by eliminating the other 2-0 road; Red's $100 wager turns confidence into a literal stake; TDS spends heavily in the market without buying a win; Austin buys breathing room with a victory while simultaneously losing a season-long keeper resource; Chili's joke acquires a social cost for Duckhook.

**Strength:** unifies all six matchups through cost/resource/consequence rather than scoreboard.
**Risk:** can over-financialize emotional/world material.
**Best opening image:** Archive/market records accumulating receipts, bids, wagers and missing inventory.
**Best closing pressure:** the undefeated standard now has a price on it entering DK–ObiWan.

## Thesis B — WHAT WINNING DOES NOT FIX
**Argument:** Week3 exposes the limits of the weekly result. HMB wins but becomes weaker in season resources. Slob loses despite elite cumulative scoring. TDS's roster work is real despite defeat. Mud Dogs loses unbeaten status without becoming irrelevant. LLC wins without proving its whole strategy correct. Chili wins, but its deeper value is cultural consequence.

**Strength:** most mature literary thesis; directly advances Novel doctrine that weekly matchup is only collision point of season-long arcs.
**Risk:** less immediately epic; requires disciplined intercutting and strong physicalization.
**Best opening image:** six ledgers closing on Sunday with none telling the whole truth.
**Best closing pressure:** records are true, but incomplete.

## Thesis C — THE WORLD BEGINS TO REMEMBER
**Argument:** Three weeks create memory. The bridge anecdote returns as echo; chili becomes lore; $100 becomes receipt; the Belt's meaning changes under a 1-2 start; DK's standard becomes the only unbeaten one; Red's recovery becomes repeatable history rather than isolated response.

**Strength:** strongest bridge from Prologue's Archive/history argument into live-season literature; explains how jokes and results become mythology.
**Risk:** may over-promote memo-cultural material and underweight roster/resource mechanics.
**Best opening image:** Archive clerks discovering that this week's objects already require labels.
**Best closing pressure:** Week4 begins with people acting inside stories Week3 has now created.

## Bullpen debate
- **Literary Architecture:** favors B. It produces a novel chapter rather than a premium recap and allows contradiction.
- **Continuity:** favors B with C as secondary motif. C naturally connects to Prologue but must not let retrospective lore overwrite temporal truth.
- **Fantasy Evidence:** favors A/B hybrid because FAAB, keeper loss and records are unusually strong verified resource consequences.
- **World Building:** favors C as visual substrate, not governing thesis; objects/geography can show memory accumulating.
- **Pacing:** favors B because six matchup outcomes can intercut around contradiction instead of six equal sections.
- **Outsider Readability:** favors B; a reader need not understand fantasy mechanics to understand that victory does not repair every loss.
- **Editorial Restraint:** rejects a DK-centered “last undefeated” chapter thesis. That is cover-worthy Memo logic, not sufficient Novel logic.

## The Closer
**Recommended architecture: Thesis B as governing argument, Thesis C as the chapter's memory/world motif, Thesis A as the resource/economic substructure.**

Working literary proposition:
> **By the third week, winning and improving were no longer the same thing.**

This supports all twelve campaigns without pretending all twelve deserve equal page time.
