## Pilot A — What the road requires
**POV:** CHAR-WILSON-LOOK (D0nkey K0ng). **Proposed setting:** Highland Stronghold `LOC-DK-HIGHLAND-STRONGHOLD` / Upper Valleys, not a claim the Week 4 contest was hosted there. **Temporal position:** after verified W4 first defeat to ObiWan; before W5. **World texture:** road services, mountain-pass materials, wagons and road labor; inferred society rather than named new institutions.

The messenger had chosen a poor hour to arrive.

Below the stronghold, two wagons waited beside a broken stretch of upland road. A wheel lay apart from its axle. At the gate, a roadman argued that no loaded cart could make the turn without carving another rut deep enough to hold water.

Wilson knew the argument before the man had finished it. He had traveled that bend often enough to learn its weaknesses by the weight in his four legs. On wet days the road punished speed. On dry ones it punished certainty.

The message lay open in his hand. One hundred thirty-nine point six seven. On the facing line, one hundred fifty-seven point three three.

A week earlier men had called him undefeated. Now another number had rendered the word historical. No walls fell. No stone changed owners. The stronghold's roofs still held against the rain.

The wagon did not move.

His attendant approached with the ceremonial cloth folded over one arm. It had been brought out for an announcement, and now had nowhere sensible to go.

"Shall I send them down to the lower road?" the attendant asked.

Wilson looked at the waiting carts. To send them away would delay the supplies. To assign his own repair crews would draw labor from the preparations others expected of him. The expense would be ordinary. People were less forgiving of ordinary expenses than they were of a defeat.

"Give them the teams from the outer yard," he said.

"For the road?"

"For the road."

"They'll ask what it means."

Wilson returned the notice to its leather case. "They may ask."

He went down himself, the iron edges of his hooves testing the stable ground before the collapsed rut. The roadman stepped aside—not out of allegiance, Wilson thought, but to give the heavier body room to pass.

Together they took the load from the damaged cart. No one cheered when the first wheel cleared the bend.

It took effort to remain useful after a word like *unbeaten* had ceased to belong to you. The road had never needed the word.

**Editorial proof question:** Does the cost of reallocating labor make the loss more consequential without awarding/stripping literal territory? **No permanent repair transaction has been written to World Evolution.**

## Pilot B — The mark beside the causeway
**POV:** CHAR-BOBBY-MITCHELL (Mud Dogs). **Proposed setting:** `LOC-MUD-DOGS-SWAMP`, Southern Wetlands; any precise survey location is speculative until approved. **Temporal position:** after verified W5 LLC victory over Mud Dogs. **World texture:** raised access, wetland craft, workers, cargo and imperfect knowledge of a commercial proposition; no invented named surveyor/civic law.

The notice had survived the rain. That alone made Bobby suspicious of it.

Someone had fixed the paper above the causeway landing, high enough that the floodwater could not touch the seal. It said the road approaches were to be measured. It did not say by whose permission, only that a commercial party had requested the work.

His people had seen the mark before he did. The boat crews moved their nets farther from the landing. A carter stopped loading sacks. A woman who ferried goods along the side channel watched Bobby's approach with the patient hostility of someone who had already lost half a morning.

"Are they closing it?" she asked.

"They haven't said."

"That's not an answer."

"It's the one they sent."

He knew the raised ground beneath the timber, where the bank held and where it pretended to. To an outsider a straight causeway could look like a convenient line. To those who worked the water, the line was a bargain with mud that had to be renewed after every season.

News of the Contest had reached the landing first. The LLC's number stood larger on the posted result. Two figures, one above another, separated by an ink stroke. People were already mistaking the order for a hierarchy of possession.

Bobby could tear the survey notice down. The act would be clear, public and temporarily satisfying.

It would also leave the ferries uncertain about the approach tomorrow.

"Keep working," he told the carter. "Nobody has given an order to close this road."

"And the people who put up the paper?"

"I'll hear what they're asking."

The ferryman looked at him, expecting a larger declaration. He had no larger declaration to give.

Bobby placed a paw against the causeway timber, felt the familiar give of the wet wood, and looked toward the waiting boats. A victory could make a rival louder. It could not make the mud agree.

He left the notice where it was. He took a copy of its wording and told the crews to record any stranger who asked to place a stake.

That choice would not settle the question. It would make it impossible to pretend nobody had asked.

**Editorial proof question:** Does the ordinary community's dependence on the crossing constrain Bobby's choices and counter corporate leverage? **The proposed survey, notice and civic response are fictional test candidates only; no authorized land claim or active LOC mutation.**
