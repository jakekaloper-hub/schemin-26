## Simulation — Board meeting, final sitting (Day 29)
**Closer (simulated):** What do we truly possess after the first twenty-eight days?
**Librarian (simulated):** A world graph, source-defined histories, an institutional Belt, twelve strict principal POVs, tens of thousands of words of past drafts, and a hard chronology. We do not yet possess a novel-quality central engine.
**Beat Writer (simulated):** We have written responsible characters reacting to certified facts. We should write **desiring characters who cause their own trouble**, even before a score arrives.
**Atlas (simulated):** Epic scale must arise from traversed labor and geographic inconvenience; “seven regions” cannot substitute for actual cultural differences.
**Character Director (simulated):** One fictional person's physical anatomy is not a personality. We need ambition, error, contradiction, grudges, relationships and costly choices for every owner.
**Civic desk (simulated):** A town's customs, trade, law and work do not exist solely to educate the hero.
**Scout (simulated):** An “inventive rewrite” does not entitle anyone to falsify a result, keeper injury, actual trade or scene geography.
**Visual Lead (simulated):** Monumental landscapes need human-scale objects in the same pages: cracked tools, food, ferrymen, merchants and witnesses. Final character illustration must await reference-source resolution.
**Reader engineer (simulated):** The twelve pathways should withhold and recontextualize information, not generate twelve incompatible timelines.
**Adversarial Umpire desk (simulated, NOT independent acceptance):** The plan remains paper architecture until two substantive opening samples produce a measurable reaction in real readers.

### Board interrogates the old outline
**Question:** If fantasy football ceased for a month, would there still be a conflict worth reading?
**Consensus hypothesis:** At present, weakly. Remedy: sustained obligations that change through the weeks and outlive wins.
**Question:** What does a score change beyond reputation?
**Consensus hypothesis:** Access to labor, bargaining position, perceived reliability, civic trust and social pressure, but never automatically a deed, crown or Belt.
**Question:** Why would a reader care about a stone bridge?
**Disputed answer:** Not because it is old. Because someone the reader knows relies on it and a rival's decision makes access uncertain.
**Question:** Is our first scene the League Archive?
**Opposing positions:** Archive supports mythic legitimacy; first scene needs a character who risks loss. Test with two openings, do not force an institutional prologue.
**Question:** Should all 12 owners appear in Book One's first 50 pages?
**Decision recommendation:** No; earn entrances through actions. All remain licensed as principal paths; no invented 13th POV.
**Question:** Are we ready to rewrite the whole manuscript?
**Board recommendation:** Not until competing openings show which story-level promise is genuinely compelling. Preserve prior manuscripts as evidence and literary salvage.

## Day 30 candidate creative brief
**Proposed thematic promise:** *Twelve owners can win the Contest. None alone gets to decide what their victories mean to the people who live between the arenas.*
**Proposed central question:** *Who has legitimate power to shape the Commonwealth once public fame begins altering old obligations?*
**Counterpressure:** People need food, safety, roads and trustworthy evidence regardless of who wins. When a protagonist acts on an incomplete story, their attempted good may injure another character or community. The world reacts back.

### Proof A — The Unopened Letter
**Controlling POV:** ObiWan. **Source home:** LOC-TRADE-JEDI-MOUNTAIN-BASE. **Before first result.**
1. Open in immediate physical work with Obi deciding between sending a courier urgently to protect his own strategic route and using the same capacity to answer a civilian need.
2. Obstacle: sender's authority is ambiguous; Jake's skepticism protects him from scams but makes him slow to help.
3. Irreversible micro-cost: his chosen messenger leaves; what he did not send cannot reach its intended place by the same route.
4. World detail should be earned by the goods on the wagon and the people waiting; not a pasted Atlas entry.
5. Ending promises a consequential return of the choice when Week 1 evidence begins.
6. **HOLD** all courier content, civic claim and resident identity as fictional candidates pending source/canon review.

### Proof B — The Bank Before the Banner
**Controlling POV:** Mud Dogs at LOC-MUD-DOGS-SWAMP, or Byars at LOC-BELT-KEEPER-KEEP if Atlas rejects first option. **Before first result.**
1. A familiar working problem divides people who rely on the protagonist; show their opposing legitimate positions in dramatic dialogue.
2. The owner could help one group immediately but worsens a different obligation; neither choice is abstractly correct.
3. Someone withholds or distorts a material detail. The owner notices too late, producing surprise without falsifying a real league result.
4. The first public contest proof arrives only after the reader understands an immediate personal risk.
5. End on new debt or lost trust, not a sermon about winning.
6. **HOLD** legal rights, inherited town histories, water events and finances as fictional proposals.

### Common success thresholds for real literary review
- First 900 words: a focal character *wants*, *risks*, *acts*, *pays*.
- A character's choice causes the next scene; not merely chronological matchups.
- At least one relationship that changes and cannot be restored by explaining a score.
- At least one wonder or cultural specificity grounded in actual established site/person.
- Focalizer knows only what they witness or receive.
- A naive reader understands the events without fantasy terminology primer or ESPN knowledge.
- All score evidence, identities and Atlas joins verifiable.
- Actual blinded reader result recorded separately from this imaginary session.
