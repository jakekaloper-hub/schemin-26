# CHAPTER I — WHAT A RESULT CAN CARRY

**POV: Chili Cheesers · CHAR-BRANDON-PRYOR**
**Editorial state: NEW REWRITE CANDIDATE — NOT CANON**

The Dark Horse did not like the square.

He liked the road beyond it, where the dust was honest and every sound had room to travel. In the square, voices struck the walls and came back changed. A sentence spoken as a joke returned as a challenge. A rumor returned as a fact, unless somebody bothered to carry the paper that proved otherwise.

The Chili Outlaw kept the reins loose.

Across the square, a notice board stood beneath an awning. Its upper half was crowded with older proclamations: bridge repairs, market weights, an argument over who maintained a stretch of causeway. At the bottom was an empty space reserved for the next certified Contest result.

The empty space drew more attention than any notice above it.

A boy approached the horse with a scrap of paper. “Who wins?”

“Someone,” the Outlaw said.

“That isn't an answer.”

“It's the only one the board can stand behind yet.”

The boy frowned, disappointed by a man whose reputation suggested better entertainment.

Brandon watched him go. He could have named an opponent, promised a rout, made the crowd laugh. Words cost nothing until the results came. Afterward, the same words could be carried to a different town and set against their speaker like a witness.

A rider arrived at the far end of the square. The crowd moved toward him before he had dismounted. Brandon did not move. The rider handed a sealed notice to the board keeper, who inspected it while everyone tried to read through the paper.

The Dark Horse shifted his weight.

“Easy,” Brandon murmured.

The keeper broke the seal, read the contents, and began to copy the result in a hand large enough for the people at the back to see. Brandon could not make out the figures from where he sat. He waited rather than borrow somebody else's shout.

When the board keeper finally stepped away, the square fell briefly silent.

A number on a board was not a battlefield. It could not show the effort that produced it or settle the arguments that would follow. Yet people were already turning from the notice to look at one another differently.

Brandon gathered the reins. He had come to see whether the result would be posted. Now he understood the more dangerous question: what would everyone do once they believed it?

The Dark Horse found the road eagerly. Behind them, the first argument began.
