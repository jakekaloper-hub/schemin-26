# CHAPTER II — THE THINGS PEOPLE SEND

**POV: Red Leopards · CHAR-KEVIN-ZEEK**
**Editorial state: NEW REWRITE CANDIDATE — NOT CANON**

A message could cross the Commonwealth faster than its author.

Kevin Zeek knew this because the message had arrived before the courier who could explain it.

The folded paper rested beneath a stone on the table. It contained no score, no signature he trusted, no mark of certification. The words were bold enough. Their evidence was not.

Outside, the Red Leopards' approach road curved past wet stone and a stand of trees where travelers sometimes stopped to shake rain from their coats. Someone had hung a strip of red cloth from a branch. It moved in the wind like a flag that had forgotten which army it belonged to.

Kevin opened the message again.

The courier's second note described who had paid for the delivery. That was useful. It was not the same thing as proving who had written the first message, or why. People would confuse those questions as soon as the story left his gate.

He put the notes side by side.

A retort occurred to him, sharp and easy. He could send it back immediately and have the road carry his answer before sunset. That would make the exchange symmetrical, which was precisely what the sender seemed to want.

Kevin took a clean sheet instead.

On it he wrote only what he could verify: when the packet had arrived, what it contained, and which courier had delivered it. The rest he left blank.

A Leopard scout appeared at the doorway. “Are we answering?”

“Not yet.”

“They'll think we have nothing to say.”

“Then they can think.”

The scout looked at the blank sheet and understood less than Kevin had hoped.

Outside, another rider passed on the lower road. Kevin watched the red cloth snap in the wind. A message was already becoming a story in other mouths. He could not stop that. He could decide whether to give the story another unverified sentence.

He folded the evidence carefully.

What returned to a sender was not always a reply. Sometimes it was the knowledge that the recipient had kept the receipt.
