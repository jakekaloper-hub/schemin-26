# CHAPTER III — THE HILL IS NOT THE KINGDOM

**POV: D0nkey K0ng · CHAR-WILSON-LOOK**
**Editorial state: NEW REWRITE CANDIDATE — NOT CANON**

Four hooves found the firmer edge of the road where the slope began to rise.

The mud at Mire Hill was deeper than it looked. Wilson knew because the weight of his centaur body had found every soft place on the approach. Rain clung to the Arsenal red across his shoulders. His axe rested against one forearm; he had carried it long enough that its absence would have felt stranger than its weight.

Below the ridge, the last of the field crews worked in the rain. The Contest was over. The ground remained.

A certified result had reached him: D0nkey K0ng, 172.48. Mud Dogs, 126.86.

The numbers were exact. The meaning other people wanted to assign them was not.

A standard-bearer waited at the path toward the summit. “Shall we raise it?”

Wilson looked at the boundary stones. They had been here before his victory. They would be here after it. A road crew was already repairing the drainage cut beside the northern track. None of them asked who had won.

“Keep it dry,” he said.

The standard-bearer hesitated. “For the summit?”

“For the road home.”

Wilson turned on the lower path. His four legs carried him past the broken stakes without claiming a single one. Behind him the hill remained what it had been: a place where a Contest had been decided, not a kingdom transferred.

The record would show three wins and no losses. It would not show a crown.

**POV: ObiWan Jacoby · CHAR-JAKE-KALOPER**

The Week 3 result reached the mountain room as a verified score, not a rumor from the crossing.

ObiWan read it twice: 136.73 to 114.50. Three Dreaded Snake had lost. He had won.

Across the table lay a map with the bridge and lake marked in faded ink. The old rivalry had made both places seem larger than their actual distances. That was the trouble with geography once people began telling stories about it: a crossing could become a debt, and a lake could become a verdict.

The golden retriever lifted her head when he moved the marker.

He did not place it on the far side of the bridge. The Contest had been decided; the roads had not changed ownership. Nor had a single result settled what either rival would do next.

He marked the result in the ledger and closed it.

There were still offers to weigh, routes to travel, and another week approaching. The blade at his side stayed dark.

A win could change a plan. It could not do the planning for him.

**POV: His Majesty's Blood · CHAR-AUSTIN-BYARS**

The Belt had not moved.

Austin Byars kept it where the morning light could reach the metal. That was not vanity. A keeper needed to remember what he held, especially on days when other things could not be kept.

The Week 3 result was certified: His Majesty's Blood, 136.00. Seven Deadly Chins, 125.54.

Beside it lay a different report. De'Von Achane, the keeper on whom so much of the season's strength had depended, was gone for the season. The report did not undo the victory. The victory did not repair the loss.

Outside, the settlement carried on with the work of the day. Wagons moved between buildings; a worker called for a missing tool. Nothing in those sounds knew the difference between winning a Contest and losing the means to win another.

Byars touched the edge of the Belt and withdrew his hand.

It was still his to keep. That fact had not become easier.

He put the two reports together, not because they agreed, but because they belonged to the same day.
