# PROLOGUE — THE ROAD BEFORE THE ANSWER

**POV: ObiWan Jacoby · CHAR-JAKE-KALOPER**
**Editorial state: NEW REWRITE CANDIDATE — NOT CANON**

The mountain road had two exits. ObiWan knew because he had spent the morning looking at both.

One descended through the pines toward the settlements of the Central Basin, where travelers traded news before they traded anything of value. The other climbed toward the ridge and vanished behind the stone shoulder of the mountain. It was a poor road for carts, a good road for anyone who wanted to arrive without being announced.

Neither road told him what would happen.

He spread a map across the war-room table. The paper had softened at its folds; at the edges, someone had written the names of old crossings in three different hands. Some names belonged to places that still existed. Others survived because the people who had lost them kept speaking as though possession were a matter of pronunciation.

The golden retriever at his feet raised her head when the wind caught the shutters.

“Not a visitor,” he told her.

She considered that answer, then rested her chin on his boot.

Beyond the window, the Commonwealth lay in pieces the map could not hold at once: roads between divided loyalties, settlements that depended on those roads, a chamber where claims might someday be certified. None of it was an empty stage waiting for twelve men to make it important. People had lived here before the Contest, and would have to live here after it.

ObiWan set a small marker at the crossing nearest his home.

He had learned to distrust the neatness of markers. A line on a map could be called a border by one man and a promise by another. A victory could be called a right. A right could become a demand. The change usually happened before anyone agreed to it.

On the opposite side of the table lay an unopened packet. Its seal was intact. Until the evidence inside could be checked, it was not knowledge. That distinction was easier to make in the quiet of a room than in a crowded square.

He left it closed.

The dog rose and walked to the door, impatient with the human practice of preparing for things that had not occurred.

ObiWan folded the map along its oldest crease. Down in the basin, a bell rang once for the start of ordinary business. It was not a signal of victory, nor of disaster. It was a reminder that the world would keep its own hours.

He took his field jacket from its peg. The green blade remained unlit.

There would be time to draw it when there was something worth defending.

For now, the roads were open. The answer had not arrived.
