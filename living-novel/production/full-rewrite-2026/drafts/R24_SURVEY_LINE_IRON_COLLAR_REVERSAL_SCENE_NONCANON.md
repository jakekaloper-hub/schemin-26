# THE SURVEY LINE — THE IRON COLLAR
**Developmental revision scene / NONCANON. Insert candidate between Survey Line V2 sections III and IV, not a World State transaction.**
**POV: CHAR-BOBBY-MITCHELL — Mud Dogs; at LOC-MUD-DOGS-SWAMP, after Week 5.**

The medicine carrier laid the iron collar beside the company's letter. It had come loose from the working crossing when the timber shifted. One side bore a straight cut beneath the mud.

Mud Dogs lowered his broad head. Cold iron. Old water. A touch of grease unfamiliar from the ferry yard. That was what his senses allowed him. They did not give him the name of whoever had damaged it.

“The company's men,” the elder ferryman said.

“The collar was here before their line,” the younger boatman replied.

Both were certain. Neither could prove it.

The woman who carried the medicine put one hand on the page. “My next delivery needs that crossing.”

The old ferryman wanted the collar displayed in the market as evidence against the company. The younger boatman argued that accepting the proposed inspection could reveal whether the whole timber structure was failing. The carrier wanted the work done before another wagon attempted the bank. Mud Dogs watched all three. He had ordered his people to brace the crossing earlier. If the damage had been hidden then, he might have made it worse.

He was capable of crushing the metal. He could not make the cut disappear.

He pushed the collar toward the elder, then tapped the sealed letter. Preserve both, he meant. No accusation before the inspection.

The elder did not agree. He would carry the evidence but refused to attend any meeting paid for by the LLC. The younger boatman agreed to attend and announced that he would speak for the carriers who wanted better access. Mud Dogs allowed both choices. His authority over the region did not convert them into one opinion.

That was the first tangible loss of the day: the community would not enter the next negotiation united.

**POV: CHAR-DAVID-BABB — The LLC; at LOC-LLC-BOARDROOM, after an undated messenger transit between regions.**

The drawing of the iron collar reached the boardroom with the wetlands' response. The LLC set it beside the development budget.

His broker called it useful. If a crossing was defective, people might welcome a replacement road.

“Useful to whom?” the LLC asked.

“Us, if we move quickly.”

The mark on the iron might be sabotage. It might be an old repair that failed. It might have nothing to do with the company's sketch. No one in the room knew. The broker proposed a public notice about unsafe infrastructure and the firm's willingness to solve it.

The LLC looked at the warehouse estimate. He had already diverted some of that budget toward an inquiry that might never produce a project. To promise a solution now would be an inexpensive sentence attached to an expensive future.

“No statement about the cause,” he said. “First inspect the present crossing. No work on the bank without the people who use it.”

“That could kill the proposal.”

“Yes.”

The broker left with an instruction he disliked. The LLC kept the iron drawing and opened a new line in the ledger: safety assessment before commercial design.

His victory over Mud Dogs remained on the certified sheet—193.73 to 127.09. There was no place on that sheet to record the money he might spend learning that he had no project at all.
