## Ask the Bullpen itself — hard questions and provisional answers
**Q1. What do the protagonists WANT beyond winning?**
A. Individualized desires are present but weakly tested: Obi seeks verifiable leverage, Wilson meaning after collapse, Chili acclaim without abandoning workers, Mud civic protection, Byars lawful retention under scarcity, TDS identity, LLC expansion. Turn each into an objective with a deadline/temptation or a named sacrifice.

**Q2. Where do two GOOD things become incompatible?**
A. Warehouse repair versus possible wetland inquiry; ferry access now versus safety later; accurate history versus politically effective myth. Make the incompatibility irreversible without writing unapproved Commonwealth law.

**Q3. Who can be wrong?**
A. Too few. The current seven repeatedly decline false claims and behave ethically. Invent rival character blind spots that cause real story damage without depicting real league owners' private faults as fact.

**Q4. What is the CENTRAL BOOK QUESTION?**
A. Candidate: *Who may shape the Commonwealth when victory gives a person influence but no legitimate right to govern?* This is a literary hypothesis, not a new constitutional canon.

**Q5. What would surprise a reader who knows the six scores?**
A. Consequences not determined by results: a witness contradicts a public account, a loyal retainer refuses an order, an apparent civic benefit divides a community, a saved road reveals old damage, a contender chooses to protect a rival's source of power. Never falsify actual outcomes.

**Q6. Is every character merely a lesson about responsible power?**
A. Currently too often yes. Give TDS pride, private projects and active strategies; Chili recklessness and charm; Wilson resource appetite as well as restraint; Byars conflict between protection and title; Jake opportunism; LLC impatient ambition; Mud territorial instinct AND awareness of others' choices.

**Q7. Does the Commonwealth live WITHOUT the Twelve looking at it?**
A. Increasingly, through ferries, cooks, clerks and tools. Now create recurring ordinary citizens with specific causes and persistent object histories; support them as non-POV actors, never accidental thirteen narrators.

**Q8. What is the visual and temporal payoff?**
A. Map movements only through sourced graph routes, keep hair and gorilla-centaur anatomies, but make geography matter as a barrier and decision, not decorative backdrop.

**Q9. Could an unfamiliar reader understand Chapter I?**
A. Not reliably without testing; first 900 words should ground one danger/goal before listing every W1 score.

**Q10. Does the selective POV/inkjs idea strengthen the novel?**
A. Yes only if each owner path has distinctive knowledge and obligations. Branching must reveal complementary truths, not contradictory league outcomes or repeated scenes with swapped names.

**Q11. How could this manuscript make the reader feel grief, desire and wonder, not just intellectual satisfaction?**
A. Give the ordinary inhabitants things they cannot simply replace; let public victories endanger intimate relationships, erode trusted histories or cost someone their work. Invest sensory awe in the established seven-region world, never fabricated land-scale measurements.

**Q12. What does each victory make harder?**
A. A rule for writers: after every result, ask what new obligation/public misunderstanding, resource scarcity, competing loyalty or delayed consequence it creates. If the result only triggers a speech, the scene fails.
