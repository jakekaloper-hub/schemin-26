# The Record He Cannot Keep

**2015 periods 8–15 candidate sequence — NONCANON.**

## I. Three Marks

The three favorable slates arrived one at a time, which saved Jake from believing in them too quickly.

After the seventh loss, he had expected the doorway to empty. Instead, people began arriving with written returns and small refusals. A fisher declined rope from the store because her sister had not yet paid for the previous coil. A mason asked for a lamp but left a tool in pledge. Mara made columns for each of them, and the columns became a kind of relief. No one had to pretend the room was generous. They only had to admit what they owed.

When the first of the three better results came, someone brought sweet bread. Jake sent it to the passage and kept none. When the second arrived, Mara allowed herself a smile. When the third came, the storekeeper asked whether the new record meant the old loan rule had been vindicated.

“It means we have three marks,” Jake said.

“You make success sound ungrateful.”

“I am trying to make it useful.”

The storekeeper laughed once, without pleasure. “Useful success is still success.”

Jake wanted to agree. He wanted the three slates to settle the argument that had begun at the first door. But he had learned enough by then to distrust a settlement that arrived in his preferred shape. He ordered the board left open through the night.

## II. What the Board Cannot Hold

The next three results did not permit celebration.

One was sharp, one was close enough to make the room recalculate every earlier choice, and one was another unmistakable reversal. The bread stopped arriving. The columns remained.

On the morning after the last of them, Mara found a page missing from the ledger. Not a total. A name.

“Someone took it,” she said.

“Or you misplaced it.”

“I do not misplace pages.”

Jake believed her. That did not tell him who had taken it or why. The easy response was to search everyone who came through the threshold. The harder response was to admit that a record which made people answerable could also make them afraid.

He ordered no search.

The storekeeper called it weakness. Mara called it an invitation. Jake called it a delay, and none of them liked the word.

That evening he read the visible pages aloud—not the amounts, only the promises that had been met. The room listened because nobody knew whether the missing name would be theirs. When he finished, he said, “A ledger cannot keep a promise for you. It can only remember that you made one.”

No one applauded. One person left. Another stayed to add a return to the margin. Jake saw then that the board could not make a community honest. It could only make dishonesty require a shape.

## III. The Last Two Slates

The final two slates came after the contests had moved into their later arrangement. Jake did not name that arrangement a prize. It was simply where the record had placed his team, and there were still people in the passage who needed a door.

Both marks favored him. The numbers were large enough that no one asked for an explanation. This, Jake found, was its own danger. Success without an argument invited people to forget the arguments that had made it survivable.

Mara brought the missing page back on the evening of the second result.

“It was under the board,” she said.

Jake took it. The edge was dirty, but the name was legible. He could ask who had put it there. He could reopen every suspicion. He could let the last two slates license a clean ending.

Instead he set the page beside the first slate, the one that had begun the year with its impossible difference.

“We keep it,” he said.

“As evidence?”

“As a reminder that we did not solve everything.”

Outside, the passage was quieter than it had been at the start. Not peaceful. People had learned too much for that. But a cart rolled past with an axle that held, and the lamp at the doorway still burned.

Jake locked the ledger without locking the door.

The season’s record could be counted by anyone. The thing he had tried to build inside it could not. It would have to survive without being mistaken for a victory.
