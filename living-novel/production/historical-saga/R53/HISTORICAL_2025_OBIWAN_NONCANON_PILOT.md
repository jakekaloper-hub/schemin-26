# THE YEAR THAT RETURNED THE QUESTION

The record did not change when Jake read it again.

That was almost insulting.

He had spent the preceding year learning to live with a result that everybody wanted to make permanent. In the first days after the 2024 title he had resisted their certainty. Nothing in the Contest promised that a champion would remain one; the records promised only that a champion had existed at a particular moment. Yet even careful people could grow used to being treated as an exception.

And now the final said 161.07 to 109.56.

The Immortal had won.

Jake laid the paper flat. He had been given a private room to receive the certification, a courtesy that felt indistinguishable from a place to hide. Across the wood of the table lay the same ordinary grain he remembered from the year before. Nothing had happened to it because his title was no longer current.

Someone outside asked whether he would acknowledge the announcement.

He could decline. Every defeated finalist had that freedom. People would then supply an explanation for the silence and carry it farther than any words he might choose.

"Come in," he called.

The person who entered was carrying two copies of the record. Jake almost laughed. One defeat apparently required a spare.

"Has the result been certified?"

"Yes."

"All of it?"

"All of it."

"Then publish it."

The messenger hesitated. "Without a statement?"

"What statement would improve a true result?"

The question landed harder than he intended. He saw that immediately. The messenger looked down at the papers, as if the figures had begun to require defending.

Jake softened his voice. "That isn't a criticism of your work."

"I understand."

"I don't think you do. Neither do I."

After the door closed, he examined the losing number again. It was large enough to have won other contests. That was not the problem. It had not won this one.

He remembered the moment he had begun to believe the next title might follow naturally from the last. There had been no declaration, no sudden arrogance. Only small permissions he had granted himself, one after another: the assumption that a good decision would be rewarded, that successful habits were a kind of protection, that repetition made a result more likely because he wanted it to.

This final had none of those obligations.

The Immortal's path through the postseason had included a survival by four tenths of a point against Duckhook. Jake could imagine how narrowly the story had escaped being a different story. Had that semifinal bent the other way, another opponent would have reached him, perhaps with another set of strengths and fears. But it had not. The records were not material for a different past. They were proof of this one.

A knock interrupted him once more.

This time an attendant brought the case used for the championship insignia. The fittings had been cleaned. The hinges made the same faint sound when the lid opened.

"I've been instructed to prepare it," the attendant said.

Jake looked at the metal inside.

"Of course."

"I thought you might want another moment."

There it was: pity, offered with exquisite manners.

Jake did want another moment. He wanted an hour, a winter, an argument with the calendar. He wanted to return to the opening round of the previous year, to the first time the numbers had made people speak as if he were inevitable, and warn himself that a number could never promise that.

He said, "No."

The attendant waited while Jake folded the final record. He did not put it in the case. The case was leaving; the record was his to keep.

A title could change hands without surrendering the year in which it had been earned. That was the only generosity the evidence offered. He had been champion in 2024. He had lost the final in 2025. Neither fact canceled the other. The rest—whether this was disgrace or instruction, the beginning of decline or merely a reversal—belonged to people who could not resist filling an empty space with prophecy.

Jake closed the case.

"Take it."

When the attendant had gone, he returned to the table and placed two sheets side by side.

**2024.**

**2025.**

He had thought the first was a conclusion. The second proved that both were entries.

Outside, the world was beginning its next season of arguments. Some voices called for a new age. Others spoke as though the old champion had lost something he had never deserved. The noise reached the room in fragments.

Jake listened long enough to understand that it would continue whether he answered it or not.

Then he took a clean sheet from the drawer. A title could not prepare the next campaign for him. Neither could the loss of one. What he needed was a record of choices rather than an inventory of grievances.

The first question he wrote was not *How do I get it back?*

It was *What did I fail to see?*

He stared at it a long while. He knew the answer would take more than a night, and that some parts of it would remain unknowable even after the next set of results. He was not pleased by that.

But he had learned, at some cost, to prefer a difficult question to an easy lie.

The final record stayed open beside him.

This time he did not turn it over.
