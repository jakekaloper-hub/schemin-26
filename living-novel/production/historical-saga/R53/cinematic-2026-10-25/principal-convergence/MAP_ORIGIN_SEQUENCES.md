## Sequence 1 — At the crossing

2018. THE ROOM'S MAP IS BEING MADE BY PEOPLE WHO USE THE PLACES IT SHOWS.

INT. ROOM — DAY

A blank sheet hangs on the wall. A proposed boundary has no place on it.

FISHERWOMAN
The stair I use. I want to know who else uses it.

A PORTER draws an alley. The fisherwoman adds her stair and steps aside for a CARTER.

He points below the alley, near the well.

CARTER
Not that way when it's wet.

Jake touches the alley.

CARTER
Here.

His finger moves to the crossing. He draws another line. It crowds the first.

Jake offers a clean sheet.

JAKE
We can start again.

CARTER
I have to go.

JAKE
Tell me, then.

The carter takes the charcoal instead. Beside the crossing he writes **DRY ONLY**, pressing until a corner breaks from the stick. He hands the short piece back and leaves.

Jake touches the edge of the words with his thumb. The black spreads beneath the well.

FISHERWOMAN
Which part are you changing?

JAKE
Where it fits.

She puts her finger where the carter's had been.

FISHERWOMAN
It fits here.

LATER — THE MAP HAS MORE HANDS ON IT

Jake holds a small, clean drawing of the well over the blot. The drawing covers the end of the warning.

He shifts it. The clean well no longer conceals the crooked one.

He puts the little drawing in the drawer. The shared map stays on the wall.

LATER IN2018

A GIRL follows a route with her finger. Lamplight catches her hand before it reaches the crowded crossing.

GIRL
Who made this safe?

Jake looks where she has stopped. The end of ONLY is nearly lost beneath the well.

JAKE
That crossing is for dry weather.

GIRL
Today?

JAKE
I don't know.

She takes her finger off the paper.

He moves the lamp until the words can be read. When he asks where she needs to go, she points toward the fisherwoman already leaving and runs to catch her.

Mara brings a hook. Jake holds the lamp while they hang it below the warning.

The clean drawing remains in the drawer.

## Sequence 2 — Twenty explanations

2019. THE WALL MAP WILL NOT FIT ON A CARRIED SHEET.

INT. ROOM / PRINTER'S WORKROOM — SELECTED DAYS

Jake holds paper against the wall. The stair ends beneath his thumb. Turned sideways, the sheet loses the well. Lengthwise, it loses the road.

The PRINTER puts a wrapped block on the table.

PRINTER
How many?

JAKE
Enough that they needn't come here.

PRINTER
That's not a number I can price.

He counts people he remembers at the wall.

JAKE
Twenty.

PRINTER
Who checks the copy?

JAKE
I do.

She unwraps the sample: a carved flower whose stem bends around a knot. Her finger follows the bent stem without touching its edge.

PRINTER
One block. Twenty sheets. A mistake travels twenty times.

Jake pays for twenty impressions and a trial first.

LATER

The small clean drawing of the well lies beside his copied map. Three hands' crossing lines have become one firm line. The cramped warning has moved away from the curve.

The printer asks about the little word.

JAKE
Only.

PRINTER
Only what?

He brings the copy closer.

JAKE
Dry only.

PRINTER
Does it need the words?

Jake studies the line he has made so easy to follow.

JAKE
Could you leave a break?

PRINTER
Anywhere. Will a stranger know what it means?

JAKE
We'll explain when we give them out.

She taps TWENTY on the agreement.

He looks from the number to the crossing.

JAKE
Leave the break.

She records his decision on the tracing. He begins enlarging the title.

LATER — TRIAL

The printer holds a fresh impression by two corners. An uneven patch of road has failed to take ink. Jake reaches before she has finished; his finger marks the paper beside the well.

She puts it with the spoiled sheets and makes another impression.

The road prints clearly. So does the gap he chose. Apart from the tracing, the gap resembles the accidental pale patch on the discarded trial.

JAKE
Put the words back.

PRINTER
You had that space cut away.

She gives him the repair price, including further trials. He looks at the proof. People have already been told the maps will be ready.

They agree that he will write a separate sentence at the bottom of **every** sheet.

NIGHT

Mara offers to check the first ten against the proof while he writes. Jake draws the stack toward himself.

JAKE
I can finish them.

MARA
I didn't say you couldn't.

She leaves the lamp.

He writes: **BROKEN LINE: CROSSING USABLE ONLY WHEN DRY.**

The eighth sheet has less room beneath the title. His writing narrows.

On the tenth his hand cramps. The sentence stops after **CRO**.

He slides it beneath the other nine for morning.

## Sequence 3 — The road without him

2019. THE FIRST TEN IMPRESSIONS ARE STILL BENEATH THEIR CLOTH.

INT. PASSAGE / WORKROOM — DAY

People leave the result board. Jake takes the stack out before they go. One asks about the latest unfavorable mark; Jake asks where the man needs to travel.

Sheets pass from his hands.

He holds one flat while its recipient folds it. The well remains visible. Jake watches the fold, not the sentence beneath the title.

The printer arrives with the other ten impressions.

PRINTER
All ten?

The first stack is gone. Jake can recall a face and a fold. He cannot recall finishing the bottom sentence.

PRINTER
Ask for them back.

He writes seven recipients he remembers. Two sheets went to people on behalf of others. The last recipient gave no name.

SHORT SEARCH

One person asks him to sit while she looks. At another door the map has already been passed on. Beneath a window he hears himself described as the man taking back his own gift.

LATER

A damp folded map lands on the workroom table. Jake tries to bring the carter farther inside.

CARTER
Here is well enough.

Jake opens the sheet. **CRO**. Nothing after it.

JAKE
I have a corrected copy.

CARTER
I have a wheel sunk to the hub.

The carter keeps his hand on the damp sheet.

CARTER
The driver had this. He'd never seen the wall.

The carter has already helped unload the cart and bring it back another way. Two sacks are wet. He needs help separating grain before it spoils.

Jake asks whether the crossing is passable on foot.

CARTER
Come with me.

Mara appears in the doorway. People are waiting at the board.

JAKE
Will you come back after?

CARTER
The grain will still be wet.

Jake takes his coat. The printer turns the unused bundle facedown.

LATER — GRAIN SPREAD ON CLOTH

Seven recovered originals dry on a line. Three are still missing.

Jake breaks a damp clump apart. Beneath a dry-looking surface is more wet grain. He moves it into a thin layer while the carter works beside him.

At dusk somebody calls that people are still waiting at the board.

Jake offers to pay for the whole load.

CARTER
You haven't looked at all of it.

He opens another clump. There is grain worth saving.

Jake kneels again.

THREE EVENINGS' WORK, WITHOUT A FINISHED RECONCILIATION

The ruined portion is separated. Jake pays for that portion. The carter accepts without retracting his complaint.

Jake offers him a new proof to examine.

CARTER
I wrote it clearly the first time.

The carter returns to his work. The proof stays in Jake's hand.

## Sequence 4 — The block he touched

LATER2019. A SMALLER, CROSSING-ONLY RUN HAS BEEN COMMISSIONED. IT IS A NEW BLOCK, NOT THE BLOCK THAT PRINTED THE TEN DISTRIBUTED ORIGINALS.

INT. PRINTER'S WORKROOM — DAY

A prospective patron's credit line competes with the warning for space. Jake tries another arrangement. Then another. In each, the warning looks like a qualification to somebody's gift.

He turns the sheet over. He draws only the crossing at a larger scale, omitting the distant road and ornamental title.

PRINTER
That fits.

JAKE
He won't pay for it.

She waits. He takes back the unsigned offer.

LATER

Jake pays for a smaller run with money from selling his coat clasp. The printer works on the new block. His coat will not fasten at the neck.

ANOTHER EVENING

He has told someone the work is nearly finished without asking her.

The printer has left. Her request for more time lies beside the new block.

Jake turns the request facedown.

On practice wood he cuts a little road. Then another. Neither stays straight. He looks at the new block: the main crossing is finished, a small space waits for a directional mark.

He cuts an arrow there.

He sets the block back without taking an impression.

MORNING

The printer pulls a proof. The arrow points the wrong way.

PRINTER
Who touched this?

The ASSISTANT puts both hands behind his back.

Jake looks at them.

JAKE
I did.

She asks which tool. He points. She sets his practice piece beside the proof; a cut and its impression face opposite ways.

The assistant begins cleaning the block. Jake reaches to help. The assistant moves it beyond his reach.

ASSISTANT
Ask her first.

The printer measures the damaged space. An insert must be fitted and cut again.

JAKE
I can do the rough work.

PRINTER
Sweep.

LATER

Jake holds a pan beneath the table. News of a favorable result makes him straighten too fast. Shavings spill.

The assistant laughs once and bends to help gather them. Jake asks what work he had been doing before the maps.

ASSISTANT
Labels.

An oil seller's order is waiting. The assistant needs the printer to finish it; the insert has taken her time. He is paid when the labels are delivered.

Jake looks at the separate order. Wood and cutting are on his account. This wait is not.

AT SUPPER TIME

He brings enough for the assistant's missed payment.

PRINTER
Put it on the table.

The assistant can choose whether to take it. Jake sets it down. Neither invites him to remain.

Outside, crumbs catch at his unfastened collar as he eats his own bread.
