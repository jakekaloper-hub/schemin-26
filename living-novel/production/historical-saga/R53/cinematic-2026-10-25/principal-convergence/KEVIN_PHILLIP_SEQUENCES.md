## Sequence 1 — Before the meal

AFTER THE SECOND CONTEST,2026. KEVIN HAS BEATEN BRANDON. THE RETURNED DELIVERY SLIP HAS BECOME A PUBLIC JOKE.

EXT. CEREMONIAL AVENUE — DAY

Kevin holds the slip while Brandon reads the original taunt. Kevin turns it over to show the result.

KEVIN
Delivery.

The laugh travels down the avenue. Brandon, with a cracked lens and damaged shoulder cloth, studies him.

BRANDON
You could bring it faster next time.

Kevin bares his teeth. Brandon has joined the joke instead of giving him the anger he prepared for.

They hold opposite ends of the slip. Kevin lets go first and walks past. This time he does not look back to see where the crowd is looking.

LATER — UNDER AN AWNING OFF THE AVENUE

A RETAINER brings news: a river guild has canceled dinner with Brandon's quartermaster. Its messenger waits with a request to speak to Kevin's people.

The retainer is smiling. Kevin almost asks why. His own expression supplies the answer.

KEVIN
Bring him here.

The MESSENGER opens the request. A meeting. No cargo listed, no agreement offered.

KEVIN
How many places at the table?

MESSENGER
I was told to ask for a conversation.

KEVIN
People speak over food.

The messenger holds out an empty hand for the reply.

Kevin looks past him at the red banners. He could have the same audience again, seated and fed, watching Brandon's loss become his new company.

He folds the request.

KEVIN
Tell them to send the subject first. Then we'll discuss a meal.

RETAINER
They could go somewhere else.

KEVIN
They already did.

The retainer starts to explain. The messenger shifts his weight. He still has no reply he can carry.

Kevin turns to him and repeats it: the subject first. The man repeats it back and leaves.

RETAINER
A refusal?

KEVIN
A question.

RETAINER
Suppose being seen here was the subject.

Kevin watches the people following the banners.

KEVIN
Then I may have asked badly.

He stays beneath the awning after the retainer has gathered the papers. There is no dinner to exhibit tonight. Nobody has promised that a useful meeting will follow.

## Sequence 2 — The brace

AFTER THE FOURTH CONTEST,2026. PHILLIP HAS BEATEN BOBBY. THE OUTING IS OVER; THE BLIND STILL HAS TO BE PUT AWAY.

EXT. TEMPLE STONE — DAY

A hunting blind lies across the approach. Phillip holds a rail against his armored torso. One of his three heads watches a hinge, another the far end. The middle one watches Bobby.

PHILLIP
The cord.

Bobby lifts a muddy forepaw. Nothing moves.

Phillip looks at the other paw. Bobby lifts it. The frame sags. Phillip catches it; all three necks withdraw from the upper rail.

PHILLIP
Still.

BOBBY
I was still.

Bobby sits beyond the loose cord. Phillip lowers the frame, changes the order of the folds and tries again.

The hinge meets the brace he added to make the side rigid.

Bobby points his muzzle toward it.

PHILLIP
I know what that does.

BOBBY
It's doing it.

Phillip looks at the rigid side. The successful improvement is preventing him from putting the thing away.

He lifts the rail and asks Bobby to draw the trapped cloth clear. Bobby takes an edge carefully in his teeth, pulls a little, then lets go.

BOBBY
It will tear.

PHILLIP
The handle, then.

They both look at it. It is placed for an upright person. Bobby would have to put his jaw beside the hinge that is closing.

PHILLIP
No. Leave it.

He sets the apparatus down. The approach would remain obstructed if he left it assembled. He studies the brace, then goes for a tool.

LATER

Bobby lies with his forepaws either side of the lower rail, keeping it from sliding. Phillip works at the fastening. The middle head turns toward the whole apparatus whenever his hands stop, measuring how much of his design he is undoing.

At last the brace drops against the stone.

The panels meet. Phillip lifts an end. Without the brace, the folded structure twists.

BOBBY
Now?

PHILLIP
Inside.

They move it in short stages: Phillip lifts; Bobby pushes at the lower rail with his shoulder. A doorway catches the finish that survived the outing. The scrape leaves a pale line.

INT. ADJOINING ROOM — CONTINUOUS

Phillip sets the blind against the wall. The removed brace rests beside it. Its fastening cannot simply be put back as it was.

Bobby noses the door wider and turns to leave.

Phillip looks from him to the loose pieces. Another invitation could keep the help here. It would not make the next problem small.

PHILLIP
Thank you.

Bobby goes.

From the threshold the blind looks compact. Phillip moves it farther into the room. Tomorrow he will have to pass the loose brace, not merely admire the reduced outline.

## Sequence 3 — Start with the crossed-out one

AFTER THE FIFTH CONTEST, BEFORE THE SIXTH. KEVIN HAS LOST TO JAKE. THE FAILED PUBLIC EXHIBITION IS BEHIND HIM; ITS APPARATUS AND INJURED WORKER ARE NOT REPAIRED BY THIS CONVERSATION.

INT. COVERED SPACE AT THE RUINS — DAY

Kevin's practice sheets lie beneath the certified result. Jake will have to move the result to reach them.

Jake pulls out a chair. Kevin has moved his own back to make room. He remains standing over the table, claws against its edge.

The retriever settles by Jake's boot. Kevin moves the lamp away from its tail.

KEVIN
Before it happened. What did you expect me to do?

Jake looks at the result.

KEVIN
Not what you can explain now.

Jake takes a folded note from his coat. Kevin reaches toward it.

JAKE
I haven't offered it.

Kevin takes his hand away. One claw leaves a pale mark in the wax of his own tablet.

KEVIN
What would you take?

Jake's slight smile makes the bargain visible. Kevin wanted knowledge; he has offered to buy the winner's account.

Jake looks at the questions beneath the result.

JAKE
Your preparations would give me something to compare.

KEVIN
I asked what you expected of mine. You want me to supply them first.

Jake leaves the note folded on the table.

JAKE
You could show this to anyone.

KEVIN
Yes.

JAKE
Even the wrong parts.

KEVIN
Yes.

Jake opens the first fold.

JAKE
Read it with me. Not in exchange for yours.

Kevin pulls his own chair forward and sits.

KEVIN
The one you've crossed out.

Jake's fingers cover a lower line.

JAKE
That is about someone else.

Kevin looks at the covered part. He could make access to the whole sheet the next contest. Instead he pushes his own question toward the lamp.

KEVIN
The crossed-out one.

Jake reads his expectation: the failed exhibition would make Kevin change all his preparation.

KEVIN
You thought I'd replace it because people watched the show fail.

JAKE
I thought you might.

KEVIN
That isn't what you wrote.

JAKE
I wrote badly.

KEVIN
The wording isn't what I'm asking you about.

Jake looks up.

KEVIN
I kept the preparation. I stopped the show.

JAKE
Then I was wrong.

KEVIN
And won.

JAKE
Yes.

Kevin leans back. He had wanted a usable mistake, something he could remove from himself to close the distance. The winner's mistake has survived inside a victory.

KEVIN
Next line.

This note offers two incompatible possibilities. Kevin asks which Jake prepared for. Jake begins an explanation, stops, and admits he cannot separate the earlier thought from what seems obvious afterward.

Kevin waits. Rain strikes stone outside the dry space. The pause gives him no instruction.

He draws a new schedule from beneath the result. Its crowded lines leave almost no uncommitted time.

He tears it through the middle.

JAKE
You haven't shown me that.

KEVIN
I wanted you to tell me the hours would fix it.

Jake looks at the halves. Kevin puts them beneath the lamp and takes a clean sheet. He keeps the ordinary practice. Beside it he leaves a space.

JAKE
What goes there?

KEVIN
I don't know.

The gap remains. It is less comforting than another task.

KEVIN
You can keep your notes.

Jake reaches.

KEVIN
I'm still reading.

LATER

Kevin returns the paper. His own preparation sheets have not changed hands.

Jake marks the edge beside the wrong guess before folding it. The visible mark interrupts the clean outline.

JAKE
Will you tell me what helps?

KEVIN
When I know.

The dog rises. Jake follows it out. Kevin places the certified result beside the torn schedule. Neither covers the other. He reads the ordinary practice again.

## Sequence 4 — The hand at the edge

AFTER THE FIFTH CONTEST, BEFORE THE SIXTH. PHILLIP COMMISSIONED A PAIRED PORTRAIT BEFORE LOSING TO AUSTIN. THE SITTING DOES NOT TRANSFER CHAMPIONSHIP CUSTODY.

INT. PAINTER'S WORKING SPACE BY TEMPLE STONE — DAY

Phillip's painted figure occupies one side of a panel. Space has been left for Austin. The continuous border already surrounds both.

Phillip regards it from three angles with one body. His hand indicates the center.

PHILLIP
Move me here.

PAINTER
Where does the other figure go?

He points to the edge.

PAINTER
Your arm is there.

PHILLIP
Paint it again.

The painter studies the completed folds around the painted wrist.

PAINTER
Another sitting.

PHILLIP
Then another sitting.

PAINTER
I have another customer.

A boot sounds behind him. Austin has arrived, black armor and the Belt still at his waist.

The painter measures him against the unpainted space. Phillip lowers his hand. The room has found a use for Austin before Phillip has found an explanation for inviting him.

AUSTIN
I can leave it empty.

PHILLIP
It isn't empty.

The painter asks Austin to turn. The cloak hides the fastening. She suggests bringing the cloak farther across it.

AUSTIN
No.

Phillip makes a low sound.

AUSTIN
You asked for me.

PHILLIP
Before I knew what standing beside you would say.

Austin studies the narrow strip below the figures.

AUSTIN
Put the result there.

PHILLIP
Then no one can miss what stays with you.

Austin touches the Belt and lets his hand fall. The result has not changed its custody. Touching it will not answer Phillip.

PHILLIP
I wish I hadn't needed you in it.

The painter sets the brush down.

PAINTER
Two panels.

She lays a straightedge against the wood. It crosses Phillip's painted hand.

Austin looks at the line. A fresh panel could preserve the hand. He has the means to offer one.

AUSTIN
I won't buy that back for you.

PHILLIP
I haven't asked.

Austin draws his cloak clear of the wet edge.

AUSTIN
No.

Phillip holds the straightedge while the painter marks the back. One head sees the painted hand. Another sees the line behind it. The third has no view that makes the choice cost less.

PAINTER
Tomorrow. When it is dry.

PHILLIP
You have another customer.

PAINTER
Today. Tomorrow is what I'm offering.

Austin remains beside the empty chair.

PHILLIP
Would you want your half?

AUSTIN
There is nothing on it.

PHILLIP
There could be.

AUSTIN
Inside your border.

The painter examines her marked line.

PHILLIP
I wanted people to stop being surprised I could stand beside you.

Austin looks at the painted figure.

AUSTIN
For this sitting, standing together would have been enough.

Phillip gives the straightedge back.

PHILLIP
Separate it tomorrow. Leave the hand as it is.

PAINTER
It will look unfinished.

PHILLIP
Yes.

PAINTER
You can bring it back.

He almost agrees. A promised restoration would make this easier to authorize.

PHILLIP
Wrap it when it's dry.

Austin goes toward the door. Phillip does not escort him.

AUSTIN
Ask me again. For what you actually want.

Phillip gives no answer. Austin leaves alone.

The painter washes the brushes intended for the second figure. Phillip watches the dark color leave them. The line is marked; the panel is still one piece tonight.

PHILLIP
Don't disguise the edge.

PAINTER
They'll see it.

He keeps his hands away from the wet paint.

PHILLIP
They will.
