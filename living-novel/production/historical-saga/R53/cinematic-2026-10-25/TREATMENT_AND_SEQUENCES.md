## The choice of story

A champion commissions a public image of victory, then must decide what to do with it when the next championship belongs to someone else. His first honest response costs him control over how his success will be remembered.

The dramatic engine is a proposed commission, its unfinished physical object, and the people whose work cannot be made to disappear because the patron has lost. This develops the existing 2024 and 2025 Jake pilots beyond solitary reflection over certified papers. Jake's principal arc is from allowing others to mistake a temporary distinction for permanent authority to accepting a place in a shared history he cannot curate. All personal motives, workshop exchanges, commissions, money obligations and props are invented dramatic candidates. ## Feature treatment

### Movement 1 — A likeness that will not stand still

After the 2024 title, Jake finds that a craftsperson has made several trial impressions for a commemorative panel. In the preferred image, his figure is so large that the other people in the composition have become ornamental. He dislikes it but likes being recognizable. Instead of canceling the commission, he asks for a truer picture. The craftsperson asks what that means in usable terms. Jake cannot answer without naming what he wants to keep.

He removes the crown first. That is easy; crowns imply authority the championship does not grant. The harder change is the border. The draft makes his place look eternal. Jake says there must be space for whoever comes next. The craftsperson follows his instruction by leaving a bare section beside him. He finds the blank embarrassing. Before anyone else can see it, he asks whether it can be covered temporarily. Thus a small practical decision establishes his contradiction: he wants to be modest without appearing temporary.

People in the workshop have concerns unrelated to his public image. A worker needs the bench freed for ordinary repair jobs. An apprentice wants their finished labor recognized instead of cut away every time the patron changes his mind. Neither becomes an omniscient narrator. Jake observes hands, stops, and answers; their private thoughts remain theirs. The film gives them leverage through possession of the unfinished work, deadlines and the right to decline another alteration, not through speeches about humble commoners.

The semifinal enters through the surviving practice impression: the composition once assumed an effortless ascent, while the recorded 185.54–177.78 result made Jake's confidence look less simple. He cannot pretend every step was a coronation. The public sees the finished portion and applauds the winning image. Jake hears praise for the very certainty he thought he had removed. He accepts the applause. That is the first consequential failure.

### Movement 2 — The cost of a temporary solution

The year turns explicitly. The bare part of the object remains covered. Its temporary cover has become the visible normal arrangement. The film does not invent interim scores, opponents or trades. A short passage of changing work around the object conveys elapsed time, not unverified competition history. A repaired handle wears smooth; a paid job leaves the bench; the panel stays.

As the 2025 campaign reaches its recorded conclusion, Jake's choices concern the people waiting on his commission. He has deferred delivery because completion will fix an image of him he cannot keep improving. The workers can finish the object or continue to absorb his hesitation. He promises a decision after the championship. This promise is the trap he constructs himself: he makes ordinary work wait on an outcome that has no obligation to favor him.

The 0.40 semifinal margin is not staged as Jake witnessing an unverified venue or sensing an ordained enemy. Once results are public, he learns that The Immortal has advanced. He could indulge an alternate history in which another finalist arrives. The cut goes instead to the covered space. The audience understands how little the commission depends on the particular rival's identity. Someone will eventually occupy what Jake has kept concealed.

The final is decisive: 161.07–109.56. We do not translate numerical scale into a lethal battle, ruined city or newly invented injury. Sound drops from public reaction into the workshop's repeated ordinary work. Nothing on the bench has changed. The commission is still owed a decision. Jake arrives expecting discretion; the craftsperson has prepared the object for delivery. For the first time, his privacy and another person's livelihood cannot both be protected by delay.

### Movement 3 — The offer to make him smaller

The craftsperson proposes a practical solution: remove Jake's figure and reuse the whole surface. It is not mockery. Reuse would salvage labor and make a clean object. Jake discovers that he does want the winning year remembered. His difficulty was never simply vanity; it was his inability to distinguish remembrance from immunity to change.

He refuses erasure but cannot demand that others preserve his image for nothing. He must give up a benefit, accept completed work with its imperfections, and stop asking the workshop to subsidize his indecision. The specific fee and material terms are not fixed here; period economy requires evidence. What matters cinematically is that the craftsperson's agreed obligation ends. The patron no longer has infinite revision rights by virtue of having won.

The cover comes off. There is no prepainted new hero beneath it, no surprise prophecy, no fresh dynasty. It is unused surface. Jake must look at a place he does not control. The correct 2025 result can be acknowledged without the film assigning a current human identity to an unresolved historical slot.

The climax is a choice about display. Jake can move his image to a private room where the neighboring space will never trouble him. He chooses instead to let the two years be read together. The choice does not crown him morally above the new champion. The camera gives the new result its own uninterrupted attention. Jake is no longer the person entitled to explain it.

### Coda — An obligation, not a trailer

The object leaves the workshop. Its departure frees the bench for ordinary repair. One worker turns it over to check a joint and the grand composition becomes, briefly, a piece of wood that must survive handling. Jake moves aside so the work can pass. The film ends on a completed human action, not a teaser for a future contest.

The eleven-season saga can later revisit the distinction between a place in history and possession of the present through other principals, locations and obligations. No screenplay scene must be changed to validate this film. A later shared-road story can resonate with the same limit without inheriting this invented panel, workshop, debt or supporting cast. Recognition is optional; comprehension is complete within this feature.

## Written sequence A — The uncovered place

PROPOSED SCREENPLAY MATERIAL. 2024, after certified final. Principal focalization: Jake. Historical location/appearance unresolved. No exterior establishing shot, map or date-specific weather. This is new cinematic development, not a replacement for a locked opening.

INT. WORKSPACE — TIME UNFIXED

A carved hand fills the frame. It is too large for the rest of the figure.

JAKE presses his own hand beside it, without touching the surface.

The CRAFTSPERSON watches from the other side of the bench. Behind them a WORKER is trying to carry a long piece of broken furniture through a gap made narrow by the panel.

JAKE
Is that how it looked?

CRAFTSPERSON
From where?

Jake studies the carved crowd. Their faces are small enough to vanish when he steps back.

JAKE
From there.

He points to the smallest figure.

The craftsperson goes around the bench. They crouch until their eyes are level with the carving, then look up at Jake.

CRAFTSPERSON
They would mostly have seen your arm.

Jake lowers it.

The worker waits. The broken chair has caught on a corner.

JAKE
You could make me smaller.

CRAFTSPERSON
I could.

JAKE
Would that improve it?

CRAFTSPERSON
It would make you smaller.

The worker eases the chair back. A loose piece falls. Jake catches it before it hits the floor, then does not know where to put it.

WORKER
With the rest.

There are three piles. The worker nods toward one. Jake places the piece there.

On the panel, above his carved head, a raised outline waits to be cut.

JAKE
That goes.

CRAFTSPERSON
The crown?

JAKE
It was never mine.

The craftsperson draws a line through it with the flat of a darkened tool. The gesture is quick. Jake had expected something more satisfying.

JAKE
And the edge.

CRAFTSPERSON
Which edge?

He follows the border around the winning figure. It closes above and below, leaving no space for anything beyond him.

JAKE
It looks finished.

CRAFTSPERSON
That is what you asked for.

Jake glances at a folded result on the far end of the bench. He does not open it.

JAKE
That year is finished.

The craftsperson looks at him for a moment, then turns the panel slightly. A broad unworked strip catches the light.

CRAFTSPERSON
Leave this.

JAKE
For what?

CRAFTSPERSON
You tell me.

He starts to answer. Stops.

From beyond the doorway comes someone calling his name. Friendly, impatient. The worker has finally found room to bring the chair through.

Jake steps toward the voice, then sees the bare strip again. Beside the carved celebration it looks less like a promise than a mistake.

JAKE
Until there's something to put there—

The craftsperson waits.

JAKE
Could we cover it?

The worker lifts a scrap of cloth from the bench. Holds it up, asking without words.

Jake nods.

The cloth goes over the strip. Now the picture seems complete.

Outside, his name is called again.

He goes.

The craftsperson looks at the covered edge, then slides the unfinished panel away from the place where the worker needs to set the chair.

CUT TO BLACK. A year card, when the film resumes, must say 2025.

## Written sequence B — Delivery

PROPOSED SCREENPLAY MATERIAL. 2025, after certified championship. Same proposed interior; no assertion that a historical workshop has been verified. All speech invented. The Immortal remains a source team name without a historical human-owner casting assignment.

INT. WORKSPACE — TIME UNFIXED

The covered panel stands upright. The bench beneath it is bare except for curled shavings caught in a crack.

Jake enters carrying a folded sheet. He keeps it in his hand.

The craftsperson reaches for the panel.

JAKE
You heard.

CRAFTSPERSON
Yes.

JAKE
Then there isn't much reason to hurry.

The craftsperson releases the panel. Not in agreement.

On the floor is a small table awaiting repair. It occupies the space where the broken chair stood a year earlier; the workshop's ordinary jobs have moved on.

CRAFTSPERSON
I need the room.

Jake looks around as if the answer might be elsewhere.

JAKE
I didn't ask you to stop working.

CRAFTSPERSON
No.

They turn the panel. Jake's figure is visible. The covered section remains beside it.

CRAFTSPERSON
We could take it down. Use the surface again.

JAKE
All of it?

CRAFTSPERSON
Enough.

Jake puts the folded sheet on the bench. It opens under its own weight. A proposed audience-facing production insert reads: 2025 CHAMPIONSHIP — THE IMMORTAL 161.07; JAKE / OBIWAN JACOBY 109.56. These labels identify the focal character for a new viewer; this is not a facsimile of a verified historical document.

JAKE
The first year still happened.

CRAFTSPERSON
I know.

JAKE
So it should stay.

CRAFTSPERSON
It can stay.

The worker enters, sees them occupying the bench and stops. This time Jake notices before the worker asks.

He moves the paper.

JAKE
What is left to do?

The craftsperson points to the back of the panel. A small brace has been fitted but not secured.

CRAFTSPERSON
This. Then you take it.

JAKE
And if I want—

He looks at his carved hand. Still too large.

CRAFTSPERSON
Then it is another piece of work.

Jake almost smiles. It would let him pretend the answer was a joke. He does not.

JAKE
Finish this one.

The craftsperson works. Jake waits. He cannot shorten the task by being gracious about it. The worker settles at the cleared part of the bench and begins an unrelated repair.

When the brace is fixed, the craftsperson lays their tools down.

Jake takes the cloth off the adjoining strip.

There is nothing underneath.

For a moment the blank seems larger than the image.

JAKE
I thought it would be worse.

CRAFTSPERSON
What?

Jake shakes his head. It was not a question they could answer for him.

He places the 2025 record beside the earlier work. A small proposed label on the earlier portion reads 2024 — JAKE / OBIWAN JACOBY, CHAMPION. We see both years long enough to read them. No hand covers the 2025 winning name.

The worker unfolds a protective cover and reaches toward the panel.

WORKER
Storage?

Jake looks at the available cloth. He could cover the blank again and leave the object unseen.

JAKE
Display.

He sets the 2025 sheet in the adjoining space. When the worker moves to lift it clear, Jake keeps a finger on the edge.

JAKE
That goes with it.

The worker folds the cover back beneath the base to protect it during handling, leaving the two years visible. Exact public display site remains a production-design hold; the choice to display both is staged here.

The worker shifts to lift the panel.

Jake reaches to help, then waits for the worker to indicate where. Together they turn it sideways for the doorway. His figure disappears. We see the back: the new brace, the old grain, the necessary joints.

At the doorway Jake cannot both lead and carry his end.

He lets the worker go first.

The craftsperson returns to the bench. They brush the trapped shavings out of the crack. The work surface is clear.
