## Written sequence 1 — What stays behind

BEFORE THE FOURTH CONTEST RESULT.

EXT. COMMONS COURT — LATE DAY

Three empty repair carts wait inside a crowded gate. A paid timber load waits too. On a bench: two traveling covers, a smaller oilcloth and the diminished contents of a food roll.

WILSON stands beside the bench. His attendant has finished unpacking. There is nothing more impressive at the bottom.

Beyond them, the rear roof of BEN'S hall has been opened. Workers are preparing Wilson's covers for the gap. A woman from the kitchen, DELLA, checks an edge against the light.

MARA holds a paper flat on a board.

MARA
The covers stay. You contribute to the replacement. Your name here.

Wilson reads the few lines. His shoulder makes lifting the sheet uncomfortable; he lowers it to the board.

WILSON
And the carts?

MARA
The wheelwright gets the wood he paid for.

A driver at the gate calls to another that Wilson has finished. Someone begins clearing him a way.

Mara watches the opening widen.

MARA
There. They have moved him again.

WILSON
I haven't asked to leave.

She waits. The opening remains.

Earlier, he helped a blocked wagon. Then he used the space ahead of a woman who had waited longer. Now another useful act is making another passage for him.

Wilson leaves the unsigned sheet.

At the barrier, two drivers offer incompatible accounts of whose place he took. He starts to settle it for them. The woman managing the rope has already lifted a hand.

WILSON
Where do you want me?

She points behind a wagon of empty jars.

He goes. A child shifts his feet to copy Wilson's forelegs. A driver looks back, wondering whether to let him through anyway. Wilson looks toward the gate, waiting for the woman to call him.

The queue takes its time.

LATER

The paper lies on a better board. Wilson's attendant looks from the two covers to the small oilcloth left for their own gear.

ATTENDANT
If we need them before the roof is back?

Wilson takes the pen.

WILSON
Then we need them.

To Mara:

WILSON
Write until the roof is back.

He signs.

LATER — BEHIND THE HALL

The covers sag over the opening. Ben is moving an empty barrel. Wilson recognizes the better cover by its edge.

BEN
You can stop inspecting it.

WILSON
I'm not asking for it.

BEN
Good.

Wilson settles his weight across all four legs. Ben keeps his hands on the barrel rim.

WILSON
I thought helping would make the passage mine.

BEN
They make room for people they think they'll need.

WILSON
They need you.

Ben looks up at the covered gap.

Wilson follows his look. Neither says that the roof is repaired.

WILSON
When I lose—

BEN
You'll find out.

A wagon moves beyond the gate. The paid timber leaves first. The road crew's cut hall beam follows it, its fit still untested.

Wilson takes his place behind another wagon. Ben brings two pieces of bread, one for him and one for the attendant.

Wilson glances toward the kitchen.

WILSON
You paid for these?

Ben's hand closes around the second piece.

BEN
Yes.

He gives it to the attendant and goes back inside.

Wilson has a piece of bread, a question he cannot retrieve and less covering than he arrived with. He leaves when the wagon ahead moves.

END SEQUENCE 1.

## Written sequence 2 — The request after the lift

AFTER THE FOURTH CONTEST. WILSON HAS LOST FOR THE FIRST TIME.

EXT. STRONGHOLD ROAD APPROACH — DAY

A courier waits with a result while six workers attend to a shifted slab. Wilson holds a replacement rope. His four hooves stand clear of the workers' feet.

WILSON
A moment.

The courier waits. Wilson gives the rope to the foreman and takes the paper. The earlier victories have become a run that ended.

A worker calls. Wilson folds the result and takes the corner the foreman indicates. They move the slab a short distance, rest, then settle it.

By the time Wilson looks up, the courier has gone. His reply has missed its ride.

On the supply cart, somebody has crossed out a charcoal crown.

FOREMAN
We can wash it off.

WILSON
Leave it.

His own glove moves over the mark. Crown and cancellation blur together.

The foreman watches, then lifts the frayed rope from the cart.

FOREMAN
We need more of this. Two sound supports as well.

Wilson looks at the seated stone.

WILSON
For what is still wrong here?

FOREMAN
For the work we borrowed them from.

The foreman shows him the empty place where the longer pieces should lie. Wilson's finished lift has consumed a useful part of another job.

WILSON
Send the stores a list.

FOREMAN
Will they send wood?

Wilson looks at the empty place again.

WILSON
I'll have a list.

The foreman puts the rope down.

Wilson could keep the cart until somebody brings him a better answer. The workers are already climbing onto it. He steps aside.

FOREMAN
Before the next rain.

Wilson nods. The request leaves with the crew, still a request. He stands beside the seated slab with the worn rope in his hand.

END SEQUENCE 2.

## Written sequence 3 — Both pieces

AFTER THE FIFTH CONTEST. A SECOND DEFEAT. THE REQUEST IS STILL OPEN.

INT. STRONGHOLD STORES / ADJOINING FEED PLATFORM — DAY

The ROADKEEPER turns over a promising length of spare wood. Its hidden end is soft. He sets it aside. The next piece is split around an old fastening.

Wilson watches the useful pile disappear without anyone carrying it away.

The GROOM stands in the stable doorway.

GROOM
There are two under the feed.

The platform keeps sacks above a wet floor. Wilson looks at its straight supports.

WILSON
Let him look.

GROOM
Looking is what I said.

They empty it. Wilson carries sacks low enough to see his forehooves. The groom makes space on a dry bench, then runs out of bench.

The roadkeeper examines the uncovered pieces.

ROADKEEPER
Both are sound.

WILSON
Would one do?

ROADKEEPER
It gets us started. Then we stop for the other.

Wilson points to the rejected timber.

WILSON
Put those under this.

GROOM
We did. That's why I built it again.

Wilson looks at the platform as if he might see the earlier failures underneath the finished work.

At the door, the last sacks wait where people need to pass.

WILSON
Take both.

The roadkeeper stays where he is. The groom holds a sack against his knee.

GROOM
And these?

WILSON
Inside.

GROOM
Where?

Wilson starts to repeat the word. Then he goes into his own room.

INT. WILSON'S ROOM — CONTINUOUS

He clears the low bench beneath the dry wall. The crossed-out banner he chose to keep is still lying there. He puts it on a higher shelf; part slides down. He pushes it back without making a ceremony of folding it.

The groom carries in a sack.

GROOM
The door stays clear.

WILSON
Leave it clear, then.

GROOM
I'm talking to you.

Wilson moves. The next sacks occupy the place where his hindquarters normally follow his turn.

He tries to pass the bench. His hind hoof catches a basket. The groom catches it before it tips.

WILSON
Not there.

The groom looks around the room. Wilson does too.

They put the basket above the sacks.

Outside, the roadkeeper marks the supports. The saw begins. Wilson pauses with the last sack against his chest. He could still call through the door.

He carries the sack in.

EXT. STORES — LATER

The first piece has separated. An offcut lies beside the cart.

Wilson lifts it and looks toward the empty platform. It will not span what the whole support spanned.

ROADKEEPER
Keep the ends?

Wilson sets it down inside the doorway.

WILSON
Here.

GROOM
We'll need a platform.

WILSON
I know.

GROOM
You knew last time.

The smith's nephew arrives carrying his drawing: a duck with a club on Wilson's broad back. Wilson looks at it, then at the boy's free hand.

WILSON
This end.

The boy tucks away the drawing. They carry the remaining support to the cart together.

The groom returns to the sacks. Wilson has not acquired a new platform by donating the old one's supports.

END SEQUENCE 3.

## Written sequence 4 — The part without the procession

AFTER THE SIXTH CONTEST. WILSON HAS BEATEN KEVIN. THE EXHIBITION IS SEPARATE FROM THAT RESULT.

EXT. TEMPORARY FIELD BETWEEN RUINED ARCHES — AFTERNOON

Pale lines cross the stones. Rain has thinned one corner. Beneath an overhang, the line remains bright.

KEVIN stands over a small ball. His own people wait behind red cloth. Across the court, Wilson's visitors lift their Arsenal banners whenever he turns toward them.

Kevin sends a hard pass.

Wilson checks his forward movement, lets the ball travel through the space between the sound stones and turns. His rear feet require room. Kevin goes left too soon. The return reaches him harder than he expected.

He catches it with his foot. His people shout.

KEVIN
Again.

Wilson looks toward his visitors.

WILSON
We said a few.

Kevin sends the next ball toward the far opening. Wilson uses more of the court to answer than Kevin expected. Kevin must turn sharply clear of his equine flank. The visitors roar.

WILSON
Invite us back.

KEVIN
Get off my field.

Kevin's people applaud the answer. He looks at them. The line has pleased them more than anything he has done with the ball.

A girl waits with an empty collecting basket. Kevin beckons for another ball. She points at his foot. He is standing on the last one.

He lifts his foot.

GIRL
Finished?

KEVIN
No.

Wilson frees his scarf from an armor fastening.

WILSON
They were promised a circuit with me.

Kevin takes the ball from the girl. He could strike it into the basket hard enough to end the afternoon with a sound everybody would hear.

Instead he offers it to Wilson.

KEVIN
Come back without them.

WILSON
For what?

KEVIN
For the turn. Before the lines go.

A retainer comes close.

RETAINER
We can arrange a better occasion.

KEVIN
I don't want an occasion.

RETAINER
The children are waiting.

At the back of the crowd, small faces look between elbows. Kevin looks from them to the ball he is offering for his own second chance.

He gives it to the girl.

KEVIN
Let them send it. One at a time. From where they choose.

Wilson asks the adults for more turning room. The first child sends the ball almost sideways. It runs under a bench.

Kevin goes after it. When he returns, Wilson is showing the child where to stand.

The next ball is too short. Kevin retrieves that one too. Nobody shouts that he has answered his defeat. He finishes the exhibition he promised.

LATER — THE SAME COURT

Wilson walks with the visitors. They sing. After two defeats, he likes being the person the song is about again.

At the circuit's end Kevin waits without his shoulder cloth. The armor beneath it is wet. He points to the damaged line.

KEVIN
That goes tomorrow. The covered end will last.

Wilson looks beneath the arch. There is room for passes, less for his widest turns, none for the following crowd.

WILSON
You had another pass.

KEVIN
I went the wrong way. I want to see it again.

Wilson waits. Kevin offers no complaint about the field.

A banner-bearer asks about another circuit. Wilson tells him to wait.

His hind hoof crosses the pale line. A little chalk drags into the wet.

BRIEF RECALL — WILSON'S ROOM

The basket knocks beneath his hind hoof. The groom catches it. Feed sacks occupy the turn. The first saw stroke sounds outside.

BACK TO THE COURT

Wilson studies the narrow covered end. Winning has not given his room its space back. He has no reason to seek another constrained turn.

Kevin holds the ball, waiting for an answer. The hard pass had required Wilson's attention. It had asked something of his body besides being looked at.

WILSON
Without banners.

Kevin nods.

WILSON
Your red ones too.

Kevin looks toward the hanging already fastened beneath the arch.

KEVIN
They are up.

Wilson waits.

Kevin goes to the fastening. His retainer reaches to help; Kevin stops him and draws the loose end through himself. The red cloth comes down.

The visiting banner-bearer shifts his pole.

BANNER-BEARER
We are going around.

WILSON
Take that with you.

The procession moves past. Wilson stays. Its absence leaves an ordinary patch of wet stone around him.

Kevin brings the ball.

WILSON
Now?

KEVIN
They've gone.

Wilson looks after them. He had meant another day. He could still say it.

He places his front hooves, adjusts one rear foot and backs farther from the wall. Kevin watches. He offers neither applause nor an easier version of the turn.

WILSON
Low. Not hard yet.

Kevin sends it low.

Wilson checks it, turns and returns it to the wrong side. Kevin reaches. The ball strikes the base of the arch and rolls between them.

From the far court comes the beginning of the loudest part of the song. There is still time for Wilson to join it.

Kevin puts his foot behind the ball.

KEVIN
Again?

Wilson nods.

The second pass leaves Kevin's foot before the distant applause arrives.

END OF MODULE.
