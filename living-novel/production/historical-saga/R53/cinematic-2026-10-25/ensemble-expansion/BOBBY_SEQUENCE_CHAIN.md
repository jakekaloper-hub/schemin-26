## Why these three moments belong together

Bobby wants people and goods to survive the swamp, and wants outsiders to remain dependent on his knowledge. Sometimes these wants cooperate. Sometimes he uses the first to conceal the second. His body can keep a load from falling; it can also make someone afraid to disagree.

M21 is needed to establish the real price of disclosure: a sound route becomes remembered by visitors. M35 reverses its decision: protecting a confrontation with absent outsiders delays medicine and diverts labor. Its aftermath forces a specific surrender of control over information, with a real paper reply connecting to David. M40 tests the same desire against a person who also possesses useful knowledge. Bobby protects a person after helping put her in danger. Her judgment survives his useful act.

This is a longitudinal character chain, **not proof that the three crossings are one location or that an earlier incident physically causes a later one**. Each unit has its own immediate causal sequence. Separate time/location cuts are required. Removing M21 leaves Bobby's later fear of information escaping less earned; removing M35 loses the public contradiction between allowing questions and intimidating questioners; removing M40 leaves that contradiction too comfortably corrected. None is an additional victory over a villain.

## Sequence 1 — A route given away

**Week2 aftermath. Local rise beside the damaged crossing of M21. No inferred travel connection to later scenes.**

The clerk tries to read a result over the water. Bobby has Jake's boot in his mouth.

Jake follows, one stockinged foot searching for purchase. The retriever splashes after Bobby and gets in Jake's way. Bobby climbs onto the rise and drops the boot. Jake turns it over. Mud falls out. Bobby watches with undisguised pleasure.

A stake comes free below them.

The ferryman's shout stops two handlers with their shoulders still caught in the oil-sledge straps. One continues pushing after the other has stopped. Their load turns a little toward the water. Behind them, covered grain waits.

Bobby looks down the failed approach, then toward a root at the edge of the rise. Beyond it, leaves conceal another way through.

The ferryman follows his look.

“For them?”

Bobby puts a forepaw on the root.

“They'll remember. They'll tell others.”

One of the handlers shivers. Bobby looks at the man, then steps beyond the root.

The ferryman calls Jake's people into a single line. His people place the loads. Visitors follow their marks. Jake lowers his blade and gathers the drivers.

Bobby tests the shoulder before the first wheel reaches it. The driver watches every turn. When the wagon clears the narrowest point, the man looks back along the way he has learned. Bobby notices. He moves ahead to test the next stretch anyway.

A second load follows. Then the oil. The sound changes from men struggling together to wheels moving one at a time.

Later, a permanent-crossing offer lies on a crate. Bobby pins its corner while the ferryman reads. An intermediary mentions payment. Bobby pushes the paper back.

“Then tell me what you want changed.”

Across the landing a driver studies the rise before leaving. Bobby turns from him to the failed stake.

“Not today,” the ferryman says.

The intermediary folds his offer. No agreement accompanies him away.

A worker's bucket of bent nails stands close to the water. Bobby nudges it inland. The worker catches its handle and places it on the rise.

“Here tomorrow?”

The ferryman looks at Bobby. Bobby lies beside the bucket, where the work must start.

**Adaptation boundary:** Jake's separate sketch and root-watermark lesson remain M21 material, not Kes's later sheet. This concentrated Bobby selection omits that subplot without claiming it did not occur. The intermediary is not identified with the later stake setters or David's broker.

## Sequence 2 — The people made to wait

**Week5 aftermath. M35 working landing and wagon path. New location establishment; no matched stake or bucket implying continuity with M21.**

A cord crosses the path at Bobby's chest. The stakes have neatly angled tops. The medicine wagon waits behind him.

“Men from the road,” its driver says. “They had papers.”

The young boatman reaches for a stake.

“Leave it.”

Bobby places his paw beside the cord. He could break it. Instead he faces the road, leaving the line intact for whoever comes back to explain it.

The driver points to the tied medicine chest. Bobby tells him to try the higher path.

Two ferry hands come when Bobby calls. One looks back toward the boat. Bobby is still looking down the road.

The elder wants the stakes left. The younger wants them thrown into the water. The woman unloading lamp oil wants a route available when the ferry cannot work.

Bobby swings his head. His chain drags over a root.

The woman stops speaking. She takes her jars and carries them past him. Bobby has gained quiet. The road remains empty.

The wagon returns. Two sacks have been unloaded; the driver will not tip the medicine chest into mud to make the higher turn.

The medicine carrier climbs down.

“We need the working timber.”

The young boatman reaches again. The elder catches his wrist: removing the cord will let the strangers say they were driven away.

“Tell them I did it,” the carrier says. “Move.”

Bobby steps over the cord before she reaches it. Both men retreat. For a moment he has all the room he wanted. Nobody is moving the wagon.

“Lift the cord. Keep the stakes.”

The young man holds it clear. Bobby sets his shoulder against the working timber. The driver brings a wheel forward. The carrier walks beside the chest, palm on its cover.

The timber moves. Bobby drives his paws deeper. The chain catches beneath him and tightens at his throat. He cannot shift without letting the support go.

The wheel clears.

When Bobby withdraws, the outer end of the timber sinks. The driver keeps moving until the chest reaches firmer ground. The carrier thanks Bobby.

“What about tomorrow's flour?” the elder asks.

The young man lowers the cord. Its straightness remains; the support does not.

### The same landing, later — the reply

A messenger brings an invitation. He has no tools and says he has only brought the paper. Bobby examines him, then the road beyond him. Whether he knows the stake setters is not established.

The elder reads: an account of existing use, examination of the water, a meeting. No order to build.

The younger boatman is ready to hear it. The elder reaches the condition giving the company the findings first.

The medicine carrier asks whether the sunken support will be examined. The messenger has no further instructions. She insists the question go into the answer.

The oil carrier asks about her heavier loads. The elder says she should use the ferry. She asks whether he will carry the jars at a price she can afford when the water rises.

Bobby watches the damaged timber. This time he does not turn toward her.

“We have hands,” the elder says.

“Whose hands?” the medicine carrier asks.

Two workers are already pulling a boat against the current. The young boatman has a load waiting. Nobody approaches the sunken end.

Bobby asks for the company condition again. The elder reads it.

“No.”

The younger starts to object.

“Not that way.” Bobby nudges the page toward the medicine carrier. “Say what you want to know. You get the answer too.”

The elder begins folding the invitation before the oil carrier has finished.

“Keep it open.”

Questions go into the reply: examination of the support; responsibility for damage during that examination; a copy obtainable without visiting the company's office. The elder will not promise to attend. The younger will come without him. A meeting place on the working bank will keep the carts in view.

Bobby recalls the two guards. The stakes are untouched. He sends them to help with the landing, but the ferry needs one of them.

He looks once down the road.

One worker returns to the boat. The other clears light bundles. Bobby goes to the timber. Heavy loads wait. The carrier adds that fact before the reply leaves.

The elder tells the younger that he will not attend a meeting bought by the LLC. Bobby stays low beside the damaged support. He lets the man finish.

## Sequence 3 — Which part she means

**Week6 aftermath. M40 crooked-root basket crossing. Establish this smaller foot crossing independently; it is not M35's wagon timber. Latest v3 controls.**

This root carries baskets in dry weather; when water rises, people use another way. Bobby pins a damp drawing near it. Austin waits opposite him, black armor under the crimson cloak, the Belt visible. Water gathers around Bobby's claws.

“You will put a hole through it.”

Bobby lifts his paw a little. Then sets it down again, clear of the drawn line.

Kes arrives with cut reeds and cloth around her wrist. She sees the sheet.

“That's upside down.”

Austin asks which part.

“Your whole idea of where you're standing.”

She lowers the reeds. Austin reaches to turn the paper. Bobby bares his teeth. The hand stops.

“Do you want to read it? Or keep it?”

Water taps the root behind Kes. Bobby hears it and releases the sheet.

Turned around, the drawing reveals a hook beside the straight line. Kes acknowledges the hook. Bobby moves toward her.

“You showed them.”

“I drew where feet could go.”

She describes carriers asking for a way through. Austin asks who. Her pause is enough for Bobby to take another step. Kes retreats toward the water.

Austin holds the page to the light. Different ink; no reliable order of authorship. He asks about payment.

“For drawing.”

He asks whether it bought information or permission.

“I don't give permission for this place.”

She gestures toward the ground, then sees Bobby watching her hand and withdraws it.

Austin rises. His sword shifts. Kes flinches.

“Neither of us is going to hurt you.”

“Speak for yourself.”

Austin looks down at the fading margin instead of stepping between them.

Kes catches the sheet. The fold tears. Austin retains one half; Kes holds the margin. Bobby lunges toward it.

The bank gives beneath her heel.

She drops beside the root. Her hand catches wood. Her other hand closes on the paper. She tries to stand. Her boot stays under the fork.

“Stop moving,” Bobby says.

“Then stop coming at me.”

He stops.

Austin catches her forearm and lifts. Her body twists against the trapped foot. She cries out.

“Leave the arm,” Bobby says. “Her foot.”

He backs his weight away from the soft edge. Austin places his paper half on reeds and draws his sword. Bobby bares his teeth again.

“For the boot.”

The blade will not fit. Austin puts it aside and reaches into the water. His Belt catches against his arm; he pushes it back to make room.

Bobby takes the other approach, setting his paws on buried roots. His jaws come close to Kes's face. She turns away and shields the paper.

“I'm not giving it to you.”

“I am not asking.”

She keeps shielding it.

“My collar,” he says. “Not the chain.”

Kes cannot release the root and will not open the fist. Austin offers to take the paper. The wood splits farther. Her hand jerks down.

Austin reaches for wrist and sheet. He closes on the wrist.

The margin opens in the water. An inked fragment drifts free. Bobby watches it go, without deciding what it once said.

“Now.”

Kes catches his collar. Austin loosens the boot. Her foot comes clear; the boot stays in the fork. Bobby backs along the buried roots as Austin supports her from the bank.

The root rolls into the run after she is clear.

Her fingers remain locked around Bobby's collar. He waits until she releases him.

### Raised ground

Kes refuses Austin's cloak. She presses her wrist cloth to the cut on her hand. When Austin starts toward the boot, Bobby blocks the approach and indicates another patch of bank. Austin goes that way and retrieves it with a branch.

The sole is torn.

“I can have that mended.”

“Will you have my mark mended too?”

Austin turns the boot so its opening faces her sound foot. She does not reach for it.

He retrieves his half from the wet reeds. The long line remains; the hook ends at the tear. The margin is gone.

He asks who commissioned the drawing. Kes will not supply a name for them to turn into a verdict. When he says he would ask the man, she looks at him.

“The way you asked me?”

Austin offers a later account to someone absent from this encounter. He cannot name a listener she trusts. Kes wants him there to hear, not tell. He agrees. She gives him no reassurance in exchange.

Bobby tries the broken root with his weight. It will not rise. He stops before he worsens the bank.

“Leave a mark,” Kes says.

He looks toward the surviving sheet.

“Not that kind.”

He takes reeds from her bundle in his teeth and lays them across the old approach, pointing toward firmer ground. A branch behind them makes the obstruction harder to miss.

“People will move it.”

“I will be here.”

“All the time?”

No answer.

Austin offers Bobby the paper.

“Ask her.”

Kes takes it and studies the long line.

“I didn't draw that.”

“I know.”

“You know I said it.”

Bobby lowers his head a little. She folds the sheet into her reed wrapping. She carries the bundle herself. When Austin offers an arm, she puts weight on her own foot before leaning on him.

Bobby goes ahead, close enough to hear her stop.

“You frightened me.”

He turns.

“Before I fell. I want you to know which part I mean.”

“I know.”

Kes adjusts her grip on Austin's arm. They start again. Bobby follows her pace along the firmer ground. The branch remains behind, across a path someone else may still try to use.

Bobby's narrow defeat and resentment remain; rescue does not reverse the result or transfer the Belt.
