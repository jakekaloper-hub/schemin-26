## Sequence 1 — The edge he has now
*Week2 aftermath. Country Club of Jackson. Compressed adaptation of M17; staged within the existing proposed-fiction window.*

The club's green is cut in two by the cart's track.

A detached wheel leans against the grounded frame. Farther off, Jordan holds one corner while a grounds worker knots a rope beneath it. His dog watches from sound grass. Jordan's drink is on the earth, tilting slowly.

Under the veranda, members discuss the morning's play as if the only question is where to begin.

Zach stands beyond their shade. His cap is in his hand. The groundskeeper pulls a measuring line through water standing in a rut.

Zach points toward the wrecked azalea.

**ZACH**
That too.

The groundskeeper does not lift the line. He follows a little run of water toward the surviving grass.

Zach follows him, placing his shoes where he remembers the ground being firm. One sinks. He lifts it with an undignified sucking noise. The nearest member stops talking.

The groundskeeper gives him a peg.

Zach places it at the old edge.

The man pulls the line back. Its wet end marks Zach's sleeve.

**GROUNDSKEEPER**
Here.

A hand's width inside. Then another. Zach looks toward the azalea, seeking a fixed point that will make the difference seem temporary.

He sets the peg where the man indicates.

**MEMBER**
Can we start from this side tomorrow?

Zach draws breath. The groundskeeper is kneeling beside the low point, dirty water rising between his fingers. He is not waiting for an instruction. He is finding out what work exists.

**ZACH**
I can't give you tomorrow.

The member waits for a better answer. Zach holds the peg.

Across the ground, Jordan lowers the cart too soon. The worker's sharp call makes him lift again. The rope goes tight; they drag the frame to the margin. The gouge remains.

Zach's line trembles with the pull at the other end. He steadies it. Beyond his hand lies enough room to imagine the course restored. Under his hand is the smaller piece they actually have.

**ZACH**
I still want the flower bed measured.

The groundskeeper marks the water's reach before answering.

**GROUNDSKEEPER**
After this.

Zach keeps his place. The members go inside to change their plans. He hears them assigning inconvenience to somebody, not quite softly enough.

*Exit consequence:* the current edge displaces the desired one and tomorrow remains unpromised. A measurable injury survives the score. Do not follow this with a restoration montage.

## Sequence 2 — The second shot
*Week4 aftermath. Proposed ruined practice court at LOC-RED-RUINS. Selective adaptation of M27; no alteration of its Contest result.*

A practice ball rolls through a cloth chute and stops beneath the platform.

Zach holds the borrowed demonstration club. Its old wrapping conceals a split. His own equipment waits beside a pillar.

Behind the wicker screen, an attendant frees the ball. Zach sees a gray sleeve, then the man's face briefly between two strands.

**ZACH**
Clear behind?

**STEWARD**
Clear.

The steward is facing the audience.

Zach looks at him. Looks down at the ball.

His first shot passes cleanly through the hoop. Applause fills the court. The attendant, now out of view, returns the ball.

Kevin approaches the platform.

**KEVIN**
You've shown it.

Zach hears an exit offered in a victor's voice. He could take it. He holds the poor club a moment longer, letting the audience anticipate another success.

**ZACH**
One more.

At the steward's direction, the attendant turns the hoop for the people on the stair. Zach objects to the changed angle. Then studies it. A harder version. Still possible.

He draws back.

The attendant appears in the opening.

Zach arrests the swing. The ball stays on its mark. His feet leave the platform before the spectators understand that nothing has been struck.

The attendant's sleeve is caught in the net. A shout from the stair makes him step forward. The net pulls the hoop. Its upper fastening slides.

Zach puts the club through the falling iron and braces its head against the lower frame. The shaft bends. Wicker hits his shoulder. He cannot see the attendant's face now.

A second weight lifts the screen: Kevin.

The attendant crawls clear. Zach frees his wrist from the net and releases a club already broken at its old split.

Someone claps twice.

Nobody joins.

The steward begins an account of a fastening that must have worked loose. Zach carries the broken club past him and puts it on the ground.

Under the awning the attendant touches his cheek, then his shoulder. A late guest pauses beside the cushion occupied by the worker and looks for another place.

Kevin orders the apparatus taken down.

**STEWARD**
We could say the exhibition concluded—

**ZACH**
I saw him before the first shot.

The steward turns, ready to use this.

Zach does not look away.

**ZACH**
I asked you. You hadn't looked. I took the answer.

The attendant listens from the cushion. He does not offer Zach gratitude for saying it.

Later, Zach writes on the blank side of the unfinished announcement. His account includes the worker's presence, the unchecked answer, his agreement to continue, the stopped shot, the falling hoop. He leaves no space for a title above his name.

Kevin brings the damaged club. He offers another ground. Private. No audience. They could attempt the shot safely.

Zach glances through the open passage toward it.

Then back to the worker, who is explaining a movement of his shoulder to someone carrying a cloth.

**ZACH**
That wouldn't be this shot.

He signs the account.

Kevin takes it with the writing outward.

Zach retrieves his own equipment. The borrowed club stays with its owner. The attendant stays seated. The dismantled runway occupies the part of the court where guests expected to stand.

*Exit consequence:* technical skill does not erase the chosen exposure of another worker. The intact ball, broken club, injured attendant and retained account prevent a triumphant rescue ending. Further injury and later recovery remain UNKNOWN; this adaptation grants neither instant healing nor universal forgiveness.

## Sequence 3 — Time before the bags
*Week6, after the certified Encounter and before any Week7 outcome. Country Club of Jackson. M41III–IV adapted, with a new same-afternoon working arrangement in the final movement. No assertion that today's wet line is the old cart gouge.*

Place cards spill from a runaway handcart into wet sand.

The caddie runs after it, laughing at the absurd acceleration. By the time a guest makes a joke about the lie, he is kneeling in the mess and no longer laughing.

Zach hears the joke from the veranda. He laughs a little late.

The chosen bank is under water. The steward proposes a nearer edge.

Zach looks at the audience, then at the caddie collecting their names from the sand.

The young man brings the ruined cards back. He asks if the display is cancelled.

Zach starts to call it moved. He remembers a steward facing a crowd while answering a question about a place he has not checked.

He stops before offering a location.

**ZACH**
Where have you been playing?

The caddie indicates higher ground. Retrieves his own worn iron from the service rack. The dark binding is practical, not decorative. Zach had taken it for discarded equipment.

The steward mentions the bags required after the meal.

**CADDIE**
I agreed to those.

He does not put down his club.

Zach looks from it to the bags. This could become a short charming exhibition: the servant demonstrates, everyone applauds, the servant returns to work. He can see the entire generous afternoon before it happens.

**ZACH**
Bring yours.

A guest reaches for his own bag. Another waits to see whether this is part of the entertainment.

Zach shoulders his equipment and walks toward the higher strip. The caddie follows carrying only his own iron.

At the shot, Zach offers a straighter club.

The caddie keeps his.

He shows Zach the dark seam through the grass. Water moves along it. When the ground is wet enough, the ball can follow. When it is not, his attempt fails.

He plays. The ball takes the line, then stops short.

A sympathetic sound comes from the veranda.

The caddie collects his peg. Turns to Zach.

**CADDIE**
Yours.

Zach considers the ordinary route around. A recognizable shot, a manageable finish.

He plays the wet line too hard. The ball clears the rise and vanishes.

One guest applauds before asking where it went.

The caddie shoulders his club. He is already starting toward the rough.

Zach follows with his bag.

They find the ball against coarse grass. Zach sees a gap from his own angle and points it out. The caddie comes around to look. He shakes his head.

With that iron, from that lie, he wants another route.

Zach reaches toward his bag to offer the straighter club again. Stops. He puts the bag down and crouches on the caddie's side of the grass.

They disagree for several minutes. Neither discovers a way to prove his answer without changing the other's shot.

The toast is called from the veranda.

Zach looks back.

Then asks the caddie to try his route.

The young man does. The ball escapes the grass but takes a different turn than either expected. They hurry after it together, briefly ridiculous, both wanting to reach it first.

When they return, the meal has begun. Another worker waits beside the remaining bags. His own tools are wrapped for departure.

**BAG WORKER**
I said after my work. Not instead of going home.

The caddie's expression closes. His club lowers behind his leg.

Zach begins to say the interruption was his. That would be true and settle nothing for the man still waiting.

**ZACH**
Which ones were his?

The worker identifies them. Zach takes one. The caddie sets his iron against the service rack and lifts another. Their game has not made the agreed carrying disappear.

The worker keeps his wrapped tools. He chooses one remaining bag and takes it to the waiting guest.

**BAG WORKER**
This one. Then I'm done.

Nobody enlarges the offer.

At the last guest, Zach has to wait while a buckle is adjusted. His own bag is back at the veranda. He cannot improve the wait with a better shot.

When they are finished, the caddie returns for his iron. He tests the damp binding with his thumb. Zach wants to ask whether they could have played the last ball differently.

Instead:

**ZACH**
When can we finish that argument?

The young man looks toward the departed guests. Then at the steward checking the next carrying list.

**CADDIE**
Before I put my name there.

Zach asks the steward for the list. It is not a vacant afternoon. Names and loads already occupy it.

He does not cross one out.

He brings it back to the caddie.

**ZACH**
Tell me which time you haven't promised. I'll ask for that.

The caddie reads. His finger moves down, stops, goes farther. He names an interval; the actual hour is not specified in this development draft.

Zach starts to write his own name beside it, then turns the list so the caddie can enter his availability himself.

**CADDIE**
And my club.

Zach looks at the iron.

**ZACH**
I was hoping you'd bring it.

**CADDIE**
You were hoping I'd use yours.

Zach smiles. The young man smiles back without yielding the iron.

He adds no promise to fetch Zach's balls or carry his bag. Zach makes no claim on the unchosen hours.

The caddie leaves with his club. The bag worker has already gone.

Zach returns to the veranda for his equipment. There is room to sit now. There was room earlier too; somebody else had been waiting.

He takes the bag and starts after the distant small figure, then checks himself. Their next game has been asked for. It has not begun.

*Exit consequence:* a real game displaces labor, and both players help finish the existing commitment. Future playing time is negotiated **before** carrying work is accepted; no worker is conscripted as substitute and no principal buys a permanent partner. The caddie keeps his own equipment and an unresolved competitive disagreement. Zach still wants to win it.
