## A. What victory cannot buy — after the fifth contest

INT. DAVID'S OFFICE — DAY

A BROKER opens a wetland sketch across the edge of the result on David's desk. David has beaten Bobby's side. The broker taps the result while naming the crossing.

DAVID
How much interest?

BROKER
Enough to bring the carriers to a meeting.

DAVID
That is attendance.

An ANALYST sets down a second sheet: timber, transport, maintenance, and a separate allowance for finding out how raised ground would change the water.

DAVID
Whose agreement?

The broker taps Bobby's side of the result again.

DAVID
The people using it. Not the mark.

BROKER
I don't know.

David looks at the sketch. The drawn route would make his warehouses useful to people who can currently choose someone else's. He brings forward an estimate already approved for warehouse work.

DAVID
The development reserve.

ANALYST
That is the reserve.

DAVID
Put the inquiry under speculation.

ANALYST
The people doing it will still want paying.

David turns the estimates toward himself. The arithmetic stays the same.

The broker offers an announcement: the company is preparing to secure the southern route. David asks him to write it.

He reads the sentence when it is finished.

DAVID
Which word stays true if they refuse to meet?

The broker crosses out “secure.”

DAVID
Keep it here. Send an invitation.

David authorizes the limited inquiry. He reduces the warehouse allocation to urgent work. The second initial takes longer than the first.

ANALYST
What do I tell the warehouse about the rest?

DAVID
Send me their question.

The analyst keeps the reduced estimate on the desk instead of taking it away.

David adds a condition to the invitation: the company sees the findings first.

ANALYST
The people supplying the information?

DAVID
Can ask for copies.

He watches the condition being written. Paying first is still meant to buy an advantage.

The elder does not promise attendance. Their reply comes back with several positions, not unanimous consent. ## B. The work that will wait — later, after the reply

INT. DAVID'S OFFICE — DAY

The warehouse's question has arrived first.

CLERK
Which work are we leaving unfinished?

David asks for the estimate. The clerk has brought it.

The broker enters with a softened announcement. He has replaced “secure” with “discussion.” David tells him to wait.

The wetland reply is unfolded over the estimate. Some carriers will meet. The elder has not promised. The present timber must be examined. Those supplying knowledge want the findings at the same time as the company. Heavy loads are waiting.

BROKER
Pay for the crossing. They'll have a reason to listen.

DAVID
And if they listen and want nothing else?

BROKER
They will have a reason to thank you.

David leaves the price column empty beside that suggestion.

He reads the first-look condition again. A competitor could use shared findings without paying for them. The broker offers the simpler option: refuse the copies; let them refuse the meeting.

David crosses out the company's first look.

The clerk starts gathering the papers.

DAVID
Put the second warehouse allocation back.

CLERK
From the inquiry?

DAVID
No. Leave the inquiry.

CLERK
There is not enough for both.

David lays down the pen. He has asked for the same money twice.

He could withdraw from the bank with a sentence about unworkable terms. The broker has room to write it. David picks up the pen again.

DAVID
Name what waits. All of it.

He signs the warehouse notice before the reply. The clerk keeps the wet ink away from the invitation.

The inquiry remains limited: present use, water, the support already failing. Shared findings. No construction order, no promise that a new road will be wanted.

BROKER
Can I announce the meeting?

DAVID
Some carriers have agreed to hear the proposal.

The broker looks at the favorable result. David puts the warehouse copy over it.

CLERK
Loading cover postponed. One bale at a time in wet weather.

DAVID
The waiting carts?

CLERK
Arrival order. Unless you want someone moved ahead.

David writes a regular customer's name. Pauses with his other hand still on the inquiry estimate. Strikes through the name.

DAVID
Send that copy.

The name remains readable under the line. The clerk folds the notice without concealing it.
