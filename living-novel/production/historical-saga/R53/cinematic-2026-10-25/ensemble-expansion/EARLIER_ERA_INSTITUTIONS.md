## Choice and reason

Develop the **2019 printer and assistant's boundary between compensation and ownership of labor**. M07 already gives the printer and assistant actual choices, expertise, money and unfinished obligations. They need no invented historical-owner association. The assistant's return of only part of Jake's payment is stronger than another generous-principal scene: he calculates the wait as a distinct loss, retains compensation for that wait, receives payment for completed ordinary work, and declines to let either amount purchase absolution. The printer determines access to tools and the limits of the job she agreed to perform.

The hypothesis is that the Commonwealth's practical institutions can emerge on screen through repeated negotiated boundaries: a paid trial, an agreed quantity, a worker's separate loss, proof checking and limits on a purchaser's access to skilled work. **This does not establish the founding of a guild, an enforceable legal code, common wage law or a centuries-old printing office.** The scene tests a particular workroom's conduct. Its later resonance is available without declaring direct institutional descent.

The 2015 clamp loan is a useful earlier pressure, but a weaker next non-Jake scene: its surviving action largely makes civilians react to Jake's generosity. The 2019 assistant has an independently payable order and can define what Jake's money covers. Prioritize that center of action rather than invent an unrelated civilian plot merely to widen the roster.

## Written sequence — The price of waiting

*Proposed adaptation of M07 VII–X, concentrating the ending on the unnamed printer and her assistant. New dialogue and blocking below are cinematic proposals, not recovered quotations or a new novel principal POV. The named principal is present as a customer with an unfinished obligation. No new sporting event is dramatized.*

Jake altered the printer’s block without permission. His reversed arrow required an insert repair, delaying the assistant’s separately paid oil-label order. Three distributed originals remain unrecovered after a defective sheet circulated; the six corrected sheets cannot recall them.

INT. PRINTER'S WORKROOM — DAY, AFTER THE FINAL 2019 RESULT

A parcel of oil labels waits on a dry part of the table.

The ASSISTANT puts his hand on it when the SELLER OF OIL comes through the door. Not to withhold it. To keep it from the wet edge where JAKE is cleaning a carved block.

The printer taps Jake's wrist. He lifts the rag. A dark insert interrupts the lighter wood.

PRINTER
Around it.

Jake folds the rag smaller. The printer watches his next movement before looking away.

The seller sets down payment. The assistant moves the parcel across the dry space and counts what has been left. The labels leave in the seller's hands.

For a moment the assistant keeps the counted payment separate from the other money on the table.

Jake looks up from the block.

The assistant brings that other money toward him. Stops. Draws a portion back and pushes the rest across.

ASSISTANT
You covered the wait.

He indicates what he has kept.

ASSISTANT
That much. Not the whole job.

Jake looks at the returned portion. Then toward the doorway through which the labels have gone.

The assistant gathers the payment for the labels separately. Neither sum needs Jake's approval now.

Jake starts to speak. The printer turns the block so he can reach the ink trapped beside the insert. He bends over it again.

His first pass misses a dark line. She waits. He finds it without her pointing.

At the edge of the workbench are the spoiled proofs. A corner has been cut from each. Six corrected copies lie apart from them; the small square surrounding the repaired arrow is visible on every one.

Jake touches a proof by its missing corner. He does not add it to the finished stack.

JAKE
Three are still out.

The printer closes a tool into its cloth.

JAKE
I will keep asking for them.

She nods. Packs the next tool. The promise gives her no further paid work and returns none of the missing sheets.

The assistant checks the cleared space where the labels waited. Jake's block still occupies the wet part of the table. He waits for Jake to lift it before wiping beneath it.

Jake lifts it with both hands. The assistant's cloth passes through the place it occupied.

No one asks whether the year went well.

CUT TO:

INT. ROOM WITH THE WALL MAP — LATER

MARA holds one of the six corrected sheets beside the sprawling wall drawing. The well and distant road will not fit on the smaller page. The warning fills the place where the large title used to be.

MARA
Will you make the rest?

JAKE
I haven't paid for the rest.

MARA
That wasn't what I asked.

Jake lays the block down. The insert is darker than the wood around it. Beside it he puts the proof with the missing corner.

The money returned by the assistant is still in his hand.

He divides it from the money for paper. Finds the scrap on which he cut the arrow backward. Takes up the practice tool.

Mara stays. He makes a first cut.

The shot does not show another print run, the return of three missing copies or a new commission. It ends with practice that cannot finish the assistant's other work for him.

END OF SEQUENCE.
