## Why the selection changed

Recovery makes a material difference. M07 already supplies Jake's printing commission, expensive corrections, injured trust and work displaced by vanity. M16 gives Phillip an inconvenient picture he lets circulate. M29 has Jake correct an oversized arm and crown on a boy's drawing. M38 has Phillip/Austin's unfinished paired portrait, competing paid work and an irreversible cut. Another invented picture would repeat these rather than connect the history.

The stronger 2024–2025 film is already latent in M12 *The Door After the Crown* and M13 *The Place Left Empty*: Jake and Sorel build a useful working partnership, mistake private mutual credit for public equality, and discover that an order can win a contest while destroying the relationship needed to carry the next one. Austin's parallel movement is not a lesson in gracious cooperation. He wants to win without accepting a lesser place or making his rival the author of his dignity. The film ends with an earned title transfer and an unhealed departure.

**Governing question:** Can Jake keep the people whose judgment he needs while retaining the right to make choices they would refuse?

**Bounded answer:** He can continue commanding, but cannot keep Sorel by renaming obedience as consent. Sorel completes agreed work and leaves his return undecided. Austin wins, gains obligations, and does not become king. This closes the particular partnership bargain without pretending to settle their future or the Commonwealth's history.

## Developed feature movement

### 1. A voice made together — 2024

Start with the existing draft's practical arrangement: Jake cannot decide where to stand for a gathering; Sorel offers a position beside the table where he cannot hide behind it. They work at a piece of cloth together afterward. A relationship begins in shared work, not a narrator announcing a trusted adviser.

Their sentences become popular. A phrase shaped by Sorel travels as Jake's principle. Both enjoy their private acknowledgment. Neither insists on testing that acknowledgment in public. This is the seed of the rupture: Sorel's access and influence grow while his status remains whatever Jake needs it to be in a given room.

Do not illustrate every weekly result. Three dramatic pressures suffice: a welcome that succeeds, a question Sorel suppresses while Jake benefits, and an invitation Sorel makes without asking because he knows Jake would agree. Each moves the boundary of their partnership. Sports chronology remains in the source record; the edit follows changing permission. Their successes do not prove either man innocent.

The championship gives Jake the audience he wanted and gives Sorel a door full of competing requests. Teren arrives with a packet from the defeated side. She does not dispute the result. Jake admits her partly to show he is unafraid; Sorel fears that an evening he has organized will be used against its host. Both have reasons beyond generosity or malice. Teren asks for a reply that can reach her without Sorel's control. Jake agrees before designing how it will work.

Jake publicly thanks Sorel for the food, lamps and room. Applause makes the demotion audible. The man who has shaped access and speech is praised as the servant who arranged the furniture. In private they cannot settle the disagreement by thanking each other again. Sorel brings the full burden of messages. Jake asks him to stay at the door, but bring through what arrives. That is a working arrangement, not reconciliation. The unopened packet remains a promise, not permission for the adaptation to invent its contents.

### 2. Understanding mistaken for control — 2025

The year changes explicitly. The partnership continues because both need it, not because the earlier hurt vanished. Jake asks workers' names and finds that some are taking down barriers while he thanks them. Holding an announcement for the workers only sends Sorel to answer impatient spectators; the aide misses the recognition he arranged. Good intentions redistribute absence.

Commands now replace speeches as the formal problem. Jake first explains too much, then demands repetition, then sends paired messengers. Each system has a cost: noise defeats long explanations; perfect repetition consumes the moment when an order is useful; two messengers carry different snapshots of a changing decision. Do not use one battle montage or exact tactical distances to pretend these are verified sport mechanics. The audience follows a nearby messenger, the visible signal and Jake's obstructed view.

Neris begins acting when waiting would make the order useless. Jake is pleased when this works and still wants to inspect every part of her decision. The narrow victory does not certify his control. When he says he knew whom to trust, the audience remembers his alarm while she moved without him. Trust is something he claims after the risk has passed.

Sorel then commissions the green pennant with a pale border for workers whose contribution is otherwise fleeting. Its appearance is not a reward from Jake; Sorel initiates it. Jake approves when approval costs nothing. This makes the coming loss personal without declaring a feud or turning cloth into a supernatural relic.

### 3. Two semifinals, two kinds of exposure

In Jake's semifinal, Pitts refuses the expected bait and moves toward the pennant. Jake sees another opening. Reinforcing the visible position would spend the strength needed to exploit it. He chooses withdrawal. Neris makes explicit that the people left behind can be taken. Jake proceeds. The film keeps the difference between a valid competitive decision and informed participation: the people beneath the pennant have not chosen their symbolic sacrifice because the commander has decided it is useful.

The result favors Jake. Pitts returns the torn cloth. Sorel listens to a worker instead of sharing the champion's relief. Winning does not reverse the harm; neither does the hurt invalidate the result. This is the feature's irreversible turn.

In the parallel event, Austin falls awkwardly and gets up without converting the fall into a pose. He survives Duckhook by0.40. The source's taunt about how a year can beautify the account gives him a temptation, not a prophecy. He wants the final and receives it. He has learned a form of exposure Jake still avoids: useful action may look undignified and require no explanatory speech.

Between contests Jake tries to describe the pennant's sacrifice as the workers' choice. Sorel refuses to stand beside him for that account. He will finish messages and arrivals; he will not authenticate a lie with his presence. Their conflict cannot be solved with extra pay, a repaired seam or public gratitude. Jake crosses out the false consent. A worker leaves during the shortened address. The film lets the departure occur without a rebuttal.

### 4. A result that does not repair a relationship

Austin refuses the lower seat at the pre-final gathering. Leaving costs him conversations and information he wanted. He is not merely asserting a perfect principle: he knows pride has made tomorrow harder. Jake offers belated accommodation; Austin refuses to make the evening another demonstration of Jake's generosity. They part still wanting to beat each other.

The final puts Jake in a field that changes faster than his instructions. No traitor is needed. Austin responds to small openings; Jake keeps looking for a single decisive reversal that would redeem the whole story. It never comes. The certified result161.07–109.56 is allowed its full size. Jake cannot convert it into a narrow defeat for dignity's sake.

Austin accepts custody under rules that grant no kingdom. His first requests arrive before the case has closed. He is now required to give usable answers about time, place and labor. The film does not punish his victory; its pleasure and weight coexist. In the crowd he sees Jake's unsuccessful gesture toward Sorel. He cannot hear or interpret their conversation for the audience.

The next morning Sorel has completed practical work, including finding, washing and returning Neris's white signal cloth. The repaired green pennant wraps his papers. Jake asks whether he will come back. Sorel has not decided. Jake cannot turn the question into an instruction. Neris is ready to leave. Jake goes with those still willing to travel with him. Sorel's bundle is no longer visible.

That is an ending: the champion has changed, the partnership's old terms have ended, and Jake leaves without manufacturing consent. It is not a reunion, a permanent firing, a promised next-season outcome or closure of Teren's packet. Those remain earned open obligations.

## Earlier history earns the pressure; it need not explain it aloud

M01–06 follow Jake's tools, returns, relationships and shared information through2015–2018. M07's2019 printing failure is the strongest preparatory story because Jake removes a warning for appearance, attempts a correction himself, and injures work beyond his own job. M08's2020 guest and M09's2021 partial witnessing deepen the fact that a truthful account can still serve the teller. M10–11 retain social choices that do not revolve around being corrected: keep pleasure, games, refusals and other people's lives in the historical slate.

Do not simply insert ten flashbacks. The removal test is emotional and specific: without the earlier information failures,2025 command problems read as sudden incompetence; with them, Jake is repeating a known desire to make something act correctly at a distance. Without2024 co-authorship, Sorel's2025 refusal risks becoming another wise worker instructing a hero. With it, the refusal costs both a functioning partnership. The two years can stand alone while earlier works change how the audience understands the recurrence.

## Original cinematic adaptation pages A — The room after the praise

Source: M12 PDF107–109. 2024 after final. Jake focalization; interior venue unapproved. Dialogue is adapted from source, with compression and new blocking marked here as PROPOSED. No new result, packet contents, authority or private motive is asserted as history.

INT. GATHERING ROOM — AFTER THE GUESTS

The applause has ended. A plate taps another plate.

SOREL carries a stack toward the outer room. JAKE still holds the sheet from which he thanked him. There are names written in the margins.

JAKE
Leave those.

Sorel keeps walking.

SOREL
They need counting.

Jake catches up before Sorel reaches the doorway. He is in the place where he admitted Teren earlier. This time he prevents someone leaving.

He notices and moves.

Sorel sets the stack down. A chipped edge faces Jake. Sorel turns it toward the wall.

JAKE
I meant to thank you.

SOREL
You did.

Jake looks at the paper, as if the failure might be corrected in its wording.

JAKE
You were answering for me.

SOREL
People asked where they stood.

JAKE
They could have asked me.

Sorel's laugh is brief and unguarded.

SOREL
You enjoyed three questions. Try thirty.

The packet Teren delivered lies by the untouched cup. Jake reaches toward it, then stops at the cord. He still has not opened it.

JAKE
Was there another message?

SOREL
This afternoon.

JAKE
Bring it.

Sorel does not move.

JAKE
All of them.

Sorel goes out. Jake sits. The room is quiet enough for the opening drawer to be heard.

Sorel returns with more than one hand can comfortably hold. Good paper, reused scraps, congratulations mixed with complaints. He places the bundle beside the championship records.

Jake shifts his cup to make space. It is not enough. He moves the speech too.

SOREL
Do you still want me tomorrow?

JAKE
Yes.

SOREL
At the door?

Jake looks at the bundle, then at the opening beyond Sorel. The question is a practical one. It is also the thing they have been avoiding.

JAKE
At the door. Bring me what comes through.

Sorel nods once. Jake cannot tell what the nod accepts.

When Sorel leaves, Jake takes Teren's packet. A fingernail works beneath the knot. He could cut it. He keeps working.

The lamps burn lower. No answer has been sent.

END SEQUENCE A.

## Original cinematic adaptation pages B — The absent place

Source: M13 PDF123–125; after verified2025 semifinal, before final. Jake focalization. No need to display another scoreboard. The source's green pennant and white signal cloth remain distinct; this sequence never substitutes one for the other.

INT. PREPARATION TENT — DUSK

The green pennant rests on a stool. A pale seam crosses its border.

Sorel gives Jake the short address. Jake reads the first line, marks out the greeting, starts lower.

JAKE
They stood when standing served the whole company.

Sorel watches the pennant.

JAKE
Too formal?

SOREL
They did not know you were leaving them.

Jake lowers the page.

JAKE
They were holding a position.

SOREL
They thought you would reinforce it.

JAKE
So did Pitts.

The answer lands. Jake hears the satisfaction in it after Sorel has.

Outside, a hammer strikes a peg. Too hard. Another voice asks for it to be eased out first. The ordinary correction interrupts the silence.

JAKE
We won.

SOREL
Yes.

JAKE
Help me explain it.

Sorel takes the page. He folds it across the writing instead of revising it.

SOREL
Say you chose.

Jake puts a hand flat on the table. It is not an order yet.

JAKE
You wanted them seen.

Sorel goes still.

Jake could withdraw the words. He lets them remain.

Sorel lifts the pennant and folds it along the tear. The new stitching disappears inside the fold. His care with it continues after his agreement has ended.

SOREL
Do you want me beside you?

JAKE
It would help.

SOREL
Whom?

Jake looks toward the entrance. People are already waiting beyond it.

SOREL
The messages and arrivals for the final—I will finish those. I will not stand there while you tell them they chose this.

He leaves the pennant on the table. Takes neither the speech nor Jake's offered attention with him.

Jake unfolds the paper. He crosses out the sentence that gives the workers a choice they did not have. He reads the next sentence. Crosses that out too.

EXT. GATHERING BESIDE THE FIELD BARRIERS — CONTINUOUS

The small platform has a place beside it for Sorel.

It is empty.

Jake stands before the workers. Neris is among them, arms folded. The address is shorter now.

JAKE
I ordered the withdrawal.

JAKE
I withdrew the support to reach the far marker. I left you to hold that position.

This short proposed adaptation line makes the source choice audible without saying the workers chose it. Jake has given a reason the audience can assess, not an account of their consent. Only then does attention move from his speech to the people hearing it.

A worker looks at the repaired pennant and walks away.

Jake sees him leave. He does not call after him.

The man's departure is quiet. No one needs to endorse it for it to count.

END SEQUENCE B.

## Original cinematic adaptation pages C — Departure

Source: M13 PDF131–132. After final161.07–109.56, before any2026 result. Separate cinematic coda, not the DogTable opening or a rewrite of a closed manuscript.

EXT. DEPARTING GROUNDS — MORNING

Ropes come down. Workers carry away the arrangement that made this ground important yesterday.

Jake finds Sorel beside the wagons. A bundle of papers is wrapped in the repaired green pennant. The white signal cloth has already been returned to Neris; it is not in this bundle.

JAKE
Are you coming?

SOREL
Not with the first party.

JAKE
I didn't mean the wagon.

SOREL
I know.

Jake looks at the careful wrapping. Sorel has not thrown the cloth away. That gives Jake no answer.

JAKE
I need someone who tells me when I'm wrong.

SOREL
You had that.

A driver checks a strap nearby. Jake waits through the work rather than answering.

JAKE
Will you come back?

SOREL
I haven't decided.

The driver asks whether the bundle is going. Sorel says yes. The bundle moves into the wagon; this small question has an answer.

Jake steps back so it can pass.

JAKE
Tell me when you do.

Sorel nods. The nod gives no date.

Neris waits with the final record. Jake unfolds it. The year and result are visible; the camera does not ask a cold viewer to know which score belongs to him. He folds it again.

JAKE
The earlier party can go.

NERIS
And us?

JAKE
When you're ready.

NERIS
I am ready.

He follows her.

They pass the place where the platform stood. Someone presses a peg hole closed with a heel. Sorel's former place has no distinguishing mark.

Jake turns once toward the bundles. He cannot find the green cloth.

Neris calls his name. He goes.

END SEQUENCE C. No reunion, new title, canonical location or future victory is implied.
