# What the Order Costs — ordered feature development reading

NONCANON sequence draft, revision2. Twelve connected sequence blocks; not a finished screenplay or a measured runtime. Source mapping and period geography limits accompany this reading. Current film opens in2024 and ends after2025; earlier history is not silently reassigned.

## 1. Before the answer — 2024

INT. GATHERING ROOM — BEFORE PEOPLE ARRIVE

JAKE stands behind a table with a sheet in his hand. SOREL is untying cloth by the doorway.

JAKE
Where would you stand?

Sorel comes forward. Stops beside the table.

SOREL
Here.

Jake joins him. Reaches back to put his cup down. Finds he can do it without turning his back on the room.

JAKE
What would you say?

SOREL
What do you want them to do?

JAKE
Nothing. Be glad they came.

Sorel looks at the page.

SOREL
Then not that.

Jake looks at his opening: thanks for their patience. Crosses it out.

LATER

The gathering is ending. The people nearest Jake have stayed to speak. He sees Sorel still working at the knot.

Jake joins him. Takes the loose end. Sorel keeps the knot. Together they get the cloth open.

INT. SAME ROOM — ANOTHER GATHERING

A WOMAN who supplies the lamps stands before Jake. Sorel is beside her.

LAMP WOMAN
Before the speeches. While we are still here.

JAKE
I thank everyone afterward.

LAMP WOMAN
We are clearing afterward.

Jake looks toward Sorel. Sorel waits.

Jake draws a line above the opening of his speech. The woman gives him four names. He attempts one; she says it again, slowly. He repeats it.

LATER

People are still taking their places when Jake reads the names. He finishes them and begins his own remarks. The room becomes quiet for those.

At the doorway the woman carries an empty tray past him.

INT. OUTER ROOM — AFTERWARD

The woman is packing lamps. Jake and Sorel stand beside the cloth.

JAKE
I will wait for quiet next time.

LAMP WOMAN
Then wait.

Jake reaches for a lamp, stops. Sorel fits it into the opening the woman has left. She passes him another.

Jake holds the cloth steady. Neither asks him to speak.

## 2. The useful benches — later in 2024

INT. GATHERING ROOM — EVENING

Jake arrives late. SOREL and TWO LENDERS wait beside benches that have made room for a larger gathering.

FIRST LENDER
He said our people could speak first next time.

JAKE
About what?

FIRST LENDER
We were told you knew.

JAKE
I knew the benches were coming.

Sorel's hand is flat on the back of one bench. He takes it away.

SECOND LENDER
When should we collect them?

No one raises a voice. The room becomes visibly temporary.

JAKE
I can hear your subject. I cannot promise that order.

The first lender puts on his coat. The second tells Sorel where the benches must be returned. Sorel writes the place down. Jake does not interrupt the directions.

AFTER THE LENDERS LEAVE

SOREL
They will not lend again.

JAKE
Did you ask?

SOREL
I heard him.

JAKE
So did I.

Two of the people who set out the benches join them. Sorel and Jake take one together. Jake has the lighter end; at the doorway he stops to change his grip. They wait for him. Carry it through.

The room looks smaller without being any smaller.

INT. GATHERING ROOM — NEXT GATHERING

The lamp woman brings three stools. Sorel sets one where a bench stood. Jake puts down another. Some people will have to stand.

Jake waits. The last conversation falls quiet.

Only then does he read the workers' names. The lamp woman hears hers and nods once. Goes back to her work.

INT. PREPARATION ROOM — LATER IN THE YEAR

Lists of lamps, food and borrowed cloth lie between Jake and Sorel. The plans have become larger again. No shot promises that the absent lenders have returned.

JAKE
What do you want from this?

Sorel looks at the lists before answering.

SOREL
To be asked before the answer is needed.

JAKE
That's fair.

They resume work. Here they want the same thing. The agreement has not yet met its test.

## 3. A reply through another door — 2024, after the final

INT. INNER PREPARATION ROOM — EVENING

Sorel spreads three result records across a table. Jake draws the final toward him: his victory over The King. Outside, people keep calling his name.

SOREL
They need to know when you will speak.

JAKE
Tomorrow. Tonight they can eat.

Sorel has taken trouble over his borrowed coat. One cuff is pinned. Jake sees it and says nothing.

JAKE
What would you have me say?

Sorel produces a sheet. Blank spaces wait for names. The last sentence promises that those who stood with the champion will stand together next year.

JAKE
A large promise for supper.

SOREL
Something they will be glad to hear.

Jake sets the sheet beside the result.

JAKE
Let me see who is here.

SOREL
I will bring them in.

JAKE
I'll come out.

INT. OUTER GATHERING ROOM — CONTINUOUS

People make space for Jake and close it again around him. A GUEST touches his sleeve.

GUEST
Now we can begin.

JAKE
What?

The guest looks at Sorel. Before an answer, another person greets Jake.

At the main door TEREN stands with a narrow packet. An attendant keeps himself between her and the room.

JAKE
Who is that?

SOREL
A message. From The King's people.

JAKE
Have they disputed the result?

SOREL
Not to me.

Jake goes to the door. Teren waits without bowing.

JAKE
For me?

TEREN
Yes.

He invites her in. Conversations stop. Jake allows the silence to last a moment before leading her toward the table.

She sets the packet by his untouched cup.

TEREN
Can a reply reach us without passing through him?

She indicates Sorel.

JAKE
I haven't read it.

TEREN
I haven't asked for a favorable answer.

Sorel comes beside the table.

SOREL
It helps if people say why they want his attention.

TEREN
Helps whom?

SOREL
That depends on whether they intend to waste it.

Teren turns to Jake.

TEREN
The result is not disputed. May I leave this, and return for an answer?

Jake says yes. Sorel looks at him. No time or door has been arranged.

Jake invites her to eat. The guest who spoke of beginning has the nearest chair. There is space for another chair, but Jake waits. The guest stands and gathers his coat.

Teren sits. Thanks the attendant for the plate and begins eating. She does not thank Jake for teaching somebody else a lesson.

Sorel gives Jake the speech. Jake takes it.

He thanks the people who made the gathering possible. When he comes to Sorel, he names food, lamps and the room. The applause is warm. Sorel stands in it empty-handed. Jake still holds the sheet they prepared.

## 4. The room after the praise — 2024

INT. GATHERING ROOM — AFTER THE GUESTS

The applause has ended. A plate taps another plate.

SOREL carries a stack toward the outer room. JAKE still holds the sheet from which he thanked him. There are names written in the margins.

JAKE
Leave those.

Sorel keeps walking.

SOREL
They need counting.

Jake catches up before Sorel reaches the doorway. He is in the place where he admitted Teren earlier. This time he prevents someone leaving.

He notices and moves.

Sorel sets the stack down. A chipped edge faces Jake. Sorel turns it toward the wall.

JAKE
I meant to thank you.

SOREL
You did.

Jake looks at the paper, as if the failure might be corrected in its wording.

JAKE
You were answering for me.

SOREL
People asked where they stood.

JAKE
They could have asked me.

Sorel's laugh is brief and unguarded.

SOREL
You enjoyed three questions. Try thirty.

The packet Teren delivered lies by the untouched cup. Jake reaches toward it, then stops at the cord. He still has not opened it.

JAKE
Was there another message?

SOREL
This afternoon.

JAKE
Bring it.

Sorel does not move.

JAKE
All of them.

Sorel goes out. Jake sits. The room is quiet enough for the opening drawer to be heard.

Sorel returns with more than one hand can comfortably hold. Good paper, reused scraps, congratulations mixed with complaints. He places the bundle beside the championship records.

Jake shifts his cup to make space. It is not enough. He moves the speech too.

SOREL
Do you still want me tomorrow?

JAKE
Yes.

SOREL
At the door?

Jake looks at the bundle, then at the opening beyond Sorel. The question is a practical one. It is also the thing they have been avoiding.

JAKE
At the door. Bring me what comes through.

Sorel nods once. Jake cannot tell what the nod accepts.

Sorel returns to the door. Jake makes room for the bundle of messages beside the unopened packet. The task is larger than the space he has cleared.

The lamps burn lower. No reply has been sent.

## 5. The instruction that arrived late — 2025

EXT. CONTEST FIELD — SIXTH TRIAL

A MESSENGER repeats Jake's instruction back to him. Jake corrects a word. The messenger repeats it again.

On the far side an opening narrows.

Jake sends him. The man reaches the place, stops and turns back for another signal. Someone crosses between him and Jake.

Neris calls the near line herself. The messenger gets clear. The opening does not return.

AFTER THE RESULT

The record shows a narrow loss for Jake. It cannot identify one physical error as its cause.

The messenger sits beside a barrier, taking a stone out of his shoe. Jake stops near him.

JAKE
You did what I asked. You should have gone sooner.

The stone drops. The messenger does not pick it up.

Jake sits on the end of the barrier, leaving room between them.

JAKE
I held you.

MESSENGER
Yes.

Neris collects the signal cloth. Jake stays while the man puts on his shoe.

TITLE: LATER THAT SEASON — FOURTEENTH TRIAL

Neris stands with Jake near enough to see the people receiving the signals. Across the field a position can be held or another reinforced. Neris moves to cross.

Jake begins to call her back. Stops with the first word in his mouth.

The near line gives ground. The people Neris took across are there to receive it. A final call. A favorable result by one tenth, identified as 140.74 to 140.64. No intervening result is invented to explain the change.

AFTERWARD

Neris unties the cloth from her wrist.

JAKE
You were right.

NERIS
This time.

He lets her finish untying it. Later, when Sorel asks what changed, Jake says he knew whom to trust. The film has already shown the doubt he leaves out.

## 6. The cloth he commissioned — 2025, before the semifinal

EXT. PRACTICE GROUND — DAY

NERIS brings only the messengers who can leave their other work. Others remain at the barriers.

Jake stops a change of direction halfway through. Turns to the YOUNGEST MESSENGER.

JAKE
The instruction arrives after the position is gone. What do you do?

YOUNGEST MESSENGER
Find her.

He indicates Neris.

JAKE
And if you cannot?

The messenger looks at the others. Neris does not answer for him.

YOUNGEST MESSENGER
Bring the people back.

Jake considers the field they would surrender.

JAKE
Tell whoever you can find that you have done it.

The messenger nods. It is permission he can use, not a promise that every withdrawal will be approved afterward.

AFTER PRACTICE

Sorel waits until the others begin to go.

SOREL
Would you object to something that lets them be seen together? Without waiting for a speech.

JAKE
What sort of thing?

SOREL
I haven't settled it.

JAKE
Tell me when you have.

Sorel goes with the workers. Jake stays. We see the group making its own arrangements, but do not invent or overhear terms the source does not supply.

INT. PREPARATION SPACE — TWO EVENINGS BEFORE THE SEMIFINAL

Sorel opens a small green pennant with a pale border. It is finished. Jake has been shown neither a design for approval nor an account to authorize.

Sorel holds the border while Jake examines it. Sorel's pride needs no speech.

Jake approves. The cloth remains in Sorel's hands.

CUT: the same pennant standing with the workers on the left side of the contest ground.

## 7. The last strip — Austin's semifinal

EXT. CONTEST FIELD — LAST INTERVAL

Two routes toward a marker. One crowded, one apparently open. DUCKHOOK waits beside the open one.

AUSTIN
Move.

DUCKHOOK
You misunderstood the invitation.

Duckhook looks briefly toward the crowded route. Austin sees the glance. Spectators laugh at the waiting; neither man can be certain at whom.

Austin enters the apparently open space.

Duckhook moves away from him toward the nearer marker. Austin must cross a moving line to answer him.

He goes low and falls. Knee into earth; palm slides. The crowd roars. Austin gets up without making the fall look intentional.

Duckhook looks back. Austin reaches the marker during the glance and catches its hanging strip. They hold it from opposite sides.

Austin's arm shakes. He cannot see the arbiters.

A call. He holds.

A second call. Duckhook releases first. Austin nearly pulls the strip free of the post and catches himself.

DUCKHOOK
Fine entrance.

AUSTIN
You should have watched it less.

Some spectators like the answer. Austin looks at the cloth still in his hand. Releases it.

The arbiters compare records. Duckhook drinks water on a low barrier. Austin moves his sore knee, then stops when somebody watches.

The result names Austin the winner: 152.20 to 151.80. He has reached the final.

DUCKHOOK
You could say it was comfortable.

AUSTIN
No one would believe me.

DUCKHOOK
Give them a year.

A worker worries at the knot their struggle tightened. Neither finalist-to-be nor defeated opponent helps him.

LATER, OFF THE FIELD

An attendant cleans Austin's gear. Austin stops the hand when it reaches the scuff from his fall.

AUSTIN
Leave that.



## 8. The pennant and the absent place — the other semifinal and its aftermath

EXT. CONTEST GROUND — DAY

Spectators stand outside the marked boundary. Inside it, workers hold a position beneath the green pennant. This is the semifinal contest. Pitts, Jake’s opponent, moves toward their marker.

Neris reaches Jake. His supporting line stands between the pennant and another part of the field.

NERIS
The pennant?

JAKE
Bring the supporting line away.

NERIS
You are leaving the others there.

Jake nods. Neris signals the supporting line away. Only then does space open on the far side. Jake sends his near line toward the far marker.

At the last call, his company holds the markers it needs. Pitts returns the fallen green pennant; its edge tore when the pole went down.

The result favors Jake. Sorel is listening to one of the workers who stayed beneath the pennant. He does not turn toward the cheering.

INT. PREPARATION TENT — DUSK

The green pennant rests on a stool. A pale seam crosses its border.

Sorel gives Jake the short address. Jake reads the first line, marks out the greeting, starts lower.

JAKE
They stood when standing served the whole company.

Sorel watches the pennant.

JAKE
Too formal?

SOREL
They did not know you were leaving them.

Jake lowers the page.

JAKE
They were holding a position.

SOREL
They thought you would reinforce it.

JAKE
So did Pitts.

The answer lands. Jake hears the satisfaction in it after Sorel has.

Outside, a hammer strikes a peg. Too hard. Another voice asks for it to be eased out first. The ordinary correction interrupts the silence.

JAKE
We won.

SOREL
Yes.

JAKE
Help me explain it.

Sorel takes the page. He folds it across the writing instead of revising it.

SOREL
Say you chose.

Jake puts a hand flat on the table. It is not an order yet.

JAKE
You wanted them seen.

Sorel goes still.

Jake could withdraw the words. He lets them remain.

Sorel lifts the pennant and folds it along the tear. The new stitching disappears inside the fold. His care with it continues after his agreement has ended.

SOREL
Do you want me beside you?

JAKE
It would help.

SOREL
Whom?

Jake looks toward the entrance. People are already waiting beyond it.

SOREL
The messages and arrivals for the final—I will finish those. I will not stand there while you tell them they chose this.

He leaves the pennant on the table. Takes neither the speech nor Jake's offered attention with him.

Jake unfolds the paper. He crosses out the sentence that gives the workers a choice they did not have. He reads the next sentence. Crosses that out too.

EXT. GATHERING BESIDE THE FIELD BARRIERS — CONTINUOUS

The small platform has a place beside it for Sorel.

It is empty.

Jake stands before the workers. Neris is among them, arms folded. The address is shorter now.

JAKE
I ordered the withdrawal.

JAKE
I withdrew the support to reach the far marker. I left you to hold that position.

A worker looks at the repaired pennant and walks away.

Jake sees him leave. He does not call after him.

The man's departure is quiet. No one needs to endorse it for it to count.

## 9. The lower seat — 2025, before the final

INT. FINALISTS' GATHERING — EVENING

A STEWARD leads AUSTIN past people talking about tomorrow's field. Austin slows to listen. The steward keeps moving and Austin follows.

There are two places prepared. Jake's is raised by a shallow step. Austin's is lower.

The steward indicates the lower seat, relieved to have delivered him.

Austin does not sit.

AUSTIN
Another one?

STEWARD
You are the challenger.

AUSTIN
I know who I am.

The steward lowers his voice. The conversations nearest them stop.

Jake turns from his place. There is space beside him, but no seat there.

STEWARD
Only tonight.

Austin looks toward the people whose conversation he had wanted. They are looking at him now.

AUSTIN
I will eat elsewhere.

He leaves. The steward lifts the unused seat out of the passage.

EXT. OUTSIDE THE GATHERING TENT — CONTINUOUS

Jake catches Austin beyond the ropes.

JAKE
You could have asked me.

AUSTIN
I wasn't offered your seat.

JAKE
I would have moved mine.

AUSTIN
Then they would have watched you be generous.

Jake stops. Austin takes another step, then stops too. Neither has arranged where to stand now.

JAKE
What do you want tomorrow?

AUSTIN
To beat you.

JAKE
Besides that.

Austin looks back at the entrance. Someone who had seen the semifinal is speaking inside again. Austin cannot hear the words.

AUSTIN
You in the field. Not explaining it from the edge.

JAKE
You think that's how I won?

AUSTIN
I want to see when you stop knowing what to tell everyone.

JAKE
I want to see what you do without a last strip of cloth to save you.

Austin almost answers. His hand moves toward the scuff left by his semifinal fall. Stops short of displaying it.

AUSTIN
Come and see.

INT. AUSTIN'S TEMPORARY QUARTERS — LATER

He eats alone. No knowledgeable guest has followed to replace the conversations he gave up.

An ATTENDANT indicates the scuffed gear.

ATTENDANT
Clean it now?

Austin nods.

The attendant works. Austin finishes his food. He has chosen an expense the film does not refund with a better source of advice.

## 10. Many small losses — the final

EXT. MARKED CONTEST FIELD — DAY

Spectators outside the boundary; markers inside. No armies, weapons clash or invented casualties. This is proposed staging of the source's contest action, not a verified physical explanation of the certified result.

A messenger runs toward a lowered signal. Jake raises his arm to recall him. A moving line interrupts their view.

Austin sees space where the messenger is going. He moves into it while it remains available.

Jake reaches Neris and redirects her line. She obeys. Austin does not attempt to hold every piece of ground. He yields one position to reach another sooner.

JAKE
Left.

NERIS
There isn't room.

JAKE
When they move.

NERIS
They aren't moving.

Jake looks again. The opening closes during the pause.

He hears his name from two directions. Goes to the nearer trouble. The farther marker changes hands while he is occupied.

Neris gathers with him at the center. Her breath is harsh. Jake gestures toward the edge of the field, offering her a chance to step out of the action.

JAKE
Come out?

NERIS
Do you need to?

He shakes his head.

NERIS
Then stop asking.

They hold. A movement in the crowd becomes an expectation: perhaps this is the beginning of a reversal.

Austin does not give them one.

He takes the ground left available. Stops when pressing farther would make his next movement slower. He makes no gesture for the spectators to enlarge.

Jake keeps looking for a single decision that can save the whole account. Instead another marker is lost. Another route closes.

The last call comes. People lower their arms. Jake is still preparing to move.

Neris touches his sleeve.

NERIS
It's over.

Jake crosses the field before the result is announced. Austin meets him halfway.

JAKE
You made it difficult.

AUSTIN
Yes.

Jake has no graceful reply to add. Steps aside.

The official record identifies Austin as the winner, 161.07 to Jake's 109.56. The film neither hides the size nor turns the numbers into injuries. Jake looks toward his people at the barrier. They still need him to speak to them.

## 11. The weight of custody — immediately after the final

EXT. CUSTODY TENT — DAY

Two attendants bring the case. Jake stands beside it without touching the lid. Austin watches its movement, then makes himself attend to the CUSTODIAN.

The words establish custody of a championship, not sovereignty. The case is opened. Jake looks down once, then at Austin.

JAKE
Take it.

Austin accepts. The applause is large enough to hide anything he might have said. He lets it.

He turns toward the place where Duckhook waited after their narrow semifinal. We do not supply Duckhook as a conveniently present witness.

Jake steps back. Three people approach Austin before the lid closes.

FIRST MAN
A private audience—

SECOND MAN
The field crews are waiting—

THIRD MAN
We have been here longest.

Austin looks from one to the next. None is congratulating him.

AUSTIN
After the field is cleared.

FIRST MAN
We can arrange—

AUSTIN
After.

The man withdraws only a little. Austin sees that the argument has a later time now, not a resolution.

At the edge of the gathering, Jake offers a hand toward Sorel. Sorel adjusts the bundle carrying the green pennant instead. Jake lowers his hand. Austin cannot hear their conversation. He watches longer than he watches the applauding crowd.

CUSTODIAN
Where shall it be kept?

Austin looks back into the case. The question needs a place somebody can find.

AUSTIN
The place set aside for me here at the grounds.

The custodian closes the lid. Austin takes one handle. An attendant takes the other.

The lift changes Austin's stance. He waits until they have the weight together before moving.

## 12. Departure — the following morning

EXT. DEPARTING GROUNDS — MORNING

Ropes come down. Workers carry away the arrangement that made this ground important yesterday.

Jake finds Sorel beside the wagons. A bundle of papers is wrapped in the repaired green pennant. The white signal cloth has already been returned to Neris; it is not in this bundle.

JAKE
Are you coming?

SOREL
Not with the first party.

JAKE
I didn't mean the wagon.

SOREL
I know.

Jake looks at the careful wrapping. Sorel has not thrown the cloth away. That gives Jake no answer.

JAKE
I need someone who tells me when I'm wrong.

SOREL
You had that.

A driver checks a strap nearby. Jake waits through the work rather than answering.

JAKE
Will you come back?

SOREL
I haven't decided.

The driver asks whether the bundle is going. Sorel says yes. The bundle moves into the wagon; this small question has an answer.

Jake steps back so it can pass.

JAKE
Tell me when you do.

Sorel nods. The nod gives no date.

Neris waits with the final record. Jake unfolds it. The year and result are visible; the camera does not ask a cold viewer to know which score belongs to him. He folds it again.

JAKE
The earlier party can go.

NERIS
And us?

JAKE
When you're ready.

NERIS
I am ready.

He follows her.

They pass the place where the platform stood. Someone presses a peg hole closed with a heel. Sorel's former place has no distinguishing mark.

Jake turns once toward the bundles. He cannot find the green cloth.

Neris calls his name. He goes.
