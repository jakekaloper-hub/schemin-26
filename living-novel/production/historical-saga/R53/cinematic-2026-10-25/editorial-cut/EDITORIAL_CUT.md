# What remains between us — a three-part cinematic treatment

PROPOSED / NONCANON editorial cut. Primary format recommendation: a three-part limited-series development treatment. This is a reading treatment, not a commissioned series, finished screenplay or measured runtime. Dates orient selected history; action, dialogue and civilian institutions remain proposed adaptations. No historical owner gap, future result, held interior voice or formal creative approval is supplied by this cut.

## Part I — What comes back

### The room and its accounts

**2015. A room beside a public passage.**

Jake has lent a cart borrower four clamps when the man asked for two. Now the borrower brings two back, freshly oiled, although the road has not yet dried and he is entitled to keep them longer.

“I need a price I can meet.”

Jake offers to let him keep one. The man leaves his hands beside it. Another thing to keep is not a price. Jake asks him to bring the next person with a failing axle to this door. A visitor in the passage stops talking. The obligation has acquired another witness before the borrower can work out whether it has become lighter.

After he leaves, Mara stands where he stood. “He was asking how to finish owing you.”

The storekeeper waits to know what to enter. Jake has no settled return to give him.

**2016. The same lending practice has acquired another book and another condition.**

A woman brings her brother to witness a loan of oil. He will attest what she takes. He cannot promise what she will have when repayment is due.

Jake leaves the witness space empty. Without that assurance he will not make the loan.

The woman puts her measure in her bag. Jake tells her it belongs to the store. She turns its dented base toward him. It came with her. Mara sets the actual store measure on the table, its handle wrapped in cord. Jake has been using it all morning.

His apology gets the woman neither oil nor a reason to stay. Her brother offers to go with her. “I came for oil. I can get home.” She leaves him beside the table. When he asks whether Jake will require him to come again, Jake cannot answer.

That night WITNESS is crossed out and RETURNER written beneath it. The woman's requested amount remains; the name beside it is still blank.

Later in the year, after a narrow public defeat, the storekeeper offers Jake something he can act upon: clamps still outstanding at the east bend. Jake declines to collect. Mara writes UNCLAIMED beside the debt. The final result changes neither the entry nor the whereabouts of the material.

**2017.**

The newer ledger has been kept. A repairs column distinguishes a child's accident from a debt, but the lamp it records remains cracked.

The child arrives with a cloth ready to carry it. There is oil at home. The sound lamp on the shelf belongs to another borrower's promised evening.

Mara takes the candle beside her own book and asks for a cup. The child brings one. Jake holds it while she sets the candle upright. When he charges it to the store, she strikes through the entry. “Mine.”

The child need not return what burns away. Mara goes back to her book without its light. She asks Jake to turn the public result board inward for an evening. He leaves it facing out. Helping him hold the cup has not made her agree with him.

### The map that will not fit

**2018.**

The tower keeper brings a split strip of bell metal. The iron Jake offers matches one edge, not the other. There is a wider piece, the last one, and other repairs need it. The keeper accepts the smaller patch and goes to find the rest elsewhere.

That evening the thin bell brings people to the tower with their own tools. Jake watches a woman pass up her hammer, then wait with her hand raised until it comes down again.

A wall map is growing in the room. A fisherwoman adds the stair she uses. A porter draws an alley. A carter points near the well: the crossing is not usable when wet.

The lines crowd one another. Jake offers a clean sheet. The carter has to go. He takes the charcoal and writes DRY ONLY precisely beside the crossing, pressing until the stick breaks. The fisherwoman stops Jake from moving the words to a tidier place. The meaning belongs where the carter pointed.

Jake makes a clean little drawing of the well. Held over the blot, it covers the warning. He keeps it in the drawer instead.

Later the keeper returns. The patch failed. Jake offers the wide iron now; the keeper wants his own tools. He has found somewhere else to work. Jake has lent one of those tools to someone else. Mara fetches it. The keeper thanks her and leaves without promising to return to the committee.

His chair stays empty until another visitor needs it. Jake must move the map he has displayed against its back. The public result behind it becomes visible again.

A girl tracing the map asks who made the crossing safe. Jake explains the dry-weather restriction. Today? He does not know. He moves the lamp until she can read the small words. She runs to catch the fisherwoman rather than wait for a better answer.

At year's end the keeper brings a bell made from patched iron and harbor scrap. It makes an unpleasant note when he sets it on the closed ledger. He wants it beside the map, its cord low enough for someone to call for reading help after the room empties.

The map must move to make room. Mara holds the sheets; Jake steadies the bell while the keeper ties the cord. The keeper declines a name beside the work. When the bell rings, two people look in. Mara shows one of them the new stair. The keeper gathers his tools.

### Twenty explanations

**2019.**

Jake holds a sheet against the wall map. Sideways, it loses the well; lengthwise, it loses the road. He wants something useful that can leave his hand and travel without him.

The printer asks for a quantity. Twenty, he decides. One block will repeat each mark twenty times. He pays for a trial and agrees to check the copy himself.

On the tracing he makes one clean line from the crowded crossing. The printer asks about the little word beside it. Only what? Dry only. Could she leave a break in the road instead? She can cut a break anywhere. Will a stranger know its meaning?

Jake says they will explain when they give the sheets out. She taps the number twenty. He keeps the break and enlarges the title.

The first trial has an accidental pale patch where the ink failed. The next prints cleanly, including the gap he chose. Without the tracing, the intended gap resembles the mistake.

Putting the words back into the block will take more work and more trials. People have already been told the maps are nearly ready. Jake chooses a handwritten sentence beneath the title of every sheet.

Mara offers to check while he writes. He draws the first ten toward himself. On the eighth, the letters narrow to fit. On the tenth his hand cramps after CRO. He slides it beneath the others for morning.

The next day people are leaving the public result board. Jake hurries the first stack into the passage, answering a question about his defeat with a question about where the reader needs to go. When the printer arrives with the second ten, the first ten are gone.

He remembers seven recipients. Two sheets were taken for others. The last man gave no name. Asking for them back discovers how far a useful object can travel without its maker.

A carter puts a damp sheet on the table. Its warning ends after CRO. Jake offers a corrected copy. The carter has a wheel sunk to the hub. The driver he sent had never seen the wall.

Two sacks are wet. Jake wants the man to come back after he has answered people at the board. “The grain will still be wet.” Jake takes his coat.

Seven original sheets are recovered; three remain missing. For three evenings Jake separates grain with the carter. Paying for the whole load would not save the portion still usable. He works, then pays for what must be thrown away. When he offers a new proof, the carter declines: he wrote it clearly the first time.

A prospective patron will pay for paper if his generosity is named. Every arrangement makes the warning look like a qualification to the gift. Jake turns the sheet over and draws the crossing alone at a larger scale. The patron will not fund that sheet. Jake sells the clasp of his coat and pays for the smaller run.

One evening the printer is gone, her request for more time beside the new block. Jake has already said it is almost finished. He turns her request facedown. An arrow looks simple enough to cut himself.

In the morning its impression points the wrong way. The assistant puts both hands behind his back when the printer asks who touched it. Jake admits he did. He offers to help repair it. She tells him to sweep.

The insert takes time from an oil-label order the assistant needs the printer to finish. He is paid upon delivery. Jake leaves compensation on the table where the boy can decide whether to take it; he is not invited to remain for supper.

By the final result there are six corrected copies, separate from the ten distributed originals. The repaired square is visible around the arrow. The assistant has cut corners from spoiled proofs so they cannot be mistaken for finished sheets.

The oil seller collects the labels and pays. The assistant counts that payment, then returns part of Jake's earlier money. He keeps the amount covering the wait, not the whole job. Jake has no decision left to make about either sum.

The three originals are still out. He promises to keep asking. The printer packs her tools. At the wall Mara asks if he will make the rest of the map. He has not paid for it. That was not her question.

He lays the block beside a spoiled proof and begins again on practice wood, with an arrow.

### A visit, not a return

**2020.**

The driver arrives with a folded original map. A woman had used it in a parcel of thread. Its handwritten warning is complete; the old ambiguous break remains. Jake places it beside seven recovered sheets. Eight back. Two still missing.

He offers a corrected copy. The driver declines.

Their difficulty has become larger than the map. Jake has repeated a private story after promising not to. He admits it, but the errand does not buy him a renewed invitation or an answer about forgiveness.

They do eat together later. Jake puts out beans, bread and water before the light goes. The driver has a load to rearrange and work after dark. When Jake mentions the missing maps, the driver has no more news. Jake wanted something they could talk about together.

“Ask about the load.”

Jake suggests turning the long piece. The driver has tried. The ordinary problem remains his own. He leaves with beans in the bowl and tells Jake not to repeat this account either. Jake washes the bowls after he has gone.

**2021.**

A cart wheel catches broken paving. Jake and the driver lift at different counts, laugh, put it down and try again. Jake sees an empty bed when the wheel clears.

Later the driver asks him to say so. A seller says four bundles were sent; the recipient says three arrived. The driver's fee is withheld. He asked Jake to describe the cart, not spend the confidence shared over supper.

Jake nevertheless offers to stand by the delivery. Asked when he saw the cart, he discovers that its relation to the load comes from what the driver told him afterward. Faced with similar cord, he cannot identify the one he saw.

When the seller, recipient and driver meet with him again, he withdraws the assurance. He saw an empty cart while they lifted it. He saw neither loading nor unloading. Yes, he believes the driver. His belief is not another thing he witnessed.

The opposed counts stay on the table.

The driver later accepts a smaller payment negotiated on his own terms: settlement of the fee, not agreement about the count. Jake moves to supply the difference. The driver covers the paper. He is telling him, not asking him to finish it.

The seller will not let Jake remove the first assurance from the record. The right words must remain beside the wrong. The driver wants that overstatement kept too. Jake cannot give either reader a cleaner document by himself.

### Something worth playing

**2022.**

While retrieving a small borrowed counter from beneath a chest, Jake stops sharply and rests his hand on the lid. Afterward, simple movements are uncomfortable. An employed woman brings water and sweeps; the printer shares food; the assistant helps with a coat. Each has work elsewhere.

Jake offers money for more visits than the woman agreed to provide. She takes her usual payment. “I have not offered those hours.” When he cancels and then needs her again, his former time belongs to another customer. The later visit changes when he can eat.

The printer sends beans and greens. Jake's grateful praise brings more greens he does not want. He admits it. She is making her own food, not a special supper. They divide the portions; she keeps the greens and her bowl. Later, when he offers work or paid hands to repay her, her arrangements are already made. She wants the little dish back.

At the end of the year the assistant asks for his counter. Jake returns it without explaining what retrieving it cost. The invitation comes afterward: will he come to their game?

“Would I have to do anything?”

“That is usually the point.”

**2023. A communal game room.**

Jake considers bringing bread or a better lamp. He brings neither.

A woman sorts blue counters. A man holds a red one in his teeth while looking under a chair. It is his, he says. It will be hers if he loses. He takes it out of his mouth. They are not playing for ownership of the counters.

She explains the game: advance two pieces to the far edge; a piece driven off cannot return until an empty turn. Jake loses quickly. He asks for another game. The assistant, busy at another board, has not watched him lose.

On a later visit Jake introduces himself as an easy win. The other player makes a mistake. Jake advances, then starts to turn it into a joke. “Let me finish.” The man works out a block and wins with Jake's second counter one space short. They test another move before packing up. The man discovers the answer and taps the table in delight. Jake has enjoyed making him work.

At home the driver has twenty minutes. Jake divides the buttons he has been using to practice. The driver asks a rule twice, then makes a move Jake did not expect. Is it good? Jake does not know yet.

They do not finish. The driver must leave. Jake offers to preserve the position. “Don't keep your table for me.” He clears it.

At the communal table the assistant eventually beats him in a full game. Jake's mistake took three good moves to exploit. He begins to name the mistake, then sees the young man studying both counters across the far edge.

“You saw it.”

“Eventually.”

They leave the pieces while they drink.

On the last selected evening Jake plays the woman again. Ordinary rules. She is tired; for a moment he is pleased by the chance of an easier opponent. The game stays close. He advances instead of driving off her piece. Three turns later she wins.

“Again?”

“Not tonight.”

The assistant has time. Jake turns the board toward him. The young man makes the first move. Jake has an answer ready, and looks again before using it.

## Part II — The place beside him

### 1. Before the answer

**2024. An unnamed gathering room.**

The gatherings accompany an annual public championship: spectators watch contestants hold marked field positions until official calls end play.

Jake stands with a speech while Sorel unties cloth by the doorway. Jake asks where he would stand. Sorel tries a place; Jake joins him and finds he can reach his cup without turning his back on the room.

“What would you say?”

“What do you want them to do?”

“Nothing. Be glad they came.”

Sorel looks at the opening.

“Then not that.”

Jake crosses it out. Later he leaves the people gathered around him to help Sorel undo the stubborn knot. One keeps the knot, the other the loose end. They get it open.

At another gathering a woman supplying lamps asks Jake to name the workers before the speeches. He usually thanks them afterward.

“We are clearing afterward.”

She gives him four names. He needs one repeated. He reads them while people are still talking; his own remarks receive the quiet. Afterward he finds her packing lamps and promises to wait next time.

“Then wait.”

Sorel fits a lamp into its allotted space. Jake holds the cloth steady.

#### The bargain Sorel made

Later, two lenders wait beside benches that have enlarged the gathering. Sorel promised their people first speaking place next time. Jake knew only that the benches were coming.

“When should we collect them?”

Jake offers to hear their subject but will not promise its place. The lenders give Sorel the return directions. He writes them down. After they leave, he says they will not lend again.

“Did you ask?”

“I heard him.”

“So did I.”

Jake and Sorel carry a bench out together. At the door Jake has to change his grip; everyone waits. The next gathering has stools and people standing. Jake waits until conversation ends before reading the workers' names. The lamp woman hears hers, nods and resumes work.

Later, among larger plans, Jake asks Sorel what he wants.

“To be asked before the answer is needed.”

“That's fair.”

They return to work they both want to do.

### 2. The door after the crown

**After the2024 final. Jake has defeated The King.**

Outside the preparation room, people call Jake's name. Sorel brings a speech with spaces for the workers' names and a promise that those beside the champion will stand together next year. His borrowed coat has a pinned cuff.

“A large promise for supper,” Jake says.

“Something they will be glad to hear.”

Jake goes out himself rather than waiting for Sorel to bring selected guests in. Somebody touches his sleeve: now they can begin. Begin what? The guest looks toward Sorel; another greeting interrupts.

Teren waits at the door with a packet from The King's people. Sorel has heard no dispute of the result. Jake admits her. She sets the packet beside his untouched cup.

“Can a reply reach us without passing through him?”

Jake has not read the packet.

“I haven't asked for a favorable answer.”

Sorel says people should explain why they want Jake's attention.

“Helps whom?”

“That depends on whether they intend to waste it.”

Teren asks Jake whether she can leave the message and return. He says yes without arranging a time or door. He invites her to eat. A guest relinquishes the nearest chair. She thanks the attendant for the plate.

Jake reads the speech. When he names Sorel, he thanks him for food, lamps and the room. The applause is warm. Sorel stands empty-handed; Jake holds their prepared words.

Afterward Sorel carries plates away. Jake tells him to leave them.

“They need counting.”

Jake follows, briefly blocking the doorway, then moves aside.

“I meant to thank you.”

“You did.”

“You were answering for me.”

“People asked where they stood.”

“They could have asked me.”

Sorel laughs once.

“You enjoyed three questions. Try thirty.”

Jake asks for the messages. All of them. Sorel returns with more than one hand can comfortably hold. Jake moves his cup, then the speech. The packet is still unopened.

“Do you still want me tomorrow?”

“Yes.”

“At the door?”

“At the door. Bring me what comes through.”

Sorel returns to the doorway. The lamps burn lower beside the backlog. No reply has been sent.

### 3. Permission that can be used

**2025. Contest field, then later occasions that season.**

Jake makes a messenger repeat an instruction, corrects a word, asks again. Across the field an opening narrows. Sent at last, the messenger stops for another signal. Someone passes between them. Neris calls the near line herself and gets the man clear. The opportunity does not return.

After the narrow loss, Jake finds the messenger removing a stone from his shoe.

“You did what I asked. You should have gone sooner.”

The man drops the stone. Jake sits, leaving space.

“I held you.”

“Yes.”

Later that season Neris moves to reinforce a position. Jake starts to recall her, then stops. A narrow victory follows.

“You were right.”

“This time.”

Before the semifinal, Neris brings only messengers free of other work. Jake asks the youngest what to do when instructions arrive after the position has gone. Find Neris. And if she cannot be found?

“Bring the people back.”

Jake considers the ground that would be surrendered.

“Tell whoever you can find that you have done it.”

Sorel later asks about something that would let the workers be seen together without waiting for a speech. Jake asks him to say when he has decided. Two evenings before the semifinal Sorel presents a finished green pennant with a pale border. Jake did not approve its design or authorize its account. He approves the cloth now. It stays in Sorel's hands.

### 4. Two semifinals

**The semifinal round.**

Austin faces Duckhook at an apparently open route to a marker. Duckhook's glance toward the crowded route may be an invitation or a trap. Austin enters the open space. Duckhook moves toward the nearer marker; Austin crosses the moving line to answer him and falls.

Duckhook looks back. Austin gets up and reaches the hanging strip during the glance. They hold opposite ends. At the second call Duckhook releases. Austin almost pulls the strip from its post.

“Fine entrance.”

“You should have watched it less.”

The result names Austin the winner by a narrow margin. A worker struggles with the knot they tightened. Neither contestant helps. Later Austin stops an attendant cleaning the scuff from his fall.

“Leave that.”

#### Jake's semifinal

Workers hold a position beneath Sorel's green pennant. Pitts moves toward it. Neris asks Jake about the pennant.

“Bring the supporting line away.”

“You are leaving the others there.”

Jake nods. She withdraws the support. Only then does space open elsewhere; Jake sends his near line toward the far marker.

His side wins. Pitts returns the fallen pennant, torn at the edge. Sorel listens to one of the abandoned workers instead of turning toward the cheers.

In the preparation tent, the repaired pennant lies on a stool. Jake tries the address Sorel brought.

“They stood when standing served the whole company.”

“They did not know you were leaving them.”

“They were holding a position.”

“They thought you would reinforce it.”

“So did Pitts.”

Jake hears his satisfaction after Sorel has heard it. A hammer outside strikes a peg too hard. Someone asks for it to be eased out first.

“We won,” Jake says. “Help me explain it.”

Sorel folds the page across its writing.

“Say you chose.”

Jake puts his hand on the table.

“You wanted them seen.”

Sorel goes still. Jake does not withdraw the words. Sorel folds the pennant along its tear, hiding the stitching while protecting it.

“Do you want me beside you?”

“It would help.”

“Whom?”

Sorel will finish messages and arrivals for the final. He will not stand beside Jake while Jake says the workers chose this.

At the address, Sorel's place is empty. Jake says he ordered the withdrawal and left them holding. One worker looks at the repaired cloth and walks away. The admission does not bring either person back.

### 5. The lower seat

**Before the final.**

A steward leads Austin past conversations he wants to hear. Two places await the finalists. Jake's is raised by a shallow step; Austin's is lower.

“You are the challenger.”

“I know who I am.”

Austin chooses to eat elsewhere. Jake follows him beyond the tent ropes.

“You could have asked me.”

“I wasn't offered your seat.”

“I would have moved mine.”

“Then they would have watched you be generous.”

Neither knows where to stand now. Jake asks what Austin wants tomorrow besides victory.

“You in the field. Not explaining it from the edge.”

“You think that's how I won?”

“I want to see when you stop knowing what to tell everyone.”

Jake wants to see Austin without a last strip of cloth to save him. Austin's hand approaches the scuff, then stops.

“Come and see.”

He eats alone. No guest follows to refund the conversations he gave up. When the attendant asks about the scuffed gear, he permits its cleaning.

### 6. Custody and departure

**The final, then its immediate aftermath and the following morning.**

Jake redirects Neris while Austin uses spaces before they close. Jake raises an arm to recall a messenger; a moving line interrupts their view. Austin yields one position to reach another sooner.

“Left,” Jake tells Neris.

“There isn't room.”

“When they move.”

“They aren't moving.”

One marker changes hands while Jake attends to trouble closer to him. At the center, Neris breathes hard. Jake gestures toward the edge, offering rest.

“Come out?”

“Do you need to?”

He shakes his head.

“Then stop asking.”

They hold. The audience expects a reversal. Austin does not supply one. Jake keeps looking for a decision that can save the whole account; another route closes. At the last call he is still preparing to move. Neris touches his sleeve.

“It's over.”

Jake crosses to Austin before the announcement.

“You made it difficult.”

“Yes.”

Austin wins decisively.

At the custody tent, the case opens. Jake tells Austin to take it. The formal words establish a championship, not sovereignty. Three requests reach Austin before the lid closes: private audience, field crews, a person who has waited longest.

“After the field is cleared.”

The requesters withdraw only a little. Asked where the case should go, Austin names the place set aside for him here at the grounds. He takes one handle; an attendant takes the other. They wait until they share the weight before moving.

#### The following morning

Workers dismantle yesterday's arrangements. Sorel has wrapped papers in the repaired green pennant. Neris's white signal cloth has already been returned and is not in the bundle.

“Are you coming?” Jake asks.

“Not with the first party.”

“I didn't mean the wagon.”

“I know.”

Sorel has kept the cloth. That answers none of Jake's larger question.

“I need someone who tells me when I'm wrong.”

“You had that.”

A driver checks a strap. Jake waits through the work.

“Will you come back?”

“I haven't decided.”

Asked whether the bundle is going, Sorel says yes. Jake steps aside for it.

“Tell me when you do.”

Sorel nods without giving a date. Neris waits with the final record. Jake reads it, folds it and says the earlier party can leave. When Neris asks about their own departure, he says when she is ready.

“I am ready.”

He follows. Someone presses a peg hole closed where the platform stood. Sorel's former place has no distinguishing mark. Jake turns toward the bundles but cannot find the green cloth.

Neris calls his name. He goes.

## Part III — What remains in use

### The account —2026, after the second contest

The championship Belt catches the table edge when Austin sits. He moves his chair back and must lean farther to reach the account. Two defeats have not removed the title. They have made the explanation more elaborate.

The steward reads a sentence praising somebody's contribution, then another making it proof of Austin's command. Austin stops the second. People are waiting in wet clothes while he considers what should replace it.

“Eat.”

A hand approaches the cup nearest his place and retreats. “That one too.”

Conversation begins at the far end. Austin looks toward it, then drinks. The address still waits beside his plate.

Elsewhere, a medicine load is not yet the problem. At an earlier damaged crossing, Bobby plants four paws on the rise and reveals a concealed way past a root. The ferryman warns that drivers will remember. Bobby tests the shoulder ahead of the wheel anyway. Jake gathers the drivers into the ferryman's line. One wagon clears; its driver looks back at the route he now knows.

An offer for a permanent crossing later lies on a crate. Bobby pushes it back. The ferryman will not settle it today. A bucket of bent nails stays on the rise, beside the work still to be done.

### The second shot —after the fourth contest

Kevin's steward has assembled a demonstration in the crowded practice court after Kevin's victory. Kevin moves through his guests toward the apparatus.

Zach, called Duckhook in earlier contests, has seen the attendant behind the wicker screen. When he asks whether it is clear, the steward answers while looking at the spectators. Zach takes the answer.

The first shot passes through the hoop. Kevin approaches: “You've shown it.”

Zach keeps the borrowed club. “One more.”

The hoop is turned for people on the stair. Zach objects, then studies the harder angle. As he draws back, the attendant appears in the opening. Zach stops without striking the ball. The man's sleeve catches the net; a fastening slides. Zach puts the club through the falling iron and braces it. Kevin lifts the screen. The attendant crawls out, and the borrowed shaft breaks at its old split.

Two claps sound alone.

The worker sits beneath the awning, touching his cheek and shoulder. The steward begins a useful story about a fastening. Zach puts down the broken club.

“I saw him before the first shot. I asked you. You hadn't looked. I took the answer.”

The steward starts an announcement praising their honored guest. Kevin takes the sheet.

“The rest of the exhibition is canceled. Nobody has to make that sound better.”

Later, Kevin offers a private attempt elsewhere. Zach looks toward the worker.

“That wouldn't be this shot.”

Zach takes the unfinished announcement and writes what happened on its reverse. He signs it. Kevin folds it with the writing outward. The dismantled runway occupies the space intended for guests.

On another road after his own first defeat, Wilson, one integrated gorilla-centaur with four legs, helps workers settle a shifted slab. The foreman asks for rope and two sound supports. Wilson looks at the work they have just finished.

“For the work we borrowed them from,” the foreman says.

Wilson promises a list. The foreman wants wood before the next rain. The crew leaves with a request, not replacements.

### Where the cost goes —fifth-contest windows

The spare wood is rotten at one end, split at another. Wilson's groom identifies two sound supports beneath the feed platform. Looking is all he has agreed to. They unload the sacks; dry bench space runs out.

One support would start the road work, then stop it until the other arrived. Wilson asks whether rejected timber could hold the feed instead. The groom has tried that before.

“Take both.”

“And these?”

Wilson carries the feed into his own room. Sacks occupy the space his rear legs need to follow a turn. A hind hoof catches a basket; the groom catches it. Outside, the saw starts. Wilson carries in the last sack instead of calling it off. An offcut will not span the emptied platform. Winning again will not change its length.

In a separate covered space Kevin asks Jake what he expected **before** beating him. A folded note lies between them. Kevin offers to buy it; Jake asks to see Kevin's preparations. Neither bargain provides the answer Kevin wanted.

Jake opens the note for shared reading without that exchange. Kevin asks for the crossed-out expectation. Jake thought the failed exhibition would make him change everything.

“I kept the preparation. I stopped the show.”

“Then I was wrong.”

“And won.”

“Yes.”

Kevin tears the new schedule crowded with corrective work. He keeps ordinary practice and leaves a space beside it. Jake asks what belongs there.

“I don't know.”

He returns the note when he has finished. The blank is not filled by an instruction from the winner.

### The cord and the reply —separate fifth-contest settings

At another working landing a cord bars a medicine wagon. Stakes stand neatly in the ground; the driver says men with papers put them there.

Bobby waits for an explanation. A woman carrying lamp oil asks for a route when the ferry cannot work. His swinging head and dragging chain silence her. The wagon tries the higher way and returns. The driver will not tip the medicine chest into mud.

The carrier reaches for the cord. Bobby steps over it. Both boatmen retreat, and for a moment he has the room he wanted. The wagon still cannot move.

“Lift the cord. Keep the stakes.”

He braces the working timber while the wheel advances. The chain catches under him. He cannot shift until the wheel clears. When he withdraws, the timber's outer end sinks. The medicine moves on.

“What about tomorrow's flour?”

In David's office a broker spreads a wetland sketch beside the result of David's victory over Bobby. The broker taps the result while discussing agreement. David asks about the people using the crossing. The broker does not know.

An analyst prices an inquiry into use, timber and water. Its money is also the warehouse reserve. David reduces warehouse work to urgent needs and authorizes the inquiry. He rejects an announcement that the company will “secure” the route. An invitation goes instead, with a condition: the company sees the findings first.

That invitation reaches Bobby's landing. The messenger has no tools. Nobody establishes that he knows the stake setters. The medicine carrier wants the sinking support examined. The oil carrier asks about heavier loads. The elder wants her to use the ferry; she wants a price she can afford when the water rises.

Bobby asks to hear the first-copy condition again.

“Not that way.”

He pushes the page toward the medicine carrier. “You get the answer too.”

The reply adds questions, damage responsibility and copies obtainable without a visit to David's office. The elder declines to promise attendance; the younger will come without him. Heavy loads are still waiting when the answer leaves.

David reads it over the warehouse estimate. He crosses out his company's first look and asks to restore the postponed warehouse work too. There is not money for both.

The broker waits with room to write a withdrawal. David takes the pen.

“Name what waits. All of it.”

Loading cover waits. In wet weather, one bale at a time; carts in arrival order. David begins a preferred customer's name, then strikes it through. He signs that notice before the reply. The inquiry remains an inquiry. Nobody orders a road built.

### The room needed to turn —another fifth-contest window

El Niño's force moves through Brandon's stable street. An awning falls. Hot chili spills. Across the street Vera asks for help unloading a mule. Brandon sees the slipping bundle and takes his own horse inside first.

The mule's crooked load catches in the doorway. Fallen cloth covers its head. The stable hand braces the door and cries while holding his place. The enclosure Brandon has secured occupies the room the mule needs to turn.

Brandon pulls its gate down with the hand's help. He starts toward his horse, then gives that job to the hand, who names tools aloud while moving it clear. Vera cuts her load straps. One bundle drops; another tears at the jamb. Brandon leads the mule sideways through the demolished space. Vera stops him from going straight back into the weather for the load.

Later he brings in the torn blue wrapper. Inside lies her sister's worked cloth. An unfinished bird was unfinished before the damage. His offer of people who sew does not replace it.

Outside, men praise the rescue. Brandon first accepts their laugh. Later he tries to tell them about his horse-first choice and the closing order. Someone insists rescuing the mule settles it. The flattering song survives his correction.

Inside, Vera works without thanking him for speaking. He moves broken timber away from the animals. He has cleared it, not rebuilt the partition. Horse and mule must share the space tonight.

### A place in somebody's performance —later in the fifth-contest window

At Jordan's camp a cart has been stripped of its wheels to carry a throne of empty kegs. Ben declines the offered triumph. “Give me your part.”

Jordan climbs up. Ben's first joke about finding somebody else to do the heavy work gets a laugh. He adds: “Laugh properly. He hasn't let you drink yet.” A worker covers his mouth. Jordan climbs down.

“No second anything.”

The cart pin is missing between the stones; bedding still waits below. Jordan will carry it. Ben offers to help, and Jordan tells him to go.

On the road Ben repeats his first line. He still likes it. The second does not follow. His own work is waiting.

### What the promise leaves —after the sixth contest

Jordan has won, but the cooks stop serving. Senn lays out what her group brought and what they were promised. The sacks by the shelter are nearly empty. His coins are not enough.

“We can carry grain. We can't carry soon.”

Jake offers travelling provisions for part of the shortage, then a written exchange giving him future use of stores and space, even through somebody he sends. Senn asks what happens if the workers are owed when that man comes.

“The claim would be written.”

“So is ours, now.”

Jordan enjoys Jake's exposure until she reminds him that the offer still puts food on the cart. His own rear chest holds food promised to his followers. He drags it into view; the lid cracks. A follower asks what they receive now.

“A bad promise.”

“Your bad promise.”

“Yes.”

Senn sorts the stores. Jordan keeps none from that chest. Jake counts coins for the remaining shortage, then strikes out the transferable claim. He can ask for a meal; Jordan can refuse. Senn's group must be paid before deciding whether to work again. Three copies include the cart owner. Her marks represent her group, not everybody's consent.

The follower pours his drink onto the earth and leaves. Jordan serves singed food, answers a joke too sharply, and moves one platform stone to give a cart more room. The remaining stones and the cold pots wait. He listens for the absent step while scraping. It does not return.

At a smaller basket crossing, separately established, Bobby and Austin question Kes over a damp drawing. She was paid to draw where feet could go; she grants no permission for the place. Bobby advances. Austin's sword shifts when he rises. She flinches. When the page tears and Bobby lunges toward its margin, the bank gives under her heel.

Austin first pulls her arm against a trapped boot. Bobby stops him. They change their approaches: Austin frees the foot while Kes takes Bobby's collar. An inked scrap escapes into the water. Austin reaches for her wrist instead of the sheet. Neither man can recover what the fragment said.

On raised ground, Kes asks whether Austin will mend her mark as readily as her boot. She refuses to name someone they can turn into a verdict. He agrees to be present to hear a later account, not deliver it for her. No trusted listener has yet been secured.

Bobby blocks the broken approach with reeds and a branch. Kes takes the surviving drawing. On the way out she tells him precisely which part frightened her: before she fell. He follows her pace. The rescue has not answered that accusation.

### Company without the claim —separate sixth-contest afternoons

Kevin wants to end the public demonstration and see the turn he misread, but the children are still waiting. He gives the ball back to the collecting girl. One at a time they choose where to send it. He retrieves their short passes while Wilson makes room.

After the circuit, Kevin waits beneath the arch. Wilson remembers the sacks crowding his own turn. The covered strip is narrow too. Yet Kevin's hard pass asked something of his body beyond being looked at.

“Without banners,” Wilson says. “Your red ones too.”

Kevin unfastens the cloth himself. The procession leaves. Wilson sets four feet, adjusts his rear stance and asks for a low ball. He returns it to the wrong side. It strikes the arch.

“Again?”

Wilson nods before the distant applause reaches them.

In Brandon's separate yard, cooled chili spills from Ben's wagon during their show. Brandon settles his horse beyond the food and leaves it with the hand, clear of the still-broken enclosure. Ben takes the laugh. Brandon offers him the other role.

The cook allows the empty tub, not supper. Ben accepts one sleeve of Brandon's coat on condition that Brandon tell the part as Ben. The wagon stands still. When the sleeve catches and the audience laughs harder, Brandon leaves Ben room to answer instead of adding the next insult. The two laugh together. Then the cook needs passage and both move.

Asked about another performance, Ben looks at Brandon. “Not before he asks me.” No date follows. This afternoon belongs wholly to neither of them. The damage at the stable remains outside the joke.

### The sample —another sixth-contest window

David folds a cart charge beneath his invitation. His own arrival-order rule prevented the warehouse cart bringing the sample ahead of waiting freight; a borrowed cart brought it and remains outside, charging for time.

Phillip, called Pitts in earlier contests, stands between two low benches: one reptilian humanoid body, exactly three serpent heads. A maker fits the empty carrying frame. At his turn a crosspiece touches his neck. He could turn his whole body and conceal it. He stops instead.

“The top is high.”

“It keeps the load away,” David says.

“From which part of me?”

After an adjustment the maker permits a small sack. Weight changes the fit. She calls stop; Phillip takes another half-step. A buckle binds. He pulls, a rail cracks, and she gets her bench beneath the shelf to relieve the pressure.

“I pulled.”

“It pulled you too.”

A visitor thinks it might suit an ordinary porter. All three heads turn toward him. “Then buy it.” He does not.

Repair would preserve the standard sample. A different fit would consume it. Two visitors offer to return with somebody David's size. That remains a real commercial option. David looks at the cart note and asks the maker to investigate the other shape.

“Who pays for the trial?”

“If it works—”

“The afternoon is spent either way.”

He writes her price on the invitation. She reminds him that the sample can still be repaired. He clears the cloth from her work.

“Cut it.”

The saw does not promise a product. The visitors leave without an appointment.

Later, the small test load travels two steps and a turn. Phillip raises a hand, listens to the strap, and asks for it off. He does not pull this time. Better, for that movement. Neither can say how much more.

The maker needs the pieces at her workshop to compare the marks. Phillip asks when to return. She will send word when there is something different to test.

David slides cloth beneath what he meant to sell. His invitation carries a payment commitment; the borrowed cart still has to be dealt with. The maker leaves holding the split rail apart so it cannot catch the cloth. No sale remains on the table. A trial does.
