# Part I — What comes back

## Cut

### The room and its accounts

**2015. A room beside a public passage.**

Jake has lent a cart borrower four clamps when the man asked for two. Now the borrower brings two back, freshly oiled, although the road has not yet dried and he is entitled to keep them longer.

“I need a price I can meet.”

Jake offers to let him keep one. The man leaves his hands beside it. Another thing to keep is not a price. Jake asks him to bring the next person with a failing axle to this door. A visitor in the passage stops talking. The obligation has acquired another witness before the borrower can work out whether it has become lighter.

After he leaves, Mara stands where he stood. “He was asking how to finish owing you.”

The storekeeper waits to know what to enter. Jake has no settled return to give him.

**2016. The same lending practice has acquired another book and another condition.**

A woman brings her brother to witness a loan of oil. He will attest what she takes. He cannot promise what she will have when repayment is due.

Jake leaves the witness space empty. Without that assurance he will not make the loan.

The woman puts her measure in her bag. Jake tells her it belongs to the store. She turns its dented base toward him. It came with her. Mara sets the actual store measure on the table, its handle wrapped in cord. Jake has been using it all morning.

His apology gets the woman neither oil nor a reason to stay. Her brother offers to go with her. “I came for oil. I can get home.” She leaves him beside the table. When he asks whether Jake will require him to come again, Jake cannot answer.

That night WITNESS is crossed out and RETURNER written beneath it. The woman's requested amount remains; the name beside it is still blank.

Later in the year, after a narrow public defeat, the storekeeper offers Jake something he can act upon: clamps still outstanding at the east bend. Jake declines to collect. Mara writes UNCLAIMED beside the debt. The final result changes neither the entry nor the whereabouts of the material.

**2017.**

The newer ledger has been kept. A repairs column distinguishes a child's accident from a debt, but the lamp it records remains cracked.

The child arrives with a cloth ready to carry it. There is oil at home. The sound lamp on the shelf belongs to another borrower's promised evening.

Mara takes the candle beside her own book and asks for a cup. The child brings one. Jake holds it while she sets the candle upright. When he charges it to the store, she strikes through the entry. “Mine.”

The child need not return what burns away. Mara goes back to her book without its light. She asks Jake to turn the public result board inward for an evening. He leaves it facing out. Helping him hold the cup has not made her agree with him.

### The map that will not fit

**2018.**

The tower keeper brings a split strip of bell metal. The iron Jake offers matches one edge, not the other. There is a wider piece, the last one, and other repairs need it. The keeper accepts the smaller patch and goes to find the rest elsewhere.

That evening the thin bell brings people to the tower with their own tools. Jake watches a woman pass up her hammer, then wait with her hand raised until it comes down again.

A wall map is growing in the room. A fisherwoman adds the stair she uses. A porter draws an alley. A carter points near the well: the crossing is not usable when wet.

The lines crowd one another. Jake offers a clean sheet. The carter has to go. He takes the charcoal and writes DRY ONLY precisely beside the crossing, pressing until the stick breaks. The fisherwoman stops Jake from moving the words to a tidier place. The meaning belongs where the carter pointed.

Jake makes a clean little drawing of the well. Held over the blot, it covers the warning. He keeps it in the drawer instead.

Later the keeper returns. The patch failed. Jake offers the wide iron now; the keeper wants his own tools. He has found somewhere else to work. Jake has lent one of those tools to someone else. Mara fetches it. The keeper thanks her and leaves without promising to return to the committee.

His chair stays empty until another visitor needs it. Jake must move the map he has displayed against its back. The public result behind it becomes visible again.

A girl tracing the map asks who made the crossing safe. Jake explains the dry-weather restriction. Today? He does not know. He moves the lamp until she can read the small words. She runs to catch the fisherwoman rather than wait for a better answer.

At year's end the keeper brings a bell made from patched iron and harbor scrap. It makes an unpleasant note when he sets it on the closed ledger. He wants it beside the map, its cord low enough for someone to call for reading help after the room empties.

The map must move to make room. Mara holds the sheets; Jake steadies the bell while the keeper ties the cord. The keeper declines a name beside the work. When the bell rings, two people look in. Mara shows one of them the new stair. The keeper gathers his tools.

### Twenty explanations

**2019.**

Jake holds a sheet against the wall map. Sideways, it loses the well; lengthwise, it loses the road. He wants something useful that can leave his hand and travel without him.

The printer asks for a quantity. Twenty, he decides. One block will repeat each mark twenty times. He pays for a trial and agrees to check the copy himself.

On the tracing he makes one clean line from the crowded crossing. The printer asks about the little word beside it. Only what? Dry only. Could she leave a break in the road instead? She can cut a break anywhere. Will a stranger know its meaning?

Jake says they will explain when they give the sheets out. She taps the number twenty. He keeps the break and enlarges the title.

The first trial has an accidental pale patch where the ink failed. The next prints cleanly, including the gap he chose. Without the tracing, the intended gap resembles the mistake.

Putting the words back into the block will take more work and more trials. People have already been told the maps are nearly ready. Jake chooses a handwritten sentence beneath the title of every sheet.

Mara offers to check while he writes. He draws the first ten toward himself. On the eighth, the letters narrow to fit. On the tenth his hand cramps after CRO. He slides it beneath the others for morning.

The next day people are leaving the public result board. Jake hurries the first stack into the passage, answering a question about his defeat with a question about where the reader needs to go. When the printer arrives with the second ten, the first ten are gone.

He remembers seven recipients. Two sheets were taken for others. The last man gave no name. Asking for them back discovers how far a useful object can travel without its maker.

A carter puts a damp sheet on the table. Its warning ends after CRO. Jake offers a corrected copy. The carter has a wheel sunk to the hub. The driver he sent had never seen the wall.

Two sacks are wet. Jake wants the man to come back after he has answered people at the board. “The grain will still be wet.” Jake takes his coat.

Seven original sheets are recovered; three remain missing. For three evenings Jake separates grain with the carter. Paying for the whole load would not save the portion still usable. He works, then pays for what must be thrown away. When he offers a new proof, the carter declines: he wrote it clearly the first time.

A prospective patron will pay for paper if his generosity is named. Every arrangement makes the warning look like a qualification to the gift. Jake turns the sheet over and draws the crossing alone at a larger scale. The patron will not fund that sheet. Jake sells the clasp of his coat and pays for the smaller run.

One evening the printer is gone, her request for more time beside the new block. Jake has already said it is almost finished. He turns her request facedown. An arrow looks simple enough to cut himself.

In the morning its impression points the wrong way. The assistant puts both hands behind his back when the printer asks who touched it. Jake admits he did. He offers to help repair it. She tells him to sweep.

The insert takes time from an oil-label order the assistant needs the printer to finish. He is paid upon delivery. Jake leaves compensation on the table where the boy can decide whether to take it; he is not invited to remain for supper.

By the final result there are six corrected copies, separate from the ten distributed originals. The repaired square is visible around the arrow. The assistant has cut corners from spoiled proofs so they cannot be mistaken for finished sheets.

The oil seller collects the labels and pays. The assistant counts that payment, then returns part of Jake's earlier money. He keeps the amount covering the wait, not the whole job. Jake has no decision left to make about either sum.

The three originals are still out. He promises to keep asking. The printer packs her tools. At the wall Mara asks if he will make the rest of the map. He has not paid for it. That was not her question.

He lays the block beside a spoiled proof and begins again on practice wood, with an arrow.

### A visit, not a return

**2020.**

The driver arrives with a folded original map. A woman had used it in a parcel of thread. Its handwritten warning is complete; the old ambiguous break remains. Jake places it beside seven recovered sheets. Eight back. Two still missing.

He offers a corrected copy. The driver declines.

Their difficulty has become larger than the map. Jake has repeated a private story after promising not to. He admits it, but the errand does not buy him a renewed invitation or an answer about forgiveness.

They do eat together later. Jake puts out beans, bread and water before the light goes. The driver has a load to rearrange and work after dark. When Jake mentions the missing maps, the driver has no more news. Jake wanted something they could talk about together.

“Ask about the load.”

Jake suggests turning the long piece. The driver has tried. The ordinary problem remains his own. He leaves with beans in the bowl and tells Jake not to repeat this account either. Jake washes the bowls after he has gone.

**2021.**

A cart wheel catches broken paving. Jake and the driver lift at different counts, laugh, put it down and try again. Jake sees an empty bed when the wheel clears.

Later the driver asks him to say so. A seller says four bundles were sent; the recipient says three arrived. The driver's fee is withheld. He asked Jake to describe the cart, not spend the confidence shared over supper.

Jake nevertheless offers to stand by the delivery. Asked when he saw the cart, he discovers that its relation to the load comes from what the driver told him afterward. Faced with similar cord, he cannot identify the one he saw.

When the seller, recipient and driver meet with him again, he withdraws the assurance. He saw an empty cart while they lifted it. He saw neither loading nor unloading. Yes, he believes the driver. His belief is not another thing he witnessed.

The opposed counts stay on the table.

The driver later accepts a smaller payment negotiated on his own terms: settlement of the fee, not agreement about the count. Jake moves to supply the difference. The driver covers the paper. He is telling him, not asking him to finish it.

The seller will not let Jake remove the first assurance from the record. The right words must remain beside the wrong. The driver wants that overstatement kept too. Jake cannot give either reader a cleaner document by himself.

### Something worth playing

**2022.**

While retrieving a small borrowed counter from beneath a chest, Jake stops sharply and rests his hand on the lid. Afterward, simple movements are uncomfortable. An employed woman brings water and sweeps; the printer shares food; the assistant helps with a coat. Each has work elsewhere.

Jake offers money for more visits than the woman agreed to provide. She takes her usual payment. “I have not offered those hours.” When he cancels and then needs her again, his former time belongs to another customer. The later visit changes when he can eat.

The printer sends beans and greens. Jake's grateful praise brings more greens he does not want. He admits it. She is making her own food, not a special supper. They divide the portions; she keeps the greens and her bowl. Later, when he offers work or paid hands to repay her, her arrangements are already made. She wants the little dish back.

At the end of the year the assistant asks for his counter. Jake returns it without explaining what retrieving it cost. The invitation comes afterward: will he come to their game?

“Would I have to do anything?”

“That is usually the point.”

**2023. A communal game room.**

Jake considers bringing bread or a better lamp. He brings neither.

A woman sorts blue counters. A man holds a red one in his teeth while looking under a chair. It is his, he says. It will be hers if he loses. He takes it out of his mouth. They are not playing for ownership of the counters.

She explains the game: advance two pieces to the far edge; a piece driven off cannot return until an empty turn. Jake loses quickly. He asks for another game. The assistant, busy at another board, has not watched him lose.

On a later visit Jake introduces himself as an easy win. The other player makes a mistake. Jake advances, then starts to turn it into a joke. “Let me finish.” The man works out a block and wins with Jake's second counter one space short. They test another move before packing up. The man discovers the answer and taps the table in delight. Jake has enjoyed making him work.

At home the driver has twenty minutes. Jake divides the buttons he has been using to practice. The driver asks a rule twice, then makes a move Jake did not expect. Is it good? Jake does not know yet.

They do not finish. The driver must leave. Jake offers to preserve the position. “Don't keep your table for me.” He clears it.

At the communal table the assistant eventually beats him in a full game. Jake's mistake took three good moves to exploit. He begins to name the mistake, then sees the young man studying both counters across the far edge.

“You saw it.”

“Eventually.”

They leave the pieces while they drink.

On the last selected evening Jake plays the woman again. Ordinary rules. She is tired; for a moment he is pleased by the chance of an easier opponent. The game stays close. He advances instead of driving off her piece. Three turns later she wins.

“Again?”

“Not tonight.”

The assistant has time. Jake turns the board toward him. The young man makes the first move. Jake has an answer ready, and looks again before using it.
