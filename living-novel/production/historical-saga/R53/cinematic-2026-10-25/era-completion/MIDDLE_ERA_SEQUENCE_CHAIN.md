## Sequence 1 — Three received, four sent

**2021. Unnamed street, then separate unnamed rooms. Source M09 I–IV, VIII, X–XII. Selected omissions do not cancel the intervening failed offers.**

A cart wheel catches a broken edge of paving. Jake gets his shoulder under the low rail. The driver counts too fast. They lift at different moments and have to put it down.

The driver laughs. Jake gives him a look, moves a loose cord out of his grip, and makes him count again. This time the wheel clears. The empty bed knocks against its frame as the driver goes on.

CUT: later. The driver sits in his coat opposite Jake.

“What was in it?”

“Cord.”

“Anything else?”

Jake shakes his head.

The driver explains the held fee. Four bundles sent, the seller says. Three received, the woman says. The driver says he unloaded what he had been given.

“Will you tell them you saw it empty?”

Jake reaches for paper before asking where to go.

“I can make them listen.”

“I asked if you would tell them.”

The driver stops Jake's writing hand.

“Only that. Nothing from supper.”

Jake sets down the pen.

CUT: seller's table. Three samples of cloth lie in front of the seller. They are not the disputed goods.

“Before he collected from me or after?”

“After.”

“How do you know?”

Jake describes the broken paving, the lift, the cord. The seller waits until the description runs out.

“He told me afterward.”

“Then you know he told you.”

CUT: a further meeting. The recipient places a bundle cord in a circle on the table. Jake looks at its twist. The seller asks what he can actually stand by.

“He brought an empty cart back. I can stand by his delivery.”

“You saw him deliver?” the woman asks.

Jake starts explaining what a man hiding cloth would or would not do. The woman closes her hand over the cord. She has gained no new count from his confidence.

CUT: beside the driver's things. Two ordinary lengths of similar cord lie beneath Jake's hand.

“Which one?” the driver asks.

“I cannot tell.”

“Neither can I.”

Jake admits what he has offered to stand by.

“I asked about the cart.”

CUT: the later hearing. No heraldry, bench of judges or invented legal office. The original tally says four sent. The woman's note says three received. Both stay on the table.

The seller asks whether Jake stands by his earlier words.

“No.”

The room stills before he can qualify it.

“I saw the cart empty when we lifted its wheel. I did not see where it had been loaded. I did not see it unloaded.”

The seller asks when. Before Jake's first season result; he cannot supply an hour. The delivery connection came from the driver afterward. The woman asks whether Jake believes him.

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Jake looks at the driver. The private supper is available to him as an impressive answer. He does not tell it.

“My belief isn't another thing I saw.”

The woman dates the corrected statement, leaving its original assurance legible. The opposed counts remain. Nobody produces the fourth bundle, the fee or a verdict.

CUT: late-season evening, Jake's room. Two bowls are out. The driver places an acknowledgment beside Jake's statement.

“I took it.”

“The whole fee?”

“The smaller amount. With the words saying it settles my fee. Not the count.”

Jake reads. A phrase calling the delivery incomplete is crossed out; the seller has initialed the change. This concession belongs to the driver's negotiation, not to a hidden solution Jake secured offscreen.

“Was it enough?”

“No.”

Jake moves as if to offer the difference. The driver puts his hand over the acknowledgment.

“I am telling you. I am not asking you to finish it.”

They sit until a lamp is needed. The driver folds his paper and keeps it. Jake keeps his own statement. Asked about eating, the driver declines tonight.

Jake puts one bowl away.

Jake copies both. The driver's later request that the overstatement remain gives the record two interested readers, neither a convenient prosecutor nor a forgiving friend. We do not stage the final cart lift as another tableau of corrected behavior; M09 retains it, while this selection ends on the unerasable paired statements and the driver's settled fee with an unsettled count.

## Sequence 2 — What the visit buys

**2022. Unnamed room and printer's workroom. Source M10 I–II, IV–VI, VIII, XI–XII. No medical causation beyond the manuscript's fictional action.**

A wooden counter is visible beneath a chest. Jake pulls without emptying the chest. One corner sticks, then comes. He lets go sharply and rests his hand on the lid.

The counter is within reach now. His coat sleeve is not.

An assistant holds the coat while Jake works his arm into it. He offers to push the chest back. Jake refuses too quickly. The assistant has work elsewhere and leaves. A full jug waits beyond Jake's comfortable reach. An almost empty cup lasts until he must knock next door.

CUT: the employed woman brings water and sweeps. Jake directs the jug to its usual shelf.

“Can you reach it there?”

“Yes.”

She places it there and goes to her other rooms.

Alone, Jake can get the handle but cannot support the base. The jug tilts toward the edge. He stops. His favorable result slate is below it; he moves that first.

The woman returns when called. She puts the jug on the table. He offers extra money for the second visit. She takes it, then asks him to move so she can dust the shelf.

CUT: a later visit. A smaller borrowed pot is available now. Jake dismisses it because his shoulder feels easier. Later, carrying his own full jug, he spills water and chips its foot. He waits beside it until someone can carry it the rest of the way.

When he asks for the small pot again, it has been returned to its lender. Tomorrow, the woman says. Today she half-fills his jug. The chipped vessel goes back on his table; the borrowed one was not broken.

CUT: money on the table for more visits than agreed.

“I have not offered those hours.”

“You might need the money.”

“I might. I still have the other work.”

She takes her usual payment. His extra coins remain beside the cup.

CUT: after Jake cancels a visit. Used bowls and no clean water. The assistant can fetch water quickly, but Jake adds a bowl, the chest, the tablecloth.

“You cancelled her?”

“I did not need the whole visit.”

“You need the pieces of it.”

Jake asks for water alone. When he resumes the woman's visits, one former time belongs to another customer. He asks whether that person can change.

“I could ask you.”

He accepts the later time. The new arrangement changes when he can eat; no sympathetic exception returns his old slot.

### A meal divided

A covered bowl arrives from the printer. Beans, greens along one side. Jake says it is good and eats around the greens.

Another visit brings more of the same. His thanks have become an order he never placed.

At the workroom he carries the bowl close, stopping to adjust his grip. The printer is making her own food, not a separate convalescent supper. He admits he dislikes the greens.

“Don't make something different,” he says. “I can get the rest.”

“Then give the greens back before you mix them in.”

She places a small dish beside the bowl. They divide the food. She keeps what she wants and her bowl. He goes home with beans in her small dish. No offense, gratitude speech or proof of deserving care is required.

Later he offers to move workroom boards to repay the assistant's help. Trying to demonstrate his strength with the repaired printing block, he has to put it down again. He offers to pay another worker. The assistant asks the printer instead of accepting on her behalf.

She has already arranged the hands. What she wants is her dish back.

Jake washes it. The assistant takes it to the job that exists without Jake's contribution.

### The small thing returned

At the end of the year Jake turns a favorable slate toward the assistant.

“I've seen it. Have you got my counter?”

The counter sits beside the cup. The one from beneath the chest. Jake gives it back without an account of what retrieving it cost him.

The assistant pockets it and asks whether Jake wants to come to the game.

“Would I have to do anything?”

“That is usually the point.”

Jake laughs. He asks what they play. No offer to carry chairs or provide a meal follows. When the worker arrives at her changed time, he asks her to hold his coat. She does; he steps aside so she can begin the work for which she came.

## Sequence 3 — Something worth playing

**2023. Unnamed communal game room; local table relations only. Source M11 I–V, VIII–XII. Distinct visits, not one impossible evening.**

Before attending, Jake considers bread, then a better lamp. He brings neither.

A woman sorts blue counters. A man holds a red one in his teeth while looking under a chair.

“Don't put that in your mouth.”

“It's mine.”

“It will be mine if you lose.”

He removes it. Jake laughs before he knows whether it was an argument. They are not gambling for counters. The woman explains: advance two pieces to the far edge; a piece driven off can return only after an empty turn.

Jake asks her to move a far-side piece where he points. She does. Nobody turns the accommodation into a toast.

He loses quickly. His explanation stops when she asks him to show the move. She restores it, answers, and the opening becomes visible.

“Another?”

The assistant, absorbed at another board, has not watched him lose.

“Yes.”

CUT: buttons on Jake's home table, an opening rehearsed. At the next visit his opponent chooses a different beginning. He plays his prepared answer anyway. She drives the exposed piece off. He has an empty turn in which to listen to the other man's humming.

CUT: another game. Jake introduces himself as an easy win. The humming man makes a mistake. Jake advances into it, almost apologizes, then tries to make another joke.

“Let me finish.”

Silence. The man works out a block. Jake loses with his second counter one space short.

“That was unpleasant,” his opponent says.

“For you?”

“Who else would I mean?”

Jake enjoys having made someone work. They test another move before putting the pieces away. The opponent finds the answer at once and taps the table in delight.

### Nuts and a different kind of win

Jake offers a bag of nuts to whoever wins most games. Counting sharpens the evening. He wins twice. The assistant reaches into the bag and Jake covers it.

“I'll buy you some.”

“I don't want some later.”

The woman pushes her board back and asks whether they are still playing. Jake removes his hand. The assistant takes a nut but does not immediately sit again.

On a later visit the woman proposes exchanging sides midway, rescuing the position one's opponent has left. Jake complains that the opening will be pointless.

“Yes. Shall we?”

He agrees, then dislikes the poor position she gives him. He is playing; his displeasure is part of the game, not a cue for her to teach him a lesson.

CUT: Jake now knows enough to beat the assistant. He starts directing the young man's move.

“Let me put it down.”

The counter goes where Jake advised against. Jake demonstrates both sides of the mistake.

“Are we playing or are you showing me?”

They restart. Jake keeps his hands on his own side. The assistant's second long pause produces a stronger move than Jake expected. Jake still wins, more slowly. The assistant leaves to play someone else.

### The driver has twenty minutes

At home, Jake is practicing with buttons. The driver has a little time. Jake gives him half the pieces instead of explaining both sides indefinitely.

Water still occupies the easiest reach of the table. The driver asks a rule twice. Jake answers without moving for him.

A button advances.

“Is that allowed?”

“Yes.”

“Is it good?”

“I don't know yet.”

The driver smiles. For the first time Jake has to think.

They do not finish. The driver needs to leave. Jake offers to preserve the position.

“Don't keep your table for me.”

Jake clears it. The count of cloth, private supper and missing maps receive no settlement in exchange for this pleasure.

### Better opponents

At the communal table Jake finally defeats the woman when she is trying to win. His second piece crosses. She laughs once and gathers the reds.

He explains the advance. She starts setting up. He tells the assistant what she missed.

“I noticed. Too late.”

“It was there for three turns.”

“I was playing.”

She beats him rapidly in the next game. He calls it revenge with a smile that lets him retreat from the accusation.

“Of trying to win?”

He asks for another game next visit. Will he explain the whole thing afterward?

“Only my mistakes.”

“Then we'll be here late.”

CUT: the assistant alone with a position Jake remembers winning. He wants to try an escape. Four attempts produce two Jake wins, a loss, then disagreement over an empty turn. The woman will not adjudicate moves neither can establish. They reset to the last shared position. Jake surrenders an advantage, irritated, and resumes because defeating the concentrated player opposite him is more interesting than retaining the argument.

Later the assistant wins a full game. Jake's mistake required three good moves to exploit. Jake starts to name the mistake, then sees the assistant looking at both counters across the far edge.

“You saw it.”

“Eventually.”

They leave the pieces while they drink. Jake studies the defeat to prevent its recurrence, not to qualify the assistant's pleasure.

### Ordinary rules

Final visit selected for this movement. Nuts go into a bowl in the middle. The assistant takes one before sitting. Jake chooses blue; the woman chooses red.

“Ordinary rules?”

“Yes.”

“Good. I'm tired.”

Jake briefly brightens at the prospect of an easier opponent. He sets his pieces out. The game is close. He advances instead of driving off her piece; three turns later she reaches the far edge and wins.

“Again?”

“Not tonight.”

The assistant has time. Jake turns the board toward him and moves the nuts aside. The young man makes the first move. Jake has an answer prepared. He looks again before using it.

End with the next contested choice. No final toast, repaired friendship, cured shoulder or debt erased by leisure.
