## Sequence 1 — A price he can meet

2015. A CART BORROWER RECEIVED FOUR CLAMPS WHEN HE ASKED FOR TWO. THE LOAN WAS TO LAST UNTIL THE ROAD DRIED. TWO FAVORABLE CONTEST RESULTS HAVE FOLLOWED JAKE'S OPENING DEFEAT.

INT. ROOM BESIDE THE PASSAGE — DAY

MARA brings the borrower to the table. He puts down two clamps, cleaned and freshly oiled.

JAKE
The road isn't dry.

BORROWER
I know.

Jake looks at the two pieces. There were four.

MARA
He brought these back early.

BORROWER
I need a price I can meet.

The storekeeper's errand list lies beside the clamps. Jake had taken this man's name off it. The blank space still looks deliberate.

JAKE
Keep one.

The man does not reach for it.

BORROWER
That is another thing to keep. It isn't a price.

Jake draws one clamp toward him, then stops short of lifting it.

JAKE
Bring the next person with a broken axle here before it breaks the rest of the way.

The borrower looks toward the passage. A visitor there has stopped speaking. Now someone besides him has heard what the extra clamp means.

Jake leaves it within reach. There is no clean exchange of iron for an amount the man can count.

LATER

The borrower has gone. Mara stands on the other side of the table, where he stood.

MARA
Every cart that hears about this will know where to come.

JAKE
They still have to ask.

MARA
He was asking how to finish owing you.

Jake looks at the gap in the errand list. It no longer looks like a release.

The storekeeper waits to know what to record. Jake has no settled return to give him.

END SEQUENCE 1.

## Sequence 2 — The empty measure

2016. THE NEW BOOK HAS NAMES AND AMOUNTS. SOME OLD PROMISES REMAIN IN A LOCKED DRAWER. JAKE NOW REQUIRES A SECOND PERSON TO WITNESS A LOAN'S RETURN.

INT. THE LENDING ROOM — DAY

A WOMAN puts her oil measure on the table. A dent crosses the bottom. Her BROTHER remains beside her rather than taking the visitor's chair.

Jake has written her name. The next space is for the witness.

WOMAN
My brother.

BROTHER
I can say what she takes.

Jake waits with the pen.

BROTHER
Not what she'll have when you want it back.

The woman looks at him. He keeps his hands away from the table. He has agreed to come here; he has not agreed to become evidence of a future he cannot know.

JAKE
Then I can't enter it as a loan.

The woman draws her empty measure toward her.

WOMAN
You told us the door was open.

JAKE
The store can't promise what comes back.

She puts the measure into her bag.

JAKE
Leave that.

She stops.

JAKE
The measure stays here.

She turns it in her hand so the dent faces him.

WOMAN
This came with me.

Mara reaches beneath the table. She sets down the store's measure, its handle wrapped in cord.

Jake recognizes the one he has been using all morning.

JAKE
I'm sorry.

The woman closes her bag. Her brother moves to help her away from the table. She raises a hand.

WOMAN
Go back to your work.

BROTHER
I'll come with you.

WOMAN
I came for oil. I can get home.

She passes him and leaves.

Jake looks at the brother, still there.

JAKE
You could witness what she takes another time.

BROTHER
Will I have to come again?

The pen remains above the empty space. Jake has not asked what this visit cost the man.

JAKE
I don't know.

The brother rubs his palm with his thumb and goes after his sister.

Mara gathers the unused requests. She leaves the woman's where it is.

MARA
What does she take home from that answer?

Jake reaches for the store's measure and stops. Giving himself a better final sentence would not put oil in hers.

NIGHT

He crosses out WITNESS. Beneath it he writes RETURNER.

The space remains empty. The requested amount of oil is not crossed out.

END SEQUENCE 2.

## Sequence 3 — The unclaimed line

LATE2016.

INT. THE LENDING ROOM — EVENING

The latest contest has gone against Jake by a narrow margin. Beyond the table people are still arguing about how little would have changed it.

The STOREKEEPER follows Jake's glance from the result to the outstanding loans.

STOREKEEPER
The cart at the east bend still has our clamps.

Jake puts a hand on the book. The suggestion offers an action that can happen tonight. Someone outside the room could be made to feel the result.

JAKE
Leave them.

STOREKEEPER
Then why keep the book?

Jake does not close it.

Mara writes UNCLAIMED beside the debt. The word neither returns the iron nor gives it away.

LATER — AFTER THE FINAL RESULT

The book is still open at that line. The clamps are still out. The year has ended badly without settling the loan.

MARA
Does the rule go forward?

Jake looks at names under RETURNER. Some promised returns failed; others were carried farther than expected.

JAKE
Not as a law.

MARA
What do I carry, then?

JAKE
Who paid for what I asked.

She takes the book. The old ledger remains in the drawer. Neither book has made a clean end of the other.

END SEQUENCE 3.

## Sequence 4 — Her light

2017. THE PREVIOUS YEAR'S LEDGER HAS BEEN KEPT. A NEW REPAIRS COLUMN DISTINGUISHES A CHILD'S ACCIDENT FROM A DEBT. THE LAMP IT RECORDS IS STILL BROKEN.

INT. THE LENDING ROOM — TOWARD EVENING

The CHILD holds a strip of cloth ready to carry the lamp.

CHILD
Can I take it?

Jake turns the cracked side away, then turns it back. Hiding it from his own view has not made it hold oil.

JAKE
Not with oil inside.

CHILD
We have oil.

The storekeeper lifts a sound lamp from the shelf. Jake looks at it.

STOREKEEPER
Someone is coming for this tonight.

Jake begins to speak. The child watches both lamps.

Mara takes the cracked one and rests it on the table. Its base rocks.

MARA
Leave it here.

She takes the candle from beside her book.

MARA
Bring a cup.

The child looks at the candle.

CHILD
Do I bring back what's left?

MARA
No.

The child leaves and returns with a cup. Mara works the candle upright while Jake holds it. The storekeeper places the reserved lamp where its borrower can find it.

Jake writes a candle against the store's inventory.

Mara reaches across and strikes out the entry.

MARA
Mine.

He looks toward the place beside her book. It is empty now.

The child takes the cup carefully in both hands. Mara watches him into the passage, then puts the damaged lamp beneath the board.

MARA
Turn that inward.

Jake looks at the public contest results.

JAKE
They'll still count us.

MARA
Let them do it somewhere else for an evening.

He leaves the board facing out.

She returns to her book without the candle. Her help has not made her agree with him.

END SEQUENCE 4.

## Sequence 5 — A return not yet made

LATER2017. MATERIAL IS RESERVED FOR AN ABSENT COURIER WHO HAS BEEN ASKED TO BRING SOMEONE WILLING TO ANSWER THE ROOM. OTHER PEOPLE ARE WAITING FOR THE SAME STOCK.

INT. THE LENDING ROOM — DAY

A man wants more than Jake will release.

JAKE
He asked before you.

MAN
Is he here?

Jake points to the reserved amount. The man takes the smaller quantity without thanking him.

When the next favorable result draws more visitors, Jake opens the old ledger again. The word UNCLAIMED is still readable.

Mara lays her hand across the page.

MARA
We know what you kept. Ask what they need to know.

Jake starts to explain the book. Her hand stays.

LATER

Three people discuss a rule with him. One asks for a list of what the store cannot supply, visible before someone has to request it in public.

The list goes beside the result board. A newcomer reads it, then leaves without approaching the table. Jake has no refusal to make and no gratitude to receive.

LATER — BEFORE THE FINAL RETURN

The storekeeper wants the reserved material released. Jake has written that it cannot wait indefinitely. He holds the message without passing it to anyone.

Mara reads over his hand.

MARA
Ask if he still needs it.

He crosses out the threat. She takes the question.

LATER

Mara returns alone. The courier has found someone, but will not give that person's name until they come together.

At the door another requester waits for the reserved stock. Jake goes to tell him it is still unavailable. Mara puts the original request back beside the inventory.

FINAL WINDOW

The courier arrives with a VISITOR who once offered to remove the old ledger. His name has also appeared beneath a disputed light ration: he has needed something this room's accounts did not first recognize.

Jake looks from him to the request.

JAKE
You wanted the book gone.

VISITOR
I wanted it to stop punishing people.

The courier places a hand beside the written amount. Mara checks it against the remaining stock.

VISITOR
I will bring this back with him.

Jake draws the ledger closer.

VISITOR
Not today. Today it leaves.

The pen pauses over the return column.

Jake enters the material as a loan. The visitor signs beside the courier's request. The return stays blank.

Mara releases the stated amount. The critic leaves with a job he has chosen, not a declaration that the room has become fair.

END SEQUENCE 5.

## Sequence 6 — The keeper's work

2018.

INT. THE ROOM — DAY

The TOWER KEEPER lays a cracked strip of bell metal beside a piece of iron Jake has offered. One edge matches. The other projects beyond it.

KEEPER
Anything wider?

Jake looks at the last wide piece on the shelf.

JAKE
That's the last.

The keeper waits. Other repairs need it too; their owners have not brought broken parts into the room today.

Jake moves the smaller piece toward him.

JAKE
Enough for a patch.

The keeper wraps it with the old strip.

JAKE
When will it be ready?

KEEPER
When I find the rest.

EXT. FOOT OF THE TOWER STAIR — EVENING

The damaged bell gives a thin call. People arrive with their own tools.

Jake watches a woman pass a hammer upward to a person beyond his view. When that work is done, she keeps her hand raised until the hammer comes down again.

The tools keep their owners. The work does not wait for entry in his book.

LATER

Jake asks whether the fitted patch has worked.

KEEPER
For now.

The keeper listens to the fading note. Jake waits beside him, unable to turn the answer into a finished receipt.

INT. THE ROOM — LATER THAT YEAR

The split strip lies on the table again.

KEEPER
The patch failed.

Jake turns to the wide iron. It is still there.

JAKE
Take that now.

The keeper does not approach the shelf.

KEEPER
I've found somewhere to work.

JAKE
Then take it there.

KEEPER
I came for my tools.

Jake moves aside.

The keeper finds his hammer under a cloth, checks its head and puts it into his bag. He asks for the smaller tool. Jake has lent it to somebody else. Mara goes to fetch it.

The two men wait. The wider iron remains available and unaccepted.

Mara returns. The keeper thanks her, checks the smaller tool and leaves. He makes no promise about the committee's next meeting.

Jake starts to enter a return under the keeper's name.

JAKE
He didn't bring anything back.

MARA
He collected it.

She takes the pen to record the tool's return. The keeper's ordinary chair stays empty.

LATER — ANOTHER MEETING

Jake has stood the map against that chair's back, facing the door. It gives an arriving visitor something to look at before the latest unfavorable result.

A visitor asks for the chair.

Jake lifts the map and puts it on the wall. The visitor reads the result before sitting down. The drawing no longer speaks for the absent keeper.

FINAL LOWER-LADDER WINDOW

The keeper comes through the door carrying a bell made from patched iron and harbor scrap. Jake reaches for the ledger.

The keeper sets the bell on its closed cover. An unpleasant short note fills the room.

JAKE
Does it ring properly?

KEEPER
It rings.

He points above the map. Someone arriving after the room empties needs a way to call for help reading it.

He has brought a hook and cord. The hook is too large for Jake's proposed position.

KEEPER
Lower.

JAKE
People will hit it passing.

The keeper looks at the map rather than revising the bell to suit the existing arrangement.

Mara takes the loose sheets. Jake lifts the edge clear of its peg. A fold tears a little. He holds it flat while she finds another peg.

The keeper fixes the bell where its cord can be reached. Jake asks about putting his name beside it.

The keeper shakes his head. He is trying to knot the cord while holding the bell steady.

Jake holds it. The keeper finishes.

The bell rings. Two people look in. One asks whether the map has changed.

Mara shows him a new stair. The keeper gathers his own tools. Jake moves the ledger clear and closes it without entering a returned loan for a bell the room never lent.

END OF MOVEMENT.

## Dramatic decision and unresolved costs

The movement's progression is not Jake learning to be generous. A loan adds an obligation the borrower wanted to finish; a rule denies oil and spends a brother's time; declining collection leaves the store short; Mara solves one evening with her own candle while rejecting Jake's display; a critic consents to a future return without announcing it complete; and a keeper retrieves property before returning with work made elsewhere. Jake participates in these actions but does not own all their outcomes.

The oil woman is not brought back to thank him. The child is not given another family's reserved lamp. The courier's first return is not shown. The tower is not declared safe because a patched bell can ring. The keeper's independent work does not prove an accepted institution or engineering repair. No extra score or named modern opponent is necessary to understand these choices.

Mara's crossed-out inventory entry and dissent over the board are deliberate independent actions. The visitor can have criticized the ledger and still choose a particular return without hypocrisy. The keeper has his own project and tools; helping him tie the cord is not Jake's authorship of it. These choices must survive editing even if the principal's reflective lines are shortened.
