# The part you offer

Continuous cinematic reading unit, revision 1. NONCANON adaptation. This is an editorial reading, not a completed film or a replacement of any manuscript. Dates and scene changes are part of the reader-facing text. All dialogue is proposed adaptation.

## I. The absent place — 2025

Jake commands a company in a public contest. Sorel is his working partner: he helps shape Jake's words and manages messages and arrivals. Sorel has commissioned a green pennant so the workers behind their victories will be seen.

EXT. CONTEST GROUND — DAY

Neris reaches Jake while the workers hold their position beneath the green pennant. Their opponent is advancing toward them. Farther away, an opening has appeared at the marker Jake needs.

NERIS
They are waiting for support.

JAKE
Withdraw it. Send it to the far marker.

NERIS
They can be taken.

Jake looks from the pennant to the opening. Gives the order again. Neris goes.

The next image is the torn green cloth being returned. Jake's company has won. Nobody beneath that pennant was asked to choose the sacrifice.

INT. PREPARATION TENT — DUSK

The green pennant rests on a stool. A pale seam crosses its border.

Sorel gives Jake the short address. Jake reads the first line, marks out the greeting, starts lower.

JAKE
They stood when standing served the whole company.

Sorel watches the pennant.

JAKE
Too formal?

SOREL
They did not know you were leaving them.

Jake lowers the page.

JAKE
They were holding a position.

SOREL
They thought you would reinforce it.

JAKE
So did Pitts.

The answer lands. Jake hears the satisfaction in it after Sorel has.

Outside, a hammer strikes a peg. Too hard. Another voice asks for it to be eased out first. The ordinary correction interrupts the silence.

JAKE
We won.

SOREL
Yes.

JAKE
Help me explain it.

Sorel takes the page. He folds it across the writing instead of revising it.

SOREL
Say you chose.

Jake puts a hand flat on the table. It is not an order yet.

JAKE
You wanted them seen.

Sorel goes still.

Jake could withdraw the words. He lets them remain.

Sorel lifts the pennant and folds it along the tear. The new stitching disappears inside the fold. His care with it continues after his agreement has ended.

SOREL
Do you want me beside you?

JAKE
It would help.

SOREL
Whom?

Jake looks toward the entrance. People are already waiting beyond it.

SOREL
The messages and arrivals for the final—I will finish those. I will not stand there while you tell them they chose this.

He leaves the pennant on the table. Takes neither the speech nor Jake's offered attention with him.

Jake unfolds the paper. He crosses out the sentence that gives the workers a choice they did not have. He reads the next sentence. Crosses that out too.

EXT. GATHERING BESIDE THE FIELD BARRIERS — CONTINUOUS

The small platform has a place beside it for Sorel.

It is empty.

Jake stands before the workers. Neris is among them, arms folded. The address is shorter now.

JAKE
I ordered the withdrawal.

JAKE
I withdrew the support to reach the far marker. I left you to hold that position.



A worker looks at the repaired pennant and walks away.

Jake sees him leave. He does not call after him.

The man's departure is quiet. No one needs to endorse it for it to count.


The final contest follows. Austin defeats Jake. There is no reversal of the result and no repair of the partnership in the announcement.

EXT. DEPARTING GROUNDS — MORNING

Ropes come down. Workers carry away the arrangement that made this ground important yesterday.

Jake finds Sorel beside the wagons. A bundle of papers is wrapped in the repaired green pennant. The white signal cloth has already been returned to Neris; it is not in this bundle.

JAKE
Are you coming?

SOREL
Not with the first party.

JAKE
I didn't mean the wagon.

SOREL
I know.

Jake looks at the careful wrapping. Sorel has not thrown the cloth away. That gives Jake no answer.

JAKE
I need someone who tells me when I'm wrong.

SOREL
You had that.

A driver checks a strap nearby. Jake waits through the work rather than answering.

JAKE
Will you come back?

SOREL
I haven't decided.

The driver asks whether the bundle is going. Sorel says yes. The bundle moves into the wagon; this small question has an answer.

Jake steps back so it can pass.

JAKE
Tell me when you do.

Sorel nods. The nod gives no date.

Neris waits with the final record. Jake unfolds it. The year and result are visible; the camera does not ask a cold viewer to know which score belongs to him. He folds it again.

JAKE
The earlier party can go.

NERIS
And us?

JAKE
When you're ready.

NERIS
I am ready.

He follows her.

They pass the place where the platform stood. Someone presses a peg hole closed with a heel. Sorel's former place has no distinguishing mark.

Jake turns once toward the bundles. He cannot find the green cloth.

Neris calls his name. He goes.


## II. The chair — 2026, after the fifth contest

EXT. JORDAN'S CAMP — LATE AFTERNOON

A cart rests on blocks. Four men stand beside its removed wheels. On the bed: empty kegs arranged into a throne.

JORDAN presses down on the highest keg. It drums beneath his hand.

JORDAN
That will hold him.

A man lifts a wheel.

MAN
And how does he arrive?

JORDAN
Carry it.

The men look at the cart. Jordan looks at their arms, then toward the low wall where BEN sits with a wrapped hammer between his knees.

Jordan goes to him. No procession starts.

JORDAN
You beat me. Let me give you a triumph.

Ben looks past him at the dismantled cart.

BEN
It seems to have happened already.

Jordan explains the entrance with his hands: himself walking before the throne, Ben rising above the empty kegs. His hands become less expansive when Ben stops smiling.

BEN
And then you tell them what I'm full of.

JORDAN
You won't need me to.

BEN
Give me your part.

JORDAN
I lost.

BEN
I can explain that.

One of the men laughs. Jordan turns. The man crouches beside an axle and finds work to do.

Ben watches the change.

JORDAN
I was trying to give you something.

BEN
A place in your story.

A beat. Ben looks toward the road out of camp. He could take it. Instead he points to the highest keg.

BEN
Get up.

Jordan puts his metal bat on the ground. Climbs onto the cart. The kegs drum again, better this time.

Ben hears the sound and raises an arm.

BEN
Behold the man who loses, then finds somebody else to do the heavy work.

The men laugh. Jordan smiles with them, a little late.

Ben waits until the laugh has nearly ended.

BEN
Laugh properly. He hasn't let you drink yet.

A worker covers his mouth. Jordan notices. His smile disappears before the worker's hand comes down.

Ben leaves room for an answer. Jordan could give him one. He looks at the empty kegs beneath himself, then climbs down.

BEN
Too much?

JORDAN
No second bow.

BEN
It wasn't a bow.

JORDAN
Then no second anything.

Jordan picks up the bat. The nearest man shifts away. Jordan sets it across his own shoulders, changing the shape of the movement.

BEN
You asked for a show.

JORDAN
I asked you to be funny.

BEN
I was.

Jordan looks at the worker who covered his mouth. He has no answer that would make the laugh untrue.

A man asks about the cart. Its missing pin has fallen between the stones. The bedding and gear it was supposed to fetch are still at the lower shelter.

JORDAN
Find the pin. I'll carry the bedding.

BEN
I can—

JORDAN
You can go.

Ben waits. Jordan lifts down a keg. It is light enough to throw. He sets it on the earth carefully.

EXT. ROAD OUTSIDE THE CAMP — LATER

Ben stops to tighten the cloth on his hammer. His raccoon reaches for the loose end. Ben moves it away without looking.

BEN
Finds somebody else to do the heavy work.

The first line still pleases him. He waits for the second. Does not say it.

The sun has moved. He shoulders the hammer and starts down the road toward work he has left waiting. No applause follows him.

## III. The other part — after the sixth contest

EXT. BRANDON'S STABLE YARD — AFTERNOON

A separate yard. Under an awning, a red-orange shawl lies folded above the traffic. The damaged stable partition is closed off. BRANDON brings the DARK HORSE into the open yard; the broken enclosure is not part of the show.

BEN arrives driving a wagon. Open tubs are lashed behind the bench. A COOK checks a tub at the rail.

COOK
Cooled, as you asked. Keep some for supper.

Brandon tips his hat. Across the yard a COURIER waits with a folded result. Brandon sees him and lifts one finger: a moment.

BRANDON
You brought supper.

BEN
I brought the way it gets here.

The audience laughs. Brandon rides around the first post. Ben brings the wagon toward the turn. The cook calls for them to slow.

Brandon moves closer to be heard over the wheels.

BRANDON
Try not to feed the fence.

Ben turns his head. A wheel finds the rut. One tub rises against its lashing.

Brandon looks up just before the cooled chili comes over him.

His hat goes. The Dark Horse recoils. Brandon keeps his seat, one hand down against its neck. Chili slides from the brimless line of his hair onto his sunglasses.

Ben laughs first. Then the yard.

BEN
Fence is safe.

Brandon dismounts. His boot slips; the cook catches his elbow.

COOK
Not on my ladle.

He takes the horse's lead and brings it beyond the spilled food. A stable hand arrives with water and a cloth. Brandon stays until the horse stops tossing its head, then lets the hand take over. The damaged enclosure remains closed.

The courier reads the result. The words we need are plain.

COURIER
Ben won. Brandon lost.

Someone at the rail calls that the wagon has won twice. Ben removes his cap for the applause.

Brandon watches him. Wipes his glasses with a sleeve that makes them worse.

BRANDON
Again.

The crowd answers before Ben can.

BRANDON
You showed them how to carry it. Show them how to wear it.

The cook plants the empty tub between them.

COOK
Not with my supper. This is all you get.

A spectator calls for another load. She hands him a cloth instead. He looks around for somebody to give it to, then begins wiping the rail.

Brandon pulls off his wet duster and holds it out. Ben looks at the narrow shoulders. At the crowd. At Brandon's outstretched arm.

He could leave with both laughs. He puts down his cap.

BEN
One sleeve. And you tell it as me.

He hands Brandon the cap. Brandon's smile changes; the role he offered has returned with a condition.

Ben pushes one arm into the duster. The rest hangs from his shoulder. He carries the empty tub into the yard and sits on its rim.

Brandon climbs onto the stationary wagon bench. The Dark Horse remains with the stable hand beyond the rail. Nothing moves to make the spill happen again.

BRANDON
I arrived with a plan so delicate nobody could see it.

BEN
I saw it. Rode directly underneath.

Laughter. Ben attempts Brandon's hat flourish. The sleeve catches, the tub shifts, and for a moment he is trying simply to stay seated.

The crowd likes that better.

Brandon does not cover the pause with another insult. Ben sets both feet down. The crowd quiets enough for him to speak.

BEN
The wagon leaned.

BRANDON
And the rider stayed where a man with sense would not.

BEN
Waiting for the driver to have some.

Brandon laughs. Ben laughs with him. Neither has to supply the next line immediately.

The cook lifts a pot of unspilled food.

COOK
Room.

Both men get out of her way. Ben stands too fast and the narrow coat traps his elbow behind him.

BEN
Help.

Brandon pulls the sleeve free, without ceremony. Ben's shirt has a broad red stain. They exchange cap and coat.

BRANDON
Next time you know the words.

A woman asks when that will be. Ben opens his mouth. Looks at Brandon.

BEN
Not before he asks me.

Brandon nods. Gives no date.

LATER

Ben sits on the wagon step. A clean shirt lies beside his stained one. Beyond the wheel, two spectators disagree about who had the better line.

Ben listens. He has lost the version in which the afternoon belongs entirely to him. He stays long enough to miss the first call to supper.

Brandon has gone to collect his horse. Neither man calls after the other.

CUT TO BLACK.

## IV. A different room — another post-sixth-contest window

TITLE: ELSEWHERE. A CARRYING-FRAME DEMONSTRATION.

No journey connects the stable yard to this appointment. Ben's coat does not become the frame's design, and no one here has heard his performance.


INT. DAVID'S DEMONSTRATION ROOM — DAY


A clerk's note lies beside DAVID's invitation: the borrowed cart is still waiting to unload. He is being charged for the time. His own arrival-order rule has put his sample at the back of the queue.

David folds the note beneath the invitation before the first visitor reaches the table.

A carrying frame stands between two low benches. Its maker tests the bench she brought. The other bench belongs to the room. David's polished table remains unused.

Two VISITORS discuss how the frames would stack. A THIRD looks for a price.

DAVID
After you have seen it used.

PHILLIP enters. All three heads survey the same room from different angles. His middle head stays toward David. No head becomes a separate character or a second body.

DAVID
You beat me yesterday. Today I would like to sell you something that fits.

DAVID
You kept the appointment.

PHILLIP
Was it cancelled?

David indicates the frame.

The maker sets it on Phillip empty. Straps settle against his dark gear. He raises an arm, lowers it, then walks between the benches.

The third visitor steps back for a better view. Another asks Phillip to face the window.

Phillip looks at him. The visitor looks down at a buckle instead.

At the turn, the crosspiece touches the left side of Phillip's neck.

He stops. It would be easy to turn his whole body and make the fitting look successful.

PHILLIP
The top is high.

DAVID
It keeps the load away.

PHILLIP
From which part of me?

The maker asks for the turn again, slowly. Phillip gives the room a second view of the problem. The wood reaches the same place before he can look where he wants.

VISITOR
You could turn from the feet.

Phillip holds still while the maker removes the frame. She lowers a strap, fits it again, and checks the empty movement. Only then does she permit the small test sack.

Its weight changes the fit. The shelf draws back. The upper piece begins to rise.

MAKER
Stop.

Phillip was already stopping. He takes another half-step before accepting the word. Now the shelf is farther from the bench.

PHILLIP
Take it off.

The loaded buckle will not release easily. David comes closer, then finds that his help would occupy the maker's working space.

Phillip bends toward the bench. A strap catches. He pulls.

A short crack.

The maker slides the bench beneath the shelf. Pressure comes off the straps. She removes the frame and sets the sack on the floor.

Phillip's heads turn away from the visitors. Not three reactions for them to select from.

PHILLIP
I pulled.

The maker puts a thumb beside the split rail.

MAKER
It pulled you too.

One visitor says the frame might still fit an ordinary porter.

All three heads turn toward him together.

PHILLIP
Then buy it.

The man looks at the crack. He does not answer.

David asks what repairing it would take. The maker lays the sound rail beside the damaged one. Repair would preserve the pattern. A different fit would consume the sample.

The third visitor puts on his gloves. He has seen enough today. David lets him go. The two others offer to return with a porter close to David's size.

Their offer remains on the table. It has not become foolish merely because Phillip needs something else.

David looks toward the folded cart note. The sample was meant to make his postponed warehouse work easier to defend. He has incurred a charge to bring it here, and could still leave with a product to test for those customers.

DAVID
Can you find the other shape in this?

The maker marks a possible change with her fingers, not a measured blueprint.

MAKER
I can find out something. Not finish it today.

DAVID
Find it out.

She leaves the saw on the bench.

MAKER
Who pays for the trial?

DAVID
If it works—

MAKER
The afternoon is spent either way.

David asks her price. We do not hear a number. He writes the agreed figure on the back of his invitation. She reads it.

The cart charge is still running. Phillip has made no demand. There is no convenient arrogance for David to refuse.

MAKER
You lose the sample.

DAVID
I can see that.

MAKER
You can still repair it now.

The visitors wait. David folds the cloth out of her way.

DAVID
Cut it.

She marks the wood and adjusts her line before beginning. The sawing is work, not a flourish.

The two visitors say they will come back when there is a standard sample.

DAVID
When there is one.

They leave without an appointment.

Phillip stands near the window. David looks at the emptied places, then at the possible customer who remains.

DAVID
If it works, people should hear where it came from.

Phillip's left head turns toward him. The other two still face the interrupted work.

PHILLIP
Let her finish finding out first.

David picks up the detached piece. It cannot join the rails now. He lays it within the maker's reach.

LATER IN THE SAME TRIAL

The altered frame looks less finished. Binding ends remain long. The crack is visible on the bench. Nothing has been polished into proof of success.

The maker asks for the small sack. David reaches for the larger, changes his grip when she repeats the request.

Phillip lets her adjust the fit. He indicates where the turning stops instead of demonstrating how much discomfort he can conceal. David waits for the maker before placing the load.

Two steps. A turn. Phillip raises a hand before David can speak.

He is listening to the strap.

Another step. Back to the bench.

PHILLIP
Off.

The sack comes away. This time he does not pull at the frame.

DAVID
Better?

PHILLIP
For that.

DAVID
How much more?

PHILLIP
I don't know.

Phillip touches the split rail. David watches the hand.

PHILLIP
That part was mine.

DAVID
The whole thing is mine.

PHILLIP
The pull.

DAVID
I understood.

Neither withdraws his version.

The maker needs the pieces back at her workshop to compare the fit marks. Phillip says to take them. David has not answered.

PHILLIP
Let her take them.

David slides the cloth under the pieces. The room empties of the thing he had meant to sell.

Phillip asks when to return. The maker will send word when there is something different to test. No date is supplied to soothe either man.

At the door, David tries one last reduction of the day.

DAVID
After losing to you, I thought today might go differently.

Phillip turns.

PHILLIP
You still have a trial.

DAVID
And no sample.

PHILLIP
Not yet.

He leaves. The maker follows with the split rail held apart so it cannot catch the cloth.

David has the invitation with the payment commitment and no saleable sample. The cart must still be dealt with. No payment, product launch or completed repair is shown.


END OF READING UNIT.
