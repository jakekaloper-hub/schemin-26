## Concrete revisions required

### 1. Define the immediate stakes of the opening contest without adding unsupported history

“Jake commands a company in a public contest” is too broad to settle what “They can be taken” means. A reader unfamiliar with the league could reasonably picture soldiers left to die. Later loss to Austin and the score record do not retroactively resolve that uncertainty. The text tells us workers did not choose the sacrifice but does not show the material consequence of losing their position.

Fix: use a short source-supported visual or plain description of what losing that position does to the people there. If the source does not establish injury/capture, do not add either. Establish only the verified rule/outcome needed to distinguish defeat at a marker from lethal abandonment. Jake's exact numerical score is unnecessary for comprehension; the human cost is necessary. If the source cannot clarify the physical stakes, retain the ambiguity as an explicit unresolved development issue rather than certify cold-reader clarity.

### 3. Specify the honest form of integration

I→II and III→IV have no demonstrated event-to-event causal link. That is legitimate. The actual unit integrates them through the repeated question of who chooses another person's part: Sorel refuses Jake's public story; Ben seizes Jordan's role then allows Brandon a shared one; Phillip and the maker resist David's standard fit and simple ownership of the outcome. II→III is the strongest character-causal progression. I and IV are thematic counterpoints, not stages in the same plot.

Fix: describe this deliverable as a four-part thematic cinematic sequence with one continuous Ben strand, or revise its transition rhythm so the independent blocks read as deliberate counterpoints. Do not market it as a converged historical plot. A clean end-of-part pause and a direct new place/date card can suffice; a fabricated messenger, inherited object, shared workshop origin, or claim that Jake's confession caused Ben's change would make it worse.
