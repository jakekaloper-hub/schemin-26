## Required repairs

### A1. Resolve the frame/cart contradiction

Part IV says the borrowed cart is “still waiting to unload,” and David's arrival-order rule “has put his sample at the back of the queue.” Immediately afterward, the carrying frame already stands between benches inside the room. The reader cannot tell whether there are two samples, whether the note is old, or whether the demonstration occurs before unloading.

**Repair:** distinguish the denied/delayed ordinary warehouse cart from the separately borrowed cart that brought the sample and now waits outside accruing a charge. Alternatively, explicitly date the note and show the sample's arrival. Do not leave a single sample simultaneously outside in an unloading queue and inside in use. No exact rate or paid invoice is necessary.

### A2. Make the opening's actual physical stakes intelligible without inventing combat

An advancing opponent, a vulnerable position, “They can be taken,” a torn pennant and “Nobody ... was asked to choose the sacrifice” suggest battle, capture or injury. The opening never establishes whether workers are eliminated from a rule-bound game, physically attacked, abandoned under dangerous conditions, or simply denied expected support. Sorel's grievance could be grave betrayal or coercive symbolism; that difference matters to the audience's judgment of Jake. The torn cloth alone cannot answer it.

**Repair:** return to the supported manuscript action and state only what it supplies. If the mechanics or bodily consequences remain unknown, remove the invented contest-ground reconstruction and begin with the aftermath/conflicting account. Do not invent deaths, injuries, military sacrifice or a convenient harmless rule to solve the gap. An explicit, limited account of the choice can preserve the ethical rupture without pretending to show how the fantasy result occurred.

“So did Pitts” also introduces an unexplained name at the moment the reader needs clarity. Establish the limited identity relevant to the sentence or remove the unsupported shorthand; no biography is needed.

### A3. Remove unsupported exact chronology

“You beat me yesterday” narrows a post-sixth-contest window to one day. The unit provides no need for that precision, and the rest of its framing deliberately avoids exact travel timing. Use “You beat me” or “After that result” unless an independently verified scene-time source supports yesterday. Part IV's date can remain another post-sixth-contest window, with order relative to Part III unasserted.

### A4. Resolve the structural claim: connected story or linked reading suite

Part I ends a Jake/Sorel episode in2025, then both disappear. Parts II and III provide one causal development through Ben. Part IV explicitly says nobody there has heard the performance and no journey connects it. This is honest separation, but it leaves the opening and ending functioning as thematic companions, not as consequences of what came before. Calling the arrangement “Continuous” asks readers to expect convergence the unit does not deliver.

**Repair:** either identify it openly as a linked cinematic reading suite with a historical prologue, a two-part Ben arc and a separate ensemble counterpoint, or develop an actual supported connective choice from the manuscripts. Do not manufacture identical carts, cloths, makers, timbers or secret messages to create apparent unity. Thematic association is legitimate; it should be judged under the right claim.
