# Round 1 — Ursula K. Le Guin Published-Method Lens

**Status:** INDEPENDENT / LOCKED  
**Domain:** POV, narrative distance, voice, cultural interior

## Governing diagnosis

Edrin is an effective consciousness, but his usefulness has become a **narrative monopoly**. He sees, interprets and often summarizes the Twelve. The live novel now needs viewpoint to become a source of difference rather than a single archival filter applied to different events.

## Preserve

1. Edrin's epistemic discipline: "write what happened, not motive."
2. Oren's counterpressure.
3. The Archive's distinction between fact and interpretation.
4. Close observation of physical detail.
5. Restraint around unsupported private psychology.

## Highest-leverage weaknesses

1. Edrin is present at implausibly many consequential scenes.
2. Principal dialogue often carries similar dry/sardonic cadence.
3. Narration frequently explains the thematic lesson after Edrin has already perceived enough.
4. The Twelve are visually distinct but not yet equally distinct as perceiving minds.
5. Institutional vocabulary can leak into scenes where another viewpoint would notice different things.

## Recommendations

- Adopt a formal POV constitution.
- Edrin/Archive remains a licensed POV, not the default POV.
- Principal POV eligibility requires evidence-bound knowledge envelopes and fictionalized interiority clearly derived from canon rather than claims about real owners.
- Each POV packet should define attention bias: what this person notices first, ignores, misreads and values.
- Separate narrator knowledge from character knowledge.
- Run dedicated voice passes after structural revisions.

## Do not change

Do not solve the problem by adding free omniscience. More POVs require tighter knowledge discipline, not less.

## Confidence

Very high.
