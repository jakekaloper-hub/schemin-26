# Round 1 — Brandon Sanderson Published-Method Lens

**Status:** INDEPENDENT / LOCKED  
**Domain:** Systems, limitations, reader legibility, promise/payoff

## Governing diagnosis

The manuscript's systems are strongest when a rule becomes a **limitation that creates conflict**—the Belt does not transfer after one win; standings alter markets; waivers expose scarcity. They are weakest when the book explains system state because the weekly data exists.

## Preserve

1. Belt succession limitation.
2. Certified result vs significance distinction.
3. Scarcity and recruitment market.
4. Environmental advantage in the swamp.
5. Rules discovered through argument.

## Highest-leverage weaknesses

1. Reader-facing system load grows every chapter.
2. Exact scores can look like source data pasted into prose rather than story mechanics.
3. New institutions risk being added whenever a production need appears.
4. The reader does not yet have a compact sense of which rules are durable and which are situational.
5. Some conflict is explained after the fact instead of anticipated through known limitations.

## Recommendations

- Create a reader-facing rule budget: only rules required to understand current conflict enter prose.
- Deepen existing systems before adding new ones.
- Track limitations/costs explicitly in architecture packets.
- Let readers anticipate consequences from known rules.
- Comprehensive statistical truth belongs in artifacts; narrative foregrounding requires causal significance.
- Before inventing a new institution, prove an existing institution cannot carry the function.

## Do not change

Do not turn the world into a hard-system manual. Legibility is not exhaustive explanation.

## Confidence

High.
