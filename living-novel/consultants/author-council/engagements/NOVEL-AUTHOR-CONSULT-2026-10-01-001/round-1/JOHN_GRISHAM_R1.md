# Round 1 — John Grisham Published-Method Lens

**Status:** INDEPENDENT / LOCKED  
**Domain:** Propulsion, chapter discipline, adult readability

## Governing diagnosis

The prose is readable and often funny, but Chapters I–II contain **too many good scenes**. Each chapter performs several independent jobs, which weakens the pressure of the main one. The reader is repeatedly re-oriented to a new matchup, rule, location and mini-resolution.

## Preserve

1. Strong cold opens: six envelopes; returned parcel.
2. Concrete stakes and objects.
3. Short dialogue exchanges.
4. Institutional humor.
5. Final images: significance column; empty chair.

## Highest-leverage weaknesses

1. Nine or ten titled sections inside one chapter dilute chapter objective.
2. Exact score presentation repeatedly interrupts propulsion.
3. C-story scenes can feel obligatory even when well written.
4. Explanatory thematic sentences often arrive after the scene already landed.
5. The chapter endings are strong, but the middle has repeated reset points.

## Recommendations

- Give each chapter one sentence-length objective and cut/relocate scenes that do not tighten it.
- Use 3–5 major movements rather than servicing six matchups.
- Put comprehensive scores in artifacts/ledgers; prose should foreground only numbers that change a decision or relationship.
- Enter scenes later and exit earlier.
- Require every scene to alter stakes, knowledge, relationship, resource or direction.
- Allow a strong weekly event to wait for a later chapter instead of forcing immediate coverage.

## Do not change

Do not shorten simply to create uniform chapter length. Compression must serve pressure.

## Confidence

Very high.
