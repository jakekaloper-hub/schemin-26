# Round 1 — Joe Abercrombie Published-Method Lens

**Status:** INDEPENDENT / LOCKED  
**Domain:** Tight POV, distinct character voice, human-scale epic action, revision

## Governing diagnosis

The characters look radically different, but too many scenes are still experienced from the **same tonal altitude**: Edrin watches, a principal says something dry, the institution records the consequence. The book needs more scenes that could only be told through one specific character.

## Preserve

1. Physical comedy rooted in consequence rather than parody.
2. Jake in the swamp.
3. Austin at the closed gate.
4. Brandon keeping the returned receipt.
5. The damage/repair continuity.

## Highest-leverage weaknesses

1. Principal voices often converge on dry competence and witty deflection.
2. Epic encounters are frequently aftermath reports rather than embodied experience.
3. Edrin can become a safe observational buffer between reader and humiliation/fear/ambition.
4. Character defeats sometimes become neat thematic examples too quickly.
5. Revision needs to be performed one POV at a time, not only chapter-wide.

## Recommendations

- Give future major scenes a POV owner whose body and attention shape the prose.
- Build character-specific voice/attention packets.
- Let some consequences remain ugly, unresolved or misinterpreted.
- Preserve injuries, embarrassment, damaged spaces and altered relationships across chapters.
- Cut dialogue that another principal could say unchanged.
- Keep humor, but make it character-specific.

## Do not change

Do not imitate grimdark tone, profanity or violence. The useful method is tight viewpoint and ruthless revision.

## Confidence

High.
