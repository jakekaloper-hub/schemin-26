# Round 1 — George R.R. Martin Published-Method Lens

**Status:** INDEPENDENT / LOCKED  
**Domain:** Ensemble causality, multi-POV, long-form epic architecture

## Governing diagnosis

The manuscript is no longer a memo, but Chapters I–II still retain the **schedule as hidden outline**: one fantasy week becomes one chapter, all six outcomes receive coverage, and Edrin provides connective tissue for nearly everything. The result is strong scene writing arranged by coverage rather than by the strongest causal chain.

## Preserve

1. Jake/Austin proof-versus-possession tension.
2. Red/Chili provocation and return.
3. Mud Dogs / D0nkey K0ng emerging as new power centers.
4. Edrin/Oren as institutional witnesses.
5. The book's refusal to turn one result into destiny.

## Highest-leverage weaknesses

1. **Week = chapter** is too close to recap architecture.
2. **Six matchups must be serviced** creates episodic accumulation.
3. **Edrin witnesses too much**; the Twelve rarely control sustained viewpoint.
4. Several scenes are excellent but only loosely cause the next scene.
5. Secondary threads are often closed within the same chapter instead of crossing and colliding later.

## Recommendations

- Chapter boundaries should follow causal arcs, not scoring periods.
- A chapter may cover parts of multiple fantasy weeks; a fantasy week may generate multiple chapters or only an artifact/interlude.
- Limit normal chapters to one primary dramatic question, one causal spine and usually 1–3 POV carriers.
- Let events disappear off-page if their consequences are not yet story-significant.
- Use Edrin as chorus, bridge or institutional counterweight rather than mandatory camera.
- Build an open-loop ledger so provocation, humiliation, bargains, injuries, reputation and objects can remain unresolved across chapters.

## Do not change

Do not force equal POV rotation among twelve principals. Equality of coverage is not ensemble architecture.

## Confidence

Very high. This is the most important structural issue visible in the current manuscript.
