# Round 1 — J.K. Rowling Published-Method Lens

**Status:** INDEPENDENT / LOCKED  
**Domain:** Long-horizon setup/payoff, mystery, accessible world entry

## Governing diagnosis

The book has many memorable objects and institutions, but it often **pays meaning immediately**. A receipt appears and the chapter explains what it means; a standings board changes behavior and the narrator states the consequence. The long-form novel will become stronger if more information is planted before it is interpreted.

## Preserve

1. Receipts, Belt, empty cradle, returned parcel, rejected bids and damaged objects.
2. Recurrent Archive spaces.
3. Institutional rules learned through conflict.
4. Humor that reveals character or bureaucracy.
5. The outsider-friendly Prologue.

## Highest-leverage weaknesses

1. Too many symbols are interpreted in the same scene they appear.
2. The reader has limited unresolved mystery beyond "who will win."
3. Recurring institutions sometimes repeat their function rather than reveal a new layer.
4. Exact scores and rule explanations can crowd out curiosity.
5. The first two chapters resolve nearly every weekly subplot before moving on.

## Recommendations

- Maintain a promise/open-loop ledger.
- Distinguish **plant**, **development**, **partial payoff**, **payoff** and **abandon with reason**.
- Let some objects travel for several chapters before their significance becomes clear.
- Introduce fewer proper nouns/rules at once; delay explanation until the reader needs it.
- Build recurring places so each return reveals a new function or relationship.
- Do not require each week's provocations to resolve inside that week's chapter.

## Do not change

Do not manufacture mystery by hiding facts a POV naturally knows. Mystery must come from genuinely incomplete information.

## Confidence

High.
