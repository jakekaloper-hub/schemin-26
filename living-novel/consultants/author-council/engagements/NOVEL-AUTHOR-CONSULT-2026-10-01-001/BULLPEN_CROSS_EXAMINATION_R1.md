# Bullpen Cross-Examination — After Round 1

## Question 1 — Why did Chapters I–II cover all six weekly outcomes?

**Martin / Grisham challenge:** Was this a literary requirement or a proof-of-concept requirement?

**Beat Writer / Groundskeeper response:** The Week 1 production packet explicitly set out to reconstruct Week 1 as the first controlled test of the pipeline from evidence through manuscript. It required preservation of all six locked results and league recognition. Chapter II repeated that operating grammar to prove continuity. No active Novel doctrine says a future chapter must service all six weekly outcomes.

**Ruling:** coverage completeness was a prototype constraint, not permanent chapter law.

## Question 2 — Why does Edrin witness so much?

**Le Guin / Abercrombie challenge:** Is Edrin the novel's permanent viewpoint, or was he a safe frame for initial world introduction?

**Beat Writer / Librarian response:** The Prologue deliberately made Edrin/Oren an epistemic frame so real league facts could enter fiction without unsupported psychology. Week 3 constitution already permits a layered chronicle model rather than a permanent mascot narrator. No frozen evidence requires Edrin to witness every major event.

**Ruling:** Edrin remains canonical and valuable but is not default POV for all future scenes.

## Question 3 — Can the world support stories away from the Archive?

**Tolkien challenge:** Does current world infrastructure support geographically independent story?

**Architect / Librarian response:** Yes. Author Room Universe Hydration, Location Control Plane, World Engine V1.1 and Universe OS/Atlas provide active location, route, state and history inputs. Early chapters predate or underuse that maturity.

**Ruling:** prospective chapters should consume world/location packets and may leave the central city whenever causality requires.

## Question 4 — Must exact scores appear in prose?

**Grisham / Sanderson challenge:** Is full numeric fidelity required inside the narrative body?

**Scout / Umpire response:** League truth must remain preserved, but preservation can occur in source evidence and in-world artifacts. A number belongs in prose only when it materially changes action, interpretation or relationship.

**Ruling:** comprehensive score truth remains mandatory; comprehensive score foregrounding does not.

## Question 5 — Do we already have a long-horizon promise/open-loop system?

**Rowling challenge:** Where are unresolved setups tracked across chapters?

**Librarian / Architect response:** Novel OS contains generic promise validation, but no engagement-level open-loop ledger was located that binds plants, developments and payoffs to chapter architecture.

**Ruling:** gap accepted.

## Question 6 — Can principal-character POV be used safely when characters derive from real league owners?

**Le Guin challenge:** How do we avoid presenting invented real-world psychology as fact?

**Librarian / Umpire response:** Fictional character interiority may exist only as Schemin-character narrative construction under evidence/canon constraints. It cannot be cited as real-owner motive. POV packets must separate verified knowledge, fictional inference, unknowns and prohibited claims.

**Ruling:** principal POV is allowed prospectively under a fail-closed POV constitution.

## Question 7 — What happens to the three closed manuscripts?

**Full Council challenge:** Does this diagnosis reopen Prologue / Chapters I–II automatically?

**Librarian / Closer response:** No. Each is HARD MANUSCRIPT CANON / CLOSED. This engagement can diagnose and preserve future-edition recommendations, but the current implementation target is Chapter III+ architecture. Any rewrite requires a separate explicit canon-reopen decision.

## Bullpen conclusion

The central Round 1 diagnosis survives cross-examination:

> **The early chapters proved faithful weekly translation. The mature Novel must now prioritize causal story architecture over weekly coverage architecture.**
