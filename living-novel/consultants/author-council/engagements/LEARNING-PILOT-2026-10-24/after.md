The road keeper shouted again. Below Wilson, two laborers had turned the broken axle across the lower path, but its far end still lay in the wheel track. A wagon waited on the northern shoulder. The driver held his team where the track widened; farther down, wheels stood still behind him.

Wilson shifted his hands on the axe. The split shoulder strap let the armor drag against his ribs. He could reach the path, but not without picking his way down the churned slope. Below the ridge, Mud Dogs were withdrawing toward the causeway. For a moment their leader showed through the rain, a chain touching stone. Then the rise hid him.

The attendant reached Wilson carrying the wet standard across both arms.

“Summit?”

Wilson looked up. A custodian was bent over a boundary marker; another drove a temporary stake into the ground. The hammer stopped, and in the space it left he heard a wheel knock against wood below.

The laborers had lifted the axle clear at one end. The other end swung back into the track. Neither could let go long enough to shift his grip.

“Not yet,” Wilson said.

“Keep that clear of the wheels.”

The attendant stepped uphill with the standard. Wilson set his axe beside him and worked his way down to the lower path. He took the end of the axle that kept swinging back.

“Toward me,” one laborer said. “Not up.”

Wilson lowered his hands. The edge of his armor pressed into his ribs as he bent, and for a moment he could do nothing but hold. The laborer shifted his grip. Together they dragged the axle sideways, leaving a dark furrow across the wet track. When it reached the verge, Wilson let go and stepped back onto the slope.

Neither laborer looked toward the summit. One waved the driver forward; the other stayed beside the broken axle. Wilson climbed the few steps to retrieve his weapon.

The first wagon moved. Its wheels sank into the old ruts, rose, and carried on around the northern shoulder. Wilson stood clear as the next driver brought his team through. Above him the summit was still bare. The attendant had not asked again.
