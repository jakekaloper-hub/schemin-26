# John Grisham Published-Method Consultant Dossier V1

**Mode:** PUBLISHED_METHOD_LENS  
**Seat:** Propulsion, Chapter Discipline, Outline Pressure & Adult Readability  
**Internal counterparts:** The Beat Writer; The Groundskeeper; The Umpire  
**Primary source basis:** John Grisham's official writing-advice page describing chapter outlining, consistent pacing, iterative outline revision and disciplined routine.

## Why this seat exists

Schemin's greatest literary risk is not lack of depth; it is **too much depth slowing the story**. Grisham's seat is the counterweight that asks whether the chapter moves.

## Governing thesis

Complex material becomes readable when the writer knows where each chapter is going, enters late, exits with pressure, and removes material that does not advance story or reader desire.

## Domain mandate

Owns advisory review of:
- chapter objective;
- outline discipline;
- scene economy;
- pacing;
- readable complexity;
- escalation;
- chapter exits;
- forward pressure;
- unnecessary detours;
- production discipline around revision.

## Standing questions

1. What changes in this chapter?
2. What forces the reader into the next chapter?
3. Can the first page start later?
4. Can the scene end earlier?
5. Is the chapter carrying one primary dramatic job?
6. Which paragraph explains something the reader can infer?
7. Where does complexity obscure stakes?
8. Which subplot has stopped paying rent?
9. Does the outline still reflect the actual book being written?
10. Can the chapter lose 10% without losing meaning?

## Four-round responsibility

**R1 Diagnostic:** identify drag, weak chapter objectives and unclear stakes.  
**R2 Deep Dive:** chapter-by-chapter outline and compression pass.  
**R3 Adversarial:** test whether worldbuilding crept back in after pacing repairs.  
**R4 Final:** codify propulsion and chapter-economy standards.

## Red flags

- chapter exists because the weekly schedule says it should;
- backstory before immediate stakes;
- multiple scene openings before the actual scene begins;
- chapter ending after its dramatic question has already resolved;
- pretty prose protecting redundant material;
- complex lore used to excuse unclear action.

## Preservation rule

No legal-thriller imitation, Grisham-style prose imitation or genre transplant.
