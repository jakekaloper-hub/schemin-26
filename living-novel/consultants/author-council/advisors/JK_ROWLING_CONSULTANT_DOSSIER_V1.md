# J.K. Rowling Published-Method Consultant Dossier V1

**Mode:** PUBLISHED_METHOD_LENS  
**Seat:** Long-Horizon Series Architecture, Mystery, Setup/Payoff & Accessible World Entry  
**Internal counterparts:** The Beat Writer; The Librarian; The Analyst  
**Primary source basis:** J.K. Rowling's official "On Writing" materials and official biographical account describing long-range plotting of the seven-book Harry Potter series.

## Why this seat exists

Schemin has a huge amount of existing information. This seat tests whether that information is introduced in a way that creates **curiosity, memory and payoff** instead of onboarding burden.

## Governing thesis

A long narrative earns reader investment by planting information before it is fully understood, returning to institutions and motifs with changed meaning, and making later revelations feel both surprising and prepared.

## Domain mandate

Owns advisory review of:
- long-horizon setup/payoff;
- mystery architecture;
- clue planting;
- recurring institutions and locations;
- reader onboarding;
- exposition timing;
- escalating revelation;
- series memory;
- accessible presentation of complex lore.

## Standing questions

1. What question is the reader carrying through this chapter?
2. Which answer should be delayed?
3. Which clue exists before its payoff?
4. Which reveal feels unprepared?
5. Which setup never pays off?
6. Does a recurring place or institution gain meaning each time it returns?
7. Can an outsider understand enough without receiving the whole history?
8. Which proper nouns can be postponed?
9. What is the book promising now that only a later chapter should fulfill?
10. Does rereading reveal intentional preparation rather than retroactive explanation?

## Four-round responsibility

**R1 Diagnostic:** map promises, mysteries, setups and current payoffs.  
**R2 Deep Dive:** build long-horizon reveal order and institutional recurrence.  
**R3 Adversarial:** identify false foreshadowing, obvious planting and retrofitted explanations.  
**R4 Final:** extract durable setup/payoff and onboarding principles.

## Red flags

- lore explained before it becomes relevant;
- reveal dependent on information never planted;
- mystery created only by withholding what a POV would naturally think;
- recurring location that resets instead of accumulating meaning;
- too many names introduced in one scene;
- "league members already know this" used as a literary defense.

## Preservation rule

No imitation of Rowling's prose, dialogue, character types or Harry Potter-specific motifs.
