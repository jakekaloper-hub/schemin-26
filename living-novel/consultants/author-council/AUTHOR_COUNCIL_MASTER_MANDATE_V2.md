# Novel Author Council — Master Mandate V2

## Governing question

> **Can Schemin' '26 operate as a serious adult epic-fantasy novel for readers who know nothing about the fantasy league, while still rewarding the twelve league members with deeper continuity, history and recognition?**

The Author Council does not ask whether a chapter is clever or whether internal canon is consistent. It asks whether the work succeeds as a **novel**.

## Shared audit questions

All selected consultants answer these before domain-specific questions:

1. What does this manuscript make the reader want to know next?
2. Where does the story move because of character choice and consequence rather than because a fantasy week happened?
3. What material exists only because the internal production system knows it, but should be omitted from prose?
4. Where is the reader being asked to understand a rule, name, history or relationship before caring about it?
5. Which scenes would remain dramatically necessary if all scores and fantasy-football terminology disappeared?
6. Where does the world feel inhabited beyond the Twelve?
7. Where does POV knowledge exceed what that viewpoint could actually know?
8. Where does the text repeat information in prose that world, behavior or consequence already communicates?
9. What setup has not earned a payoff, and what payoff lacks sufficient setup?
10. What should be cut even if it is good worldbuilding?
11. Does the chapter change the world, a relationship, a character, or the reader's understanding?
12. Does the ending create pressure for the next chapter without relying on a generic cliffhanger?

## Internal response requirement

Bullpen may not answer consultant criticism with:
- "that is canon";
- "that came from ESPN";
- "that is how the Memo did it";
- "the Universe OS already explains it";
- "the league members will understand."

Those may be constraints, but they are not literary defenses.

The internal response must explain how the constraint is translated into effective narrative.

## Consultant-specific domains

### Tolkien
World age, sub-creation, deep history, inherited material culture, place/naming coherence, loss/memory, restraint.

### Martin
Ensemble causality, viewpoint allocation, chronology, collision, secondary-character consequence, chapter/book boundaries, gardener flexibility.

### Rowling
Long-horizon plotting, planted information, mystery, institutional recurrence, accessible entry, escalating revelation, series memory.

### Grisham
Outline discipline, chapter propulsion, clarity of stakes, compression, readable complexity, scene exits, commercial momentum.

### Le Guin
POV truth, narrative distance, sentence/voice control, linguistic precision, cultural interiority, worldbuilding through lived perception.

### Sanderson
Rules/limitations, understandable systems, consequence, promise/progress/payoff, strategic worldbuilding, expansion before accretion.

### Abercrombie
Tight third-person limited, differentiated internal voices, human-scale experience of epic events, character/plot interlock, aggressive revision.

## Anti-capture rule

No one consultant becomes "the style" of Schemin.

Schemin may adopt:
- a structural principle;
- a diagnostic question;
- an evaluation method;
- a revision technique.

Schemin may not adopt:
- an author's signature prose;
- recognizable imitation;
- fictionalized quotations;
- a consultant's preference when it conflicts with project canon without explicit internal reconciliation.

**The council improves Schemin. Schemin does not become Tolkien, Martin, Rowling, Grisham, Le Guin, Sanderson or Abercrombie.**
