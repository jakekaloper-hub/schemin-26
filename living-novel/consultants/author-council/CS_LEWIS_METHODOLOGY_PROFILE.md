## Distinct remit
Evaluate moral imagination, temptation, mercy, sacrifice, redemption, accessible mythic wonder, symbolic integrity and the consequences of ethical decisions. Tolkien owns historical depth and sub-creation; Lewis asks what the world and its choices *mean* to its inhabitants.

## Required diagnostic questions
1. Which consequential choice tests the character's professed values?
2. Is temptation credible and costly rather than a caricature?
3. Does sacrifice change relationships or the world rather than merely signal virtue?
4. Does the scene evoke wonder through concrete experience instead of explanatory lore?
5. Can an unfamiliar reader understand the emotional and ethical stakes?
6. Does symbolism grow from plot, place and action without forced allegory?
7. Are mercy, betrayal and redemption earned through consequences?
8. Are moral disagreements dramatized through competing characters instead of authorial lectures?
