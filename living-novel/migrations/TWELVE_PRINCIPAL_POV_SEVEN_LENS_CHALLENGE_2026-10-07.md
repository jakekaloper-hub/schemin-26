# Twelve-Principal POV Migration — Seven-Lens Architecture Challenge

**Captured:** 2026-10-07  
**Review mode:** Internal independent method-lens analysis by the current executor  
**Consultation status:** This is not a live external-author engagement, endorsement, or a substitute for the Full Seven review required by the active Author Council program.  
**Materials reviewed:** PR #130 migration program and proposed finished-reader architecture; current POV Constitution V1; Chapter III principal-POV precedent; official Week 4 Memo classification and candidate Chapter IV integration notes.

## Shared brief used by all seven lenses

The intended product is one persistent novel-world and one canonical story graph, experienced through the Twelve owner/principal characters. Readers may take canonical book order or a principal path. Route choice can change scene access, viewpoint, and revelation order; it cannot change event truth or shared world state. The published Week 4 Memo supplies publication evidence and editorial framing. It does not determine novel chapter order, stats authority, or prose canon. Prologue and Chapters I–III remain closed hard canon until exact-byte migration gates pass.

## Independent method-lens reads

### 1. Secondary-world depth lens

**Challenge:** If Edrin disappears, does the world still feel as though it exists when no principal is looking?

**Finding:** The proposed model correctly keeps institutions, witnesses, merchants, records, and civic consequences active without granting them narrative interiority. That is the right architecture for preserving a world larger than its protagonists. The risk is making the Archive the only carrier of historical depth, then replacing it with thin, owner-centered scenes.

**Required design condition:** Keep the Archive, Record House, officials, public rules, material traces, ordinary work, and places in the shared world model. Let a principal encounter them when causally relevant. Retain institution/object records in world state even when no scene is licensed.

**Verdict:** Accept the direction; add an explicit regression that tests secondary-world agency and continuity after each back-migration.

### 2. Ensemble-causality lens

**Challenge:** Do twelve paths produce one interlocked novel or twelve parallel recaps?

**Finding:** A single event graph with curated paths is sound. The Week 4 Memo's six matchup packages must not turn into six mandatory chapters or six scenes. The ObiWan–D0nkey K0ng Chapter IV candidate has a causal reason to lead: the result tests Chapter III's distinction between public crown interpretation and sovereignty. Other outcomes can stay in shared event/state records until one creates a consequence that belongs on-page.

**Required design condition:** Every scene has one scene owner and a consequence edge. Repeated events across paths require distinct knowledge, cost, bias, or downstream consequence. A path may omit an event without erasing it from shared truth.

**Verdict:** Accept. Add a duplicate-scene test and preserve explicit BANK/BACKGROUND routing for non-foreground events.

### 3. Reader-onboarding and setup/payoff lens

**Challenge:** Can a reader choose one character without needing eleven other paths or a private league glossary?

**Finding:** Canonical Book Order plus “Choose a Principal” is understandable. Twelve starting choices create a high onboarding burden, and stand-alone path comprehension cannot be asserted from a schema alone. The Week 4 Memo is useful as public-facing context but its weekly pacing and score-first packaging should not be imported as a novel outline.

**Required design condition:** Each route needs a short, spoiler-safe orientation drawn from existing canon; each scene needs enough local context to understand stakes. Show the reader where they are in chronology and what their chosen principal can know. Test with a cold reader who knows no fantasy football and a reader who sees only one path.

**Verdict:** Accept with a usability test before release. Do not let route labels or recaps become a neutral narrator.

### 4. Propulsion and compression lens

**Challenge:** Does the route architecture create forward motion, or does it make the reader repeat the same event twelve times?

**Finding:** The “same event, different consequence” criterion protects against repetition. But twelve manifests can tempt production to fill every path with equal material. That would recreate the weekly Memo's coverage obligation at book scale.

**Required design condition:** Use a scene only when it changes a decision, cost, relationship, uncertainty, resource, rule application, or later action. Preserve unused verified outcomes in the evidence/state layer. Do not set a minimum scene count per character or per week.

**Verdict:** Accept. Add path-level redundancy and momentum QA; allow uneven path lengths.

### 5. POV legitimacy and knowledge-boundary lens

**Challenge:** Is interiority earned by a licensed principal, and is uncertainty actually theirs?

**Finding:** The required before/on-page/after knowledge envelope and real-owner firewall are strong. The El Niño/Manning exception is correctly treated as a blocker. Canon calls El Niño a storm and forbids El Niño as POV; weather cannot know, narrate, or stand in for a human character.

**Required design condition:** Resolve which of the Twelve principal character IDs owns the route and establish its legal knowledge sources under existing Character and World canon. If existing authorities do not resolve identity, do not draft that route until the Character Director and World Architect issue a governed proposal and the Umpire reviews it. Do not silently convert a storm into a consciousness.

**Verdict:** Architecture accepted conditionally; no El Niño path until its dedicated canon gate passes.

### 6. Reader-facing rules and system-legibility lens

**Challenge:** Can route behavior be explained and verified without becoming a second canon engine?

**Finding:** Generating manifests as views over one canonical story graph is correct. The proposal's offline static reader avoids a service dependency. A prototype is useful only after real scenes and route rules exist; building a full editor or backend now would be premature.

**Required design condition:** Scene identity, chronology, source evidence, POV license, knowledge envelope, state delta, spoiler boundary, route membership, and canon status must each have one authoritative field. Reader UI reads those records and cannot write them. Start with a small static route proof before packaging all twelve.

**Verdict:** Accept; keep the reader surface read-only and dependency-light.

### 7. Voice and embodied-consequence lens

**Challenge:** Can a reader identify the viewpoint from what the character notices and risks, rather than from a name label?

**Finding:** Removing Edrin can expose real differences among the Twelve, but packets and schemas do not prove literary voice. A shared clever, dry register could simply reproduce Edrin's observational smoothness twelve times. The Chapter III Wilson POV is useful evidence that principal-owned consequence can work, not evidence that all twelve voices are ready.

**Required design condition:** For each drafted path, run a prose-based differentiation pass on attention, metaphor, sentence pressure, humor, physical awareness, uncertainty, and what the character misses. Use current canon and fictional interiority only. Judge voice through actual adjacent scenes, not profile adjectives alone.

**Verdict:** Accept with manuscript-level proof required. No voice-packet completion can substitute for scene review.

## Cross-examination and decisions

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Does a path create an alternate timeline? | No. Every route is a different access path through shared outcomes and state. |
| Must every Week 4 matchup appear in foreground prose? | No. All six belong in the shared evidence/state layer; only causally earned scenes enter prose. |
| Can the official Memo determine the novel's scene order? | No. It establishes publication framing; narrative order follows causality and character consequence. |
| Can Edrin's former archive function be transferred to an unnamed narrator? | No. Records and institutions remain world objects; only the Twelve receive interior POV. |
| Can the El Niño route be written by treating weather as omniscient? | No. The path remains blocked pending a canon-valid Twelve-principal resolution. |
| Is the static reader prototype the source of truth? | No. It is a read-only edition surface over the canonical graph and generated manifests. |
| Does this review satisfy the required Full Seven gate? | No. It is a transparent internal method-lens challenge; the defined independent council round and Bullpen reconciliation remain open. |

## Architecture changes to carry forward

1. Keep one canonical event/scene graph; treat each reader route as a derived manifest.
2. Preserve the full Week 4 outcome/state set while foregrounding only causally selected events.
3. Require cold-reader tests for canonical order and for a single-principal route.
4. Keep world institutions active in state even when they have no licensed narrator.
5. Block Manning/El Niño route construction until existing canon and Character/World authority resolve it.
6. Prove voice and path independence in actual prose before declaring the corresponding route ready.
7. Defer a production-grade reader UI; first prove the route contract using static content and validators.
