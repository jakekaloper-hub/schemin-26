# Schemin' '26 — Author Council: World-Before-Plot and Cinematic Novel Expansion
**Status:** AUTHORIAL ADVISORY PROPOSAL / NOT HARD CANON
**Mission:** Bullpen #117, Living Novel 12-POV migration
**Review mode:** research-grounded craft lenses only. This document does **not** claim conversations with Tolkien, Martin, Jackson, or other creators, nor an authenticated Bullpen Director runtime.

## Core thesis
The verified league is the **consequence clock**, not the novel's entire plot. The League decides matches, injuries, transactions, rankings and title outcomes. The fictional world supplies all the causes and side-effects a character can experience **without fabricating real results**: weathered roads, ceremonies, debts, distrust, apprenticeships, rituals, meals, disputes, mysteries, relationships, settlements, and costs of travel. A great chapter stands on its own as fantasy prose and still becomes more consequential when the next real league result arrives.

## Jackson production-process precedent (NOT imitation)
The *Fellowship of the Ring* Extended Edition Appendix "From Book to Vision" groups book-to-script adaptation, storyboards/previsualization, Middle-earth production design, peoples and realms, and geographic views; Wētā artists and collaborators built lived environments and recognizable materials. The transferrable METHOD is to run a manuscript adaptation room beside an environment/prop/costume/visualization room, then let spatial and material discoveries improve prose. ## Independent analytical lenses to be instantiated as source-grounded consultant packets
**Tolkien — Age and depth.** Before a principal enters a road, establish its builders, repairs, earlier names, toll history, local fears, and inhabitants who do not know the league standings. Test: can the location support three ordinary stories with no principal on page? Reject fabricated claims about real owners.

**George R. R. Martin — Rival agency and intersecting desires.** The twelve principals need separate local objectives. Cross them at a bridge, creditor, magistrate, sanctuary, or food shortage; allow competing partial truths. Side quests must create novel-scale consequences without inventing victories, losses or privileged personal knowledge. A win can cost a relationship; a loss can produce an ally.

**Le Guin — Permission to know.** Every scene belongs to exactly one principal and only what that principal could witness, hear, remember or infer may be stated. The El Niño storm can be experienced as weather and consequence, but current Living Novel ontology forbids storm interiority/dialogue. The twelve-selector product therefore has a real unresolved Manning-path design gate, not a license to create weather omniscience.

**Grisham — Narrative contracts.** A subplot begins with an actionable question, escalates through a decision, and ends by changing the next choice. Cut travelogue that neither reveals a person nor changes the stakes. Each chapter needs a pressure line regardless of season status.

**Rowling — Reader orientation and long payoff.** Recurring objects (the empty Belt cradle, receipt, chili vessel, broken survey marker, bridge toll) should be introduced clearly and brought back with changed meaning. Friendly, local detail can make readers care before a crisis. Do not overburden the Prologue with twelve character biographies.

**Sanderson — Rules and consequences.** Make Compact procedures, neutral-site rules, weather impacts, travel permissions, wagers and challenge obligations intelligible through enacted conflict. Distinguish what is governed from what is rumor; no implied magical mechanics without canon decisions.

**Abercrombie — Embodied voice and moral friction.** One scene, one person, one concrete want, one price. Differentiate the characters by what they notice and avoid (the angle of a step, the resale value of timber, a dog's scent, the weight of a receipt). Ordinary comedy can be the cost of status rather than a disposable joke.

## Combined story model: three braided clocks
1. **Verified Event Clock:** ESPN/Flaim and signed Memo evidence. Only this clock can determine competitive outcomes. Preserve source timestamp, confidence and finality.
2. **World Clock:** Atlas hydrology, roads, market cycles, weather, social institutions, repairs and persistent locations; licensed fiction only.
3. **Character Clock:** Principal goals, escalating debts/promises, knowledge, community obligation, personal choice and changed bonds; fictional interpretive canon.

A scene may originate from clocks 2 or 3. Clock 1 can later collide with it. NEVER reverse-cause Clock 1 from invented fiction.

## Side-quest admission rules
A quest requires (a) principal POV, (b) permitted location and route, (c) a want unrelated to predicting a matchup, (d) resistance from an autonomous character/institution/environment, (e) material price, (f) at least one recoverable world-state or relationship delta, (g) closure or deliberate unresolved promise, (h) explicit evidence vs invention labels. Not all twelve quests are mandatory in every chapter.

### Episode rhythm
- **Opening life:** texture of ordinary work, rituals, place or a meaningful lack.
- **Local disturbance:** side-quest problem from an existing social/world pressure.
- **Choice and bill:** principal chooses; someone else pays or remembers.
- **Contested evidence:** a league source event arrives only when genuinely verified and chronologically available.
- **Collision:** established side quest changes how a verified result is interpreted, never the actual result.
- **Residue:** landscape, object, relationship or institution is different at chapter end.

### Decompression rule
A slow scene is earned if it makes the reader care or establishes a cause to be collected later. "Fluff" is not empty length: it is meaningful texture, an apparently modest choice whose cost becomes legible later, and occasional genuine rest.

## Gates to qualify a finished chapter
- Evidence manifest: every league assertion traceable to Flaim/ESPN or published Memo with time/finality.
- Atlas manifest: actual authoritative LOC IDs if available; otherwise UNKNOWN, not invented IDs, routes or distances.
- Character manifest: exactly one of twelve licensed POVs for every prose scene; temporal states and real-person firewall enforced.
- Object register: previous owner and future meaning of recurring items.
- Literary review: independent substantive criticism, not generic author-name labels or impersonation.
- Visual direction: source-controlled approved character references, material/lighting/environment briefs, no fictional "camera" omniscience and no unverified rendered visuals.
- Editorial compression test: remove any episode that changes nothing.
- Canon transaction: separate Founder approval for altered hard manuscripts.

## Prologue consequence
Do NOT rush the current V2 revision into canon. The risk is replacing an interesting institutional world with Jake watching a dozen entrances. Rebuild the opening into a lived threshold: a road obligation, a tangible Record House procedure, a distinct Farell memorial/Belt ritual, and one or two deeply dramatized encounters; allow the rest to enter through delayed consequences. Keep the remaining character quests available for Chapters I onward, and ensure one principal never serves as a disguised omniscient narrator.

## The first creative experiment
Select one short noncompetitive side quest at a grounded location, write its scene under a single principal, produce a world-state delta and optional non-generative storyboard/visual brief, then conduct literary red-team review. Evaluate whether the prose grows more novelistic before scaling. Do not let a merely pretty visual pass the story gate.
