# NARRATIVE ARCHITECTURE RESET — WEEK IS NOT CHAPTER V1

Status: COMMISSIONER AUTHORIZED / GOVERNING NOVEL LAW
Date: 2026-09-29

## Core ruling
FANTASY WEEK != NOVEL CHAPTER.

A fantasy week is an immutable chronological/evidence container.
A chapter is a reader-facing dramatic movement.
No Novel OS process may create, title, size or close a chapter merely because a fantasy matchup period ended.

## Three clocks

### 1. LEAGUE TIME — immutable chronology
Draft, keeper decisions, matchup periods, transactions, injuries, waivers, playoffs and verified outcomes.
League Time answers: WHAT HAPPENED, AND WHEN?

### 2. CHARACTER TIME — persistent causal arcs
Wants, resources, knowledge, relationships, reputation, obligations, costs, decisions and unresolved consequences.
Character Time answers: WHAT IS HAPPENING TO THIS PERSON/POWER ACROSS EVENTS?

### 3. NARRATIVE TIME — reader experience
Scenes, POV placement, withholding/revelation, chapter boundaries, callbacks, juxtaposition, pacing and act/book turns.
Narrative Time answers: WHAT MUST THE READER EXPERIENCE NOW?

The three clocks interact but may never be collapsed.

## Reader-facing hierarchy
BOOK / ACT
→ CHAPTER
→ SCENE
→ POV CONTRACT
→ BEAT.

Underneath:
LEAGUE TIME + CHARACTER STATE + WORLD STATE + RELATIONSHIP STATE + KNOWLEDGE STATE.

## Definitions
WEEK = immutable chronological container.
ARC = persistent causal/character thread crossing any number of weeks.
SCENE = dramatized collision/revelation that materially changes understanding, choice, relationship, resource or world state.
CHAPTER = coherent dramatic movement assembled from the scenes required to advance the central story.
BOOK/ACT = major transformation in campaign possibilities, relationships, institutions or the meaning of the season.

## Chapter admission law
A chapter exists only if it has:
1. an entering dramatic question;
2. active character pressure;
3. consequential movement or revelation;
4. an exit state different from entry;
5. a reason the reader needs this movement now.

A weekly result alone does not satisfy this test.

## Temporal freedom
A chapter may:
- span multiple fantasy weeks;
- contain only part of one week;
- revisit an earlier event from a newly relevant POV;
- intercut simultaneous events;
- omit low-significance weekly events from dramatized prose while preserving them in state;
- delay payoff of an event until its consequence becomes narratively legible.

It may NOT:
- change verified chronology;
- give a POV future knowledge;
- manufacture causation;
- move an event to a false date for convenience.

## POV law
POV does not dictate chapter naming or equal rotation.
POV is allocated according to narrative necessity.
PRIMARY, SECONDARY, OBSERVED and OFF_PAGE_ACTIVE remain valid.
All Twelve remain state-active even when absent from prose.

## Edrin / Archive law
Edrin remains the connective moral/intellectual spine, not the weekly recap host.
The Archive records and contests meaning; it does not manufacture a chapter because a scoring period ended.

## Existing manuscript ruling
PROLOGUE_DRAFT_V1 and Chapters I–III are no longer protected as final structural units.
They remain:
- evidence-respecting manuscript material;
- scene inventory;
- voice/reference material;
- source of approved world objects and narrative discoveries.

Bullpen may:
MERGE / SPLIT / REORDER SCENES / REFRAME POV / RETITLE / CUT / EXPAND / REWRITE.

Bullpen may not alter verified league facts, chronology, hard canon or memorial safeguards.

## Live-season act architecture
Do not preassign week boundaries to acts.
Acts emerge when campaign possibility materially changes.
Candidate macro movement only:
I — OPENING ORDER / expectations encounter reality.
II — FIELD TAKES SHAPE / reputations and contenders acquire consequence.
III — COST OF CONTENDING / accumulated choices and scarcity collect payment.
IV — ROAD NARROWS / future possibilities collapse toward postseason.
V — KEEPING / postseason, championship, memory and resolution.

These are hypotheses, not predetermined result arcs.

## Literary craft lenses
Mythic lens: history, inheritance, place, names, objects and institutions exert present pressure.
Political-character lens: competing wants, incomplete knowledge, finite resources, asymmetric relationships, voluntary cost and reversal drive scenes.
Never imitate another author's prose.

## New governing question
Do not ask: "What happened this week?"
Ask:
"Which unresolved story moved because of what happened, whose experience best reveals that movement, and what does the reader need next?"
