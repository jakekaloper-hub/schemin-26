# Novel Causal Chapter Architecture V1

**Status:** RELEASE CANDIDATE — Author Council Engagement 001  
**Applies prospectively:** Chapter III onward  
**Does not reopen:** Prologue, Chapter I, Chapter II

## Governing law

> **A source week is evidence. A chapter is causality. They are not the same unit.**

Verified fantasy events enter the Novel through significance grading, world/character consequence and continuity state. They do not earn prose merely because they occurred in the same scoring period.

## Chapter admission test

A proposed chapter must have:
1. one primary dramatic question;
2. one causal spine;
3. a defined entering state;
4. 1–3 primary POV carriers unless a documented exception is approved;
5. an active location/route packet;
6. a turning point that changes knowledge, relationship, resource, status, direction or world state;
7. a closing consequence;
8. explicit open loops carried forward;
9. an omitted-event ledger showing verified events intentionally left outside the foreground.

If the chapter cannot state its dramatic question in one sentence, it is not ready.

## Significance routing

Suggested default:
- **S5 / S4:** foreground scene or chapter spine candidate.
- **S3:** supporting scene, consequence beat or delayed thread.
- **S2:** artifact/interlude/background state unless continuity later increases importance.
- **S1 / S0:** evidence/state only.

Significance is not destiny. Later consequences may promote an older event into current story relevance.

## Coverage rule

The Novel must preserve all verified league truth in its evidence/state systems.

The Novel does **not** owe equal narrative coverage to:
- every matchup;
- every owner;
- every waiver;
- every score;
- every source week.

Equal coverage is a publication/memo concept, not a long-form-fiction requirement.

## Chapter-time rule

A chapter may:
- cover part of one scoring period;
- span multiple scoring periods;
- return to an earlier unresolved consequence;
- omit a scoring period entirely from foreground prose.

It may not:
- use future outcomes as earlier character knowledge;
- retroactively make an earlier decision inevitable;
- reorder causal dependencies without an explicit chronology device.

## Causal spine examples

Valid:
- a provocation leaves one domain, changes public behavior, and returns with altered meaning;
- two rivals make decisions that collide across separate source weeks;
- one undefeated status shifts suppliers, alliances and travel, causing a later confrontation;
- a failed acquisition changes the next Encounter before the Archive understands why.

Invalid:
- "cover all six Week 3 games";
- "give every owner one scene";
- "include the waiver wire because it happened Wednesday."

## Archive role

Edrin/Oren may carry:
- verification;
- institutional contradiction;
- historical distance;
- artifact handling;
- contested interpretation.

They are not required to witness every major event.

## Pre-prose packet

Every Chapter III+ dossier must include:
- decision question;
- selected source events;
- omitted source events;
- significance rationale;
- POV packet IDs;
- location packet IDs;
- open-loop inputs;
- open-loop outputs;
- reader-facing system rules needed;
- score/stat foreground decisions;
- temporal cutoff.

## Acceptance

A chapter fails architecture if its principal reason to exist is "this was the next fantasy week."
