# CHAPTER 2 — WHAT THE BOARD CANNOT HOLD
## Manuscript V2 — Continuous Story Rewrite

The package came back before the next results did.

That was why Edrin understood it mattered.

If the Red Leopards had waited until after the encounter, the returned parcel would have been celebration. Instead it arrived while the outcome was still unknowable, carrying the residue of a provocation that had begun after Chili’s enormous opening week.

Edrin found it on the receiving table.

“Food is not archival material,” he said.

Pell did not look up. “Then stop people from making history with lunch.”

The wrapping had been preserved. The contents had not.

The story around it was less tidy.

Chili’s 198 had traveled faster than any official analysis. Attention had become swagger in the public telling, swagger had become provocation, and the provocation had crossed the road toward Red.

Edrin refused to write that Brandon Pryor had expected fear.

He did not know.

He could write that the object had been sent.

He could write that Red had received it.

He could write that the city noticed.

Then the encounter happened.

Red Leopards 191.90.

Chili Cheesers 100.90.

Ninety-one points.

The returned package became something different without changing shape.

By evening it had acquired an audience.

By morning it had acquired retellings.

Someone claimed the Leopards had displayed it at the gate. Someone else claimed Chili’s people had refused delivery. A third version involved a horse, a trumpet and an insult Edrin was reasonably certain had been invented because it rhymed.

He entered only what survived verification.

The provocation existed before the result.

The result gave the object new social meaning.

The object did not cause the result.

Oren read the entry.

“You are becoming unpleasantly careful.”

“Thank you.”

“It was not praise.”

Across the city, Chili’s Week One eruption did not disappear.

That was the part public memory disliked.

People preferred reversals to coexistence.

198.00 remained true.

100.90 remained true.

The same campaign had produced both.

Red’s opening loss remained true.

191.90 remained true.

The same campaign had produced both.

The returned parcel survived because it could carry contradiction more easily than a standings line.

One and one.

One and one.

The board made them look equal.

Nobody in the city thought they had arrived there the same way.

That was the first crack.

The second came from the swamp.

Jake entered the southern road carrying the cleanest proof of Week One: he had beaten the reigning champion.

The city had used that result aggressively.

The swamp did not care.

Mud Dogs 130.84.

ObiWan Jacoby 122.91.

The report reached the Record House wet enough to curl at the edges.

Edrin laid it beneath weights.

Attached to it were the allocation sheets.

He read them once.

Then again.

“There it is.”

Oren looked over.

“What?”

“The mistake.”

“Whose?”

“Jake left points unused.”

“So did Mud Dogs.”

Edrin stopped.

Oren returned to his work.

The counterfactual was real. A different choice could have changed ObiWan’s total.

It was also incomplete. Mud had unused strength of its own.

Edrin felt the story he had been about to tell collapse.

It had been clean.

That should have warned him.

He wrote:

**RESULT DOES NOT SUPPORT SINGLE-DECISION CAUSATION.**

Then, after a moment:

**W1 PROOF PROVIDED NO PROTECTION FROM W2 UNCERTAINTY.**

That sentence Oren allowed.

By the time the southern report dried, another result had altered the shape of the board.

D0nkey K0ng 165.78.

Three Dreaded Snake 95.87.

The first week had given TDS force.

The second gave it damage.

Again public memory tried to choose one.

Again Edrin refused.

TDS had not become fraudulent because it lost badly.

Its preseason scarcity had not vanished when it scored 175.56.

Now scarcity and reversal occupied the same file.

That combination mattered because the market would open before the next encounter.

Edrin underlined nothing.

He did not need to.

Elsewhere, Slob on my Dobb erupted.

194.92 over Dr. Duckhook’s 122.97.

A campaign that had entered with abundance and failed to convert it in Week One now converted violently.

Duckhook, who had escaped the opener by 1.50, discovered how little a reputation for precision could do when the opposing total left no narrow edge to manage.

At the Hall of Measures, someone described this as the death of control.

Pell asked whether control had filed a death certificate.

The phrase disappeared from official discussion.

Seven Deadly Chins produced a quieter consequence.

The opening week had exposed a command allocation that mattered.

In the second encounter, the command changed.

Chins won.

Edrin did not call that proof of wisdom.

One correction did not establish a permanent trait.

But adaptation had become observable.

The prior cost had entered the next decision.

That was causality.

The LLC fell to zero and two.

Its pre-campaign capital remained visible. Its record did not care.

That contradiction began to irritate people who had predicted strength from resources alone.

Then El Niño denied Austin.

His Majesty’s Blood fell to zero and two.

The Belt remained.

That evening the north square filled again.

Not with celebration.

With argument.

Some people had begun calling the Belt ceremonial.

Others said two weekly losses could not erase a championship.

One merchant removed a black championship banner from his stall.

His neighbor bought it at a discount and hung it twice as high.

Edrin watched from the Record House steps.

The board read 0–2 beside His Majesty’s Blood.

Austin still possessed the one object nobody else could lawfully claim.

The title and the current campaign had begun moving in opposite directions.

That could not last forever.

It could last now.

Edrin wrote both.

The most important change, however, appeared at the top.

Only two campaigns remained undefeated.

D0nkey K0ng.

Mud Dogs.

The names were not the inherited center of the city’s preseason argument.

Now suppliers noticed.

Scouts noticed.

Bookmakers noticed.

Patron houses that had spent the opening pursuing Austin’s attention sent representatives elsewhere.

Edrin saw two of them at Wilson’s yard.

He saw another asking about travel conditions toward the swamp.

The standings had not merely recorded hierarchy.

They had begun redistributing attention.

Attention, in turn, altered conditions.

A supplier offered Wilson new terms.

A bridge contractor sent a proposal south.

Training observers appeared where they had not appeared the previous week.

The two undefeated campaigns had done more than win.

They had changed what third parties considered worth watching.

Edrin carried that observation to Oren.

“Can a record cause something?”

“A record can inform a choice.”

“That is not what I asked.”

“It is the answer you are allowed.”

Edrin hated this.

He wrote it down.

The market opened after midnight.

That was where the week stopped being six results.

People arrived because something had happened to them.

Not all of them had lost.

Not all of them needed the same thing.

TDS entered with the most visible pressure. One enormous opening, one collapse, and the old premium-capital scarcity still beneath both.

LLC representatives watched prices.

Chins’ staff pursued depth.

Other houses protected flexibility.

The market did not care whether a story sounded satisfying.

Every acquisition had a cost.

Every unspent resource preserved an option.

Every option surrendered now became unavailable later.

Edrin sat behind the transaction desk and realized he had been wrong about the purpose of the continuity ledger.

He had thought it existed to explain results after they happened.

Here, before the next encounter, the ledger showed something more useful.

It showed what the last result had made possible.

And what it had made necessary.

Near dawn he returned to the brass standings board.

Two undefeated.

Two winless.

Eight between.

The structure was correct.

It was also becoming absurdly insufficient.

Chili and Red both stood one and one.

Jake and several others stood one and one.

Austin and LLC both stood zero and two.

None of those equal records described equal conditions.

Edrin found Oren in the vestibule.

“I want to add another column.”

“No.”

“You don’t know which.”

“I know you.”

“Resources.”

“No.”

“Then consequences.”

“No.”

“Attention?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because the public standings are good at one thing.”

“They are bad at everything else.”

“Then do not destroy the thing they are good at by making them pretend to be everything.”

Edrin stared at the board.

“What do I do with the rest?”

Oren pointed toward the ledger under his arm.

“Keep it.”

“For what?”

“The next change.”

Outside, dawn entered the city.

A messenger from Wilson’s yard crossed the square.

Another from the southern road arrived behind him.

Two undefeated powers.

Already moving toward one another.

Behind them came a TDS clerk carrying market forms.

A Chili courier with a package receipt.

An LLC representative asking for updated terms.

The next week had not begun.

Its causes had.
