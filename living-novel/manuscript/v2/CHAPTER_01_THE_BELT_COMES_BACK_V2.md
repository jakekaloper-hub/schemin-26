# CHAPTER 1 — THE BELT COMES BACK
## Manuscript V2 — Continuous Story Rewrite

The first result entered the Record House folded twice.

Edrin had expected something ceremonial.

After weeks of arrivals, inspections, arguments, wagers and public predictions, he had imagined the first certified return would arrive beneath a seal large enough to require two hands.

Instead, a clerk named Toman put six envelopes on his desk and said, “They’re making noise downstairs.”

“Who?”

“Everyone.”

Edrin looked at the envelopes.

“Already?”

“They were making noise before the numbers arrived. Now they have numbers.”

That, Edrin would learn, was the dangerous stage.

He reached for the first envelope.

Oren stopped his hand.

“Which one do you want?”

Edrin looked at him.

“That is not how records work.”

“It is how people work.”

Edrin knew which one he wanted.

The whole city did.

For two seasons the Belt had traveled a short and violent historical distance. ObiWan Jacoby had taken the championship in 2024. The power now called His Majesty’s Blood had taken it back in 2025. Austin had entered the new campaign carrying the Belt. Jake had entered carrying the most recent proof that the Belt could be taken from him.

Their opening encounter had been discussed so aggressively that several taverns had begun charging for arguments.

Edrin chose a different envelope.

Oren nodded.

“Better.”

Mud Dogs 125.36.

Red Leopards 120.86.

Edrin copied the result.

Five minutes later, nothing in the Record House had changed.

Beyond the eastern wall, something had.

The Red quarter had entered the year beneath banners large enough to be seen from the river. Its weapons were obvious. Its expectations had been public. By afternoon, the same banners still moved above the grounds, but the crowd beneath them had changed tone.

A narrow loss did not destroy a strong campaign.

It did, however, deny strength the easiest form of proof.

Edrin wrote the result and stopped.

He wanted to add more.

He did not yet know what.

The second envelope gave Dr. Duckhook 147.32 and Seven Deadly Chins 145.82.

One and a half points.

Edrin read it twice.

“Closer than the first.”

“That is arithmetic,” Oren said.

The underlying allocation sheets arrived later.

That was where the result became useful.

Seven Deadly Chins had possessed another answer. Not a hypothetical player conjured after defeat, but a real command decision visible in the record. A different allocation would have changed the field.

Edrin felt the familiar temptation.

He could write the lesson immediately.

Bad decision.

Oren saw his face.

“No.”

“I haven’t said anything.”

“You have a very legible forehead.”

“The choice mattered.”

“Yes.”

“So it was wrong.”

“No.”

Edrin put down his pen.

“How can it matter and not be wrong?”

“Because consequence and stupidity are different findings.”

The sentence irritated him enough to remain memorable.

He entered the allocation in the continuity ledger.

Not as guilt.

As state.

A decision had produced a cost. The next time a similar choice appeared, the Archive would know what it was changing from.

The third envelope made the lower floor erupt.

Chili Cheesers 198.00.

The number traveled through the building faster than the clerk carrying it.

By the time Edrin reached the public board, somebody had already written **NEW STANDARD** on a scrap of paper and wedged it beneath Chili’s name.

Pell tore it down.

“Standings only.”

A bookmaker standing three people back said, “Coward.”

Pell removed him too.

Edrin stared at the total.

Before the opening, Chili had possessed premium pieces, concentrated strength and a reputation mostly built from expectation.

Now the expectation had evidence.

That was different.

Across the city, people began treating Brandon Pryor’s household differently before Edrin had finished copying the number. Messengers appeared at the western stable. Vendors moved Chili merchandise to the front of tables. Someone at Pittsy’s Book adjusted a future line.

The result had not merely described the week.

It had changed the next morning.

Edrin returned to the ledger.

PUBLIC ATTENTION: INCREASED.

He stopped.

“Can I write that?”

“Did it?”

“Yes.”

“Then write the evidence.”

So he did.

Not **Chili is feared**.

Not **the league has a new king**.

Merchants repositioned goods.

Book prices moved.

Visitors increased.

Observable things.

The fourth envelope belonged to Three Dreaded Snake.

175.56.

Another enormous opening.

Edrin had seen the preseason scarcity sheet. TDS had entered without the premium live capital other houses possessed. The clean board had hidden that. Now the first result hid it in the opposite direction.

Scarcity had not prevented force.

Force had not erased scarcity.

Edrin found himself enjoying the contradiction.

Oren noticed.

“Careful.”

“What?”

“You like it when reality becomes complicated enough to require you.”

“That is not true.”

“It is an occupational disease.”

The fifth result gave D0nkey K0ng 142.70 over Slob on my Dobb 134.96.

Again the public board made the information simple.

One and zero.

Zero and one.

Edrin’s other ledger made it less so.

Slob’s roster had entered with abundance. One defeat did not liquidate it.

Wilson’s construction had entered with promise. One victory did not crown it.

The difference was that one now possessed evidence the other did not.

The sixth envelope remained.

Edrin knew the numbers before he opened it.

The shouting downstairs had changed.

Not louder.

Sharper.

He broke the seal.

ObiWan Jacoby 141.70.

His Majesty’s Blood 131.26.

For a moment he did nothing.

Oren waited.

“The Belt stays,” Edrin said.

“Yes.”

“Even though Austin lost.”

“Yes.”

“The reigning champion loses to the man who held it before him, and nothing transfers.”

“Something transfers.”

Edrin looked up.

“What?”

“Proof.”

The word was smaller than victory and more dangerous.

They went downstairs.

The brass board had already been updated.

ObiWan Jacoby — 1–0.

His Majesty’s Blood — 0–1.

The Belt did not appear anywhere on it.

That absence had become the center of the room.

A man near the doors was saying the result proved Jake had been the better champion all along.

Another said it proved nothing except one week.

A third said Austin’s title had always been fortunate.

A fourth demanded to know whether winning the opening encounter entitled Jake to a ceremonial challenge.

Pell threatened to clear the vestibule.

Nobody listened.

Edrin watched the argument move around the correct numbers.

For the first time, he saw the difference between a result and what people tried to purchase with it.

Jake’s victory could prove he had beaten Austin now.

It could not retroactively award him 2025.

Austin’s Belt could prove he had won the prior championship.

It could not make him 1–0 now.

Both facts survived contact.

Neither cancelled the other.

That evening Edrin was sent to the registration court with a certified copy.

He found Austin’s delegation leaving.

The Belt Keeper walked among them with the same dark weight at his waist.

Nobody had taken it.

Nobody had the right to.

Yet the crowd’s posture toward him had changed.

That was the first thing Edrin noticed.

The second was the absence of collapse.

Austin had lost an encounter, not his history.

Across the square, Jake’s people were being congratulated as though they had recovered something larger than a weekly result.

Edrin understood why.

He also understood why the Archive could not agree.

Jake stood near the steps with the golden retriever beside him. The green blade remained secured. He looked toward Austin once.

Austin looked back.

Nothing theatrical happened.

No challenge.

No speech.

No magical recognition between Belt and former holder.

Two men occupied the same square carrying opposite pieces of recent history.

Edrin had the absurd impulse to ask each what the result meant.

Oren appeared beside him.

“Don’t.”

“I wasn’t going to.”

“You were leaning.”

“People lean.”

“Archivists lean toward trouble.”

They watched Austin leave.

“What do I put in the continuity ledger?” Edrin asked.

“What changed?”

“Jake is one and zero.”

“Standings ledger.”

“Austin is zero and one.”

“Same.”

“Jake has current proof against the reigning champion.”

Oren nodded.

“Austin still holds the Belt.”

“Yes.”

“Their recent championship history now has another result attached to it.”

“Yes.”

Edrin waited.

Oren waited longer.

“That’s it?”

“For tonight.”

It felt insufficient.

That became important.

The next morning, the city had reorganized itself around six results.

Chili’s number appeared in windows.

TDS’s total acquired admirers who had not mentioned TDS a week earlier.

Duckhook’s narrow victory became evidence of precision to people who had never seen the underlying allocation.

Chins’ narrow defeat became an argument about command.

Mud’s win changed how travelers described the southern road.

Wilson’s opening success attracted the first additional scouts.

Red and Slob woke beneath records that made their pre-campaign strength easier to ignore.

None of this appeared on the brass board.

Edrin began carrying the continuity ledger downstairs.

At noon, a boy approached him beneath the board.

“Which one matters?”

Edrin looked at the twelve lines.

“What?”

“The score or the record.”

“They both matter.”

“Which tells you who’s good?”

Edrin almost answered.

Instead he remembered Oren’s rule.

“What do you mean by good?”

The boy rolled his eyes and left.

Oren, standing several yards away, looked unbearably pleased.

“Don’t.”

“I said nothing.”

“You have a very legible forehead.”

By the second day, the first decisions caused by the first results had begun arriving.

Seven Deadly Chins changed its next command allocation.

Not because the Archive declared the previous choice foolish.

Because the previous choice had produced a cost visible enough to enter the next decision.

That distinction mattered to Edrin.

So did the first signs of attention around Chili.

So did the fact that TDS’s opening force had not returned any of the premium capital absent from its preseason sheet.

So did the fact that Austin’s loss had not loosened the Belt by a single buckle.

The first week had not answered the questions people had carried into it.

It had changed them.

That night Edrin reopened the 2026 ledger.

He turned to the page he had written before the first encounter.

**RECENT CHAMPIONSHIP HISTORY REMAINS ACTIVE.**

Below it he added:

**W1: OBIWAN DEFEATS REIGNING HOLDER. BELT UNCHANGED. CURRENT PROOF CHANGES PUBLIC ARGUMENT; DOES NOT REWRITE PRIOR TITLE.**

He read the sentence.

It was ugly.

Oren would hate it.

Edrin kept it.

Then he turned to Chili.

Then Chins.

Then TDS.

Then the others.

For the first time, he did not organize the notes by winner and loser.

He organized them by what would still be true when the next result arrived.

Outside, the city continued celebrating six answers.

Inside the Record House, Edrin began keeping the questions those answers had created.
