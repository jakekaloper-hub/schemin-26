# CHAPTER 3 — THE PRICE OF ATTENTION
## Manuscript V2 — Continuous Story Rewrite

Success acquired visitors.

Wilson Look learned this before the third encounter.

A supplier arrived first.

Then a scout.

Then a bookmaker.

Then two representatives from a patron house Edrin had last seen waiting outside His Majesty’s Blood.

The undefeated mark above Wilson’s name had changed none of the roads leading to his yard.

It had changed who traveled them.

Edrin stood near the gate with the continuity ledger tucked under one arm.

The supplier placed a proposal on a table.

Wilson read it.

“Higher.”

The man cleared his throat. “Demand.”

“Whose?”

“Yours.”

“Wasn’t yesterday.”

“Conditions changed.”

Wilson looked toward the road.

More riders were coming.

That was what two and zero purchased first.

Not a crown.

Attention.

Mud Dogs had purchased the same thing by a different road.

The swamp received fewer merchants and more surveyors. A bridge contractor sent estimates. Scouts who had dismissed the southern ground as ugly now asked for dry observation platforms.

The swamp did not become less hostile because outsiders had finally decided it mattered.

Two undefeated campaigns had altered third-party behavior.

Now they would meet.

The city tried to turn the encounter into a coronation.

Edrin refused the word.

There was no crown.

There was no title transfer.

There was only a category that could belong to one of them after the result.

When the return arrived, the number was decisive.

D0nkey K0ng 172.48.

Mud Dogs 126.86.

Wilson became the only three-and-zero campaign.

The visitors multiplied.

By afternoon, people who had never traveled the Arsenal road spoke as though they had always understood what Wilson was building.

That irritated Edrin more than it should have.

He opened the continuity ledger.

**SOLE UNDEFEATED.**

Then:

**ATTENTION INCREASES.**

He crossed it out.

Too vague.

He listed what he could see.

Additional scouts.

Supplier proposals.

Patron inquiries.

Public lines moving.

Opponents requesting reports.

Success had not made Wilson safer.

It had made ignorance about him more expensive.

Edrin found him later near the training yard.

“Comment?”

“No.”

“On being the last undefeated.”

“No.”

“On Mud Dogs.”

Wilson stopped.

“Still good.”

“They lost by forty-five point six two.”

“And?”

Edrin looked at him.

“And nothing.”

Wilson turned away.

That answer survived the walk back to the Record House.

Mud Dogs had lost an undefeated category.

The swamp remained.

Two prior victories remained.

The bridge proposal remained.

A first defeat did not travel backward through time destroying evidence of competence.

Edrin entered that too.

Not because Mud needed protection.

Because the record did.

At midnight, Three Dreaded Snake spent seventy-six.

The recruitment hall noticed.

Tyler Shough.

Seventy-six.

Won.

Then Emanuel Wilson.

Forty-two.

Won.

Then Bengals defense.

Thirty-five.

Won.

Edrin added the principal claims.

One hundred fifty-three.

The number became loud before anyone said it.

TDS had entered the season with scarcity visible beneath the draft sheet. The first week had made the scarcity easy to ignore. The second had made it impossible to ignore. Now Phillip’s campaign had converted pressure into expenditure.

Edrin wanted a word for it.

Aggressive.

Desperate.

Necessary.

Oren rejected all three.

“Which can you prove?”

“None.”

“Then you have numbers.”

“Numbers without interpretation are useless.”

“Interpretation without restraint is fiction wearing a uniform.”

Edrin glared at him.

“Sometimes you are unbearable.”

“Documented.”

The acquired resources remained after the market closed.

So did the cost.

Then the encounter arrived.

ObiWan Jacoby 136.73.

Three Dreaded Snake 114.50.

For several minutes Edrin had the stupid thought that the spending had failed.

Oren corrected him without looking up.

“Do not make the result judge every decision that preceded it.”

“They spent one hundred fifty-three.”

“Yes.”

“They lost.”

“Yes.”

“So what did the spending accomplish?”

“We do not know yet.”

“Nothing this week.”

“You do not know that either.”

Oren pointed to the transaction ledger.

“Did they acquire the resources?”

“Yes.”

“Did the cost return after the loss?”

“No.”

“Then two things survived.”

The answer.

And its price.

Edrin wrote:

**EXPENDITURE IS CONSEQUENCE, NOT VERDICT.**

Oren read it.

“Terrible sentence.”

“Accurate.”

“Different categories.”

Edrin kept it.

The city attached an older memory to Jake’s victory.

A bridge.

A lake.

A difficult return.

The story belonged to the history between ObiWan and TDS, and after the new result people told it again.

Edrin refused to place the bridge in the Week Three event register.

Nobody had jumped during the encounter.

The old story had not caused the score.

Memory had simply found a rhyme.

He placed it in the relationship file.

Oren approved.

This pleased Edrin more than it should have.

The Chili dog was harder to file.

It had been sent before the encounter.

That distinction mattered.

A conspicuous meal delivered from Brandon Pryor’s people to Dr. Duckhook.

A joke.

A provocation.

A warning, depending on who was telling it.

Then the result:

Chili Cheesers 132.19.

Dr. Duckhook 126.16.

Close enough for the object to survive.

Had Chili lost, the meal would have returned against him, just as the earlier provocation had returned through Red.

Instead the city began calling the result a chiliing.

Registrar Pell threatened disciplinary action against anyone who used the word on an official form.

Three people immediately did.

At the Country Club, chili briefly disappeared from a menu.

Then returned.

The food itself never entered the Archive.

The cleaned wrapper did.

Edrin labeled it:

**PRE-ENCOUNTER PROVOCATION — CULTURAL OBJECT.**

Oren looked at the card.

“Wrapper.”

“That is not enough.”

“It is if people remember.”

“That is exactly the problem.”

The hundred-dollar wager arrived on paper.

Kevin Zeek had backed himself.

Jordan Hollingshead had accepted.

Red Leopards 158.44.

Slob on my Dobb 141.86.

The money changed hands.

The receipt reached the Archive.

Red had now won twice after the narrow opening defeat.

Slob had fallen to one and two.

Yet Slob’s cumulative production stood at 471.74.

Edrin checked the total twice.

“How?”

Oren looked over.

“By scoring many points and losing two encounters.”

“You know what I mean.”

“Yes. You want the standings to answer a question they were not designed to answer.”

The receipt did not prove Red was stronger than Slob.

The cumulative total did not prove Slob deserved a different record.

Both were evidence.

The difficulty was keeping them together without forcing one to erase the other.

Then the HMB report arrived.

Austin had won.

His Majesty’s Blood 136.00.

Seven Deadly Chins 125.54.

The first victory of the campaign.

The Belt Keeper was one and two.

People in the north square cheered as though a pressure valve had opened.

Before the celebration settled, the injury report reached the Record House.

De’Von Achane.

Retained fifteenth-round resource.

Season ending.

Edrin read the diagnosis.

Then the score.

Then the diagnosis again.

The same campaign had improved and deteriorated on the same page.

“Which goes first?” he asked.

Oren understood the question.

“Neither.”

“The result was certified first.”

“Chronology is not importance.”

“The injury happened during the encounter.”

“Then record the time.”

Edrin looked at the standings.

One and two.

Better than zero and three.

The campaign ledger beside it showed a permanent absence that had not existed when Austin was zero and two.

The weekly position had improved.

The season position had worsened.

Both were true.

The brass board could hold one.

That was the moment Edrin stopped trying to fix the board.

He asked Oren for another book.

“No.”

“You have not heard the proposal.”

“I know you.”

“The standings stay.”

Oren looked up.

Edrin continued.

“No extra public columns. No opinions disguised as ranking. A second internal register.”

“For what?”

“State.”

“That is not a category.”

“It is now.”

Edrin put the HMB papers on the table.

“Record. Resources. availability. material expenditures. unresolved decisions. relationship changes. things people keep using after the event that created them.”

“Impossible to maintain.”

“We maintain the Archive.”

“That remains a poor defense.”

Edrin placed Wilson’s file beside HMB.

Three and zero.

No Belt.

More attention than before.

Then TDS.

One and two.

Acquisitions gained.

Flexibility spent.

Then Slob.

One and two.

Production that made the record look deceptively ordinary.

Then Red.

Two and one.

A wager receipt and two consecutive wins after opening defeat.

Then Chili.

Two and one.

198.00, 100.90, 132.19.

Force, humiliation, recovery.

Same campaign.

Then Jake.

Two and one.

Champion-beating proof, swamp reversal, recovery over TDS.

Then Mud.

Two and one.

First loss without erased competence.

Then LLC, finally one and two after defeating El Niño.

Relief.

Not vindication.

Then Duckhook.

Then Chins.

Then El Niño.

Twelve states.

Not twelve stories.

Oren looked at the pile.

“What would you call it?”

“Campaign State.”

“Bad.”

“Continuity Ledger.”

“Worse.”

“Then you name it.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Your bad idea.”

Oren pulled a blank volume from beneath the desk.

He placed it between them.

“Internal.”

“Yes.”

“Evidence first.”

“Yes.”

“Interpretation labeled.”

“Yes.”

“Unknown remains unknown.”

“Yes.”

“Nothing becomes true because you wrote it here.”

Edrin paused.

That was the actual rule.

“Agreed.”

Oren pushed the volume across the table.

“Begin.”

The first entry took forty minutes.

HIS MAJESTY’S BLOOD.

CURRENT RECORD: 1–2.

INHERITED STATUS: REIGNING BELT KEEPER.

MATERIAL CHANGE: FIRST WEEKLY VICTORY.

RESOURCE CHANGE: DE’VON ACHANE — RETAINED RESOURCE — UNAVAILABLE REMAINDER OF CAMPAIGN.

CAUSATION: INJURY REMOVES FUTURE AVAILABILITY. NO EVIDENCE IT CAUSED WEEK THREE RESULT.

UNRESOLVED: REPLACEMENT RESPONSE.

Oren read it.

“Acceptable.”

Edrin tried not to look pleased.

Second:

D0NKEY K0NG.

CURRENT RECORD: 3–0.

MATERIAL CHANGE: SOLE UNDEFEATED CAMPAIGN.

OBSERVED SOCIAL CONSEQUENCE: INCREASED SCOUT, MERCHANT, PATRON AND PUBLIC ATTENTION.

UNRESOLVED: EFFECT OF TARGET STATUS.

Third:

THREE DREADED SNAKE.

CURRENT RECORD: 1–2.

RESOURCE CHANGE: VERIFIED RECRUITMENT EXPENDITURE.

UNRESOLVED: FUTURE VALUE OF ACQUISITIONS; REDUCED FLEXIBILITY.

No panic.

No desperation.

Fourth.

Fifth.

Twelve.

By evening the ledger contained enough information to become dangerous.

Edrin closed it.

“Now what?”

Oren stood at the western window.

“Now you resist using all of it.”

Edrin stared.

“Then why keep it?”

“So that when something changes, we know what it changed from.”

Outside, the city had begun lighting the roads for the next cycle.

Edrin carried the new ledger upstairs.

At the Hall of Keeping, the Farrell Trophy remained where it always remained.

The Belt cradle remained empty because a living champion still carried the Belt.

Nothing in the room had changed.

Everything outside it had.

Wilson’s three-and-zero mark drew scouts toward one road.

Jake’s two-and-one campaign was already preparing to travel that direction.

Mud returned to the swamp without surrendering the competence that had made the road matter.

TDS closed accounts with less flexibility and more acquired help.

Chili’s wrapper escaped into public language.

Red’s wager receipt appeared behind a tavern bar without Kevin placing it there.

At HMB, the Belt reflected torchlight above a campaign that had finally won and permanently lost something in the same week.

The world remembered differently from the Archive.

The Archive tried to preserve what happened.

The world preserved what it could use.

Edrin opened the new ledger one last time.

Twelve names.

Twelve current states.

Some improved.

Some worsened.

Most had done both depending on where one looked.

Tomorrow the brass board would remain downstairs.

Correct.

Simple.

Useful.

He no longer resented it for being incomplete.

That was not its failure.

The failure would have been asking it to carry everything.

Edrin closed the second record.

The next road bent toward the only undefeated power.

He did not imagine the result.

That belonged to evidence not yet created.

For the first time since Oren had given him the 2026 file, Edrin understood that keeping a living history did not mean knowing what the story was.

It meant knowing exactly what the story was allowed to change.
