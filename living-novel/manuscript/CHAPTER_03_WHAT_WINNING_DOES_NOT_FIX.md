# CHAPTER III — WHAT WINNING DOES NOT FIX

## I. The Board Is True

The board was correct.

Edrin checked it four times.

This did not improve it.

The western vestibule of the Hall of Measures had become crowded before sunrise. Clerks stood on ladders. Brass nameplates lay across a felt-covered table. A mason with a level argued with a registrar about whether the third column leaned left.

"It does," the mason said.

"It is a wall."

"Walls lean."

"This one doesn't."

The mason placed the level against the stone.

The bubble moved.

Registrar Pell stared at it.

"Nobody touches the wall."

The mason looked pleased.

Edrin was less interested in the wall than the names.

He had arranged them according to the certified Week Three results.

At the top:

D0NKEY K0NG — 3–0.

Then four names at two victories and one defeat.

RED LEOPARDS.

CHILI CHEESERS.

OBIWAN JACOBY.

MUD DOGS.

Below them, seven names at one and two.

SLOB ON MY DOBB.

SEVEN DEADLY CHINS.

DR. DUCKHOOK.

THE LLC.

EL NIÑO.

THREE DREADED SNAKE.

HIS MAJESTY'S BLOOD.

No zeroes remained in the victory column.

Only one remained in the defeat column.

The board was simple.

The week had not been.

Edrin moved His Majesty's Blood one channel higher, stared at it, then moved it back.

Oren entered carrying coffee.

"Why are you rearranging certified standings?"

"I'm not."

"You are holding the champion in your hand."

Edrin looked down.

Austin's brass plate rested across his palm.

"I was checking something."

"Did his record change while you were checking?"

"No."

"Then put him back."

Edrin did.

One and two.

He stepped away.

Something remained wrong.

Oren handed him a cup.

"Say it."

"Slob is one and two."

"Yes."

"He has four hundred seventy-one point seven four through three weeks."

"Also yes."

"His Majesty's Blood is one and two."

"Remarkable. You can read two lines."

"Austin just won."

"Correct."

"But he lost—"

Edrin stopped.

The word felt imprecise.

Not the Encounter.

Something else.

Oren waited.

"Achane," Edrin said.

"Yes."

"The retained fifteenth."

"Yes."

"Unavailable for the rest of the campaign."

"That is the certified medical notice."

Edrin pointed at the board.

"Where does that go?"

"It doesn't."

"Why?"

"Because this is a standings board."

Edrin looked again at Austin's one and two.

Then at Slob's one and two.

Then at the LLC's one and two.

Then at Snake's.

Same record.

Different damage.

Different resources.

Different roads.

At the top, Wilson Look's plate occupied the only undefeated channel.

Three and zero.

That, at least, seemed clean.

Edrin looked at Oren.

Oren drank his coffee.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You looked at me."

"I am allowed."

"You're waiting for me to say three and zero doesn't mean what it looks like."

"Does it?"

"It means three victories and no defeats."

"That's all?"

"On this board."

Edrin hated the answer.

The bells began.

Six strokes.

A pause.

Six more.

Results.

People entered the vestibule almost immediately.

They looked up.

They found the undefeated name first.

Then their own.

Then the people they disliked.

A man in a Mud Dogs coat stared at the board for a long time.

A Red supporter counted the two-and-one names and smiled when he reached his.

Two young men argued over whether cumulative scoring should move Slob upward.

A woman in black stood beneath His Majesty's Blood and touched two fingers to the Belt emblem sewn at her collar.

Nobody saw what Edrin saw.

Or perhaps everyone saw a different version.

The board was not lying.

That was the problem.

It could tell the truth and still leave out almost everything that mattered.

---

By midmorning the city had given D0nkey K0ng three crowns.

The Hall confiscated two.

The third was made of bread and therefore fell outside Registrar Pell's jurisdiction.

Wilson wore it for six minutes.

Then he ate it.

Edrin learned this from a courier.

He did not write it down.

Not everything required preservation.

Outside the Hall, red standards had appeared on balconies that had not displayed them the week before. A bookmaker repainted a board. Two armor merchants moved Arsenal-style pieces to the front of their windows. A tavern changed its noon special to "Three and Oh" and charged three coins more for it.

Temporary things were producing durable behavior again.

Edrin knew this pattern now.

What he did not know was whether the behavior belonged beside the standings.

Oren found him staring through the vestibule doors.

"You want another board."

"I want a better one."

"No."

"Why?"

"Because people will mistake complexity for accuracy."

"Isn't complexity more accurate?"

"Sometimes."

"And?"

"Sometimes it is just more ways to be wrong."

Edrin looked back at the twelve brass names.

"What do we do, then?"

Oren set down his cup.

"We keep more than one record."

## II. The Empty Stall

His Majesty's Blood celebrated quietly.

That was not unusual.

The champion's compound had never needed noise to establish that it existed.

Black stone.

Narrow windows.

Runes cut into lintels.

A long hall where banners from prior campaigns hung high enough that visitors had to tilt their heads to read them.

The Belt had its own case when Austin was not wearing it.

Today he wore it.

One and two had not changed that.

Edrin arrived with a certification packet and a campaign inventory clerk named Sera who disliked stairs.

The compound had many.

"Champions," she muttered after the fourth flight.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You said champions."

"I said stairs."

"You did not."

"Archive it incorrectly."

They reached the upper stores.

Austin was already there.

No armor.

Dark shirt.

Long black hair tied back.

The Belt at his waist.

Without the sword and black plate, he looked less like a monument and more like the person required to maintain one.

On the central table lay the Week Three certification.

HIS MAJESTY'S BLOOD — 136.00.

SEVEN DEADLY CHINS — 125.54.

Edrin had seen the number before.

The room made it feel different.

"First one," he said.

Austin looked at him.

"First what?"

"Win."

"I know what you meant."

Sera opened the inventory ledger.

"We need to reconcile active campaign resources."

Austin's face changed slightly.

Not grief.

Not anger.

Edrin refused to name it.

The Archive had no instrument for that.

Sera walked the rows.

Weapons.

Field kits.

Medical stores.

Contracts.

Retainer markers.

Each retained campaign asset had a brass tag fixed to its assigned place.

Smith-Njigba.

Second.

Achane.

Fifteenth.

The second-round station remained active.

The fifteenth did not.

Its equipment had been removed from deployment inventory after the medical notice.

The brass tag remained.

That was worse.

An empty place with a name still attached to it.

Edrin had seen the same name before the season began, when draft ledgers made unequal value visible before anyone had won anything. A retained fifteenth that carried far more practical force than its acquisition cost suggested.

Now the cost remained historical.

The force did not.

Sera placed a black line through the availability column.

"Remainder of campaign," she said.

Austin nodded.

"Confirmed."

"Yes."

"Appeal?"

"No."

She initialed the entry.

Edrin looked at the Week Three certification on the table.

Then the empty station.

Win.

Loss.

Both words seemed inadequate.

Austin noticed.

"You're doing the face."

"What face?"

"The Archive face."

"Apparently I have several."

"This is the one where the evidence has offended you."

Edrin almost smiled.

"Your record improved."

Austin leaned against the table.

"That tends to happen when you win."

"Your campaign position didn't."

Austin looked toward the empty stall.

"Depends which position."

"Exactly."

Sera closed the inventory ledger.

"The standings do not track retained-resource availability."

"I know," Edrin said.

"The medical register does."

"I know."

"The transaction ledger will track replacement activity."

"I know."

She looked at him.

"Then what is the problem?"

Edrin pointed at the three documents.

"They're all in different rooms."

Austin laughed once.

Not happily.

But genuinely.

"That's your crisis?"

"It's an archival crisis."

"Sounds terrifying."

"The city is going to say you recovered."

Austin's eyes moved to him.

Edrin regretted the sentence.

Not because it was false.

Because it was not evidence.

Austin looked again at the certification.

"Let them."

"Is that—"

"No."

Edrin stopped writing.

Austin nodded at the notebook.

"You don't get that one."

"Fair."

Sera gathered her papers.

At the door, Edrin looked back.

The Belt remained.

The win remained.

The empty stall remained.

None canceled another.

That was the thing he had been trying to put on the board.

He still did not know how.

---

Seven Deadly Chins had left less behind.

Not nothing.

Never nothing.

Ben Whipple's Week Two adaptation remained part of the record. The corrected command decision had been real. So had the victory that followed it.

Week Three did not revoke either.

It added a defeat.

125.54.

One and two.

The raccoon stole the corner of the certification while Ben was signing it.

Registrar Pell recovered most of it.

The missing corner contained no material data.

This was fortunate.

Edrin filed the document under MINOR PHYSICAL DAMAGE / NO EVIDENTIARY LOSS.

Oren later crossed out MINOR.

"Why?"

"Raccoon."

Edrin accepted the correction.

## III. The Last Unbroken Mark

Wilson Look did not receive a crown.

He received visitors.

That was more useful.

By noon the Arsenal Centaur's outer yard held three merchant delegations, two bookmakers, a weapons factor, four scouts from rival houses and a young man attempting to sell commemorative horseshoes.

Wilson bought none.

He stood at the center of the yard in red armor while workers raised a new standard above the gate.

Not a championship banner.

No Belt.

No crown.

Just the house mark, higher than it had been the week before.

Three victories.

No defeats.

Across the city, people had begun using a phrase Edrin disliked.

Last unbeaten.

It sounded final.

Nothing about Week Three was final.

The result against Mud Dogs had been decisive enough that the phrase spread anyway.

172.48 to 126.86.

The two undefeated lines from Week Two had met.

Only one remained.

Edrin arrived while a supplier was explaining priority delivery.

"We can move your order forward."

"Why?"

The man blinked.

"Because—"

Wilson waited.

"Demand."

"Whose?"

"Yours."

"Wasn't yesterday."

The supplier smiled weakly.

"Conditions changed."

Wilson looked toward Edrin.

"Archive."

"Don't involve me."

"I wasn't."

"You looked at me."

"I am allowed."

Edrin hated when people used Oren's arguments.

Wilson signed nothing.

The supplier left the proposal.

A scout replaced him.

Then a bookmaker.

Then a delegation from a minor patron house that had spent Week One trying to secure an invitation from Austin.

Edrin recognized two of them.

They recognized him recognizing them.

Nobody enjoyed it.

By afternoon the yard had become a map of attention.

That was what three and zero purchased first.

Not victory.

Visibility.

Every opponent now had a reason to study the Centaur.

Every merchant had a reason to be seen offering him terms.

Every ambitious house had a reason to imagine the value of being the first to break the line.

Wilson finally escaped to the training yard.

Edrin followed.

"Do you ever leave?"

"Frequently."

"Try it now."

"I need a comment."

"No."

"About being undefeated."

"No."

"About Mud Dogs."

Wilson stopped.

The axe rested across one shoulder.

"Still good."

Edrin waited.

"That's it?"

"You wanted a comment."

"They lost by forty-five point six two."

Wilson's expression hardened.

"And?"

Edrin looked toward the yard.

"And nothing."

"Good."

The Centaur turned away.

That, Edrin thought, was more interesting than triumph.

Mud Dogs had not disappeared because its zero did.

The swamp remained.

The host remained.

Two prior victories remained.

A first defeat was not retroactive failure.

Edrin wrote that after Wilson could no longer see him.

---

The southern wetlands received Bobby Mitchell without ceremony.

No merchants met him.

No children offered bread crowns.

The swamp did not care about standings.

The great Mud Dog entered the black water until only its shoulders and head remained visible.

Workers repaired a raised path damaged during the previous week's traffic surge.

The bridge contractor's proposal still hung inside the field office.

Two and one.

The proposal had not vanished.

Neither had the reason someone had sent it.

A defeat could remove a category without removing a campaign.

Edrin did not travel south that day.

The report reached him wet.

He dried it beside a lamp.

Some traditions survived too.

## IV. The Price of an Answer

At midnight, Three Dreaded Snake spent seventy-six.

The recruitment hall noticed.

By the time the seal for Tyler Shough was opened, half the room had stopped pretending not to watch.

Edrin sat behind the Transaction Desk with a fresh ledger.

He had written the week number at the top.

Three.

Below it, he had left more space than usual.

This proved wise.

"Seventy-six," the clerk announced.

A murmur crossed the hall.

Phillip Pitts stood beneath the north lamps.

The repaired tendrils from the previous week were bound close, shorter in places, unmistakably his. No restored silhouette. No attempt to pretend the damage had not happened.

Phillip's scaled face remained still beneath the north lamps. His luminous eyes watched the clerk, then the other bidders, then returned to the seal.

The seal was stamped.

WON.

Before the hall settled, another TDS claim arrived.

Emanuel Wilson.

Forty-two.

WON.

Then Bengals defensive unit.

Thirty-five.

WON.

Edrin looked at the accumulating entries.

"That's one hundred fifty-three."

Oren did not look up.

"You can add."

"In one cycle."

"Still addition."

"Is that a lot?"

"Compared with what?"

Edrin knew better.

He opened the resource ledger.

The numbers mattered only in relation to what remained, what had been needed, what other houses had bid and what future opportunities had now been surrendered.

Markets were not moral systems.

Spending did not prove panic.

Saving did not prove wisdom.

But expenditure changed the next decision.

That was enough.

Phillip approached the desk.

Edrin slid the stamped acquisitions across.

"Busy night."

"Needed bodies."

Phillip looked at him.

Edrin wrote nothing.

That was direct speech, but not necessarily historical explanation.

"People are going to call it aggressive."

"People call weather aggressive."

"El Niño probably likes that."

The edge of Phillip's mouth almost became a smile.

Phillip gathered the papers.

"What did ObiWan spend?"

"Private until the cycle closes."

"You know."

"Yes."

"So?"

"No."

The central head leaned closer.

"You're getting better."

"At what?"

"Being annoying."

Phillip left.

The cycle closed hours later.

The hall emptied.

TDS had acquired answers.

The weekly result did not reward them with a victory.

ObiWan Jacoby 136.73.

Three Dreaded Snake 114.50.

Edrin looked at the acquisitions again.

For several minutes he had the stupid thought that the spending had therefore failed.

Oren corrected him without being asked.

"Do not do that."

"Do what?"

"Make the result judge every decision that preceded it."

Edrin leaned back.

"They spent one hundred fifty-three across three claims."

"Correct."

"They lost."

"Correct."

"So what did the spending accomplish?"

"We do not know yet."

"Nothing this week."

"You don't know that either."

Edrin frowned.

Oren pointed at the ledger.

"Did they fill needs?"

"Yes."

"Did those resources cease to exist because the weekly total was lower?"

"No."

"Did the cost return to them?"

"No."

There it was.

The cost survived the result.

So did the acquisitions.

A response could be real without being sufficient.

A decision could alter the campaign before anyone knew whether it had improved it.

Edrin wrote a note in the margin:

**EXPENDITURE IS CONSEQUENCE, NOT VERDICT.**

Oren read it.

"Awful sentence."

"Accurate."

"Those are different categories."

Edrin left it anyway.

## V. Things People Keep

The chili dog arrived at the Archive wrapped in three layers of paper and one layer of argument.

Edrin refused it.

The courier insisted.

"It's for the collection."

"No."

"Commissioner's office said—"

"No food."

"You kept the bag last week."

"The bag was evidence."

"This is evidence."

"This is perishable."

The courier considered.

"So was the bag."

Edrin hated him immediately.

Oren appeared.

"What is it?"

"A mistake."

The courier lifted the package.

"Chili dog."

Oren looked at Edrin.

Edrin looked at the ceiling.

The story had begun before the Encounter.

Brandon Pryor's people had sent Dr. Duckhook a conspicuous meal.

Not after.

Before.

That distinction mattered.

A provocation.

A joke.

A warning if one was generous.

An invitation to humiliation if one was not.

Then the result arrived.

CHILI CHEESERS — 132.19.

DR. DUCKHOOK — 126.16.

Six point zero three.

Close enough that the object survived.

Had Chili lost, the meal would have returned as evidence against him.

He had learned that lesson from Red one week earlier.

Instead the parcel changed direction.

People began calling the result a chiliing.

Edrin declined to capitalize it.

Registrar Pell threatened disciplinary action against anyone who submitted the word on an official form.

Someone submitted three.

At the Country Club of Jackson, Dr. Duckhook ordered the kitchen to remove chili from the weekly menu.

This lasted four hours.

A member complained.

Duckhook restored it.

That part Edrin wrote down.

Not because the menu mattered.

Because refusing the joke and then reversing the refusal told him something about how humiliation became culture.

The original food did not enter the Archive.

The wrapper did.

Cleaned.

Dried.

Labeled:

PRE-ENCOUNTER PROVOCATION — CULTURAL OBJECT.

Edrin showed Oren.

"Too much?"

"Yes."

"What would you write?"

"Wrapper."

"That's useless."

"Only if nobody remembers."

Edrin kept his label.

---

The hundred-dollar wager required less refrigeration.

Kevin Zeek had placed the money on himself.

Jordan Hollingshead had accepted.

The result followed.

RED LEOPARDS — 158.44.

SLOB ON MY DOBB — 141.86.

Red's second consecutive victory.

Slob's second defeat.

The money changed hands.

The receipt reached the Record House two days later.

Edrin placed it beside the Chili wrapper.

"You're building a museum of bad decisions," Pell said.

"Some were profitable."

"That does not improve them."

Kevin's wager mattered for a reason the standings could not show.

Week One had given Red a narrow defeat.

Week Two had given Red an eruption.

Week Three gave the response repetition.

One recovery could be noise.

Two could become trajectory.

The wager did not cause that.

It merely made confidence expensive enough to leave paper behind.

Slob presented the opposite problem.

One and two.

The same record as seven houses.

Yet his cumulative scoring through three weeks stood at 471.74.

Edrin checked the number twice.

Then a third time because it irritated him.

"How is he one and two?"

Oren said, "By losing twice."

"You know what I mean."

"I know what you want me to mean."

The official record was not unfair.

It was doing exactly what it was designed to do.

Wins.

Losses.

Not how impressive a losing total looked.

Not how dangerous a one-and-two house might remain.

Not whether a wager made the defeat more embarrassing.

Edrin placed the receipt in the box.

The paper did not say Red was stronger than Slob.

It said one hundred dollars had changed hands because two people had been willing to attach money to belief.

That was enough.

---

The bridge produced nothing physical.

That made it harder.

Years earlier, at a league gathering, an old story had formed around a bridge, a jump, a lake and a long return.

The details had been repeated enough that Edrin distrusted all versions equally.

What mattered for the present record was narrower.

The story existed.

It belonged to the relationship between ObiWan and Three Dreaded Snake.

And after Jake's Week Three victory, people told it again.

Not because anyone had jumped during the Encounter.

Nobody had.

Not because the old event caused the new result.

It did not.

Because memory liked rhyme.

ObiWan Jacoby — 136.73.

Three Dreaded Snake — 114.50.

Jake moved to two and one.

Phillip fell to one and two.

The city took an old story about a difficult way home and laid it beside the new result.

Edrin refused to put the bridge in the Week Three event register.

He put it in the relationship file.

Oren approved.

This pleased Edrin more than it should have.

"You're learning," Oren said.

"Don't ruin it."

## VI. Stronger Than the Record

By the third week, the standings board had become a public argument.

Nobody vandalized it.

They annotated the walls around it.

Someone posted cumulative scoring totals beside the vestibule.

Pell removed them.

Someone posted them again.

A university group added schedule strength.

Pell removed that too.

A bookmaker taped odds to a pillar.

Pell removed the bookmaker.

By noon, the vestibule looked like a debate had exploded.

Edrin stood in the middle.

One and two.

Seven houses.

Seven different conditions.

His Majesty's Blood had a Belt, a first victory and a missing retained resource.

Slob had one victory and more cumulative production than the record suggested.

The LLC had one victory after two weeks of institutional pressure.

El Niño had one victory, including the Week Two denial of the reigning champion, and now another defeat.

Duckhook had a narrow opening win, a Week Two demolition and a narrow Week Three loss.

Seven Deadly Chins had already demonstrated adaptation once and then lost again.

Three Dreaded Snake had spent heavily to rebuild and still left the week below .500.

Same record.

No sameness.

At two and one, the differences were equally sharp.

Red had now won twice in succession after a narrow opening defeat.

Chili had answered a ninety-one-point Week Two humiliation with a narrow victory and a joke that would probably outlive everyone involved.

Mud Dogs had lost for the first time after beginning undefeated.

ObiWan had followed the champion-beating opener with swamp defeat and then recovery.

Four two-and-one houses.

Four different shapes.

Only DK stood alone.

Three and zero.

Even that was incomplete.

Three and zero described what Wilson had done.

It did not describe what everyone else would now do because of it.

Edrin found Oren at the receiving table.

"I need another register."

"No."

"You haven't heard it."

"I heard the first sentence."

"The standings should stay."

"Generous."

"But we need a campaign supplement."

Oren stopped sorting.

Edrin continued quickly.

"Not public ranking. Not opinion. State."

Oren said nothing.

"Resources. Availability. material expenditures. unresolved decisions. relationships. objects that keep returning. Things that changed even when the record didn't."

"That sounds impossible to maintain."

"We maintain the Archive."

"That was not a defense."

"Things become important later."

"Everything becomes important later if an archivist lives long enough."

"Achane."

Oren's face changed.

Barely.

Edrin pressed.

"We knew the retained cost before Week One. We knew it mattered. Then we stopped writing it because the weekly result was easier. Now it matters again because it's gone."

Oren looked at him for a long time.

Edrin felt suddenly young.

Not wrong.

Just young.

Oren set the papers down.

"What would you call this supplement?"

Edrin had not considered that.

"Campaign State."

"Terrible."

"Material Consequence Register."

"Worse."

"Continuity Ledger."

"Sounds fraudulent."

"You name it."

"No."

"Why?"

"Your bad idea."

Edrin smiled.

Oren did not.

Then he pulled a blank ledger from beneath the desk.

He placed it between them.

"Internal."

"Fine."

"Evidence only."

"Obviously."

"Interpretation labeled."

"Yes."

"Unknown stays unknown."

"Yes."

"Nothing changes the official standings."

"Agreed."

"Nothing becomes true because you wrote it in this book."

Edrin paused.

That was the real rule.

"Agreed."

Oren pushed the ledger toward him.

"Then begin."

## VII. The Second Record

The first entry took forty minutes.

Not because Edrin lacked information.

Because he had too much.

He wrote:

HIS MAJESTY'S BLOOD.

CURRENT RECORD: 1–2.

INHERITED STATUS: REIGNING BELT KEEPER.

MATERIAL CHANGE: FIRST WEEKLY VICTORY.

RESOURCE CHANGE: DE'VON ACHANE — RETAINED FIFTEENTH — UNAVAILABLE REMAINDER OF CAMPAIGN.

CAUSATION: INJURY REMOVES FUTURE AVAILABILITY. NO EVIDENCE IT CAUSED WEEK THREE RESULT.

UNRESOLVED: REPLACEMENT RESPONSE.

Oren read it.

"Acceptable."

Edrin nearly celebrated.

Then he remembered where he worked.

Second:

D0NKEY K0NG.

CURRENT RECORD: 3–0.

MATERIAL CHANGE: SOLE UNDEFEATED CAMPAIGN.

SOCIAL CONSEQUENCE: INCREASED SCOUT, MERCHANT, PATRON AND PUBLIC ATTENTION.

UNRESOLVED: EFFECT OF TARGET STATUS.

CAUSATION: RECORD CONTRIBUTES TO ATTENTION. DO NOT INFER FUTURE SUCCESS.

Oren nodded.

Third:

THREE DREADED SNAKE.

CURRENT RECORD: 1–2.

RESOURCE CHANGE: VERIFIED RECRUITMENT EXPENDITURE.

He listed the principal winning claims.

Seventy-six.

Forty-two.

Thirty-five.

UNRESOLVED: FUTURE VALUE OF ACQUISITIONS; REDUCED RESOURCE FLEXIBILITY.

He did not write panic.

He did not write desperation.

Neither had been certified.

Fourth:

RED LEOPARDS.

CURRENT RECORD: 2–1.

TRAJECTORY: TWO CONSECUTIVE VICTORIES AFTER WEEK ONE DEFEAT.

CULTURAL OBJECT: VERIFIED $100 SELF-WAGER / RECEIPT.

UNRESOLVED: WHETHER RECOVERY PERSISTS.

Fifth:

SLOB ON MY DOBB.

CURRENT RECORD: 1–2.

CUMULATIVE PRODUCTION: 471.74.

TENSION: RECORD AND PRODUCTION DIVERGE.

Edrin looked up.

"Can tension be evidence?"

"No."

He crossed it out.

OBSERVATION: CUMULATIVE PRODUCTION HIGH RELATIVE TO RECORD.

"Better," Oren said.

They continued.

Chili.

Duckhook.

LLC.

El Niño.

Mud Dogs.

Seven Deadly Chins.

ObiWan.

Twelve entries.

Not twelve stories.

That distinction mattered.

Some required a page.

Some required one line.

Some needed only to remain alive where the reader could not yet see them.

By evening the ledger was full enough to become dangerous.

Edrin closed it.

"Now what?"

Oren was at the window.

Outside, the city had begun lighting roads for the next cycle.

"Now you resist using all of it."

Edrin stared.

"Then why did we write it?"

"So that when something changes, we know what it changed from."

That sentence stayed with him.

The Archive was not a story.

It was what prevented the story from pretending the past had been different.

## VIII. The World Remembers

Night settled unevenly across the twelve territories.

In the Arsenal yard, Wilson Look's standard moved above the gate.

No crown beneath it.

No Belt.

Only a mark three weeks had not yet broken.

Scouts watched from the road.

Farther north, another road was already being measured toward him.

ObiWan's people packed for it beneath mountain lamps.

Two and one.

Jake's Week One receipt remained in the Archive.

It had not protected him from Mud Dogs.

The swamp loss had not protected TDS from him.

Proof expired quickly when used as armor.

It lasted longer as history.

In the south, Mud Dogs returned to water.

Two and one.

The swamp did not become smaller because the undefeated line had ended.

At the recruitment hall, clerks closed TDS's Week Three accounts.

Numbers disappeared from available columns and reappeared under spent.

The acquired names remained.

So did the cost.
