# SCHEMIN ’26 ILLUSTRATED BOOK VISUAL MASTERPLAN V1

**Status:** PREPRODUCTION MASTERPLAN  
**Lead:** The Beat Writer  
**Book intent:** adult epic-fantasy chronicle with unusually rich visual support

## 1. Visual thesis

SCHEMIN ’26 should read like a serious epic-fantasy novel whose world can also be **seen, navigated and remembered**.

The benchmark is not “fantasy football with art.” It is an illustrated chronicle in which:
- maps orient;
- paintings immerse;
- artifacts prove;
- diagrams clarify institutions and travel;
- world-memory overlays show consequence;
- character plates reveal inhabitants only when identity can be locked.

Visuals are a **second storytelling language**. They must answer something prose should not have to stop and explain.

## 2. Three-mode visual family

All book visuals belong to one coherent family but operate in three modes.

### MODE A — CARTOGRAPHIC
Ink/engraving/line-led maps, restrained wash, deterministic typography and stable Atlas IDs.

Use for:
- orientation;
- routes;
- regional relationships;
- encounter movement;
- city/institution plans.

### MODE B — PAINTERLY CHRONICLE
Cinematic environmental paintings and character/environment plates.

Use for:
- scale;
- atmosphere;
- material culture;
- emotional threshold;
- recurring location identity.

### MODE C — WORLD MEMORY
Atlas or environment views carrying time: scars, repairs, event traces, changed roads, public receipts, flood defenses, altered objects.

Use for:
- “what happened here”;
- cause/consequence;
- history accumulation;
- showing how Weeks 1–3 remain present.

These modes share the same geography, material grammar, labels and visual hierarchy. They are not three unrelated art styles.

## 3. Visual density doctrine

For the current ~110-page Author Edition, target **20–26 visual interventions**, not 110 illustrated pages.

Recommended composition:
- 2–3 front-matter visual units;
- 5–7 cartographic units in the body;
- 6–8 painterly environment/object/chapter units;
- 5–8 smaller artifact/world-memory inserts;
- optional 4–8 page atlas appendix.

Average: one meaningful visual intervention every ~4–6 pages, but clustered around spatially complex or emotionally important sequences rather than mechanically spaced.

A visual earns space only when it provides at least one of:
1. orientation;
2. scale;
3. simultaneity;
4. material culture;
5. cause/effect in space;
6. persistent world memory;
7. character identity through environment/action;
8. a recurring object whose physicality matters.

## 4. Book-level visual hierarchy

### Tier I — Signature plates
Full page / spread. Rare.
- master world map;
- canonical painterly world plate;
- major chapter threshold;
- high-value character/environment plate once references bind;
- major world-memory map.

### Tier II — Narrative maps and environment plates
Half/full page.
- route maps;
- encounter-site diagrams;
- institutional views;
- city/region insets;
- environment aftermath plates.

### Tier III — Chronicle artifacts
Quarter/half page or integrated margin block.
- Belt / receipt / ledger / empty stall;
- wager slip;
- archive object;
- route token;
- state-change inset.

Do not use decorative filler art.

## 6. Character-plate doctrine

The Twelve are inhabitants, not collectible cards.

Do **not** place twelve profile portraits in the Prologue simply because twelve characters exist.

Instead:
- each principal receives one **earned environmental portrait** when story consequence, not introduction order, earns it;
- every plate shows place, action and material state;
- recurring objects/companions carry identity;
- a later back-matter “Plate Index” may gather produced plates without turning the narrative into a codex.

Current likely first character-bearing plates once gate closes:
- Trade Jedi — restraint / map room / dog / unlit green blade;
- Belt Keeper — title custody under pressure;
- Chili Outlaw — reputation and receipt;
- TDS — resource scarcity/expenditure;
- Arsenal Gorilla Warrior — visibility / sole-unbeaten attention;
- Duckhook — impossible lie / measured environment.

Other principals enter through environment, object, silhouette-safe/nonrecognizable scale or later consequence until their narrative moment earns a plate.

## 7. Front matter

Recommended sequence:
1. half-title;
2. title page;
3. optional frontispiece: League Archive / clean 2026 ledger;
4. **Master World Map**;
5. Prologue.

Do not add a “how to read this world” explainer unless beta reading proves orientation failure.

## 8. Body integration

Rules:
- no visual between every subsection;
- maps appear before/after spatial complexity, not randomly;
- full-page plates prefer recto reveals where pagination permits;
- artifact inserts may sit near the prose that activates their meaning;
- no score graphics that resemble fantasy-app UI;
- no generated prose or labels inside art;
- avoid repeating a scene already fully visualized by prose unless the image reveals another dimension.

## 9. Back matter

Preferred compact visual appendix:
- seven-zone Atlas overview;
- selected route index;
- recurring institutions and locations;
- world-memory index through current completed week;
- optional produced character-plate index.

Do not append every registry field, every roster or every Atlas card.

## 10. Visual rhythm

Across consecutive visual units vary:
- camera distance;
- geometry;
- human/creature presence;
- object vs landscape emphasis;
- light;
- density;
- time-of-day;
- page orientation.

Never let the book become a sequence of centered “hero posters.”

## 11. Art/material grammar

Preserve the existing Prologue art-direction law:
- worn leather, paper, iron, brass, dark timber, repaired stone, cloth, mud, water, smoke, weather;
- damage equals history;
- motivated light;
- atmospheric depth;
- painterly naturalism;
- no plastic 3D sheen;
- no random filigree;
- no generic castle shorthand;
- no arbitrary magic glow;
- no living-artist imitation.

## 12. Reader-facing map count

For the current manuscript:
- **1 master map**
- **2–3 regional/institutional insets**
- **3–4 route/event maps**
- **1 world-memory synthesis map**
- optional appendix expansion

More than ~8 body/front-matter maps in the current 110-page edition is likely too much unless beta reading shows spatial confusion. Additional Atlas plates belong in the appendix or companion digital Atlas.

## 13. Unique Schemin advantage

Traditional fantasy novels can show a static world. Schemin can show a world **changing because real events keep occurring**.

The unique signature should be:
- the same map at different temporal states;
- sites gaining scars/history;
- routes becoming famous;
- institutions reacting;
- visual objects changing meaning;
- owner domains experiencing consequences;
- later editions carrying earlier state forward.

The illustrated novel therefore becomes a visual historical record, not merely an illustrated manuscript.

## 14. Anti-amateur rules

Reject:
- arbitrary decorative art;
- too many maps;
- comic-book panel density;
- repeated portraits;
- fake parchment everywhere;
- unexplained sigils/heraldry;
- hard division borders;
- literal team-name mascotification;
- generated readable scores/text;
- inconsistent map north/orientation/label style;
- changing location placement between maps;
- pristine resets after world-state damage;
- “epic” scale with no causal geography;
- every joke becoming a landmark.

## 15. Board decision

The book should be **more visually rich than a traditional illustrated epic-fantasy edition, but less visually noisy than the Weekly Memo**.

Maps provide orientation.  
Plates provide immersion.  
Artifacts provide proof.  
World-memory visuals provide consequence.  
The prose remains the spine.
