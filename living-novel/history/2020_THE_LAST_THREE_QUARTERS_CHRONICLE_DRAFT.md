# 2020 — The Last Three Quarters

**NONCANON HISTORICAL CHRONICLE DRAFT.** Source: `living-novel/history/HISTORICAL_PRESSURE_LEDGER.md` and `CHAMPIONSHIP_SUCCESSION.md`. Archival narration only. No character identity, geography, institution, or private motivation is invented as canon.

The trouble with a great season is that it teaches the world to expect the same answer.

The Grinch had finished the regular season twelve and one. Across those contests the evidence accumulated slowly, until the accumulated record began to look less like a tally than a prediction. A challenger might have hoped to overturn it. A chronicler had no right to claim that such hope was sensible or foolish. Twelve victories and one defeat spoke for themselves.

Where Mahomies at? had traveled another path through the recorded postseason. First came Guild of Dunces, defeated 130.22 to 94.24. Then Obi-Wan Jacoby, defeated 170.21 to 149.46. Those victories did not erase the strength of the coming opponent. They established only that the challenger had reached the last test.

The final gave neither side the luxury of a comfortable margin.

Where Mahomies at?: 189.19.

The Grinch: 188.44.

Three quarters of a point separated the champion from the runner-up.

An observer reading the numbers years afterward might be tempted to make the difference larger than it was. To describe a sweeping overthrow or an irresistible ascent. Yet the score would not permit such an account. The final was close. The season that preceded it had been lopsided in another way. Both statements were true, and the historical record was diminished whenever one was used to silence the other.

The champion was Where Mahomies at?. The regular-season leader was The Grinch. The names occupied different places in the ledger because the contest had asked different questions at different times.

That distinction can become the substance of a great story, but only if it costs someone something. A fictional institution might prize the crown above the campaign; another might prize endurance above the final judgment. A character might be forced to choose between loyalty to an old standard and obedience to a result no one can reverse. Such consequences are possible material for the Authors Council, not facts established by the league record.

The chronicler's task is narrower and, for that reason, more difficult. Preserve the numbers. Preserve the losing side. Preserve the champion's name without pretending that victory explains every preceding week.

A margin of three quarters is sufficient to decide a title.

It is not sufficient to decide what the world ought to remember.
