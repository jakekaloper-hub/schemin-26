# 2015 — The Record and the Crown

**NONCANON HISTORICAL CHRONICLE DRAFT.** Source: `living-novel/history/HISTORICAL_PRESSURE_LEDGER.md` and `CHAMPIONSHIP_SUCCESSION.md`. Archival narrator; no retroactive character POV or owner identification.

The oldest surviving championship entry in this league's recovered succession begins with a name that sounds almost like a refusal to take itself seriously: Discount Belichick.

The figures beside it refuse to be dismissed.

In the semifinal, Discount Belichick defeated Here, Ok I took L's by 268.5 to 199.5. The latter had led the regular season at nine victories and four defeats. There was no gentle way to arrange those facts. A season's leading record had met a postseason result and yielded. The contest did not erase the weeks that came before it; it simply determined who would continue.

The final paired Discount Belichick with Austin's Hyena's. It ended 211.5 to 187. The champion was Discount Belichick. The runner-up was Austin's Hyena's. Hook, Line, and Zeeker finished third.

That is the complete claim the surviving championship ledger permits. It does not identify which present-day Commonwealth character, if any, should inherit the old victor's intentions. It does not tell us what was said before the final, whether a promise was made, or whether defeat was borne with dignity. Those may be subjects for fiction when better evidence and approved character histories exist. They are not properties of the score.

A historical beginning can be deceptive. Because this is the first season in the recovered sequence, it is tempting to treat it as the beginning of every rivalry and every custom that followed. But an archive's earliest available entry is not necessarily a civilization's first cause. The first surviving page is not the first thing that happened.

The result has its own value without such enlargement.

The regular-season leader did not win the crown. The semifinal victor carried that momentum through the final. The record preserved both the achievement of consistency and the authority of the last contest, allowing neither to swallow the other.

Later champions would confront similar divisions between a long campaign and a decisive ending. Later chroniclers would be tempted to read those divisions as destiny, pattern, or warning. The honest historian can say only that the pattern is visible in the recovered results. Its fictional meaning remains for authors to earn.

So the first entry stays spare.

A semifinal, 268.5 to 199.5.

A final, 211.5 to 187.

A champion named Discount Belichick.

And a warning to every later storyteller: do not confuse the beginning of the record with the beginning of the world.
