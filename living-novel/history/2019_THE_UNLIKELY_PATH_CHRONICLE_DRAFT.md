# 2019 — The Unlikely Path

**NONCANON HISTORICAL CHRONICLE DRAFT.** Source: `living-novel/history/HISTORICAL_PRESSURE_LEDGER.md` and `CHAMPIONSHIP_SUCCESSION.md`. Archival voice only.

The name in the championship ledger seemed to ask for something before the contest had even begun.

Please, Please, Please, please.

It was a name that might have belonged to a plea, a joke, or a dare. The record cannot tell us which. It can tell us what followed.

First came Convicts and Company. The opening playoff contest ended 128.54 to 124.54. Four points separated survival from departure. That was enough to continue and not enough to claim the road ahead had become safe.

The next opponent was Mr. Kenyan Drake Killed Me. Here the scores climbed: 208.97 for Please, Please, Please, please, and 172.84 for the defeated side. The margin widened, but the past had not been rewritten. The first narrow victory remained part of the same championship path.

The final paired the contender with Make Amarica Great Again, which had led the regular season at ten and three. The standings described one kind of superiority. The final would determine another.

Please, Please, Please, please scored 158.30. Make Amarica Great Again scored 113.46.

The championship belonged to Please, Please, Please, please. Make Amarica Great Again finished runner-up; Gunslinger Nation was third.

A storyteller might wish to call this a rebellion against the established order. But the evidence contains no rebellion. It contains three playoff results and a regular-season leader who did not finish champion. Fiction can grow from the contrast only when the world and characters make the contrast consequential.

In the recovered succession, the season marks another instance of the distinction between the long campaign and the final test. It also marks a caution about language. Names can be evocative without being literal. A team called a plea does not prove desperation; a defeated regular-season leader does not prove injustice.

The ledger keeps the names because the names are part of the evidence.

It refuses to let the names do the work of facts.
