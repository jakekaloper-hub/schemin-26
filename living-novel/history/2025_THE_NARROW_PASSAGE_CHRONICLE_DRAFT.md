# 2025 — The Narrow Passage

**NONCANON HISTORICAL CHRONICLE DRAFT.** Source: `living-novel/history/HISTORICAL_PRESSURE_LEDGER.md` and `CHAMPIONSHIP_SUCCESSION.md`. No verified historical result is presented as a fictional in-world event without approval.

There are victories that appear decisive from a distance and precarious when approached.

The Immortal began the recorded postseason by defeating The Chili Cheesers, 185.60 to 147.34. The margin left little ambiguity about that contest's result. The ledger offered a straightforward first step toward the title, and an impatient historian might have taken it for the shape of everything to follow.

Then came Dr. Duckhook.

The semifinal finished 152.20 to 151.80. Four tenths of a point. Not the smallest margin in the league's remembered postseason history, but small enough to deny anyone the comfort of describing the passage as secure. A season could have turned on that difference; for one contender, it did.

The Immortal advanced.

Dr. Duckhook did not.

The regular-season record complicated the story further. Dr. Duckhook had led the season at ten and four. That achievement survived the semifinal as a fact, even as the possibility of a championship did not. The record did not say that the stronger season deserved the final; it did not say that the narrow victor was destined to take it. It merely preserved the two measurements and refused to confuse them.

The last opponent was ObiWan Jacoby.

A year earlier, the championship had belonged to ObiWan Jacoby after a final against The King, 187.52 to 161.68. Now the names at the final threshold had changed, and so had the outcome. The Immortal defeated ObiWan Jacoby 161.07 to 109.56. What had nearly ended in the semifinal became a championship settled by a far wider margin.

The sequence resists a simple moral. The eventual champion was dominant in one round, nearly eliminated in the next, and dominant again at the end. If a writer made this into a lesson about inevitable greatness, the narrow passage would be erased. If a writer made it solely into a lesson about luck, the final performance would be erased. A serious history has to retain both.

There is another temptation: to bind the 2024 and 2025 results into an account of inherited rivalry or dynastic revenge. The archive does establish that ObiWan Jacoby won in 2024 and finished runner-up in 2025, and that Team 8's 2025 title followed a 2024 runner-up finish under the name The King. It does not, by itself, prove the owner continuity, private intentions, or fictional relationships necessary to tell a more personal story. Those connections require the lineage ledger and the Character Director's approval.

The narrow passage is enough for now.

It shows how a season may be remembered by the result that almost prevented its ending. It shows why the defeated belong in the chronicle beside the crowned. And it leaves the Commonwealth's future novelists a question rather than a command: what does a victor carry forward after discovering how little separated survival from disappearance?

A championship answers who won.

It does not answer what victory changed.
