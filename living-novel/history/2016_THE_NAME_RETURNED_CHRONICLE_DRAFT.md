# 2016 — The Name Returned

**NONCANON HISTORICAL CHRONICLE DRAFT.** Competitive facts from `living-novel/history/HISTORICAL_PRESSURE_LEDGER.md` and `CHAMPIONSHIP_SUCCESSION.md`. This piece does not identify historical owners or assign historical identities to current characters.

A name that had stood beneath the champion in the previous final appeared again at the end of the following season.

Austin's Hyena's had been runner-up in 2015. In 2016, the same recorded team name finished the regular season nine and four, the league's leading mark, and entered the decisive contests with a chance to make the second ending different from the first.

The semifinal introduced an almost comic difficulty for anyone who believed names alone could establish identity. The opponent was The Real Austin's Hyenas.

Austin's Hyena's defeated The Real Austin's Hyenas, 220.06 to 195.22. A historian could preserve the contest and its two names. A novelist might see in the similarity an invitation to dramatize disputes over reputation, imitation, or authenticity. Yet the surviving result proves no such dispute occurred. A resemblance of names is not evidence of a feud.

The final was less playful.

The Gunslingers scored 206.88. Austin's Hyena's scored 217.70. The margin was 10.82. A previous runner-up became champion, and The Gunslingers took second.

The records permit a simple sequence: runner-up in 2015, champion in 2016 under the same historical team name and slot. They do not permit the stronger assertion that a particular present-day owner experienced both outcomes. That question belongs to a separate ownership record, one not established by these scores.

Perhaps that is the first discipline demanded by a long history: the difference between a story that fits and a story that can be supported.

A returning name makes continuity visible. It does not make every form of continuity certain. The champion's title can be recorded without placing thoughts in a stranger's head or writing a private biography from a public tally.

At the close of the second recovered season, the succession ledger had acquired something the first could not offer: contrast. The previous runner-up had won. The first champion was no longer the latest. The record could now distinguish remembrance from present possession.

It would take many more seasons before that distinction became a recurring question.

The chronicle would have to wait for them.
