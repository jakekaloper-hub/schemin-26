## Broadcast — a rehearsal scene, not a world-state event
*In a rehearsal version of the roofed stage, the host waits beneath the canopy. The city behind him is still an unresolved skyline design. A stagehand turns a paper ledger around. The weather is not specified. These are blocking notes, not asserted canon.*

**HOST:** All right, I have a question. It's a financial one, so I apologize in advance. Why does the company with the best projections keep losing football games?

**STAGEHAND:** That's not what the report says.

**HOST:** That's absolutely what it says. Right there. First in 2025, second this year. Then four and ten, two and five. That's a company doing everything but making money.

**STAGEHAND:** You're reading two different columns.

**HOST:** It's called research. I read a lot of columns.

**STAGEHAND:** The first is where they thought he'd finish. The other is where he actually is. And 2026 isn't finished.

**HOST:** Fine. We're forecasting a disaster. Better?

*The stagehand does not answer. He moves the ledger forward.*

**STAGEHAND:** Read the old seasons.

**HOST:** Ten and three in 2018. Third place. Nine and four in 2019, fourth. Nine and five in 2021, third. Jesus. He used to be good.

**STAGEHAND:** He's been good more than once.

**HOST:** That's worse.

**STAGEHAND:** Why?

**HOST:** Because now there's a question. If he'd always been terrible, this is a thirty-second story. Guy's bad. Everybody understands. We go to commercial. But you win ten games in 2024, you're the two seed, and then you finish seventh? What am I supposed to call that?

**STAGEHAND:** Football.

**HOST:** That's a very irritating answer.

*Someone in the rehearsal audience laughs. The host points toward the empty chair beside the desk.*

**HOST:** Is Babb coming to explain it?

**STAGEHAND:** We have no confirmed arrival.

**HOST:** Have we asked him?

**STAGEHAND:** We don't have a verified reply.

**HOST:** Great. An interview chair and no interview. Can I write that off as a business expense?

*The host folds the first ledger, then opens it again.*

**HOST:** I'm making jokes, but I actually want to know. When you can win ten, and then you win four, and now you've won two out of seven—what changed? Was it the roster? Was it the choices? Did everybody else just get better? We can't tell from three numbers. Anybody who says they can is selling something.

**STAGEHAND:** So what do we know?

**HOST:** We know he wasn't always this bad. We know he's having a bad run. And we know whoever makes his preseason projections needs to stop getting invited to the earnings call.

*He considers the empty chair again.*

**HOST:** I do want him here eventually. Not to humiliate him. Well, a little to humiliate him. But I want to hear what a guy who was genuinely good thinks happened. And if he beats somebody next week, I want that in the report too. We're not gonna pretend a season ended on a Wednesday.

*He reaches for the night's league standings.*

**HOST:** Speaking of which, eight of these people are four and three. Eight. That's not a standings table. That's a crowd waiting for a bathroom.

*He reads the names—ObiWan Jacoby, Red Leopards, His Majesty's Blood, Slob on my Dobb, Chili Cheesers, D0nkey K0ng, Dr. Duckhook, and Seven Deadly Chins—and stops himself.*

**HOST:** Every one of them has an entirely different reason to be standing there. We should go find those reasons before we start calling them all contenders.

**STAGEHAND:** The other four?

**HOST:** TDS and El Niño are three and four. LLC and Mud Dogs are two and five. And no, the fact that the Mud Dogs are tied with a corporation doesn't give anyone permission to start selling the swamp.

*He taps the desk twice, thinking about the next show.*

**HOST:** Next Wednesday, I want the receipts. Not just the standings. How they got here. What happened on the road. Who they owe. Who they're going to see. And whether the person we've been making fun of deserves the last word.
