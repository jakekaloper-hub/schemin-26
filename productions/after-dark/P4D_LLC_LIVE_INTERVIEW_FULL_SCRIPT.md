### COLD OPEN — THE CORPORATE VISITOR
*The proposed roofed open-sided stage is busy. A ledger sits on the desk; the guest chair is empty for now. The host watches a crew member replace an overfilled stack of papers with another.*

**HOST:** I asked for David Babb's season history. They brought three boxes and a folder marked *miscellaneous consequences*. Three boxes! That's either a football team or a tax investigation.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** Please don't say tax investigation. We didn't investigate any taxes.

**HOST:** Right. This program is not accusing the LLC of financial wrongdoing. We're accusing them of playing fantasy football in a way that feels expensive.

**MANAGER:** And you invited the man.

**HOST:** I did. I'm told he's coming, provided we can get him here through an actual road. Which, in this Commonwealth, is more work than an invitation.

*The manager signals toward an unrendered backstage entrance. The arrival is a **proposed** movement, not a verified journey.*

### ACT ONE — THE GUEST TAKES THE CHAIR
*Proposed staging: David Babb enters from the backstage access point once the journey and venue are approved. The audience's welcome is a screenplay cue, not an established world event. The host rises.*

**HOST:** Mr. Babb. Welcome. I've got to say, you look remarkably calm for a man who is two and five.

**BABB:** Well, you look remarkably confident for a man who put an empty chair on six pages of an interview.

**HOST:** That was an investigative technique.

**BABB:** It was a chair. I could've saved you five pages and a furniture bill.

*They shake hands and sit.*

**HOST:** You understand why people are talking.

**BABB:** Of course. Two and five. Nobody needs to dress it up. But I've seen the title of your little show here. *Damage on the Balance Sheet.* You've already decided the story.

**HOST:** I decided the headline.

**BABB:** That's worse. Headlines travel farther than explanations.

**HOST:** Well, we've got an explanation chair now. You're sitting in it. Explain.

**BABB:** Ask something worth answering.

### ACT TWO — THE RECORD WON'T LET EITHER MAN OFF
**HOST:** Fine. Same franchise ID: ten and three in 2018, third place. Nine and four in 2019, fourth. Nine and five in 2021, third. You had serious seasons.

**BABB:** Thank you. Should've led with that.

**HOST:** Didn't win the championship.

**BABB:** Didn't say I did.

**HOST:** Then 2024. Ten and four, second seed, finished seventh. How do you sell that to the shareholders?

**BABB:** You're doing it again. You say ten and four like it's an embarrassment because you don't like what happened after. Every team in this league spends September praying for ten wins. I got them.

**HOST:** And seventh.

**BABB:** And seventh. I can count. You asked how I got there. If you want the actual answer, ask about who played, who got hurt, and what happened in those playoff weeks. Have you got that?

**HOST:** We've got your standings.

**BABB:** So you've got the front of the book and you're reviewing the ending.

*The host looks at the ledger, then at the manager.*

**HOST:** That's annoying, because he's right.

**MANAGER:** I did mention the lineup data.

**HOST:** Do you work for me or the guest?

**MANAGER:** For the accuracy.

**BABB:** I'd hire her.

**HOST:** You can't afford her. Your projections haven't produced any revenue.

**BABB:** Fantasy points aren't revenue.

**HOST:** See? This is why I wanted the interview. He's already fixing the show's accounting.

### ACT THREE — THE WALL, AND WHAT SURVIVED IT
*The manager presents an archival **copy** of the approved Chapter II account. No original property is taken from the LLC office.*

**HOST:** Let's talk about the building. There's a recorded intrusion and repair history. Chapter Two has carpenters working on the wall and your desk moved six feet. Six feet! I can barely get our guest chair to stay in one place.

**BABB:** You keep calling that a punchline. Something got damaged. People had to put it back together. That's work.

**HOST:** Fair. We don't know what the office looks like tonight. I'm talking about what was recorded then.

**BABB:** Good. Because I don't need someone drawing a fresh hole in my wall every time you need a picture.

**HOST:** That's actually a problem we've had.

**BABB:** I can tell. You had a whole city skyline behind me on the poster before anybody could tell me what road got me here.

*The manager folds her notes with a look of vindication.*

**MANAGER:** Thank you.

**HOST:** Great. I'm being audited by the guest and the location.

**BABB:** You wanted a corporation on the show. We read the fine print.

**HOST:** All right, but here's the real connection I see: there's a physical office that took a hit and kept operating. There's a fantasy franchise that used to be excellent and now can't seem to get its feet under it. What do you think is the difference?

**BABB:** You're asking me to make a speech out of a ledger. Here's what I can give you: what happened to the office is in the record. What happened to the football team is on the scoreboard. Those are not the same problem, and a clever analogy won't solve either one.

**HOST:** That, unfortunately, is a very good answer.

### ACT FOUR — THE FORECAST THAT OWNS THE ROOM
**HOST:** Now the part I can't get past. ESPN's preseason projection had you first in 2025. You went four and ten. This year, projected second. Through seven weeks you're two and five, eleventh. That's six and fifteen across last season and this season so far.

**BABB:** Those projections don't come with a trophy attached. Who thought I was going first? Who thought I was going second?

**HOST:** The model.

**BABB:** Then book the model next week. You can ask it why it's so impressed with me.

**HOST:** That's great. I'll put a chair out for a formula.

**BABB:** And then spend six pages explaining why the formula didn't show up?

*The host laughs before he can stop himself.*

**HOST:** Look, you get to say the projections weren't promises. But the games are real. Four and ten, now two and five. At some point the explanation has to be better than 'wait until next year.'

**BABB:** I agree with the first half. I didn't come here to tell everybody two and five is good. I came here because you were going to tell a decade-long story with three ugly numbers.

**HOST:** That's fair. But you're also avoiding the question.

**BABB:** No. I'm refusing to pretend I can prove the cause from the standings alone. Show me the actual decisions and we can fight about those.

**HOST:** That's a much less convenient guest than I'd hoped.

### ACT FIVE — EVERYBODY ELSE IS MOVING
**MANAGER:** We have the Week Eight schedule.

**HOST:** Right. Red Leopards host The LLC. El Niño goes to ObiWan. Slob meets His Majesty's Blood. Chili meets Three Dreaded Snake. Duckhook gets Mud Dogs. D0nkey K0ng faces Seven Deadly Chins. Six games. Twelve people, all carrying their own mess to next week.

**BABB:** You forgot the important part.

**HOST:** What's that?

**BABB:** Red Leopards aren't a closing argument. They're the next opponent. You don't get to write how it ends tonight.

**HOST:** You're two and five.

**BABB:** And they still have to play us.

**HOST:** Is that a guarantee?

**BABB:** No. That's a schedule. You should try reading one without predicting the finish.

*The host taps his desk twice.*

**HOST:** This is humiliating. The man I invited to explain his record has spent the evening explaining mine.

**BABB:** You're having a rough night. Call it an investment in education.

### ACT SIX — LAST WORDS, REAL CONSEQUENCES
**HOST:** I was prepared to say you don't know how to win anymore. That's easy television. But the history is better than that. You've built teams that win. You also have a real current problem. Both can be true.

**BABB:** That's what I wanted you to say while I was in the chair.

**HOST:** You realize I still have questions about how this happened.

**BABB:** Good. Get the lineup reports. Get the transaction history. Then call me back and we'll have something to argue about.

**HOST:** And the Red Leopards game?

**BABB:** Ask me after it happens.

*They shake hands. Any actual departure route and destination remain proposed and must pass Atlas continuity before this scene can be promoted.*

**HOST, to audience:** David Babb, everyone. The first guest in this building to demand more evidence than our producers. If he wins next week, we'll say so. If he loses, we'll say that too. But we are not interviewing an empty chair again.

**BABB, from beside the desk:** You can keep the chair. It's the only thing on the show with a guaranteed seat.

**HOST:** Goodnight, Commonwealth. Past performance doesn't guarantee future results. Neither, apparently, does a really good projection.
