## Editorial thesis
Babb's tragedy isn't that he can't build a winning team. It's that he has repeatedly shown that he *can*—and now can't convert expectations into a sustained competitive season. The stronger history makes the present slump funnier and more consequential. The monologue should sound like a guy discovering the contradictions with the audience, not reading a Wikipedia article.

## OPEN — THE DESK
[The host is shuffling old league ledgers. The studio and stage directions are proposed production business, not Atlas events.]

HOST: “All right. Good evening, everybody. Beautiful night in the Commonwealth. At least I assume it is. Nobody actually tells me the weather until somebody's gotta move the desk.”

“Look, I had this whole opening planned. Football, the standings, couple guys who clearly shouldn't be allowed near a trade button. But the producers handed me this—” [holds up ledger] “—The LLC's business performance. So... we're doing finance now, apparently.”

“David Babb. Smart guy. Very serious operation. It's an LLC, which, for anybody who doesn't know, is a limited liability company. You keep the business separate from your personal assets. That's a great idea when you have a fantasy team nobody should be personally responsible for.”

[Looks at the numbers again.]

“I'm being mean. I'm gonna be fair to Babb. That's the worst part of this story, actually. You look back and realize the guy used to be *good.*”

## MONOLOGUE — GOOD AT BEING GOOD, BAD AT STAYING THERE
“Ten and three in 2018. Third place. Nine and four the next year, fourth. And in 2021 he's right back in third. That's not some dude accidentally winning two games because the other guy forgot his lineup. That's a real stretch of football.”

“So when people call this a collapse... yeah. There has to be something to collapse *from*. Babb had that.”

[Beat.]

“Then 2024 comes around. Ten and four. Second seed. You're feeling pretty good if you're Babb. You got ten wins. Everybody's talking about you. You're checking your phone like you own the place.”

“And then—seventh.”

[He looks at the audience.]

“Seventh? How'd you get *seventh?* I know the playoffs can get weird, but can you imagine running a corporation where the annual report says, ‘Business was excellent right up to the part where it mattered most’?”

“Now, hang on. Seventh doesn't mean the ten wins were fake. He earned those. That's what makes it brutal. He got through the whole season proving he belonged, and the ending looks like an apology email.”

“Then last year ESPN projects him first before the season. He goes four and ten. Four wins. Ten losses. Imagine coming into a board meeting with the top forecast in the industry, and by December your PowerPoint is just a picture of a guy rubbing his forehead.”

“This year they project him second. Today? Two and five. Eleventh in the league.”

[He lifts two fingers, then five.]

“And look, it's Week 8. He can turn it around. I'm not holding a funeral for a living team. I'm just saying if your business keeps announcing record growth in August and then asking for patience in October... I'm gonna want to see a different accountant.”

“What really bothers me is that I *believed* the projections. I saw first in 2025. Second this year. Thought, ‘Yeah, Babb's back.’ Turns out I'm the idiot. I keep buying the stock.”

## DESK FEATURE — THE CHART THE COMPANY DOESN'T WANT YOU TO SEE
[An archival sports ledger is brought to the desk; no modern screen is required.]

“There's two ways to tell this story. The cheap one is Babb's washed. Everybody laughs, we go home. But the record won't let you say that. Third in 2018. Fourth in 2019. Third in 2021. Ten wins again in 2024.”

“That's the awkward thing about the LLC. There's evidence the guy knows exactly what he's doing. There is *also* a pretty impressive pile of evidence that lately it hasn't been working.”

“And I don't think we know why. Maybe the roster was different. Maybe he had bad luck. Maybe he's making choices that looked brilliant in August and awful by Sunday night. We would need the transactions, weekly lineups, opponents, and points left on the bench to say which. So I'm not gonna make that up.”

“But I am gonna say this: he has won six of twenty-one games since the start of last season. That's two different seasons, and one of them isn't finished. That's not a final legacy. That's a real problem, though.”

“You've gotta appreciate the nerve, actually. The company keeps printing beautiful projections. The season keeps putting them straight into the shredder.”

[Beat.]

“And somewhere in the Storm City boardroom, I picture Babb telling the staff they just need to execute the plan. The plan's had a couple years. Maybe somebody should ask if it knows the score.”

**Editorial annotation:** The last boardroom sentence is a clearly fictional comedic imagining, not a claim about real Babb's words or actions.

## AROUND THE COMMONWEALTH — THE TRAFFIC JAM
[Host slides the league table in front of him.]

“Okay, I can't spend the whole show on a company that's technically still operating. We have twelve teams and, somehow, half the league is standing in exactly the same line.”

“Actually—not half. Eight teams at four and three. Eight! This standings table looks like everybody showed up for the same reservation.”

“ObiWan Jacoby, four and three. The Trade Jedi is taking meetings. That's usually how you know he wants something.”

“Red Leopards, four and three. Apparently a ruined civilization has a better record than an incorporated business. Congratulations on that.”

“His Majesty's Blood is four and three. Still the Belt Keeper. I don't know how many times we need to clarify this: the belt does not migrate because somebody had a bad Sunday.”

“Slob on my Dobb, four and three. A winning record, technically. I haven't seen anything about the operation that suggests the word ‘tidy’ belongs near it.”

“Chili Cheesers, four and three. The Outlaw will hear four wins and decide it's a folk legend. Honestly, fair enough.”

“D0nkey K0ng, four and three. The Arsenal centaur is part of that crowd too. Four legs and still stuck in traffic.”

“Dr. Duckhook, four and three. A golf man in an eight-team tie. Somewhere he's arguing about the handicap.”

“Seven Deadly Chins, four and three. People's Champ, right there with everybody else. There's no parade. There is barely room in the standings.”

“Then you got Three Dreaded Snake and El Niño, both three and four. Snake's got three heads and I guess not one of them wants to admit they're under .500. El Niño is the one weather system in the Commonwealth I don't want to hear a forecast from right now.”

“At the bottom you've got Mud Dogs, two and five, right next to Babb. Which makes it worse. He can look at the standings and say, ‘At least I'm not alone.’ That's not a plan. That's group therapy.”

[He sits back.]

“Eight at four and three. Two at three and four. Two at two and five. There's nobody running away with this thing. That's actually good news for Babb. He doesn't need a miracle to become relevant. He needs to win some football games. Which, I understand, has been the issue.”

“Babb's got a new chance. Everybody does. The difference is tomorrow we start finding out which of these guys was right.”
