## ACT ONE — BEFORE THE LIGHTS
*Under the roof of a prospective open-sided pavilion, a stage manager lays a heavy bundle of papers on the host's desk. The empty guest chair sits a little too far from the desk. A crew member checks its position. No city landmarks have been fixed for the backdrop.*

**HOST:** What's with all the paper?

**STAGE MANAGER:** Eleven years of results.

**HOST:** For tonight?

**STAGE MANAGER:** You said you wanted history.

**HOST:** I wanted enough history to call a guy washed. I didn't say I wanted to study for a bar exam.

**STAGE MANAGER:** Well, you might need to pass one. You're talking about an LLC.

**HOST:** That's good. Keep that. That's our opening joke.

**STAGE MANAGER:** I wasn't joking.

*The host picks up a page marked 2018.*

**HOST:** Ten and three. Third place. Wait. This is Babb?

**STAGE MANAGER:** Same franchise ID. Different name back then.

**HOST:** Of course. They keep rebranding the company. You know what you don't rebrand? A championship. You put that on the wall.

**STAGE MANAGER:** That doesn't mean he wasn't good.

**HOST:** No, it means he was good enough to make this depressing. That's the problem.

*He studies another page, then the next.*

**HOST:** Nine and four in 2019. Fourth. Nine and five in '21. Third again. Hang on, how many ways can you get close?

**STAGE MANAGER:** More than one.

**HOST:** I can see that. We're gonna need more paper.

## ACT TWO — THE BROADCAST
*A hand signal from the wings. The host turns toward the audience, still holding the wrong page. The visible stage operations remain rehearsal business, not approved world events.*

**HOST:** Good evening. Big news in the Commonwealth tonight: apparently the best thing you can do for a fantasy football team is start an LLC. And the worst thing you can do is expect it to win.

*He glances offstage.*

**HOST:** I'm told that's too mean. Fine. Let me be fair. David Babb has run good teams. Not once. Multiple times. Ten and three in 2018. Nine and four in 2019. Third place again in 2021. I'm looking at a guy who knew exactly how to get invited to the party and somehow never came home with the trophy.

**STAGE MANAGER, offstage:** You're skipping 2024.

**HOST:** I'm getting there. God, even my crew has talking points.

*The next paper arrives. He reads it twice.*

**HOST:** Ten and four. Second seed. Seventh in the final standings. Now that's a difficult one. What do you tell the employees? 'Fantastic quarter, gentlemen. We have somehow finished behind people we spent fourteen weeks beating.'

**STAGE MANAGER:** Regular season and postseason aren't the same.

**HOST:** Thank you. That is technically correct, which is the least satisfying kind of correct.

*The host holds up a second column.*

**HOST:** Then ESPN projects him first going into 2025. Four wins, ten losses. First! I've seen more accurate predictions from a guy pointing at clouds.

**STAGE MANAGER:** You picked him, too.

*The host lowers the page.*

**HOST:** Did I?

**STAGE MANAGER:** You said he was a 'quiet contender.'

**HOST:** I say a lot of things quietly. It's called having an inside voice. Doesn't make them legally binding.

*Audience laughter is imagined for rehearsal. The joke is self-directed, not a claim about a recorded crowd.*

**HOST:** And now, through Week Seven, two and five. Eleventh. The company doesn't need another rebrand. It needs a football season that goes the direction of the PowerPoint.

*He looks toward the empty guest chair.*

**HOST:** Where is Babb, by the way?

**STAGE MANAGER:** We haven't confirmed an appearance.

**HOST:** Did he come from the game?

**STAGE MANAGER:** His Week Seven opponent was Slob. The match was hosted in Slob's territory. That doesn't tell us where Babb is tonight. Or whether he could reach this stage.

**HOST:** So he's not hiding?

**STAGE MANAGER:** We have no evidence he's hiding.

**HOST:** You're making it awfully difficult to do a hit piece.

**STAGE MANAGER:** Then don't do a hit piece.

*Silence. The host puts down the jokes and opens the older results.*

**HOST:** You know what? You're right. The easy version is 'Babb's bad.' But if you've been genuinely good four different times, and you fall this hard, I actually want to know why. Bad decisions? Bad luck? Points against? Wrong starters? I don't know. And the standings alone don't either.

**STAGE MANAGER:** Then that's the story.

**HOST:** I hate when you do that.

## ACT THREE — THE OTHER ELEVEN
**HOST:** And while we're auditing everybody's favorite corporation, the rest of the Commonwealth is having its own small medical emergency.

*The manager hands him the preliminary Week Seven game sheet.*

**HOST:** Eight clubs sitting at four and three. Eight. ObiWan lost to Seven Deadly Chins. Red Leopards beat His Majesty's Blood. Slob beat our corporate subject. Chili beat the Mud Dogs. Duckhook edged Three Dreaded Snake. And El Niño beat D0nkey K0ng. Those aren't six independent jokes. That's six events that move people, change reputations, and send people back onto roads they've already traveled.

**STAGE MANAGER:** You left out what comes next.

**HOST:** Right. The part where nobody gets to sleep. Week Eight pairs The LLC with Red Leopards. Slob meets His Majesty's Blood. ObiWan gets El Niño. Three Dreaded Snake meets Chili. Mud Dogs get Duckhook. D0nkey K0ng faces Seven Deadly Chins.

**STAGE MANAGER:** All results undecided.

**HOST:** Yes. I'm aware. I have been informed by counsel that the future hasn't happened.

*He looks out beyond the unapproved stage perimeter.*

**HOST:** There's a weird thing about this place. You can laugh at the man on Tuesday, and on Thursday he starts putting together the week that makes you look stupid. Every name on this page has somewhere they came from. Somewhere they have to go back to. And somebody waiting to hear what happened.

**STAGE MANAGER:** Including Babb.

**HOST:** Especially Babb. Keep that chair. We may need it. But don't tell the artist to put him in it until we know he can get here.
