# SCHEMIN’ AFTER DARK — SPOTLIGHT 001
## THE LLC: DAMAGE ON THE BALANCE SHEET
**P3F literary rehearsal / noncanon / NOT APPROVED FOR RELEASE**
**Continuity:** released Novel Chapters I–II; World State Ledger; Flaim historical 2018–2026; preliminary Week 7/8 intake. This screenplay is a *proposed theatrical broadcast*, not an assertion that a broadcast happened. Studio construction, supporting performers, audience attendance, weather, character movements and prop custody are all unapproved.

### COLD OPEN — THE WRONG LEDGER
*Rehearsal blocking: an open-sided roof. A host is leaning against his desk. A production manager enters with two ledgers. The city skyline stays deliberately undescribed because the proposed Central Basin location has no approved line of sight.*

**HOST:** That doesn't look like the score sheet.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** It isn't.

**HOST:** Okay. Usually the score sheet's the one piece of paper everybody here knows how to read.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** You asked why The LLC keeps losing. I brought the old records and the office history.

**HOST:** The *office* history?

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** It's a company. You want to talk about a company, you should probably know where the walls are.

**HOST:** What happened to the walls?

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** That's the interesting part.

*The host reaches toward the older ledger, thinks about it, and takes the office report instead.*

**HOST:** All right. We're already not doing the opening I wrote.

### ACT I — THE BUSINESS IS REAL
**HOST:** Good evening. Tonight we're looking at David Babb and The LLC. I want to be sensitive here, because there's a very fine line between making fun of someone's football operation and accidentally discovering they have a better filing system than we do.

*He lifts the report.*

**HOST:** I found out their office took damage. Real damage. There's an account of the wall getting repaired. Babb moves his desk six feet to the left. Six feet. That's not the kind of detail you make up when you're boasting about expansion. That's what you do when somebody's still fixing the place.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** You're using the Chapter Two account.

**HOST:** Yeah, and I'm gonna be careful. It shows what happened *then*. It doesn't tell us what his desk looks like tonight. Nobody here knows if the repairs are finished.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** Good.

**HOST:** Don't say 'good' like you're grading me. This is my show.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** It's your rehearsal.

**HOST:** Somehow worse.

*He turns a page.*

**HOST:** But here's the thing. In 2018 this franchise was ten and three. Third. In 2019, nine and four. Fourth. In 2021, nine and five. Third again. These weren't all under the exact same team name, either. The company kept finding new signs to hang up, and the football team kept finding ways to win.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** Until?

**HOST:** Don't rush me. I haven't even gotten to the part where this goes off a cliff.

### ACT II — THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN BROKEN AND BAD
**HOST:** Twenty twenty-four. Ten and four. Second seed. Finished seventh. Now I know, postseason math. Whatever. I'm not saying the ten wins didn't count. They counted. That's why seven hurts.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** You were going to call him a fraud.

**HOST:** Before I read the file, yeah. That's what I would've said. Turns out there's a big difference between 'this man has never built a good team' and 'this man built several good teams and now nobody can explain where they went.'

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** Which one is the story?

**HOST:** The second one. It's a lot more irritating. You can't just point and laugh and leave. Now you've gotta investigate.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** What do you need?

**HOST:** Weekly lineups. Transactions. Who played him. What was left on the bench. I need to know whether he made bad calls or Sunday just kicked his door in. The standings don't tell us that.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** We have his 2025 record.

**HOST:** Four and ten. ESPN had projected him first before the year. First. It was a preseason forecast, not a guarantee, but still. That's like opening a restaurant because everyone tells you it's gonna be the best in town, then realizing by November you've mostly been feeding the staff.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** And this year?

**HOST:** Through seven weeks, two and five. Projected second before the season. Eleventh right now. That's six wins in twenty-one games since the start of last season. Now, I do want to be clear—this season isn't over. But if you're the person making those forecasts, I might take a little vacation.

*He turns and notices an empty chair.*

**HOST:** Is Babb coming?

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** No confirmed booking. No confirmed journey.

**HOST:** Can he get here?

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** We haven't established where he is tonight. We know the Week Seven fixture was away at Slob. That isn't a sworn statement that Babb personally walked into Slob's camp. And the road to this proposed stage isn't even approved.

**HOST:** So the only man we're talking about can't walk in and defend himself.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** Then don't put words in his mouth.

**HOST:** I wasn't going to.

*He takes a beat.*

**HOST:** ...I was going to do an impression.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** Don't.

**HOST:** You're no fun at all.

### ACT III — WHAT'S ACTUALLY ON THE LINE
*The manager places the Week Seven provisional sheet under the desk lamp.*

**HOST:** Here's what we have from the preliminary report. Slob beat The LLC. That puts Babb at two and five. His next scheduled opponent is Red Leopards. So even while we're doing this corporate audit, the season keeps going.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** And the rest of the league?

**HOST:** Eight teams are four and three. I know. That's ridiculous. Red Leopards just got past His Majesty's Blood. Slob's next with the Belt Keeper. ObiWan's due for El Niño after losing to Seven Deadly Chins. Chili, fresh off Mud Dogs, has Three Dreaded Snake next. Duckhook gets Mud Dogs. D0nkey K0ng gets Chins.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** You just read every pair in the league.

**HOST:** I know. I promised we were going to cover everybody. I didn't promise to pretend I'd been on every road with them. We know the results that were reported. We *don't* know each person's whereabouts tonight. That's a separate investigation.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** You also don't know what Babb thinks about any of this.

**HOST:** Correct. I'm trying something revolutionary in broadcasting: not making that part up.

*He looks at the first office report again.*

**HOST:** You know what stuck with me? The six feet. Not because it's funny in itself. A guy's business gets hit; someone repairs the wall; he moves the desk to keep working. There's something stubborn about that. Not necessarily admirable—we'd have to see how he treats people—but stubborn.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** And the football?

**HOST:** That's what I don't understand. A team that can win ten games can also end up with two wins by Week Seven. Same franchise history. Completely different reality. Maybe there's something there about a man who knows how to keep an office standing but can't keep a season together.

**PRODUCTION MANAGER:** You think he'll like that?

**HOST:** Probably not. But it's an actual question. I'd rather ask him something real than make up a stupid quote and call it an interview.

*He closes the report.*

**HOST:** Keep the chair empty. Keep the invitation hypothetical. If he ever comes here, he comes through the front gate like everyone else. And if he beats Red Leopards next week, we put that right here too. No pretending the story ended just because we got a good punchline.

### TAG — THE RECEIPT
**HOST:** Anyway, this has been Schemin' After Dark. If you are a shareholder in The LLC, please remember: past performance does not guarantee future results. Apparently neither do the projections.

*Rehearsal ends. No character entered canon, no visit happened, no studio route became active, and no real-world owner's private words were fabricated.*
